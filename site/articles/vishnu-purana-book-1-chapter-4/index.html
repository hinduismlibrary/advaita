VP 1.4.1

ब्रह्मा नारायणाख्योऽसौ कल्पादौ भगवान् यथा । ससर्ज सर्वभूतानि तदाचक्ष्व महामुने ॥१॥

How at the beginning of the Kalpa did the Blessed Lord Brahmā, named Nārāyaṇa, create all beings? Tell me that, O great sage.

VP 1.4.3

अतीतकल्पावसाने निशासुप्तोत्थितः प्रभुः । सत्त्वोद्रिक्तस् तथा ब्रह्मा शून्यं लोकम् अवैक्षत ॥३॥

At the end of the past Kalpa, Brahmā, the Lord, risen from sleep at night with Sattva predominant, saw the world empty.

Śrīdhara translation

Before describing the promised creation of progeny, Parāśara first speaks of raising the earth, which is its support. Brahmā has awakened from sleep; hence sattva is predominant, as wakefulness brings clarity to the faculties.

Śrīdhara translation

‘Ladle-snouted’ and the following terms are four forms of address. His snout is the ladle, and his face resembles a boar’s mouth. His sound is the deep Sāma melody. The prāgvaṃśa is the front part of the fire hall; his body is its middle. The sacrificial sessions, including the twelve-day rites, are his joints. Pūrta is Smārta dharma and iṣṭa is Śrauta dharma; these two are his hearing.

In the act of creating those to be created, he is only the instrumental cause, since the powers of creation have Pradhāna as their principal cause.

Brahmā, in Hari’s form, is only an instrumental cause in creation, like rain: his mere presence is a common cause, and he does not act separately in each creation. Pradhāna is the principal and distinctive cause; the powers for creation arise from it.
