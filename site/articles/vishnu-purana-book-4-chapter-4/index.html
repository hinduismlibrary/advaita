VP 4.4.1

काश्यप-दुहिता सुमतिर् विदर्भ-राज-तनया केशिनी च द्वे भार्ये सगरस्यास्ताम् ॥१॥

Sagara had two wives: Sumati, the daughter of Kaśyapa, and Keśinī, the daughter of the king of Vidarbha.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

VP 4.4.12

ततश् चासमञ्जस-चरितानुकारिभिः सागरैर् अपध्वस्त-यज्ञादि-सन्-मार्गे जगति देवाः सकल-विद्या-मयम् असंस्पृष्टम् अशेष-दोषैर् भगवतः पुरुषोत्तमस्यांश-भूतं कपिलं प्रणम्य तद् अर्थम् ऊचुः ॥१२॥

When the world’s good course, with sacrifices and the rest, was disrupted by the Sāgaras who imitated Asamañjasa’s conduct, the gods bowed to Kapila, the portion of Bhagavān Puruṣottama, consisting of all knowledge and untouched by every fault, and spoke to him about that matter.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
ततश् चासमञ्जस-चरितानुकारिभिः सागरैर् अपध्वस्त-यज्ञादि-सन्-मार्गे जगति देवाः सकल-विद्या-मयम् असंस्पृष्टम् अशेष-दोषैर् भगवतः पुरुषोत्तमस्यांश-भूतं कपिलं प्रणम्य तद् अर्थम् ऊचुः (tataś cāsamañjasa-caritānukāribhiḥ sāgarair apadhvasta-yajñādi-san-mārge jagati devāḥ sakala-vidyā-mayam asaṃspṛṣṭam aśeṣa-doṣair bhagavataḥ puruṣottamasyāṃśa-bhūtaṃ kapilaṃ praṇamya tad artham ūcuḥ) — When the world’s good course, with sacrifices and the rest, was disrupted by the Sāgaras who imitated Asamañjasa’s conduct, the gods bowed to Kapila, the portion of Bhagavān Puruṣottama, consisting of all knowledge and untouched by every fault, and spoke to him about that matter.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
