VP 1.5.1

यथा ससर्ज देवोऽसौ देवर्षिपितृदानवान् । मनुष्यतिर्यग्वृक्षादीन्भूव्योमसलिलौकसः ॥१॥

How this Deva created the Deva-ṛṣis, Pitṛs, Dānavas, humans, animals, trees, and the rest, whose abodes are earth, sky, and water.

Śrīdhara translation

The creation made by Brahmā in his form as the deity is now queried: how was it made? “Beings” are those whose abodes are earth, heaven, or water.

VP 1.5.2

What qualities, what natures, and what forms of the world did Brahmā create at the beginning of creation, O twice-born? Tell me all of that.

The requested qualities are the guṇas such as sattva, the inherent dispositions such as peacefulness or fierceness, and forms such as two-footed and four-footed bodies.

VP 1.5.4

सृष्टिं चिन्तयतस्तस्य कल्पादिषु यथा पुरा । अबुद्धिपूर्वकः सर्गः प्रादुर्भूतस्तमोमयः ॥४॥

As he contemplated creation at the beginnings of Kalpas, as before, an unintelligent, dark creation appeared.

Śrīdhara translation

Without avidyā, embodied beings could not have agency or experience; this is therefore the first appearance of avidyā. Abuddhi-pūrvaka means arising without deliberate attention, and tamomaya means enveloped in avidyā.

VP 1.5.5

तमो मोहो महामोहस्तामिस्रो ह्यन्धसंज्ञितः । अविद्या पञ्चपर्वैषा प्रादुर्भूता महात्मनः ॥५॥

Darkness, delusion, great delusion, gloom, and what is called utter darkness—this fivefold Avidyā arose from the Great Self.

Śrīdhara translation

The five divisions of avidyā are tamas, self-identification with the body; moha, the sense of ownership toward relations; mahāmoha, desire for sense pleasures; tāmisra, anger when opposed; and andha-tāmisra, clinging to protection through fear of loss. These are the five inversions called kleśas.

Those known as animals and the rest are predominantly Tamas, without knowledge; they follow a wrong path, considering ignorance to be knowledge.

Śrīdhara translation

Beasts and the rest are mostly dark, without discernment, following wrong paths, and claiming knowledge even while in ignorance.
