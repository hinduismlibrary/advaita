MMK 14.8

na tena tasya saṃsargo nānyenānyasya yujyate
saṃsṛjyamānaṃ saṃsṛṣṭaṃ saṃsraṣṭā ca na vidyate || 8 ||

Relation is not tenable between that and itself, nor between one thing and another. There is no thing being joined, no thing joined, and no joiner.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na tena tasya saṃsargo nānyenānyasya yujyate | saṃsṛjyamānaṃ saṃsṛṣṭaṃ saṃsraṣṭā ca na vidyate || 8 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

An opponent says that seeing and the other elements of perception must still exist because contact occurs when the three meet. Candrakīrti replies that contact would have to be either identical with the things that connect or different from them. If it were identical, the eye alone would already be a connection. If different, the eye would connect while wholly apart from form, just as though it could meet any object without relation. Neither account works; without contact, seeing and the other related processes are not established.

The opponent then proposes that even if the relation itself is absent, there must still be the thing being joined, the completed connection, and the one who joins them. Candrakīrti answers that these roles depend on a connection: without it, there is no agent, object, or act of joining. A sūtra likewise teaches that the eye does not see on its own, apart from conditions such as light and form. The wise understand this dependent process without grasping at a self or an independently existing contact.
