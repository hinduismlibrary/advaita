MMK 14.8

na tena tasya saṃsargo nānyenānyasya yujyate
saṃsṛjyamānaṃ saṃsṛṣṭaṃ saṃsraṣṭā ca na vidyate || 8 ||

Association of that with it is not reasonable, nor is association of one with another. The one being associated, the associated, and the associator do not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na tena tasya saṃsargo nānyenānyasya yujyate | saṃsṛjyamānaṃ saṃsṛṣṭaṃ saṃsraṣṭā ca na vidyate || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Seeing and the like indeed exist because association exists. Here, although it has been explained that otherness of seeing and the like does not exist, still there is association, called the meeting, the coming together of the three. And because association exists, seeing and the like indeed exist. The response: This would be so if association of them existed. But it does not. Showing how it does not, he says:

If there were association of seeing and the like, would that be imagined through identity or through otherness? There, if they are one, there is no association. For it is not said that milk alone, independent of water, is associated with water. Even if they are separate, there is no association. For milk standing separate from water is not said to be associated with water. Thus, if association of seeing and the like is imagined when they are one, it is not established, because it would follow that the eye, by itself alone, is associated. Or if there is otherness, even so it is not established, because it would follow that the eye, by itself alone and separate from form and the like, is associated. It is established that when there is no association, seeing and the like do not exist.

Here it is said: Although association does not exist, still the one being associated, the associated, and the associator exist, because they are not denied. And the one being associated, the associated, and the associator cannot exist without association. Therefore association will exist. This too is not reasonable. For when it has been established that association itself does not exist, when association is absent, from where is there the one being associated and the like? There, the one being associated is the object that is the means for the present activity of association; the associated is that for which the activity of association has been completed; the associator is the agent, established with independence in the completion of the activity. Thus, because there is no association, he does not see the one being associated and the like; concluding their denial, he says:

“The one being associated, the associated, and the associator do not exist.”

As has been said by the Blessed One in the Upāliparipṛcchā:

“The eye sees what is conjoined; there it does not see when deprived of conditions. Nor indeed does the eye see form; therefore there is the distinction of conjunction and disjunction.

“Having relied on light, the eye sees a pleasing, variegated form. But the eye that has relied on conjunction never sees.

“Those are the heroes who have gone to nirvāṇa in the world, by whom dharmas have been known according to own-being. They move unattached among the objects of desire, free from attachment, and train beings.

“There is no being and no life here; the lords of victors perform the benefit of beings. ............ There is no being, and they perform the purpose.

“Attachment does not exist here at any time ............; ............ feeling does not exist for that one in the world.

And likewise:

“Having cultivated the path, having set knowledge in motion, all dharmas are empty and without self. For the one by whom these dharmas have been cultivated, there would be limitless eloquence.”

Thus ends the fourteenth chapter, called “Examination of Association,” in the Madhyamaka commentary Prasannapadā composed by Ācārya Candrakīrti.
