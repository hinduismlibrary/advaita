MMK 14.5

anyad anyat pratītyānyan nānyad anyad ṛte 'nyataḥ
yat pratītya ca yat tasmāt tad anyan nopapadyate || 5 ||

One thing is other depending on another thing; it is not other apart from another. That which arises depending on a thing is not reasonable as other than that.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anyad anyat pratītyānyan nānyad anyad ṛte 'nyataḥ | yat pratītya ca yat tasmāt tad anyan nopapadyate || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And showing that, just as the otherness of the visible and the rest in relation to each other is unreal, he says:

Here, this entity called a pot, which is declared to be other than a cloth, becomes other by depending on another thing that is other. Without another entity, apart from another, one thing does not become other. And that entity called a pot, which becomes other by depending on another entity called a cloth, is not reasonable as other than that entity called a cloth. It is to be understood thus: because whatever becomes by depending on something is not other than that, due to dependence, as with seed and sprout, and short and long. And thus he will say:

“Whatever arises in dependence, that indeed is not that; nor is it other than that. Therefore it is neither annihilated nor eternal.”
