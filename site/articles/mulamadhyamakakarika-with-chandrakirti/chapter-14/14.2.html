MMK 14.2

rāgaś ca raktaś ca rañjanīyaṃ ca dṛśyatām
traidhena śeṣāḥ kleśāś ca śeṣāṇy āyatanāni ca || 2 ||

Likewise, desire, the desiring one, and the desirable are to be seen; likewise, by the threefoldness, the remaining afflictions and the remaining sense-fields.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rāgaś ca raktaś ca rañjanīyaṃ ca dṛśyatām | traidhena śeṣāḥ kleśāś ca śeṣāṇy āyatanāni ca || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And just as there is no association, pair by pair or altogether, of the visible, seeing, and seer:

“Likewise, desire, the desiring one, and the desirable are to be seen.”

There is no association of desire and the desiring one, nor of desire and the desirable; and there is no association of all three simultaneously. As with these, so too:

“By the threefoldness, the remaining afflictions and the remaining sense-fields.”

They do not enter into association with one another. “Threefold” means three kinds; the state of being threefold is the threefoldness. By that threefoldness are the remaining afflictions, aversion, delusion, and the like, such as aversion, the one who is averse, and the object of aversion; and the remaining sense-fields, such as ear, hearer, and what is to be heard.
