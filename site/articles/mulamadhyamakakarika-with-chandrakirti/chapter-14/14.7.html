MMK 14.7

nānyasmin vidyate 'nyatvam ananyasmin na vidyate
avidyamāne cānyatve nāsty anyad vā tadeva vā || 7 ||

Otherness is not found in what is other, nor is it found in what is not other. When otherness is absent, there is neither another thing nor that very thing.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nānyasmin vidyate 'nyatvam ananyasmin na vidyate | avidyamāne cānyatve nāsty anyad vā tadeva vā || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: If the cognition of one entity as other depends on another entity, then this fault would follow: “Therefore it is not other than that.” But we do not say this. Rather, here otherness is a universal particularity, and the entity in which it inheres is called “other” even without dependence on another entity. Therefore, on our position, there is no occasion for the faults stated. The response: This would be so if otherness itself existed. But it does not exist. Showing that, when supposed to exist, it is either supposed in what is other or in what is not other, and in both ways is not established, he says:

“Otherness is not found in what is other, nor is it found in what is not other.”

If otherness is imagined to exist in what is other, then what is the point of imagining otherness? For you imagine otherness for the sake of establishing the designation “other.” But that designation “other” is already established without otherness, because otherness is imagined in an entity in which the designation “other” has already been obtained. Thus, for the present, otherness does not occur in what is other. And otherness is not found in what is not other either, because what is not other is called one, and there exists there oneness, which contradicts otherness. Because of the contradiction, otherness does not occur in what is not other either. And since it is now absent in what is other and what is not other, because another entity apart from these two is impossible, it does not occur in an entity apart from these two either. And when otherness itself does not exist, it is established that another entity, the inherence of otherness and the cause of the arising of the cognition and expression “other,” does not exist either.
