MMK 22.3

pratītya parabhāvaṃ yaḥ so 'nātmetyupapadyate
yaścānātmā sa ca kathaṃ bhaviṣyati tathāgataḥ || 3 ||

What depends on another nature is selfless. How could what is selfless be the Tathāgata?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya parabhāvaṃ yaḥ so 'nātmetyupapadyate | yaścānātmā sa ca kathaṃ bhaviṣyati tathāgataḥ || 3 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

The reflection analogy does not establish a Tathāgata with intrinsic being. A reflection appears in dependence on its conditions, and precisely for that reason it is without a self-existing nature.

So too, if the Tathāgata is said to arise in dependence on something else, he is without self-nature. What lacks self-nature cannot be established as a Tathāgata by its own nature.
