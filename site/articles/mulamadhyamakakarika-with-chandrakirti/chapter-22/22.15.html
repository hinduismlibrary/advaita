MMK 22.15

prapañcayanti ye buddhaṃ prapañcātītamavyayam
te prapañcahatāḥ sarve na paśyanti tathāgatam || 15 ||

Those who elaborate the Buddha, who is beyond elaboration and imperishable, are all slain by elaboration; they do not see the Tathāgata.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

prapañcayanti ye buddhaṃ prapañcātītamavyayam | te prapañcahatāḥ sarve na paśyanti tathāgatam || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, when the Tathāgata is naturally peaceful, without own-nature, and beyond all elaboration, those whose understanding is dull elaborate him through conceptions such as permanence and impermanence, existence and nonexistence, emptiness and nonemptiness, omniscience and non-omniscience, and the like:

Elaborations are tied to an object, whereas the Tathāgata is without an object. How could elaborations proceed? Therefore the Tathāgata is beyond elaboration. And, because his nature is non-arising, and he does not pass over to another own-nature, he is imperishable. Those who elaborate the Buddha, the Blessed One, of this kind, through various unreal distinctions of conception, their minds stained and polluted by the false constructions imagined by themselves, are slain by their own elaborations and remain utterly distant from the abundance of the Tathāgata’s qualities. Then, like corpses, they do not see the Tathāgata in this teaching, just as people blind from birth do not see the sun. For this reason the Blessed One says:

“Those who saw me through form, who followed me through sound, have entered upon a false abandonment; those people will not see me. Buddhas are to be seen through the Dharma, for the guides have the Dharma-body. And the nature of Dharma too is not to be cognized; it cannot be known.”
