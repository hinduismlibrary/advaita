MMK 22.10

evaṃ śūnyamupādānamupādātā ca sarvaśaḥ
prajñapyate ca śūnyena kathaṃ śūnyastathāgataḥ || 10 ||

Thus appropriation and appropriator are empty in every way. And how is the empty Tathāgata designated by what is empty?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ śūnyamupādānamupādātā ca sarvaśaḥ | prajñapyate ca śūnyena kathaṃ śūnyastathāgataḥ || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, showing the very point established, he says:

Examined in every way, appropriation is empty, without own-nature, and the appropriator too is empty, devoid of own-nature. And how can the empty Tathāgata be designated by this empty appropriation? It is not possible that something nonexistent designates a Tathāgata who is nonexistent. Therefore it is not reasonable that the Tathāgata is designated in dependence upon the aggregates.

Here they say: “Alas! Our hope is indeed destroyed! For we, having abandoned our longing for the teachers of other doctrines—including Kaṇabhakṣa, Akṣapāda, Digambara, Jaimini, Naiyāyika, and the rest—who are bound in a web of very hard wrong-view creepers and palmyra trees woven by their own conceptions, have strayed from the unerring path leading to the city of Nirvāṇa and remain in the difficult wilderness of saṃsāra not yet crossed; they regard themselves as teachers of the unerring path to heaven and liberation. Having taken refuge, as seekers of liberation and of unsurpassed complete awakening, in the Tathāgata Sun, for the removal of the dense darkness of the cloud-mass of ignorance, we have taken refuge in the one who destroys without remainder the darkness of the doctrines of other wanderers; illuminates the unerring path leading to heaven and liberation; has all directions covered by the exceedingly sharp rays of the teaching of the true Dharma; is intent on opening the lotus buds of the minds of various people to be trained; has become the sole stainless eye for those who are vessels of the true meaning of things as they actually stand; has become the refuge of the whole world; is peerless; is the stainless circle of the Buddha qualities—the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, and the unique Buddha qualities; is the excellent charioteer of the Great Vehicle and the Great Method; is equipped with lofty horses and foot soldiers that are the seven factors of awakening; is intent on drying up the river in the wilderness of saṃsāra of birth, aging, and death of beings in all three worlds; is victorious in the battle with the four Māra-enemies in which arrows fall; and dispels the assault of the Rāhu-seizure of the whole world’s false grasping. By you, who say through such verses as ‘Thus appropriation and appropriator are empty in every way; how is the empty Tathāgata designated by what is empty?’ that he does not exist by own-nature, our hope of liberation and our wish to attain unsurpassed complete awakening have been destroyed. Enough, then, with you, who are like a dense cloud-mass arriving at an unseasonable time, obscuring the great sun, the Tathāgata, and making the world dark.”

We reply: Our hope has indeed been destroyed with respect to people like you, foolish people. For you, wishing for liberation, abandoned the doctrines of other followers of doctrines and accepted the Tathāgata as the supreme teacher, one who is not mistaken; yet, unable to endure the lion’s roar of selflessness, which is exceedingly profound, unsurpassed, and unlike the doctrines of all followers of doctrines, because of the poverty of your spiritual inclination, you are like deer and enter that very path of following the wanderers through the dreadful wilderness of saṃsāra, crowded with the snakes of various wrong views and followed by deluded people who have lost the way that leads to the city of nirvāṇa.

For the Tathāgatas never designate the existence of either a self or aggregates. As it is said in the Prajñāpāramitā: “The Buddha too, venerable Subhūti, is like an illusion, like a dream. The Buddha-dharmas too, venerable Subhūti, are like an illusion, like a dream.” And also: “The nature of dharma is empty, secluded; the nature of awakening is empty, secluded. Whoever proceeds in this way has an empty nature, being one who knows, but not being a childish person.”

And we do not say that the Tathāgatas, who are beyond elaboration, do not exist in every respect, such that a fault of denial would be ours.
