MMK 22.10

evaṃ śūnyamupādānamupādātā ca sarvaśaḥ
prajñapyate ca śūnyena kathaṃ śūnyastathāgataḥ || 10 ||

Thus appropriation and appropriator are empty in every way. And how is the empty Tathāgata designated by what is empty?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ śūnyamupādānamupādātā ca sarvaśaḥ | prajñapyate ca śūnyena kathaṃ śūnyastathāgataḥ || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, showing the very point established, he says:

Examined in every way, appropriation is empty, without own-nature, and the appropriator too is empty, devoid of own-nature. And how can the empty Tathāgata be designated by this empty appropriation? It is not possible that something nonexistent designates a Tathāgata who is nonexistent. Therefore it is not reasonable that the Tathāgata is designated in dependence upon the aggregates.

Here they say: “Alas, our hope has indeed been destroyed! For we, wishing for liberation and seeking unsurpassed complete awakening, having abandoned our longing for the followers of other doctrines—such as the Kaṇabhakṣas, Akṣapādas, Digambaras, followers of Jaimini, Naiyāyikas, and the rest—who claim to teach the correct path to heaven and liberation; having taken refuge in the sun that is the Tathāgata, the stainless circle of the Buddha’s qualities of the Great Vehicle, the peerless refuge of the whole world, the supreme teacher of the great vehicle of the Great Vehicle, the charioteer of the Great Vehicle, equipped with foot soldiers and horses that are the lofty limbs of awakening; who dries up the river of the wilderness of birth, aging, and death throughout the three worlds; who conquers the arrows raining down in battle against the four Māra-enemies; who dispels the seizure and attack of the Rāhu-demon that is the whole world’s grasping at what is unreal; who is intent on removing the dense darkness of the mass of ignorance; who, under the power of great compassion, in various places of birth, made himself available for people to enjoy at their own will like a common medicinal great tree of earth, water, fire, and wind; who, devoted to the arising of benefit for the whole world, undertook those diverse, unsurpassed, and exceedingly varied meritorious actions; who, with his mind set solely on the pacification of the suffering of birth and so forth of every being without remainder throughout the three realms, strove continuously in sequence for three immeasurable eons, or seven; who has attained the station of omniscience, comprehending every aspect; who is intent on awakening the buds of the minds of diverse people to be trained; who is the sole stainless eye for those fit to receive the reality of things as they actually stand; who illuminates all the quarters with the very sharp rays of the teaching of the true Dharma, which follows the path to heaven and liberation and destroys the darkness of the doctrines of all other followers of doctrines without remainder; who is the sole agent of the arising of benefit for the whole world; and who is foremost in compassion for the whole world without remainder, more than for even a beloved only son. By you, who say through such verses as ‘Thus appropriation and appropriator are empty in every way; how is the empty Tathāgata designated by what is empty?’ that he does not exist by own-nature, our hope for liberation and our wish to attain unsurpassed complete awakening have been destroyed. Enough, then, with you, who are like a mass of clouds that suddenly rains down, like a darkness over the world that conceals the great sun that is the Tathāgata.”

We reply: Our hope has indeed been destroyed with respect to people like you, foolish people. For you, wishing for liberation, abandoned the doctrines of other followers of doctrines and accepted the Tathāgata as the supreme teacher, one who is not mistaken; yet, unable to endure the lion’s roar of selflessness, which is exceedingly profound, unsurpassed, and unlike the doctrines of all followers of doctrines, because of the poverty of your spiritual inclination, you are like deer and enter that very path of following the wanderers through the dreadful wilderness of saṃsāra, crowded with the snakes of various wrong views and followed by deluded people who have lost the way that leads to the city of nirvāṇa.

For the Tathāgatas never designate the existence of either a self or aggregates. As it is said in the Prajñāpāramitā: “The Buddha too, venerable Subhūti, is like an illusion, like a dream. The Buddha-dharmas too, venerable Subhūti, are like an illusion, like a dream.” And also: “The nature of dharma is empty, secluded; the nature of awakening is empty, secluded. Whoever proceeds in this way has an empty nature, being one who knows, but not being a childish person.”

And we do not say that the Tathāgatas, who are beyond elaboration, do not exist in every respect, such that a fault of denial would be ours.
