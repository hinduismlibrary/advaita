MMK 22.4

svabhāvaparabhāvābhyāmṛte kaḥ sa tathāgataḥ || 4 ||

If own-nature does not exist, how can other-nature exist? When own-nature and other-nature are thus absent, who is that Tathāgata apart from own-nature and other-nature?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svabhāvaparabhāvābhyāmṛte kaḥ sa tathāgataḥ || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further, if some own-nature of the Tathāgata existed here, then, in relation to that own-nature, the own-nature of the aggregates would be other-nature; and the Tathāgata would exist in dependence on that other-nature. But when the Tathāgata has no own-nature at all, how could the aggregates be other [than him]? To explain this, he says:

A thing that exists would be own-nature or other-nature. But, apart from these two, what other thing exists that is designated as the Tathāgata? Therefore the Tathāgata does not exist by own-nature.
