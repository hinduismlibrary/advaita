MMK 22.16

tathāgato yatsvabhāvastatsvabhāvamidaṃ jagat
tathāgato niḥsvabhāvo niḥsvabhāvamidaṃ jagat || 16 ||

Whatever own-nature the Tathāgata has, that own-nature this world has. The Tathāgata is without own-nature; this world is without own-nature.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tathāgato yatsvabhāvastatsvabhāvamidaṃ jagat | tathāgato niḥsvabhāvo niḥsvabhāvamidaṃ jagat || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here, in this examination of the Tathāgata, the whole world of beings, including gods, demons, and human beings and the rest, has been examined. And just as this world of beings is without own-nature, so, bringing out the absence of own-nature of the container-world, from the circle of wind and so forth up to the palatial mansions of the canopy of Akaniṣṭha, he says:

“This world” means the container-world. Then, asking, “What own-nature does the Tathāgata have?”, he says:

Just as the world is without own-nature, so is the Tathāgata without own-nature. And the absence of own-nature of the world has been shown through the analyses beginning with the analysis of conditions. For this reason it is said in a sūtra:

“The Tathāgata is always a dharma without appropriation, and all dharmas are similar by the Well-Gone One. But childish minds, through grasping signs, move among unreal dharmas in the world. For the Tathāgata is like a reflection of a wholesome, undefiled dharma. Here there is neither suchness nor a Tathāgata, but a reflection is seen in the whole world.”

And it is said in the Prajñāpāramitā of the Blessed One:

Then those divine sons addressed the venerable elder Subhūti: “Venerable Subhūti, are those beings like an illusion, or are they not an illusion?” When this had been said, the venerable Subhūti said this to those divine sons: “Divine sons, those beings are like an illusion; divine sons, those beings are like a dream. For an illusion and beings are nondual; this is not a division into two. A dream and beings too are nondual; this is not a division into two. Divine sons, all dharmas too are like an illusion, like a dream. A stream-enterer too is like an illusion, like a dream; the fruit of stream-entry too is like an illusion, like a dream. In the same way, a once-returner and the fruit of once-returning; a non-returner and the fruit of non-returning; an arhat and the fruit of arhatship; a pratyekabuddha and pratyekabuddhahood; a completely awakened Buddha and complete Buddhahood: I say that all are like an illusion, like a dream.”

Then the divine sons addressed the venerable Subhūti: “Venerable Subhūti, do you say that even a completely awakened Buddha is like an illusion, like a dream, and that complete Buddhahood too is like an illusion, like a dream?” Subhūti said: “Divine sons, I say that even nirvāṇa is like an illusion, like a dream, how much more so any other dharma?” The divine sons said: “Venerable Subhūti, do you say that even nirvāṇa is like an illusion, like a dream?” Subhūti said: “Divine sons, even if some dharma more distinguished than nirvāṇa existed, I would say of it too that it is like an illusion, like a dream. For an illusion and nirvāṇa are nondual; this is not a division into two.”
