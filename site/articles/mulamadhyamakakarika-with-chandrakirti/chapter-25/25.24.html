MMK 25.24

sarvopalambhopaśamaḥ prapañcopaśamaḥ śivaḥ
na kva cit kasyacit kaścid dharmo buddhena deśitaḥ || 24 ||

The calming of all apprehension, the calming of proliferation, is peace. No dharma whatsoever was taught by the Buddha to anyone anywhere.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sarvopalambhopaśamaḥ prapañcopaśamaḥ śivaḥ | na kva cit kasyacit kaścid dharmo buddhena deśitaḥ || 24 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: “If nirvāṇa is denied by you in this way too, then this Dharma, which the Blessed One taught for the world to attain nirvāṇa—by one who follows the mass of beings with beginningless conduct, whose nature knows without error the dispositions of the whole world, dependent on great compassion, accompanied by love for the people of the entire three worlds as for a single beloved son, and suited as an antidote to their conduct—in that case comes to be entirely purposeless.”

It is said in reply: If some dharma by the name of “dharma” existed in the form of own-nature, if some sentient beings were hearers of that dharma, or if the Buddha Blessed One, some teacher, were an existent thing with own-nature, then this would be so. But when:

“The calming of all apprehension, the calming of proliferation, is peace. No dharma whatsoever was taught by the Buddha to anyone anywhere”—

then how could the fault stated by you follow for us?

Here, the calming and non-occurrence of all proliferations and signs is nirvāṇa. That very calming is peaceful, because it is naturally calmed. Or it is peaceful because speech does not proceed and proliferation is calmed, since mind does not proceed. Or it is peaceful because afflictions do not proceed, or because birth does not proceed. Or, through the abandonment of afflictions, proliferation is calmed; when every latent tendency without remainder is abandoned, it is peaceful. Or through the non-apprehension of knowable objects, proliferation is calmed; through the non-apprehension of knowledge, it is peaceful.

When the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, abide in nirvāṇa, peaceful in the form of the calming of all proliferation, by means of the yoga of non-abiding, like kings of swans in the sky, in a sky moved by the wind of their own accumulations of merit and knowledge, because the sky has nothing to grasp, then, because no sign at all is apprehended, it is to be understood that no dharma, defiled or purified, has been taught anywhere, among gods or humans, by anyone, to any god or human.

As it is said in the Noble Tathāgata’s Secret:

“Śāntamati, from the night when the Tathāgata fully awakens to unsurpassed, complete awakening until the night when he will pass into final nirvāṇa without appropriation, in the interval the Tathāgata has not uttered even one syllable, has not declared it, does not declare it, and will not declare it. Yet all sentient beings, according to their dispositions and their various elements and intentions, perceive the various speech of the Tathāgata as issuing forth. Each one separately thinks: ‘This Blessed One is teaching us this Dharma, and we are hearing the Tathāgata’s teaching.’ There the Tathāgata does not conceptualize or discriminate. For, Śāntamati, the Tathāgata is free from the proliferation of the latent tendencies of the net of all conceptualization and discrimination.” Thus, at length.

And likewise:

“Without speech, without syllables, wholly empty, peaceful and pure from the beginning: the youthful one who thus knows dharmas is called a Buddha.”

If, then, no dharma whatsoever is taught by the Buddha to anyone anywhere, how are these diverse conventions of teaching and discourse perceived? It is said: This is the rising up of their own conceptions, for embodied beings followed by the sleep of ignorance, as though they were dreaming: “This Blessed One, lord of all the gods, demons, and humans in the three worlds, is teaching us this Dharma.”

As it is said by the Blessed One:

“The Tathāgata has become a reflection of the wholesome, undefiled Dharma. Here neither suchness nor Tathāgata exists, yet an image is seen in the whole world.”

And this has been explained at length in the chapter on the Secret Speech of the Tathāgata. Therefore, since there is no teaching of Dharma for the sake of nirvāṇa, how could nirvāṇa exist through the actual existence of a teaching of Dharma? Thus it is established that nirvāṇa too does not exist.

And it was said by the Blessed One:

“‘Nirvāṇa’ was taught by the protector of the world as non-nirvāṇa; a knot made by space is released by space itself.”

And likewise:

“Blessed One, those who seek a real crossing beyond saṃsāra do not cross beyond saṃsāra. Why is that? Blessed One, that which is called nirvāṇa is the calming, the cessation of all signs, of all movements and motions.

“Blessed One, these deluded people, having gone forth in the well-taught Dharma and discipline, have fallen into the views of outsiders and seek nirvāṇa as a real thing, just as [one seeks] oil from sesame seeds or ghee from milk.

“Blessed One, those who seek nirvāṇa among all dharmas utterly extinguished—I call them conceited outsiders. The yogin, Blessed One, who is correctly practicing does not produce or cease any dharma at all; nor does he desire the attainment or direct realization of any dharma.” Thus, at length.
