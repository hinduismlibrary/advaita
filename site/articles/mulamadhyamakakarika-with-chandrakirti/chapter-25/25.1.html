MMK 25.1

yadi śūnyam idaṃ sarvam udayo nāsti na vyayaḥ
prahāṇād vā nirodhād vā kasya nirvāṇam iṣyate || 1 ||

If all this is empty, there is no arising and no destruction. Through abandonment or cessation, for whom is nirvāṇa desired?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi śūnyam idaṃ sarvam udayo nāsti na vyayaḥ | prahāṇād vā nirodhād vā kasya nirvāṇam iṣyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here, the Blessed One has explained two nirvāṇas for persons who have lived the holy life, entered the Tathāgata’s teaching, and practice in accordance with the Dharma: one with a remainder of appropriation, and one without a remainder of appropriation.

Of these, through the complete abandonment of the collection of afflictions, beginning with ignorance and passion, nirvāṇa with a remainder is accepted. Here, “that in which self-affection is placed” is “appropriation” (upadhi). By the word “appropriation” are meant the five aggregates of appropriation, which are the basis for the designation of self. “Remainder” is what remains. The appropriation itself is the remainder, the remainder of appropriation; that which occurs together with the remainder of appropriation is “with a remainder of appropriation.” What is that? Nirvāṇa. Only the aggregates remain, devoid of the thieves of the view of the perishable collection and the other afflictions, by similarity with the remaining of only a village after the whole band of thieves has been killed. That is nirvāṇa with a remainder of appropriation.

But that nirvāṇa in which not even the five aggregates exist is nirvāṇa without a remainder of appropriation, because the remainder of appropriation has departed from it. It is similar to the destruction even of the village after the whole band of thieves has been killed. And it is with reference to that that this is said:

“‘The body was broken; perception ceased; feeling too was burned up; all formations subsided; consciousness reached its end.’”

And likewise:

“‘With the body not collapsed, he endured feeling; the liberation of his mind is like the nirvāṇa of a lamp.’”

Thus, nirvāṇa without a remainder is attained through the cessation of the aggregates. How are these two nirvāṇas reasonable if there is cessation of the afflictions and the aggregates? But if all this is empty, nothing at all arises and nothing at all ceases. Then whence are afflictions, or whence are aggregates, through whose cessation there would be nirvāṇa? Therefore the own-being of entities does indeed exist.
