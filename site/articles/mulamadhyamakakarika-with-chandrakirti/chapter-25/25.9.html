MMK 25.9

ya ājavaṃjavībhāva upādāya pratītya vā
so 'pratītyānupādāya nirvāṇam upadiśyate || 9 ||

Whatever is the coming-and-going state, dependent or arisen in dependence, that is taught as nirvāṇa when it proceeds without dependence, without appropriation.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ya ājavaṃjavībhāva upādāya pratītya vā | so 'pratītyānupādāya nirvāṇam upadiśyate || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone asks: “If nirvāṇa is neither an existent thing nor a non-existent thing, then what is nirvāṇa?” It is said: Here the Blessed Buddhas teach:

“Whatever is the coming-and-going state, dependent or arisen in dependence, that is taught as nirvāṇa when it proceeds without dependence, without appropriation.”

Here, “coming-and-going state” means the sequence of coming and going, existence, birth, and death. Sometimes this coming-and-going state is designated as existing in dependence on a collection of causes and conditions, like long and short; sometimes it is designated as arising, like the light of a lamp or a seed and sprout.

In every way, whether it is designated in dependence or established as arising dependently, the non-occurrence, without dependence or appropriation, of this continuous series of birth and death is designated as nirvāṇa. And mere non-occurrence cannot be conceived as either an existent thing or a non-existent thing. Thus nirvāṇa is neither an existent thing nor a non-existent thing.

Alternatively, for those who hold that formations transmigrate, the arising and destruction that occur dependently, one after another, are called nirvāṇa when they no longer proceed dependently.

But for those who hold that a person transmigrates, the coming-and-going state of that person, who is not expressible as either eternal or non-eternal, proceeds in dependence on this or that appropriation. That very state, proceeding again and again in dependence on appropriation, is designated “nirvāṇa” when now it no longer proceeds in dependence, without appropriation. And it is not possible to conceive mere non-occurrence of formations or of a person as either an existent thing or a non-existent thing. For this reason too, it is reasonable that nirvāṇa is neither an existent thing nor a non-existent thing.
