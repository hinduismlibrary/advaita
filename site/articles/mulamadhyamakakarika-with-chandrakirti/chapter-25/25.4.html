MMK 25.4

bhāvas tāvan na nirvāṇaṃ jarāmaraṇalakṣaṇam
prasajyetāsti bhāvo hi na jarāmaraṇaṃ vinā || 4 ||

An existent thing is not nirvāṇa, for aging and death would follow; for an existent thing is not without aging and death.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvas tāvan na nirvāṇaṃ jarāmaraṇalakṣaṇam | prasajyetāsti bhāvo hi na jarāmaraṇaṃ vinā || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Those who do not accept nirvāṇa as the calming of all conceptual proliferation, but conceive nirvāṇa as existence, non-existence, both, or neither, are addressed:

“An existent thing is not nirvāṇa; aging and death would follow; for an existent thing is not without aging and death.”

Here, some who are attached to nirvāṇa as an existent thing say: “Here nirvāṇa is an entity of the nature of cessation, like a bridge that certainly blocks the stream of water: it brings about the cessation of the flow of the continuum of afflictions, action, and birth. An entity whose nature is nonexistent is not seen to perform such an action.”

But was it not said that the exhaustion, dispassion, and cessation of this craving accompanied by delight and passion is nirvāṇa? Mere exhaustion cannot be an existent thing. And it was said:

“‘The liberation of that mind is like the nirvāṇa of a lamp.’”

Nor is the cessation of a lamp reasonably an existent thing.

It is said: This is not to be understood as “the exhaustion of craving is the exhaustion of craving.” Rather, when the entity called nirvāṇa exists, the exhaustion of craving occurs in it; that is to be called “the exhaustion of craving.” The lamp is only an example. There too, it is to be understood that the liberation of mind occurs when that [nirvāṇa] exists.

When nirvāṇa has thus been established as an existent thing, the teacher examines it: “An existent thing is not nirvāṇa.” Why? It would follow that it has aging and death as its characteristic, because an existent thing is never separated from the characteristic of aging and death. Then that itself would not be nirvāṇa, because it has aging and death as its characteristic, like consciousness. This is the intended meaning.

And making clear precisely that it is not separated from the characteristic of aging and death, he says: “For an existent thing is not without aging and death.” For that which is free from aging and death cannot be an existent thing, like a sky-flower, because it is free from aging and death.
