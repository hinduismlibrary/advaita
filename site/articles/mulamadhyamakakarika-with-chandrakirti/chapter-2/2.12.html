Investigation of Coming and Going

MMK 2.12

gate nārabhyate gantuṃ gataṃ nārabhyate 'gate
nārabhyate gamyamāne gantum ārabhyate kuha || 12 ||

On what has been traversed, going is not begun; on what has not been traversed, going is not begun; on what is being traversed, going is not begun. Where is one to begin to go?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gate nārabhyate gantuṃ gataṃ nārabhyate 'gate | nārabhyate gamyamāne gantum ārabhyate kuha || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: “Going indeed exists, because its beginning exists. Here, Devadatta begins going by bringing standing to an end. And one does not begin to make a covering and the like from nonexistent tortoise hair.” In reply: Going would exist if its beginning existed. Why? Because:

“On what has been traversed, going is not begun; on what has not been traversed, going is not begun; on what is being traversed, going is not begun. Where is one to begin to go?”

If the beginning of going were to occur, it would begin either on a path that has been traversed, on one that has not been traversed, or on one that is being traversed. There, going is not begun on what has been traversed, for that is precisely what has ceased to be an act of going. If going were begun there, it would no longer be “traversed,” because of the contradiction between past and present. Going is also not begun on what has not been traversed, because of the contradiction between future and present. Nor is it begun on what is being traversed, because that does not exist, and because two acts and two agents would follow. Thus, seeing that the beginning of going occurs nowhere, he says: “Where is one to begin to go?”
