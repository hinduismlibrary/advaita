MMK 2.8

gantā na gacchati tāvad agantā naiva gacchati
anyo ganturagantuśca kastṛtīyo hi gacchati || 8 ||

First, the goer does not go; the non-goer certainly does not go. Who is the third, apart from the goer and the non-goer, who goes?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gantā na gacchati tāvad agantā naiva gacchati | anyo ganturagantuśca kastṛtīyo hi gacchati || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: “Going indeed exists, because one who possesses it is designated by it. Here, a goer is connected with going, and because he is connected with it, he goes. If going did not exist, the designation ‘Devadatta, who possesses going, goes’ would not occur, just as without a staff the designation ‘staff-bearer’ does not occur.” In reply: Going would exist if there were the expression “he goes.” Why? Because:

“First, the goer does not go; the non-goer certainly does not go. Who is the third, apart from the goer and the non-goer, who goes?”

Here, the goer is one designated “he goes.” First, he does not go; how he does not go will be explained in the next three verses. The non-goer also does not go. For a non-goer is one who is without the act of going, whereas the word “goes” arises in connection with the act of going. If he is a non-goer, how does he go? If he goes, he is not a non-goer. If it is said that one other than both of them goes, that is not so. For who is the third, apart from the goer and the non-goer, who could be conceived to go? Therefore, going does not exist.
