MMK 2.3

gamyamānasya gamanaṃ kathaṃ nāmopapatsyate
gamyamāne dvigamanaṃ yadā naivopapadyate || 3 ||

How would the going of what is being traversed be possible, when double going in what is being traversed does not at all arise?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gamyamānasya gamanaṃ kathaṃ nāmopapatsyate | gamyamāne dvigamanaṃ yadā naivopapadyate || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Even if it is conceived in this way, what is being traversed is not traversed, he says:

“How would the going of what is being traversed be possible, when double going in what is being traversed does not at all arise?”

Here, you wish the designation “what is being traversed” for a path solely through its connection with an act of going, and therefore you say, “It is being traversed.” There is one act of going here; let the path have the designation “what is being traversed” through that, as you please. But a further connection with an act of going, through the expression “it is being traversed,” is not reasonable for what is being traversed.

“How would the going of what is being traversed be possible?”

He states the reason: “When double going in what is being traversed does not at all arise.” “What is being traversed” means “is being gone over”; going twice is double going. Since one act of going is already applied to “what is being traversed,” and there is no second act, when the expression “is being gone over” is not at all possible without an act of going, then the complete sentence “what is being traversed is being traversed” has no meaning. That is the intended sense. Only “what is being traversed” by itself is possible, because there is no second act, but not “is being traversed.”
