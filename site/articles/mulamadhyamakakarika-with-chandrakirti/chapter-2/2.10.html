MMK 2.10

pakṣaḥ = position; gantā = goer; gacchati = goes; iti = thus; yasya = for whom; tasya = for him; prasajyate = follows; gamanena = by going; vinā = without; gantā = goer; gantuḥ = of the goer; gamanam = going; icchataḥ = for one who desires

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pakṣo gantā gacchatīti yasya tasya prasajyate | gamanena vinā gantā ganturgamanamicchataḥ || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

For someone who holds that a person is called a goer precisely because that person is connected with movement, saying “the goer goes” again requires a second action. The first action has already made the person a goer; the movement expressed by “goes” would have to be another. If the speaker wants the goer’s own movement, the sentence would also require a goer without movement. This is not coherent.
