Investigation of Coming and Going

MMK 2.25

tasmād gatiśca gantā ca gantavyaṃ ca na vidyate || 25 ||

Therefore, going, the goer, and what is to be gone to do not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tasmād gatiśca gantā ca gantavyaṃ ca na vidyate || 25 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

As it is said in the Ārya Akṣayamatinirdeśa Sūtra:

“‘Non-going,’ Venerable Śāriputra, is this state of moving inward. ‘Going,’ Venerable Śāriputra, is this state of moving outward. Where there is neither the state of moving inward nor the state of moving outward, that is the state of the Noble Ones. In the absence of a state of coming or going, non-arrival and non-going are the course of the Noble Ones.”

If the seed itself passes over into the sprout, it would be the seed itself, not the sprout, and the fault of permanence would follow. But if the sprout comes from something else, the fault of being causeless would follow. And a causeless thing is not produced, just like a donkey’s horn. For this very reason, the Blessed One says:

“Just as a sprout [arises] from an existent seed, it is not that very seed; nor is it something other than it, and it is not exactly that. In this way the nature of things is neither annihilation nor permanence.”

And:

“An impression from a seal is seen, but no transfer of the seal is apprehended. It is not in that [seal], nor is it from something else. In this way formations are neither annihilation nor permanence.”

Likewise:

“On the surface of a mirror, and in an oil vessel, [one] looks at an adorned woman’s face. Having produced desire there, the foolish one runs about seeking a beloved. When there is no transfer of the face, the face is never found in the image. Just as that foolish one gives rise to desire, so know all things by this example.”

Likewise, in the Ārya Samādhirāja Sūtra:

“At that time the immaculate Jina, the Ten-Powered One, speaks this excellent samādhi: ‘All existence is like a dream; no one is born, and no one dies.

“‘No being is found, no living entity, no person; these things are like foam and a plantain trunk. They are like illusions, like lightning in the sky, like the moon in water, like mirages.

“‘No person dies in this world and passes to or goes to another world. And action never perishes: what has been done gives a dark or bright fruit while one wanders in saṃsāra.

“‘There is no permanence, no annihilation, no accumulation of action, and no continuity. Nor does that very one, having done it, experience it again; nor does another experience what has been done.

“‘There is no transmigration and no return. Not everything exists, nor is everything nonexistent. No purification of view, state, or course is seen here; nor is there the conduct of beings or a course of perfect peace.

“‘For the three realms are like a dream, subject to control, light, broken, impermanent, and like an illusion. Nothing has come, nor has anything arrived here; continuities are always empty and signless.

“‘Non-arising, peace, the signless state: this is the sphere of the Sugatas and the qualities of the Jinas. Powers, dhāraṇīs, the strength of the Ten-Powered Ones—this is the highest excellence of the Buddhas.

“‘A collection of excellent white qualities of Dharma; the supreme strength of qualities, knowledge, and dhāraṇī; the highest method of displaying miraculous powers; the method of attaining the excellent five superknowledges.’”

And so on at length.

Thus ends the second chapter, called “Investigation of Going and Coming,” in the Madhyamaka commentary, the Prasannapadā, composed by Ācārya Candrakīrti.
