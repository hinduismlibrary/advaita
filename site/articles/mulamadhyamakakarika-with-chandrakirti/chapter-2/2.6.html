MMK 2.6

dvau gantārau prasajyete prasakte gamanadvaye
gantāraṃ hi tiraskṛtya gamanaṃ nopapadyate || 6 ||

If two acts of going follow, then two goers follow. For going cannot be established apart from a goer.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dvau gantārau prasajyete prasakte gamanadvaye | gantāraṃ hi tiraskṛtya gamanaṃ nopapadyate || 6 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

If there are two movements, there must be two goers, since an action depends on its means or on an agent. The action of going is grounded in an agent and therefore requires a goer. But in the one person Devadatta who is walking, no second agent is found. Without a second goer, there cannot be two goings; so what is being gone over cannot be gone over in the way proposed.

One might object that a single Devadatta can perform many actions: while standing, he may speak or see. Candrakīrti answers that the operative cause is a capacity, not the bare substance. Because the actions differ, the capacities by which they are performed differ too; standing does not make someone a speaker. Even if the substance is one, the relevant capacity is not. Nor is one and the same agent seen performing two identical actions in the same respect. Thus one goer cannot perform two movements of the same kind.
