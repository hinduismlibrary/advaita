MMK 2.14

gataṃ kiṃ gamyamānaṃ kim agataṃ kiṃ vikalpyate
adṛśyamāna ārambhe gamanasyaiva sarvathā || 14 ||

When the beginning of going is not seen at all, what is conceived as traversed, what as being traversed, and what as not traversed?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gataṃ kiṃ gamyamānaṃ kim agataṃ kiṃ vikalpyate | adṛśyamāna ārambhe gamanasyaiva sarvathā || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Although the beginning of going does not occur in what has been traversed, what has not been traversed, or what is being traversed, those three nevertheless exist; and it is not reasonable for them to exist if there is no going. In reply: Going would exist if these alone existed. For when the beginning of the act of going exists, where that act has ceased is conceived as “traversed”; where it is present, as “being traversed”; and where it has not arisen, as “not traversed.” But when there is no beginning of the act of going at all, then:

“When the beginning of going is not seen at all, what is conceived as traversed, what as being traversed, and what as not traversed?”

When the beginning is not apprehended, why is the imaginary threefold path conceived? Or what going is the cause of those designations? This is unreasonable.
