MMK 2.13

na pūrvaṃ gamanārambhād gamyamānaṃ na vā gatam
yatrārabhyeta gamanam agate gamanaṃ kutaḥ || 13 ||

Before going begins, there is neither what is being traversed nor what has been traversed. Where would going be begun? How could there be going on what has not been traversed?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na pūrvaṃ gamanārambhād gamyamānaṃ na vā gatam | yatrārabhyeta gamanam agate gamanaṃ kutaḥ || 13 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

To show how going is not possible, he says:

“Before going begins, there is neither what is being traversed nor what has been traversed. Where would going be begun? How could there be going on what has not been traversed?”

When Devadatta is standing, he does not then begin to go. Before he begins to go, there is neither a stretch of path being traversed nor one that has been traversed where going could be begun. Therefore, because there is neither what has been traversed nor what is being traversed, going does not begin in either of these two.

Someone might say: “Although before the beginning of going there is neither what has been traversed nor what is being traversed, still what has not been traversed exists, and going could begin there.” In reply: “How could there be going on what has not been traversed?” What has not been traversed is what has not yet been entered by the act of going and into which the act of going has not begun. The beginning of going there is incoherent. Thus he says: “How could there be going on what has not been traversed?”
