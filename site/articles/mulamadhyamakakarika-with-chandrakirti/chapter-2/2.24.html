MMK 2.24

sadbhūto gamanaṃ gantā triprakāraṃ na gacchati
nāsadbhūto ’pi gamanaṃ triprakāraṃ sa gacchati || gamanaṃ sadasadbhūtaṃ triprakāraṃ na gacchati || 24 ||

An existent goer does not go to the three kinds of going; a non-existent goer also does not go to the three kinds of going.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sadbhūto gamanaṃ gantā triprakāraṃ na gacchati | nāsadbhūto ’pi gamanaṃ triprakāraṃ sa gacchati || gamanaṃ sadasadbhūtaṃ triprakāraṃ na gacchati || 24 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus:

“An existent goer does not go to the three kinds of going; a non-existent goer also does not go to the three kinds of going.”

Here, “going” means that which is gone. An existent goer is one connected with the act of going; a non-existent goer is one devoid of the act of going; and a goer who is both existent and non-existent has the nature of both positions. Going, too, is to be understood as threefold in relation to its connection with the act of going. In that case, an existent goer does not go to going that is threefold—existent, non-existent, or both. This will be explained in the examination of action and agent. Likewise, a non-existent goer does not go to the three kinds of going, and neither does one who is both existent and non-existent; this too will be explained there.
