MMK 7.15

utpadyamānamutpattāvidaṃ na kramate yadā
kathamutpadyamānaṃ tu pratītyotpattimucyate || 15 ||

When this does not enter into the process of arising, how is what is arising said to depend on arising?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

utpadyamānamutpattāvidaṃ na kramate yadā | kathamutpadyamānaṃ tu pratītyotpattimucyate || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Only what is arising arises, not what has arisen and not what has not arisen.” Or you may think: “Since what is arising is not possible apart from what has arisen and what has not arisen, that is not so; here what is connected with the activity of arising is called ‘what is arising.’ Therefore, when arising exists, what is arising is established in dependence on arising. Thus what is arising itself arises, and what is arising produces arising.” Reply:

“When this does not enter into the process of arising, how is what is arising said to depend on arising?”

What was said—“what is arising exists in dependence on arising, and that is what arises”—should, indeed, be stated specifically: “This, namely what is arising, exists in dependence on its arising.” But it is not stated in this way. For it cannot be determined specifically that this is that which is arising, because it is unarisen and is grasped through its sign. Thus, because what is arising is impossible, the activity of arising too does not exist. When arising does not exist, how could what is arising depend on it? Therefore, it is unreasonable that what is arising arises and that it produces arising.
