MMK 7.1

Investigation of Birth, Abiding and Perishing

MMK 7.1

yadi saṃskṛta utpādastatra yuktā trilakṣaṇī
athāsaṃskṛta utpādaḥ kathaṃ saṃskṛtalakṣaṇam || 1 ||

If arising is conditioned, the three characteristics are suitable there. But if arising is unconditioned, how is it a characteristic of the conditioned?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi saṃskṛta utpādastatra yuktā trilakṣaṇī | athāsaṃskṛta utpādaḥ kathaṃ saṃskṛtalakṣaṇam || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “The aggregates, sense-fields, and elements certainly exist as conditioned things, because the characteristics of the conditioned, beginning with arising, exist. For the Blessed One said: ‘Monks, these are the three characteristics of the conditioned: arising of the conditioned is discerned, its passing away is discerned, and its alteration while abiding is discerned.’ And the characteristic of birth and the rest does not exist for what does not exist, like a donkey’s horn. Therefore, because the characteristics of the conditioned have been taught, the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements certainly exist.” Reply: The aggregates, sense-fields, and elements would be conditioned things according to your view if the characteristics beginning with arising existed. Here, is this arising, when accepted as a characteristic of the conditioned, accepted as conditioned or as unconditioned? In this regard:

“If arising is conditioned, the three characteristics are suitable there. But if arising is unconditioned, how is it a characteristic of the conditioned?”

That is the meaning: it is not a characteristic of the conditioned.
