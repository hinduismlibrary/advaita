MMK 7.34

yathā māyā yathā svapno gandharvanagaraṃ yathā
tathotpādastathā sthānaṃ tathā bhaṅga udāhṛtam || 34 ||

As illusion, as dream, as a gandharva city is said, so are arising, abiding, and destruction declared.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yathā māyā yathā svapno gandharvanagaraṃ yathā | tathotpādastathā sthānaṃ tathā bhaṅga udāhṛtam || 34 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “If it has been determined that arising, abiding, and destruction do not exist, then how should this statement cited from the sage, who knows without obstruction, be understood: ‘Monks, arising of the conditioned is discerned, its passing away is discerned, and its alteration while abiding is discerned’?” Reply:

“As illusion, as dream, as a gandharva city is said, so are arising, abiding, and destruction declared.”

Just as illusions and the rest, unarisen by their own nature and nonexistent, are called “illusion” and so forth and are accessible to the world’s awareness of illusion and the rest, so too arising and the rest, though nonexistent by their own nature, have been taught by the Blessed One, who wished to benefit people to be guided in that way, merely because they are established in the world. For this very reason it is said:

“Having made the conception ‘This is my wife’ upon a heap of skin, sinews, flesh, and bones, fools and simpletons generate passion for women, not knowing that they are like an illusion.

“Just as a young woman in a dream sees a son born and dead; greatly delighted when he is born, she becomes miserable at his death: understand all dharmas as examples in this way.

“Just as a gandharva city, a mirage, an illusion, and a dream, all are empty of own-nature in their conception of signs; understand all dharmas as examples in this way.

“There is no conceptualization for those seers, who are free from conditioned and unconditioned dharmas, who have attained the unconditioned in all states of existence, and who are always free from views.

“Eternal, without passion, without hatred, undeluded, their minds concentrated on its nature: this is the strength of concentration, the powerful one, who knows these empty dharmas.”

Thus, in the Prasannapadā, the Madhyamaka commentary composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti, the seventh chapter, called “Examination of the Conditioned,” is complete.
