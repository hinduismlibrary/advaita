MMK 7.10

kathamutpadyamānena pradīpena tamo hatam
notpadyamāno hi tamaḥ pradīpaḥ prāpnute yadā || 10 ||

How is darkness destroyed by a lamp while it is arising? For the lamp, not yet arisen, does not reach darkness.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kathamutpadyamānena pradīpena tamo hatam | notpadyamāno hi tamaḥ pradīpaḥ prāpnute yadā || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “What was said—‘There is no darkness in the lamp’—is rightly said when darkness has not been destroyed. But because darkness is destroyed by the lamp precisely while it is arising, it is reasonable that there is darkness in the lamp, and that there is no darkness where the lamp is. If the lamp destroys darkness, then pots and the rest would not be perceived even when the lamp has arisen, just as before it had arisen, because darkness has not been destroyed. Therefore, the lamp certainly has an illumination characterized by destroying darkness, and this is done by the lamp while it is arising.” Reply:

“How is darkness destroyed by a lamp while it is arising? For the lamp, not yet arisen, does not reach darkness.”

Because light and darkness do not occur simultaneously, there is no reaching. When there is no reaching in this way, how is it reasonable to conceive that darkness is destroyed by a lamp while it is arising, and by what means? And since the lamp while arising does not reach darkness, it is to be concluded that the lamp, because it does not reach it, illuminates nothing at all.
