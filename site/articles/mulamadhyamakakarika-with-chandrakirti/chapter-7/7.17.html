MMK 7.17

yadi kaścidanutpanno bhāvaḥ saṃvidyate kvacit
utpadyeta sa kiṃ tasmin bhāva utpadyate 'sati || 17 ||

If some unarisen entity exists anywhere, it would arise. What arises when that entity is absent?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi kaścidanutpanno bhāvaḥ saṃvidyate kvacit | utpadyeta sa kiṃ tasmin bhāva utpadyate 'sati || 17 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “What was said—‘When this does not enter into the process of arising, how is what is arising said to depend on arising?’—is not right, because this, what is arising, is possible. For example, in dependence on the arising of a pot, a pot is what is arising; that which is arising, arising produces.” This too is not right, because:

“If some unarisen entity exists anywhere, it would arise. What arises when that entity is absent?”

If, before arising, something called a pot existed somewhere, it would arise in dependence on the activity of arising. But before arising, nothing exists anywhere. When the pot is absent, what arises?

Someone might say: “Although the pot does not exist before arising, nevertheless, once it has arisen, it will obtain the designation ‘pot’; by that designation which will exist, there is no fault.” This too is unreasonable. If the activity of arising proceeded, then the entity, having become present, would obtain the name “pot.” But when the activity does not proceed because there is no connection with a future entity, where is presentness? Or, if the activity begins supported by a non-pot, it must be said: what is that non-pot? Is it a pot that is becoming, or absolutely nothing? If it is a pot becoming, how will it become a pot once arisen? If it is absolutely nothing, how does an activity based on it proceed? Or how would that become a pot once arisen? Thus, in every way, the conception that an entity exists is unreasonable. Therefore, it is established that arising does not produce even what is arising.
