MMK 7.14

notpadyamānaṃ notpannaṃ nānutpannaṃ kathaṃcana
utpadyate tathākhyātaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 14 ||

Neither what is arising, nor what has arisen, nor what has not arisen, arises in any way. It has been explained thus by what is being gone, what has gone, and what has not gone.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

notpadyamānaṃ notpannaṃ nānutpannaṃ kathaṃcana | utpadyate tathākhyātaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, showing how it does not produce another either, he says:

“Neither what is arising, nor what has arisen, nor what has not arisen, arises in any way. It has been explained thus by what is being gone, what has gone, and what has not gone.”

If anything were to arise, arising would produce it. But nothing arises, because arising is impossible in all three times. This has already been explained through what is being gone, what has gone, and what has not gone. Just as what has gone is not gone, because of the contradiction between past and present; nor is what has not gone gone, because of the contradiction between future and present; nor is what is being gone gone, because no going-in-progress separate from what has gone and what has not gone is apprehended—so an entity that is arising does not arise, because there is no arising-in-progress apart from what has arisen and what has not arisen. What has arisen does not arise, because of the contradiction between past and present; what has not arisen does not arise, because of the contradiction between future and present. Therefore, it is not reasonable that arising produces another.
