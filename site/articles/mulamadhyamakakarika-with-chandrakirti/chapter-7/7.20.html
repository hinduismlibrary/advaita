MMK 7.20

sataśca tāvadutpattirasataśca na yujyate
na sataścāsataśceti pūrvamevopapāditam || 20 ||

The production of an existent or of a nonexistent is not reasonable; and neither of an existent nor of a nonexistent, as was explained before.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sataśca tāvadutpattirasataśca na yujyate | na sataścāsataśceti pūrvamevopapāditam || 20 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further, in the position that arising produces itself and others, we need state no refutation now, because the refutation of this position was already explained:

“The production of an existent or of a nonexistent is not reasonable; and neither of an existent nor of a nonexistent, as was explained before.”

“A condition for an object is not reasonable for what is nonexistent, nor for what is existent,” and the like, already refuted arising. And:

“When a dharma arises neither existent, nor nonexistent, nor both,”

and the rest, previously refuted arising. Therefore, when arising has been refuted in this way, what opening is there for the conception that what is arising produces arising, or that arising produces itself and another? How can this be established: “Arising arises,” “what is arising arises,” or “it produces itself and another”?
