MMK 7.16

pratītya yadyadbhavati tattacchāntaṃ svabhāvataḥ
tasmādutpadyamānaṃ ca śāntamutpattireva ca || 16 ||

Whatever arises dependently, that is tranquil by its nature. Therefore both what is arising and arising itself are tranquil.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya yadyadbhavati tattacchāntaṃ svabhāvataḥ | tasmādutpadyamānaṃ ca śāntamutpattireva ca || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Oh, alas! I am very afraid of you, who are an extreme nihilist, unconcerned with entities seen and unseen. Under the pretext of explaining the word of the Tathāgata, you display only skill in refutation and destroy the ultimate truth, dependent arising characterized by conditionality, spoken by the supreme seers. Here the Blessed Tathāgata, by rejecting the doctrines of creators who assert primordial nature, God, own-nature, time, atoms, Nārāyaṇa, Jaimini, Kaṇāda, Kapila, and the rest, showed the truth of all entities: ‘When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises,’ namely, ‘formations are conditioned by ignorance,’ and so on, revealing dependent arising without reversal. And you, making a refutation of that with ‘what is arising, what has arisen, and what has not arisen do not arise,’ have acted as the killer of dependent arising, the mother who gave birth to the Tathāgata. Enough of you, universal nihilist!” Reply: “I do not kill the mother of dependent arising, mother of all the Ten-Powered Ones. Rather, because you lack faith in the very profound dependent arising, you ascertain its meaning in reverse and blame us.”

Indeed, by saying, “Depending on this, that comes to be,” the Blessed Tathāgata has clearly shown that all dharmas are without own-nature. For:

“Whatever arises dependently, that is tranquil by its nature. Therefore both what is arising and arising itself are tranquil.”

A thing that exists bears its own nature, its own unchanging nature, by itself. Because it exists, it does not depend on anything else, nor does it arise. Thus, if entities with own-nature are accepted, how can there be dependent arising? By you accepting that entities have own-nature, dependent arising is entirely refuted. And then the vision of the supreme Dharma, the Buddha, is also refuted, in accordance with the scriptural statement: “Whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dharma; whoever sees the Dharma sees the Buddha.” But I, by showing that both the cause called a seed and the effect called a sprout, which arises from it, are tranquil, devoid of own-nature, and dependently arisen, have in every way made clear the mother of dependent arising of the Blessed Tathāgatas. Since this is so, understand this plainly.
