MMK 7.16

pratītya yadyadbhavati tattacchāntaṃ svabhāvataḥ
tasmādutpadyamānaṃ ca śāntamutpattireva ca || 16 ||

Whatever arises in dependence on something is, by its nature, at peace. Therefore what is arising, and arising itself, are at peace.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya yadyadbhavati tattacchāntaṃ svabhāvataḥ | tasmādutpadyamānaṃ ca śāntamutpattireva ca || 16 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

The opponent accuses Candrakīrti of nihilism and of destroying the Buddha’s teaching of dependent arising. Candrakīrti answers that the charge reverses the point. The Buddha teaches, “When this is present, that occurs; with the arising of this, that arises.” If things existed by their own nature, they would not depend on anything else and would not arise; intrinsic existence would contradict dependent arising. Seeing dependent arising is seeing the Dharma, and seeing the Dharma is seeing the Buddha. A seed and its sprout arise dependently, and both are empty of own-nature. Thus dependent arising is peaceful: both the process of arising and what is said to arise are peaceful.
