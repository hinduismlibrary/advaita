MMK 7.12

pradīpaḥ svaparātmānau saṃprakāśayate yadi
tamo 'pi svaparātmānau chādayiṣyatyasaṃśayam || 12 ||

If a lamp illuminates itself and others, then darkness too would certainly conceal itself and others.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pradīpaḥ svaparātmānau saṃprakāśayate yadi | tamo 'pi svaparātmānau chādayiṣyatyasaṃśayam || 12 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

If a lamp illuminates itself and other things, its opposite, darkness, should also cover itself and other things. The commentary cites the Ārya Upāli inquiry, where going forth and examining the nature of things bring realization, and the Ratnakūṭa, which compares wisdom and ignorance to lamp and darkness: ignorance fades in dependence on wisdom, though they never meet, and both are empty. Darkness does not announce that it will remain after a lamp is lit; in the same way, ignorance has no fixed nature that wisdom must physically touch in order to dispel.
