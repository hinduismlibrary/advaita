MMK 7.2

utpādādyāstrayo vyastā nālaṃ lakṣaṇakarmaṇi
saṃskṛtasya samastāḥ syurekatra kathamekadā || 2 ||

The three, beginning with arising, when separate, are not sufficient for the function of characteristics of the conditioned. How could they be together in one place at one time?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

utpādādyāstrayo vyastā nālaṃ lakṣaṇakarmaṇi | saṃskṛtasya samastāḥ syurekatra kathamekadā || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further, when these, arising and the rest, are conceived as characteristics of the conditioned, are they conceived separately as characteristics, or all together, coexisting? Neither way is reasonable, he says:

“The three, beginning with arising, when separate, are not sufficient for the function of characteristics of the conditioned. How could they be together in one place at one time?”

Here, when separate, they are not suitable for the function of characteristics. If, at the time of arising, abiding and destruction were absent, then arising, without abiding and destruction, could not be a characteristic of the conditioned, like space. If, at the time of abiding, arising and destruction were absent, then there would be abiding without them. But there is no entity without arising and destruction; therefore it is not reasonable that this non-existent thing should abide, like a sky-flower. Further, what is joined with abiding would not afterwards be joined with impermanence, because it would be pervaded by a quality contrary to that. Someone might say: “First it was eternal, then it became non-eternal.” But it is not reasonable for one entity to be both eternal and non-eternal. Thus there is no abiding without arising and destruction. Likewise, if arising and abiding were absent at the time of destruction, then the destruction of what is not arisen and is without abiding, like a sky-flower, would not exist. Thus, when separate, arising and the rest are not sufficient for the function of characteristics; they are not adequate.

Now he says that even when together they are not reasonable:

“How could they be together in one place at one time?”

The meaning is: in one entity, at one time, because they are mutually opposed, like passion and dispassion or light and darkness, they are not reasonable. Who with understanding would accept that an entity arises, abides, and perishes in the very same moment in which it arises? Therefore arising and the rest, even together, have no capacity to function as the characteristics of the conditioned.
