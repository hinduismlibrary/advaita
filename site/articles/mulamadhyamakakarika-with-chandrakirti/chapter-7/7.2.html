MMK 7.2

Investigation of Birth, Abiding and Perishing

← MMK 7.1Chapter 7MMK 7.3 →

MMK 7.2

utpāda-ādyāḥ = beginning with arising; trayaḥ = three; vyastāḥ = separately; na alam = are not sufficient; lakṣaṇa-karmaṇi = for the function of characteristic; saṃskṛtasya = of the conditioned; samastāḥ = together; syuḥ = would be; ekatra = in one place; katham = how; ekadā = at one time

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

utpādādyāstrayo vyastā nālaṃ lakṣaṇakarmaṇi | saṃskṛtasya samastāḥ syurekatra kathamekadā || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The three marks do not work separately or together. If arising occurs without abiding and perishing, it cannot establish a conditioned thing; the same problem applies to abiding without arising and perishing, or perishing without the other two. A thing without the other marks would be like an unconditioned, nonexistent thing. Nor can all three characterize one thing at the same instant: arising, remaining and perishing oppose one another, like desire and dispassion or light and darkness. Who would accept that something is born, remains and is destroyed in the very same moment?
