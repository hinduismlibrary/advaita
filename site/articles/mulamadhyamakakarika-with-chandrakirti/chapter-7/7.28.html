MMK 7.28

tayaivāvasthayāvasthā na hi saiva nirudhyate
anyayāvasthayāvasthā na cānyaiva nirudhyate || 28 ||

A state does not cease by that very same state; nor does a state cease by a different state.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tayaivāvasthayāvasthā na hi saiva nirudhyate | anyayāvasthayāvasthā na cānyaiva nirudhyate || 28 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

A thing cannot cease in its very same state, because an activity cannot operate on itself in that way. Nor can a later, different state destroy the earlier state if the earlier state is no longer present. Milk and curd, for example, are not present together as destroyer and destroyed: when the curd state is present, the milk state is absent. If an absent thing could be destroyed, a hare’s horn could be destroyed too. Cessation is not established by one state destroying itself or another state.
