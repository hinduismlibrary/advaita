MMK 7.25

sthityānyayā sthiteḥ sthānaṃ tayaiva ca na yujyate
utpādasya yathotpādo nātmanā na parātmanā || 25 ||

If another abiding is posited for abiding, its abiding is not reasonable by that other abiding, nor by itself. As with arising, arising is not by itself nor by another.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sthityānyayā sthiteḥ sthānaṃ tayaiva ca na yujyate | utpādasya yathotpādo nātmanā na parātmanā || 25 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And if another abiding is conceived for the sake of this abiding, or if this abiding itself establishes itself, neither way is reasonable, he says:

“If another abiding is posited for abiding, its abiding is not reasonable by that other abiding, nor by itself. As with arising, arising is not by itself nor by another.”

Just as it was said, beginning with “How would this arising, unborn, produce itself?” that arising does not produce itself, so too it should be said that abiding does not establish itself. It is to be joined thus:

“If this non-abiding abiding were to establish itself, how? If it is already abiding and establishes itself, then what is to be established in what is abiding?”

And just as it was explained regarding arising, “If another arising produces it, there is an infinite regress,” so too it is to be explained regarding abiding: “If another abiding stabilizes this abiding, there is an infinite regress.” Thus abiding too is not reasonable. For this very reason, the Blessed One said:

“These dharmas are indeed without abiding; their abiding does not exist. Non-abiding is not established by its own nature under the name ‘abiding.’

“Neither abiding nor arising is taught by the Lord of the World. Having known the Lord of the World, know concentration in this way.”

And it is said in the Noble Conduct Verses:

“Air is supported by space; the mass of water depends on that. This earth, the ground and the world, depends on that. Thus space is the basis for beings’ karma and experience. Make the mind like space; understand this meaning—up to: ‘This abiding was spoken of by the Jina.’” This is the detailed passage.
