Investigation of Birth, Abiding and Perishing

← MMK 7.32Chapter 7MMK 7.34 →

MMK 7.33

utpādasthitibhaṅgānāmasiddhernāsti saṃskṛtam
saṃskṛtasyāprasiddhau ca kathaṃ setsyatyasaṃskṛtam || 33 ||

When the arising, abiding, and destruction of conditioned things are not established, there is no conditioned thing. When the conditioned is not established, how will the unconditioned be established?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

utpādasthitibhaṅgānāmasiddhernāsti saṃskṛtam | saṃskṛtasyāprasiddhau ca kathaṃ setsyatyasaṃskṛtam || 33 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Although arising, abiding, and destruction have been denied as characteristics of the conditioned, nevertheless the conditioned exists, endowed with distinctive characteristics. For hardness and the like, and a dewlap and the like, are taught as its distinctive characteristics. Therefore, because the conditioned exists, its characteristics too exist.” Reply: It would be so if the conditioned thing itself existed. How? Because:

“When the arising, abiding, and destruction of conditioned things are not established, there is no conditioned thing.”

When arising, abiding, and destruction themselves have been denied by the reasoning stated, where is the conditioned thing? How could its distinctive characteristics exist?

Someone says: “The conditioned certainly exists because the unconditioned, its opposite, exists.” Reply: This would be so if the unconditioned existed, because:

“When the conditioned is not established, how will the unconditioned be established?”

Some conceive space, non-analytical cessation, analytical cessation, and nirvāṇa as unconditioned. Others conceive emptiness, characterized as suchness, as unconditioned. It is clearly shown that all of this does not exist when the conditioned is not established.
