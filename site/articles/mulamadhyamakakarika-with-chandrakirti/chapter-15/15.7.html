MMK 15.7

kātyāyana-avavāde = in the instruction to Kātyāyana; ca = and; asti = exists; iti = thus; na asti = does not exist; iti = thus; ca = and; ubhayam = both; pratiṣiddham = rejected; bhagavatā = by the Blessed One; bhāva-abhāva-vibhāvinā = who distinguishes existence and nonexistence

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kātyāyanāvavāde cāstīti nāstīti cobhayam | pratiṣiddhaṃ bhagavatā bhāvābhāvavibhāvinā || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Candrakīrti cites the Buddha’s instruction to Kātyāyana: both “it exists” and “it does not exist” are rejected by the one who understands existence and non-existence. The Buddha describes existence and non-existence as two extremes, and the middle way as free from fixed appearance or establishment. To cling to either extreme keeps beings bound; the wise do not settle on either side.

The opponent asks what harm there would be if fire and other things truly existed by their own nature. Candrakīrti repeats the earlier consequence: if their nature depends on causes and conditions, it would be something made, and therefore not an independent own-nature. The scriptural rejection of existence and non-existence confirms the same analysis.
