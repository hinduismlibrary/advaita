MMK 15.3

kutaḥ svabhāvasyābhāve parabhāvo bhaviṣyati
svabhāvaḥ parabhāvasya parabhāvo hi kathyate || 3 ||

When own-being is absent, from where will other-being come? For own-being is said to be the other-being of another.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kutaḥ svabhāvasyābhāve parabhāvo bhaviṣyati | svabhāvaḥ parabhāvasya parabhāvo hi kathyate || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Although own-being of entities does not exist, other-being at least exists, because it is not denied. And when other-being exists, own-being too will exist, because other-being is not established apart from own-being. The response:

Here indeed, in the world, some own-being, in dependence on another own-being, is designated “other.” If the heat of fire were an own-being, in dependence on water, whose own-being is liquidity, it would be designated other-being. But when the own-being of something examined by those seeking liberation does not exist at all, from where would otherness come? And from the absence of other-being, the absence of own-being too is established.
