MMK 15.2

svabhāvaḥ kṛtako nāma bhaviṣyati punaḥ katham
akṛtrimaḥ svabhāvo hi nirapekṣaḥ paratra ca || 2 ||

How again can an own-being be made? For an own-being is unmade and independent of another.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svabhāvaḥ kṛtako nāma bhaviṣyati punaḥ katham | akṛtrimaḥ svabhāvo hi nirapekṣaḥ paratra ca || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: The own-being of an entity is indeed accepted as arising from causes and conditions and as being made. Therefore the consequence that it is made is not a fault for us, because we accept an own-being that is made. He says this too is not reasonable:

How can what is called an own-being be made again? “Made” and “own-being” are mutually contradictory; this is a meaning that does not fit together. For here, by the derivation “own-being is one’s own entity,” an entity that is made is not called an own-being in the world, for example, the heat of water produced by conditions of minerals and spirits, and the state of ruby and the like of karketana stones. But what is unmade is an own-being, for example, the heat of fire and the state of ruby and the like of naturally occurring rubies. Because they are not produced by contact with another entity, they are called own-being. Thus, when it has been established in worldly usage that an own-being is unmade, we now say: understand that this heat is not the own-being of fire, because it is made. Here, from the coming together of gems, fuel, grass, and the like, and from the friction of fire-sticks and the like, the dependence of fire on causes and conditions is perceived. And heat apart from fire is not possible. Therefore heat too is produced by causes and conditions; hence it is made, and because it is made, like the heat of water, it is plainly understood not to be an own-being.

But this is known by cowherd women and people: “The heat of fire is its own-being.” What, then, have we said that is not known? This is what we say: It is not fit to be an own-being, because it is devoid of the defining characteristic of own-being. But because of the influence of ignorance and error, the world has accepted the whole collection of entities, though without own-being, as possessing own-being. Just as those with an eye-disease, because of the condition of the eye-disease, cling to the nature of hairs and the like, which do not exist, as possessing own-being, so fools, because the eye of understanding is afflicted by the darkness of ignorance, cling to the entire collection of entities without own-being as possessing own-being. According to their clinging, they call the characteristic its own mark, such as the heat of fire as its own characteristic. Because it is not perceived elsewhere, they call it its own mark as something not shared with others. And by the very common understanding of ordinary people, the Blessed One established in the Abhidharma that very conventional nature of theirs as their conventional own-form. But impermanence and the like were said to be the common characteristic. When the vision of those whose eye of understanding is purified, free from the darkness of ignorance, is considered, then the noble ones, with no eye-disease, say before them that this is not the own-being of entities, just as those without eye-disease do not see the hairs perceived by those with eye-disease. As is said in the noble Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra:

“Just as a ball of hairs is falsely apprehended by people with an eye-disease, so this thought of entities is falsely imagined by fools.

“Neither own-being, nor representation, nor thing, nor storehouse are imagined by fools and bad logicians.”

And likewise:

“With reference to the non-arising of own-being, Mahāmati, I said: ‘All dharmas are unborn.’” And so forth.

Here it is said: If heat and the like of fire and so on are called without own-being because they are made, being arisen from causes and conditions, then what is the defining characteristic of that own-being, and what is this own-being? The response:

“For an own-being is unmade, independent of another.”

Here, what is a thing’s own entity is called its own-being. And what is own to what? That which is not artificial is its own; that which is artificial is not its own, like the heat of water. And that on which something depends is its own too, for example one’s servants and one’s wealth. But that which depends on another is not its own, for example, what is borrowed for a time and is not independent. Because what is artificial and dependent on another is not accepted as own-being, for this very reason heat is not an own-being of fire, since it is bound to causes and conditions, and is made by arising afterward after not existing before. And because this is so, whatever is the fire’s own form, unchanged in all three times, unmade, not arising afterward after not existing before, and not dependent on causes and conditions, like the heat of water, far and near, or long and short, that is called own-being. What is that form of fire in this way? It does not exist, nor does it not exist by its own-form. Even so, for the sake of avoiding fear among hearers, conventionally, having superimposed it, we say that it exists. As was said by the Blessed One:

“What hearing is there, and what teaching, of a dharma without letters? It is heard and taught, yet through superimposition it is without letters.”

And here too he will say:

“It is not to be said ‘empty,’ nor should it be ‘not empty’; ‘both’ and ‘neither’ are spoken for the sake of designation.”

If, then, by that superimposition you say that it exists, what is it like? That which is called the nature of dharmas, that itself is its own-form. What is the nature of dharmas? Own-being of dharmas. What is this own-being? Nature. And what is this nature? That which is emptiness. What is this emptiness? Absence of own-being. What is this absence of own-being? Suchness. What is this suchness? Being thus, unaltered, always abiding. For non-arising in every way, because of not depending on another and being unmade, is called the own-being of fire and the like.

This is what is meant: The very collection of entities perceived through the power of the darkness of ignorance, which becomes an object for noble ones whose darkness of ignorance has vanished, through the condition of not seeing, is established as their own-form, their own-being. And this is its characteristic: “For an own-being is unmade, independent of another.” The teachers established it thus, it is to be understood. But this own-being of entities, whose nature is non-arising, because it is nothing, because it is merely absence, is itself without own-being. Therefore it is to be understood that there is no own-being of entities. As the Blessed One said:

“Whoever knows entities as nonentities is never attached to all entities. Whoever is never attached to all entities touches the signless concentration.”
