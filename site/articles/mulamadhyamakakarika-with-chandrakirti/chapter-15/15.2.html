MMK 15.2

How is it possible for there to be "an essence which has been made?" Essences are not contrived and not dependent on anything else.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svabhāvaḥ kṛtako nāma bhaviṣyati punaḥ katham | akṛtrimaḥ svabhāvo hi nirapekṣaḥ paratra ca || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent accepts that an own-nature can be produced by causes. Candrakīrti asks how something made could still be an own-nature. “Own-nature” means the thing’s own, unconstructed character; a product that depends on something else is not independent in that sense. Fire’s heat is commonly called its nature, but fire arises through fuel, friction, and other conditions, and its heat depends on those causes too.

Ordinary people may treat fire’s heat as an intrinsic property, just as a person with an eye disorder sees nonexistent hairs in the sky. Those free from that disorder do not see the hairs; likewise, those whose understanding is clear do not find the natures that ordinary people impute to things. The Buddha teaches that phenomena are unborn by nature. “Own-nature” is used conventionally to point toward the unconstructed, independent, and unchanging; examined ultimately, that nature is itself emptiness, not an existent essence.
