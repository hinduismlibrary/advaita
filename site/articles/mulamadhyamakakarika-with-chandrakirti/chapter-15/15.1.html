MMK 15.1

na saṃbhavaḥ svabhāvasya yuktaḥ pratyayahetubhiḥ
hetupratyayasaṃbhūtaḥ svabhāvaḥ kṛtako bhavet || 1 ||

The existence of own-being through causes and conditions is not reasonable. An own-being arisen from causes and conditions would be made.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na saṃbhavaḥ svabhāvasya yuktaḥ pratyayahetubhiḥ | hetupratyayasaṃbhūtaḥ svabhāvaḥ kṛtako bhavet || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Own-being of entities indeed exists, because causes and conditions that produce it are taken up. Here, what does not exist has no taking up of causes and conditions that produce it, like a sky-flower. And the causes and conditions, such as seed and ignorance, that produce sprout, formations, and the like are taken up. Therefore own-being of entities indeed exists. The response: If there is own-being of entities, formations, sprouts, and the like, then what need is there now of causes and conditions for what exists? Just as the taking up of ignorance, seed, and the like is made for the arising of the existent state of formations, sprouts, and the like, so no other thing would need to be done for their arising, because their own-being exists. Showing this, he says:

If it is said: Before arising, no own-being of any entity exists, so that, because it exists, its arising would be pointless; rather, the own-being, which was previously nonexistent before arising, arises afterward in dependence on causes and conditions, even when it is held thus:

“An own-being arisen from causes and conditions would be made.”
