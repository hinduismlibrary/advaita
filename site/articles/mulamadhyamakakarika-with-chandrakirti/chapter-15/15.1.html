MMK 15.1

na = not; saṃbhavaḥ = arising; svabhāvasya = of own-nature; yuktaḥ = fitting; pratyaya-hetubhiḥ = from conditions and causes; hetu-pratyaya-saṃbhūtaḥ = arisen from cause and condition; svabhāvaḥ = own-nature; kṛtakaḥ = made; bhavet = would be

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na saṃbhavaḥ svabhāvasya yuktaḥ pratyayahetubhiḥ | hetupratyayasaṃbhūtaḥ svabhāvaḥ kṛtako bhavet || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that things must have an own-nature because causes and conditions produce them. Seeds and other causes bring about sprouts and conditioned formations; if the products had no nature, the opponent says, there would be nothing for those causes to produce. Candrakīrti replies that if a sprout or formation already possessed an own-nature, what work would its causes do? Its nature would already be present, so production would be pointless.

The opponent answers that the own-nature is absent before production and comes to be through causes and conditions. But then it is something made by conditions. What depends on causes is constructed; it cannot be an independent own-nature. Thus the very causal account meant to prove own-nature makes it something produced and dependent.
