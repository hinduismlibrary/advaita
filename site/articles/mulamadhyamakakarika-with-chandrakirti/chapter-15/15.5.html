MMK 15.5

bhāvasya ced aprasiddhir abhāvo naiva sidhyati
bhāvasya hy anyathābhāvam abhāvaṃ bruvate janāḥ || 5 ||

If being is unestablished, non-being is not established. For people call being’s alteration “non-being.”

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvasya ced aprasiddhir abhāvo naiva sidhyati | bhāvasya hy anyathābhāvam abhāvaṃ bruvate janāḥ || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Although being has been denied by you, non-being exists, because it has not been denied. And therefore being too will exist because its opposite, non-being, exists, just as non-being does. The response: Being would exist if non-being itself existed. But it does not, he says:

If some being were unestablished, non-being would not at all be established. For people call the alteration of being non-being.

Here, if some entity called being existed, then, through its alteration, there would be non-being. For pots and the like, having departed from their present state, having undergone alteration, become expressible in the world by the sound “non-being.” But when these pots and the like are not established as having the nature of being at all, from where is alteration of those whose own-being does not exist? Therefore non-being too does not exist.
