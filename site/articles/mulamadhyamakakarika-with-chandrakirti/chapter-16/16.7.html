MMK 16.7

Investigation of Bondage and Freedom

MMK 16.7

badhnīyād bandhanaṃ kāmaṃ bandhyāt pūrvaṃ bhaved yadi
na cāsti tat śeṣam uktaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 7 ||

Bondage would indeed bind if it were prior to the one to be bound; but that does not exist. The remainder has been stated by “going, gone, and not yet gone.”

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

badhnīyād bandhanaṃ kāmaṃ bandhyāt pūrvaṃ bhaved yadi | na cāsti tat śeṣam uktaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover:

“Bondage would indeed bind if it existed before the one to be bound; but that does not exist.”

Here bondage, such as fetters, is seen, established beforehand separately from the one to be bound, to bind the bound Devadatta. In the same way, if passion and the rest, as bondage, were established before the formations or the person to be bound, then by that bondage established beforehand the formations or the person would be bound.

But this is not possible, because passion and the rest, having no support, are not established. And for bondage established beforehand, its later connection with the one to be bound would be purposeless. And because the one to be bound, established separately from bondage, has no purpose in depending on bondage beforehand, bondage is not established beforehand apart from the one to be bound.

Therefore bondage does not bind anyone at all. And it is not reasonable for that which binds no one to be bondage. Therefore bondage does not exist. And because bondage does not exist, it is established that the one to be bound also does not exist.

That which is the remaining refutation here is:

“The remainder has been stated by ‘going, gone, and not yet gone.’”

This is to be understood. By changing the wording of the verse, it is to be joined beginning:

“The bound one is not bound; the unbound one is certainly not bound; the one released from being bound and unbound, the one being bound, is not bound,”

and so on.
