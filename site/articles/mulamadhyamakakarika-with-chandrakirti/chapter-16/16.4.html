MMK 16.4

It is in no way feasible that impulses go beyond misery. And it is in no way feasible that living beings go beyond misery.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

saṃskārāṇāṃ na nirvāṇaṃ kathaṃcid upapadyate | sattvasyāpi na nirvāṇaṃ kathaṃcid upapadyate || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If nirvāṇa existed as an independently real state, it would have to be the nirvāṇa of permanent or impermanent formations. Nothing can be done to what is permanent and unchanging; what is impermanent has already ceased and is absent. The same reasoning applies to a sentient being. Neither conditioned formations nor a person can be shown to attain nirvāṇa as an intrinsically existing result.

Calling the self neither permanent nor impermanent does not establish it in nirvāṇa. If it exists there, it would remain after liberation and be permanent; if it does not exist, it would be impermanent. Saying it is beyond existence and non-existence does not help: if it can be known, it still has a basis; if it cannot be known at all, it is no more established than a sky-flower. Nirvāṇa itself is taught as like an illusion or dream, not as a thing to possess.
