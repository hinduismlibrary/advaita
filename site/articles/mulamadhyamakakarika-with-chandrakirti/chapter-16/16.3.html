MMK 16.3

upādānād upādānaṃ saṃsaran vibhavo bhavet
vibhavaś cānupādānaḥ kaḥ sa kiṃ saṃsariṣyati || 3 ||

One passing from appropriation to appropriation would be non-existence. And non-existence is without appropriation. Who is that? What will transmigrate?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

upādānād upādānaṃ saṃsaran vibhavo bhavet | vibhavaś cānupādānaḥ kaḥ sa kiṃ saṃsariṣyati || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover, this self:

“Transmigrating from one appropriation to another, there would be non-being.”

Let the self’s saṃsāra be, if its saṃsāra were reasonable while it was without appropriation. Showing how its being without appropriation follows, he says:

“Transmigrating from one appropriation to another, there would be non-being.”

Here, going from human appropriation to divine appropriation, would it go having abandoned human appropriation, or without abandoning it? If it is said to go having abandoned it, then, through abandoning the former appropriation and not yet having the later appropriation, there would be non-being in between. “Non-being” is what has gone from being. “Being” is the five appropriated aggregates. It would be without them. And that non-being, without appropriation, because it is without aggregates and without the cause for designation and appropriation, would be without a cause. And what is without appropriation, stainless, unmanifest, and without a cause—what is that? No one. That is, it does not exist. And when that does not exist, because it is absent, appropriation too, having no appropriator, does not exist. What will transmigrate? There is nothing that transmigrates. Or else, what does this qualify in the activity of transmigrating? Because it is nonexistent, it will not perform the act of transmigrating at all. Thus, for the present, transmigration with abandonment of the former appropriation is unreasonable. Or if it is without abandonment, even so it is not established. Why? Because, through not abandoning the former and taking up the later, one self would have the nature of two. And this is not accepted. Therefore there is no transmigration even without abandonment.

Then it may be said: Because intermediate aggregates arise between the former and later existences, and they have appropriation, there would not be the consequence of non-being even for one who transmigrates with appropriation. This too is not reasonable, because the same consequence follows for the transmigration of intermediate aggregates from abandoning or not abandoning the former existence.

If it is said: There is no fault because abandonment and appropriation occur simultaneously, the response is: Does it abandon the former appropriation in part and transmigrate into the intermediate-existence appropriation in part, or entirely? There, if it is imagined to be by a part, then the consequence of having two natures follows, the fault already stated. Or if entirely, even so that same consequence of non-being follows. The only distinction is that in transmigration into an intermediate existence, because of great proximity, the time without appropriation would be subtle. But abandonment and appropriation are not seen simultaneously with respect to one entity, one undivided thing, in its entirety. For two actions, abandonment and appropriation, do not occur at one time for one Devadatta transmigrating in his entirety from house to house. Or if simultaneous abandonment and appropriation are imagined through abandoning one with one foot and taking up another with the other foot, then, in that case, just as there are two feet, the self would have two natures. Because it remains in the former by one part and in the later by another part, it would follow that it has many parts. Therefore even simultaneous abandonment and appropriation are impossible; this is no solution. Therefore the same fault follows even when appropriation of an intermediate existence is supposed. In every way, there is no saṃsāra even for the self.

And when there is no saṃsāra of formations or of the self, it is established that there is no saṃsāra at all.
