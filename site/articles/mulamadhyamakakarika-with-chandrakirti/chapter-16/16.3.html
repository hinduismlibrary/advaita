MMK 16.3

upādānād upādānaṃ saṃsaran vibhavo bhavet
vibhavaś cānupādānaḥ kaḥ sa kiṃ saṃsariṣyati || 3 ||

If one transmigrated from one appropriation to another, one would become something that ceases to exist. What is unappropriated—who is that, and what could transmigrate?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

upādānād upādānaṃ saṃsaran vibhavo bhavet | vibhavaś cānupādānaḥ kaḥ sa kiṃ saṃsariṣyati || 3 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Suppose a person leaves one set of appropriated aggregates and takes up another, such as moving from a human existence to a divine one. If the first basis is abandoned before the next is taken up, there is an interval with no aggregates and no basis for designating a person. Such an unappropriated being would be without cause, and none can be found. If the first basis is not abandoned, the person would have to possess both states at once. Neither account supports transmigration.

An intermediate existence does not solve the problem. The same question applies when the earlier aggregates are relinquished and an intermediate set is acquired. If both acts happen simultaneously, does the person transfer only part of itself or all of itself? A partial transfer makes the person divided between two states; a total transfer repeats the gap or contradiction already described. Thus a person cannot cross from one appropriation to another, whether or not an intermediate life is proposed.
