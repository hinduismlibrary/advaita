MMK 16.2

pudgalaḥ = person; saṃsarati = transmigrates; cet = if; skandha-āyatana-dhātuṣu = among aggregates, sense-fields, and elements; pañcadhā = in five ways; mṛgyamāṇaḥ = being sought; asau = he; na asti = is not; kaḥ = who; saṃsariṣyati = will transmigrate

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pudgalaḥ saṃsarati cet skandhāyatanadhātuṣu | pañcadhā mṛgyamāṇo 'sau nāsti kaḥ saṃsariṣyati || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent says that a person or self transmigrates, and need not be either permanent or impermanent. Candrakīrti asks us to search for this person among the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements. Like the fire-and-fuel analysis, the self is not identical with these bases, not separate from them, not their possessor, not located within them, and does not contain them. Searched for in these five ways, no person is found. If no one exists in this analysis, who could transmigrate?
