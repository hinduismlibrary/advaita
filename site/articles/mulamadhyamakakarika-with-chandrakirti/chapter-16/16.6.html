MMK 16.6

bandhanaṃ ced upādānaṃ sopādāno na badhyate
badhyate nānupādānaḥ kim avastho 'tha badhyate || 6 ||

If appropriation is bondage, one with appropriation is not bound. One without appropriation is not bound. In what state, then, is one bound?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bandhanaṃ ced upādānaṃ sopādāno na badhyate | badhyate nānupādānaḥ kim avastho 'tha badhyate || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Although there is no bondage of formations or of a being, nevertheless passion and the rest, called appropriation, exist as bondage; because they exist, bondage too will exist. The response: Appropriation would be bondage if it bound some entity; but it does not bind. Showing how it does not bind, he says:

Here, one with appropriation is one whose appropriation exists. That existent is not bound. For one who is with appropriation is already bound; what would the conjunction with bondage do for him again? And one without appropriation, without bondage, also is not bound, since he is without bondage, like the Tathāgata. That one without appropriation and without bondage is bound is unreasonable, because the two contradict one another.

And that one who, when examined in this way, is not bound whether with appropriation or without appropriation, in what state shall he now be bound? There is no such other state of his in which he could be bound: this is the meaning.

And when, examined in this way, bondage binds no one at all, then, since it binds no one, how could appropriation, passion and the rest, have the nature of bondage? Therefore bondage too does not exist.
