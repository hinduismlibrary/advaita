Investigation of Samskaras

MMK 13.1

tanmṛṣā moṣadharma yad bhagavān ity abhāṣata
sarve ca moṣadharmāṇaḥ saṃskārās tena te mṛṣāḥ || 1 ||

The Blessed One said: “That is false and deceptive”; therefore all conditioned formations are deceptive and thus false.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tanmṛṣā moṣadharma yad bhagavān ity abhāṣata | sarve ca moṣadharmāṇaḥ saṃskārās tena te mṛṣāḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Since, in this way, by the method of the immediately preceding chapter, when being made by oneself, by another, by both, or arising without a cause are examined, the existence of things is absent, and another method that produces them is absent, and because these things appear as having the form of something arisen to the eyes of understanding of childish ordinary people whose eyes are afflicted by the darkness of ignorance, therefore, although they exist without own-being, they deceive fools, like horses created by a magician, but not the wise. For this reason the Buddha, the great compassionate one, whose eye of understanding directly sees the absence of own-being of all dharmas, whose entire latent impressions of ignorance have been utterly uprooted, intent on teaching the non-inverted absence of own-being for the protection of beings reversed by the four inversions, awakener of the world, says:

“That is false and deceptive, as the Blessed One said; and all conditioned formations are deceptive; therefore they are false.”

It is said in a sūtra: “That is false and deceptive, namely, the conditioned. For this, monks, is the highest truth: that which is not false and not deceptive, namely nirvāṇa. And all conditioned formations are false and deceptive.” And likewise: “There is here neither suchness nor not-suchness; this too is deceptive; this too is of the nature of vanishing.” In this way, because the Tathāgata, the Blessed One, said, “Whatever is deceptive is false,” and all conditioned formations are deceptive, therefore those conditioned formations, through being deceptive, are false, like the girl in a conjurer’s mechanical device, and like the royal elephant that arose through the deception of an elephant made by a mechanical device fitted with a mark. There, that which deceives, being deceptive, appears falsely, like a whirling firebrand. Therefore all conditioned formations are false because they are without own-being, since they are deceptive, like water and the like in a mirage. But that which is true is not deceptive, for example, nirvāṇa alone. Thus, by the reasoning set forth and by this scriptural authority, the absence of own-being of all things is established. And from the recitation of the Prajñāpāramitā in Fifty Verses: “All dharmas are empty, through being joined with absence of own-being.”
