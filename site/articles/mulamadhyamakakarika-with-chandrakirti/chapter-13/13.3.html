MMK 13.3

bhāvānām = of entities; niḥsvabhāvatvam = lack of own-nature; anyathā-bhāva-darśanāt = because change is seen; asvabhāvaḥ = without own-nature; bhāvaḥ = entity; na asti = is not; bhāvānām = of entities; śūnyatā = emptiness; yataḥ = because

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvānāṃ niḥsvabhāvatvam anyathābhāvadarśanāt | asvabhāvo bhāvo nāsti bhāvānāṃ śūnyatā yataḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent interprets the teaching more narrowly: it shows only that things are impermanent, since their transformation is observed. The opponent reasons that, if things had no own-nature at all, no change could occur; yet change is seen. The opponent adds that a quality such as emptiness cannot exist without a thing that bears it, just as a shadow cannot belong to the child of a barren woman. Therefore, the opponent concludes, things must have an own-nature.
