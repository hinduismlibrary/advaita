MMK 26.12

tasya tasya nirodhena tat tan nābhipravartate
duḥkhaskandhaḥ kevalo 'yam evaṃ samyag nirudhyate || 12 ||

With the cessation of each [limb], the next does not proceed; thus this whole mass of suffering is completely ceased.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tasya tasya nirodhena tat tan nābhipravartate | duḥkhaskandhaḥ kevalo 'yam evaṃ samyag nirudhyate || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Just as formations cease through the cessation of ignorance, in the same way:

“With the cessation of each [limb], the next does not proceed; thus this whole mass of suffering is completely ceased.”

It is to be understood that, through the cessation of each preceding limb, the next succeeding limb ceases. In this sequence, this yogin, freed through exertion from seeing self and what belongs to self and the like, completely ceases again, without arising, the heap of suffering, which is devoid of an agent and experiencer and empty of the own-nature of entities.

As it is said in the Noble Rice-Stalk Sūtra:

“Thus, internal dependent arising arises from just two causes. Which two? It is connected with a cause and connected with a condition. There, what is the connection with a cause for internal dependent arising? This is it: formations are conditioned by ignorance; consciousness is conditioned by formations; name-and-form is conditioned by consciousness; the six sense-fields are conditioned by name-and-form; contact is conditioned by the six sense-fields; feeling is conditioned by contact; craving is conditioned by feeling; appropriation is conditioned by craving; becoming is conditioned by appropriation; birth is conditioned by becoming; aging and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, mental distress, and agitation arise conditioned by birth. In this way there is the arising of this whole great mass of suffering.

“Had ignorance not existed, formations would not be manifested. In the same way, as far as birth: had birth not existed, aging and death would not be manifested. Or, when ignorance exists, formations come into being; in the same way, as far as birth, when birth exists, aging and death come into being.

“Here ignorance does not think: ‘I produce formations.’ Formations too do not think: ‘We have been produced by ignorance.’ In the same way, birth does not think: ‘I produce aging and death’; aging and death too do not think: ‘We have been produced by birth.’ Yet when ignorance exists, formations come into being, appear; in the same way, when birth exists, aging and death come into being, appear. Thus the connection with a cause for internal dependent arising is to be seen.”

“How is the connection with a condition for internal dependent arising to be seen? Through the coming together of the six elements. Which six elements come together? Through the coming together of the earth, water, fire, wind, space, and consciousness elements, the connection with a condition for internal dependent arising is to be seen.

“There, what is the earth element in internal dependent arising? That which, by cohesion in the body, produces the state of hardness: this is called the earth element. That which performs the function of holding together the body: this is called the water element. That which digests what is eaten and consumed by the body: this is called the fire element. That which performs the function of inhalation and exhalation for the body: this is called the wind element. That which produces hollowness within the body: this is called the space element. That which produces name-and-form, the five groups of consciousness, and the afflicted mental consciousness, joined with them, is called the consciousness element, monks.

“When these conditions are absent, the body does not arise. But when the internal earth element is unimpaired, and likewise the water, fire, wind, space, and consciousness elements are unimpaired, then, through their coming together, the body arises.

“There the earth element does not think: ‘I produce the body’s state of hardness.’ The water element does not think: ‘I perform the function of holding the body together.’ The fire element does not think: ‘I digest what the body has eaten, drunk, and chewed.’ The wind element does not think: ‘I perform the function of inhalation and exhalation for the body.’ The space element does not think: ‘I produce hollowness within the body.’ The consciousness element does not think: ‘I produce name-and-form for the body.’ Nor does the body think: ‘I have been produced by these conditions.’ Yet when these conditions exist and come together, the body arises.

“There the earth element is not a self, not a sentient being, not a life, not a creature, not a human, not a person, not a woman, not a man, not a neuter, not ‘I,’ not ‘mine,’ and not anyone else’s. In the same way the water element, the fire element, the wind element, the space element, and the consciousness element are not a self, not a sentient being, not a life, not a creature, not a human, not a person, not a woman, not a man, not a neuter, not ‘I,’ not ‘mine,’ and not anyone else’s.

“What is ignorance there? This manifold not-knowing, such as the perception of unity, the perception of a mass, the perception of permanence, the perception of stability, the perception of eternality, the perception of happiness, the perception of self, the perception of a sentient being, the perception of a living being, a person, a human, the perception of ‘I-making’ and ‘mine-making’ with respect to these very six elements: this is called ignorance.

“When ignorance exists in this way, passion, hatred, and delusion proceed toward objects. The passion, hatred, and delusion directed toward objects are called formations. The recognition of a particular object is consciousness. The four formless aggregates that arise together with consciousness and are called appropriation, together with the four great elements, are name-and-form; form is that which is taken up in dependence on them. Bringing these together as one, that is name-and-form.

“The sense faculties supported by name-and-form are the six sense-fields. The meeting of three dharmas is contact. Experience of contact is feeling. Fixation on feeling is craving. The expansion of craving is appropriation. Karma produced from appropriation, which generates renewed existence, is becoming. The arising of aggregates caused by becoming is birth. The maturation of aggregates that have been born is aging. The destruction of worn-out aggregates is death. The inward burning, bound up with attachment, of a confused person who is dying is sorrow. The utterance arising from sorrow is lamentation. The experience of what is unpleasant, joined with the five groups of consciousness, is pain. Mental pain joined with mind is mental distress. Whatever other afflictions there are, of this kind and the like, are agitation.

“There, ignorance is so called in the sense of the darkness of delusion; formations, in the sense of constructing; consciousness, in the sense of cognizing; name-and-form, in the sense of mutually supporting one another; the six sense-fields, in the sense of being gateways of access; contact, in the sense of touching; feeling, in the sense of experiencing; craving, in the sense of thirsting; appropriation, in the sense of appropriating; becoming, in the sense of renewed existence; birth, in the sense of being born; aging, in the sense of maturation; death, in the sense of destruction; sorrow, in the sense of grieving; lamentation, in the sense of lamenting; pain, in the sense of afflicting the body; mental distress, in the sense of afflicting the mind; agitation, in the sense of affliction.

“Alternatively, failure to understand reality, and false understanding of it, is not-knowing, ignorance. When ignorance exists in this way, three kinds of formations arise: those leading to merit, those leading to demerit, and those leading to the imperturbable. For formations leading to merit, consciousness too is one leading to merit; for formations leading to demerit, consciousness too is one leading to demerit; for formations leading to the imperturbable, consciousness too is one leading to the imperturbable. This is called consciousness.

“‘Name-and-form is conditioned by consciousness’: the four formless aggregates, beginning with feeling, are called name because they name in this or that existence. Together with the aggregate of form, they are called name-and-form. As name-and-form develops, activities proceed and are made manifest through the six gateways of the sense-fields; that is called the six sense-fields conditioned by name-and-form. From the six sense-fields proceed the six groups of contact; this is called contact conditioned by the six sense-fields. Whatever kind of contact occurs, feeling of that kind proceeds. This is called, monks, feeling conditioned by contact.

“Whoever especially savors, delights in, determines, and remains fixed on that feeling: this is called craving conditioned by feeling. The aspiration, ‘May I not be separated from dear forms and pleasant forms that are dear to myself through savoring, delighting, determining, and remaining fixed; may there always be no relinquishing of them,’ is called, monks, appropriation conditioned by craving.

“When a person craves for some object, he takes up appropriation in order to obtain and apply that object; he aspires to it again and again. Aspiring in this way, he sets in motion karma that generates renewed existence, by body, speech, and mind. This is called becoming conditioned by appropriation. The coming into being of aggregates produced by that karma is called birth conditioned by becoming. From the increase and maturation of aggregates produced by birth comes their destruction; this is called aging and death conditioned by birth.

“Thus this twelve-limbed dependent arising, with each limb having one another as cause and one another as condition, proceeds without being either impermanent or permanent, neither conditioned nor unconditioned, neither without a cause nor without a condition, neither one who feels nor one who does not feel, neither dependently arisen nor not dependently arisen, neither subject to exhaustion nor not subject to exhaustion, neither subject to destruction nor not subject to destruction, neither subject to cessation nor not subject to cessation. It proceeds from beginningless time without being cut off, like the current of a river.

“Although this twelve-limbed dependent arising proceeds without being cut off, like the current of a river, four limbs of this twelve-limbed dependent arising proceed as causes for the production of the aggregate. Which four? Ignorance, craving, karma, and consciousness.

“There consciousness is the cause in the nature of a seed; karma is the cause in the nature of a field; ignorance and craving are causes in the nature of afflictions. Karma and afflictions produce the seed of consciousness. There karma performs the function of a field for the seed of consciousness; craving moistens the seed of consciousness; ignorance scatters the seed of consciousness. When these conditions are absent, the seed of consciousness does not come into being.

“There karma does not think: ‘I perform the function of a field for the seed of consciousness.’ Craving too does not think: ‘I perform the function of moistening consciousness.’ Ignorance too does not think: ‘I scatter the seed of consciousness.’ Nor does the seed of consciousness think: ‘I have been produced by these conditions.’

“Yet the seed of consciousness, established in the field of karma, moistened by the moisture of craving, scattered by ignorance, grows and produces a sprout of name-and-form in the mother’s womb, at the point of connection with this or that place of rebirth. That sprout of name-and-form is not made by itself, not made by another, not made by both, not made by Īśvara, not transformed by time, not arisen from nature, not dependent on a single cause, and not arisen without a cause. Yet when the parents come together, the season is suitable, and the other conditions come together, the seed of consciousness, pierced by savoring, produces a sprout of name-and-form in the mother’s womb, because causes and conditions are unimpaired. These dharmas are without an owner, without appropriation, without ‘mine,’ artificial, like space, and of the nature of illusion.

“For example, visual consciousness arises from five causes. Which five? Depending on the eye, form, light, space, and the attention belonging to them, visual consciousness arises. There, the eye performs the function of a support for visual consciousness; form performs the function of an object; light performs the function of illumination; space performs the function of non-obstruction; and the attention belonging to them performs the function of attending. When these conditions are absent, visual consciousness does not arise. But when the internal sense-field of the eye is unimpaired, and form, light, space, and the attention belonging to them are likewise unimpaired, then, through the coming together of them all, visual consciousness arises.

“There the eye does not think: ‘I perform the function of a support for visual consciousness.’ Light too does not think: ‘I perform the function of illumination for visual consciousness.’ Space too does not think: ‘I perform the function of non-obstruction for visual consciousness.’ The attention belonging to them too does not think: ‘I perform the function of attending for visual consciousness.’ Nor does visual consciousness think: ‘I have been produced by these conditions.’ Yet when these conditions exist, visual consciousness arises. The same is to be applied, as appropriate, to the remaining sense faculties.

“There no dharma passes from this world to the next world; yet the manifestation of the fruit of karma exists because causes and conditions are unimpaired. For example, monks, an image of a face is seen in a perfectly clean mirror; the face does not pass into that mirror, yet the image of the face is manifested because causes and conditions are unimpaired. In the same way, no one has died from this world and been born elsewhere; yet the manifestation of the fruit of karma exists because causes and conditions are unimpaired.

“For example, monks, the orb of the moon travels four hundred yojanas above; yet an image of the moon is seen even in a small vessel of water. The moon has not fallen from its place in the sky above and passed into the small vessel of water, yet the manifestation of the orb of the moon exists because causes and conditions are unimpaired. In the same way, no one has died from this world and been born elsewhere; yet the manifestation of the fruit of karma exists because causes and conditions are unimpaired.

“For example, fire burns when fuel is present as its condition; when fuel is lacking, it does not burn. In the same way, monks, the seed of consciousness produced by karma and afflictions produces a sprout of name-and-form in the mother’s womb, at the point of connection with this or that place of rebirth, because causes and conditions are unimpaired. These dharmas are without an owner, without appropriation, without ‘mine,’ artificial, and of the nature of illusion.

“There internal dependent arising is to be seen through five causes. Which five? Not from permanence, not from annihilation, not from transmigration, from the production of a great result from a small cause, and through a continuum similar to that cause.

“How is it not from permanence? The aggregates at the time of death are different; the aggregates belonging to rebirth are different. It is not that those very aggregates at the time of death are the aggregates belonging to rebirth. Rather, the aggregates at the time of death cease, and at that very same time aggregates belonging to rebirth appear. Therefore it is not from permanence.

“How is it not from annihilation? Aggregates belonging to rebirth do not arise among aggregates at the time of death that have already ceased, nor among aggregates that have not ceased. Rather, aggregates at the time of death cease, and at that very time aggregates belonging to rebirth appear, like the rising and falling of a balance beam, like the image and reflection of the moon. Therefore it is not from annihilation.

“How is it not from transmigration? Different classes of beings produce birth in a class of birth that is of the same kind. Therefore it is not from transmigration.

“How does a great result arise from a small cause? A small karma is performed, and a great ripening of its fruit is experienced. Therefore there is the production of a great fruit from a small cause.

“How is there a continuum similar to that cause? Karma is performed that is to be experienced in a certain way, and its ripening is experienced in that way. Therefore there is a continuum similar to that cause. Thus, at length.”
