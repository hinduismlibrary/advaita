MMK 26.11

avidyāyāṃ niruddhāyāṃ saṃskārāṇām asaṃbhavaḥ
avidyāyā nirodhas tu jñānasyāsyaiva bhāvanāt || 11 ||

When ignorance has ceased, formations do not occur. But the cessation of ignorance comes from cultivating this very knowledge.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

avidyāyāṃ niruddhāyāṃ saṃskārāṇām asaṃbhavaḥ | avidyāyā nirodhas tu jñānasyāsyaiva bhāvanāt || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Since formations proceed only when ignorance is present, and do not proceed when it is absent, therefore:

“When ignorance has ceased, formations do not occur.”

Because the cause would be futile. But how does ignorance cease? He says:

“But the cessation of ignorance comes from cultivating this very [dependent arising] with knowledge.”

Ignorance is abandoned through cultivating this very dependent arising correctly, as it is, without distortion. For whoever correctly sees dependent arising does not apprehend the own-nature of even a subtle entity. He enters into the emptiness of own-nature of all entities, like a reflection, a dream, a fire-wheel, a fist, and the like.

Having entered into the emptiness of own-nature of all entities, he apprehends no thing, external or internal. Not apprehending, he is not confused about any dharma; and, being unconfused, he does not perform karma. In this way, through cultivation of dependent arising, he enters reality. For the yogin who sees reality, ignorance is certainly abandoned; for one whose ignorance has been abandoned, formations cease.
