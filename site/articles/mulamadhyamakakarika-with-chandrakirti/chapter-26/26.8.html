MMK 26.8

pañca skandhāḥ sa ca bhavo bhavāj jātiḥ pravartate
jarāmaraṇaduḥkhādi śokāḥ saparidevanāḥ || 8 ||

From becoming, birth proceeds; aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, mental distress, and agitation arise from birth.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pañca skandhāḥ sa ca bhavo bhavāj jātiḥ pravartate | jarāmaraṇaduḥkhādi śokāḥ saparidevanāḥ || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“From becoming, birth proceeds.”

Birth is the arising of future aggregates. It proceeds from becoming. Then later:

“Aging, death, sorrow with lamentation, pain, mental distress, and agitation—these arise from birth.”

These, aging, death, and the rest, which have birth as their cause, proceed. Their explanation is to be understood just as in the sūtra. There, aging is the maturation of the aggregates; death is the breaking up of the aggregates when they are worn out. The inward burning, bound up with attachment, of a confused person who is dying and perishing is sorrow. The speech that arises from sorrow is lamentation. The striking of the five sense faculties is pain. The striking of the mind is mental distress. Agitations are those that arise from the great multitude of pain and mental distress.
