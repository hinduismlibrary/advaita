MMK 26.9

daurmanasyam = dejection; upāyāsāḥ = distress; jāteḥ = from birth; etat = this; pravartate = proceeds; kevalasya = of the whole; evam = thus; etasya = this; duḥkha-skandhasya = mass of suffering; saṃbhavaḥ = arising

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

daurmanasyam upāyāsā jāter etat pravartate | kevalasyaivam etasya duḥkhaskandhasya saṃbhavaḥ || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

In this way, the whole mass of suffering arises through conditions, without a permanent self or anything that belongs to such a self.

Candrakīrti emphasizes the dependent sequence: each link supports the next, yet no independent owner or essence is found in the resulting suffering.
