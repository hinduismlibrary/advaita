MMK 10.16

Investigation of Fire and Firewood

← MMK 10.15Chapter 10MMK 11.1 →

MMK 10.16

ātmanaśca satattvaṃ ye bhāvānāṃ ca pṛthakpṛthak
nirdiśanti na tānmanye śāsanasyārthakovidān || 16 ||

Those who state the sameness of self and things, and their separateness, I do not consider knowers of the meaning of the Teaching.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmanaśca satattvaṃ ye bhāvānāṃ ca pṛthakpṛthak | nirdiśanti na tānmanye śāsanasyārthakovidān || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, when the establishment of pots and the like, as well as self and appropriation, depending on one another, just as agent and action, has been established, through great delusion those who imagine the arrangement of things according to the views of non-Buddhist teachers, while claiming to understand the non-inverted meaning of the Tathāgata’s words, introduce it as the meaning of the Buddhist teaching:

There, that which exists together with that is “together”; the state of being together is “sameness”: being undivided, not other, identity—this is the meaning. The teacher does not consider those who describe this sameness as learned in the meaning of the Teaching. For example: the self is designated through that appropriation. It exists together with that very appropriation; it is not separate. It exists precisely through non-separation. Likewise, the pot exists precisely not separately from the cause, clay and so on, through which it is designated. Those who describe the sameness of self and things thus do not see the reality of the most profound dependent arising, the statement of designation in dependence, which is free from permanence and annihilation. And those who state them separately—as self separate, appropriation separate, effect separate, cause separate, and so on—see otherness. The teacher does not consider these people either to be knowers of the meaning of the Teaching. As has been said:

“The world, free from identity and otherness, is like an echo. Having arrived at transition and gone, you have known it, O blameless one.”

And thus, for the yogin supreme in having attained the reality of dharma through the examination of fire and fuel, the body is not burned even by the fires of passion, hatred, and delusion, though desired at an unseasonable time. As the Blessed One said:

“As the sky has never before been burned, although it is being burned by many hundreds of aeons, so he too, knowing phenomena to be like the sky, is never burned, even in the midst of fire.

“All the blazing Buddha-fields: having stood in meditation, he makes an aspiration. ‘May this burning be completely calmed.’ Even if the earth perishes, there is no change in this.

“Just as fire arises from the friction of a lower fire-stick and an upper fire-stick, through the joining of three conditions by the effort of the hands, having done its work it quickly ceases.

“If some wise person seeks, ‘From where has this come? Where does it go?’ its coming and going are not found in any direction, wherever one seeks.

“The aggregates, sense-fields, and elements are empty; the inner is empty, the outer is empty. All phenomena, apart from every self and without abode, have the characteristic nature of space.

“These such characteristics of phenomena were awakened by you yourself in the presence of Buddha Dīpaṃkara.

“Just as you understood them yourself, so awaken the gods and humans.

“The world is burned by the faults of passion, falsely imagined contrary to reality. O leader, release the stream of immortality, cooled by the water of calming from the cloud of compassion.”

Thus, and so forth.

Thus ends the tenth chapter, called “Examination of Fire and Fuel,” in the Madhyamaka commentary Prasannapadā composed by Ācārya Candrakīrti.
