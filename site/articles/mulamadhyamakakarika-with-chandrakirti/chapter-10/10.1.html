MMK 10.1

yadindhanaṃ sa cedagnirekatvaṃ kartṛkarmaṇoḥ
anyaścedindhanādagnirindhanādapyṛte bhavet || 1 ||

If that which is fuel is that, fire, there is identity of agent and action. If fire is different from fuel, it would exist even apart from fuel.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadindhanaṃ sa cedagnirekatvaṃ kartṛkarmaṇoḥ | anyaścedindhanādagnirindhanādapyṛte bhavet || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here [the opponent] says: This is not correct: that, therefore, just as with action and agent, so too the appropriator and the appropriated are not established intrinsically. For dependent things too are seen to possess own-being. Thus fire exists in dependence on fuel; and fire is not without own-being, because its own-being consisting of heat, burning, and so forth is perceived as effect. Likewise fuel exists in dependence on fire; and it is not without own-being, because its own-being is the four external great elements. Thus, although dependent on the appropriated, the appropriator will exist by its own-being, and the appropriated dependent on the appropriator. Like fire and fuel, these two—the appropriator and the appropriated—will exist. The response: This would be so if fire and fuel existed. But they do not exist. How? Here, if fire and fuel existed, they would certainly be either one through identity or different through otherness. But neither way is reasonable. This is stated:

There, that which is being burned is fuel, consisting of wood and the like, what is to be burned. Its burner, fire, is the agent. Here, if one supposes that whatever is fuel, that very thing is fire, then there would be identity of agent and action. But this is not seen, because it would entail the identity of potter and pot, and of cutter and that which is to be cut; and that is not accepted. But if [they are] different, even so, if fire were other than fuel, then fire independent of fuel would be perceived. For a cloth, which is other than a pot, is seen independent of it. But fire is not independent of fuel. Therefore this is not reasonable.
