MMK 10.15

agni-indhanābhyām = by fire and firewood; vyākhyātaḥ = explained; ātmopādānayoḥ = of self and appropriation; kramaḥ = sequence; sarvaḥ = all; niravaśeṣeṇa = without remainder; sārdham = together; ghaṭa-paṭa-ādibhiḥ = with pot, cloth, and the rest

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

agnīndhanābhyāṃ vyākhyāta ātmopādānayoḥ kramaḥ | sarvo niravaśeṣeṇa sārdhaṃ ghaṭapaṭādibhiḥ || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The fivefold analysis of fire and fuel applies to the self and the appropriated aggregates. The five aggregates that are taken up are called “appropriation”; the person designated in dependence on them is called the appropriator, the one who takes them up. The self is not identical with the aggregates, separate from them, their possessor, inside them, or inside the self as an independent set of aggregates. As with actor and action, self and aggregates are established only in dependence on one another.

The same reasoning extends without exception to other things. A pot is an effect of clay, sticks, a wheel, water, a potter and effort; it is also a whole designated in dependence on its parts. Its shape marks it, and it is the bearer of qualities such as color. Each relation—cause and effect, parts and whole, mark and marked, quality and possessor—must be examined as fire and fuel are.
