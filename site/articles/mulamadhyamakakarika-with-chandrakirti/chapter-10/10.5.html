MMK 10.5

anyo na prāpsyate 'prāpto na dhakṣyatyadahan punaḥ
na nirvāsyatyanirvāṇaḥ sthāsyate vā svaliṅgavān || 5 ||

It will not reach; not having reached, it will not burn; not burning, it will not be extinguished; not extinguished, it will remain bearing its own mark.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anyo na prāpsyate 'prāpto na dhakṣyatyadahan punaḥ | na nirvāsyatyanirvāṇaḥ sthāsyate vā svaliṅgavān || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover, showing that, upon accepting otherness, because there is no designation “being kindled” for fire and fuel, how is there fuel being kindled, or how will fire burn fuel, he says:

If fire were other than fuel, because of its otherness it would not reach fuel, just as darkness [does not reach it]. And it will not burn, because it has not reached it: this is the meaning, as if it were situated in a distant place. And thus the statement “fuel is being kindled” is not at all established. And then there would be no extinguishing of fire. And not extinguished, it would remain bearing its own mark; that is, burning. The word “or” should be understood as emphasis, or as expressing an alternative. “The fire will remain bearing its own mark alone,” or “there is no otherness of fire from fuel.” Or [the word “or” is] in the sense of conjunction: the other will not reach, will not burn, will not be extinguished, and will remain bearing its own mark. Therefore otherness of fire from fuel is unreasonable.
