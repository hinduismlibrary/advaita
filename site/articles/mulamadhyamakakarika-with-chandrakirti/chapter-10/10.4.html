MMK 10.4

tatraitasmādidhyamānamindhanaṃ bhavatīti cet
kenedhyatāmindhanaṃ tattāvanmātramidaṃ yadā || 4 ||

If you say, “From this, the fuel being kindled comes about,” when this alone is there, by what would the fuel be kindled?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tatraitasmādidhyamānamindhanaṃ bhavatīti cet | kenedhyatāmindhanaṃ tattāvanmātramidaṃ yadā || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here [the opponent] says: This statement, “If fire is different from fuel, it would exist even apart from fuel,” is not correct. For, even when there is difference between fire and fuel, fire does not exist without fuel. Because the thing encompassed by flame, characterized by being burnable, is fuel; and fire is perceived by relying on that, not separately. And when this is so, when the designation “fuel” occurs just from connection with fire, and fire is perceived by relying on fuel, not separately, then there is no occasion at all for the consequence of the fault beginning “If fire is different from fuel,” and so on. Showing the unsoundness of this position, he says:

If you think that the thing encompassed by flame, characterized as burnable, is fuel, and fire is that which depends on it, even so, when it is supposed thus, the statement “fire burns fuel” is not established. For:

When it is supposed here that fire burns fuel, the burnable fuel encompassed by flame is [what is meant]. Apart from these two, we do not see another fire by which fuel might be burned. For only this much is perceived: namely, only the burnable thing encompassed by flame. And when there is no fire apart from these two, by what would that fuel be burned? “When this alone is there” means when only this much is there; the meaning is, when only the thing being kindled is there. Therefore fire does not burn fuel, because there is no fire apart from that. And when this is so, from where is the encompassing of anything by flame? Thus that very fault does not disappear.
