MMK 10.4

Concerning this, if one thinks that while burning it is firewood, if it is such only at that time, by what could that firewood be ignited?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tatraitasmādidhyamānamindhanaṃ bhavatīti cet | kenedhyatāmindhanaṃ tattāvanmātramidaṃ yadā || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent says fire and fuel may be different while still meeting: burning wood is called fuel because it is enveloped by flame, and fire is known through its support in the wood. Candrakīrti asks what, in that case, is doing the burning. Apart from the burning fuel and the flame that surrounds it, no separate fire is found. If only the burning wood is present, what ignites it? The same problem returns: the relation cannot establish two independently existing things.
