MMK 10.13

āgacchatyanyato nāgnirindhane 'gnirna vidyate
atrendhane śeṣamuktaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 13 ||

Fire does not come from elsewhere; fire is not found in fuel. Here, in regard to fuel, what remains has been stated through the going, the gone, and the not-yet-gone.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

āgacchatyanyato nāgnirindhane 'gnirna vidyate | atrendhane śeṣamuktaṃ gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 13 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover, just as previously the refutation of what has gone, what has not gone, and what is going was stated:

Here, in the context of fuel, the remaining refutation is to be understood by the refutation of what is going, what has gone, and what is not yet gone, with the wording stated in reverse order:

“The burned does not burn; the unburned indeed does not burn. That which is burning, separated from the burned and the unburned, does not burn.” And so forth. Since this is so, it is to be understood that there is no burning of fuel by fire.
