MMK 10.3

paratra nirapekṣatvādapradīpanahetukaḥ
punarārambhavaiyarthyaṃ nityadīptaḥ prasajyate || 3 ||

Because it does not depend on another, it is without a cause of kindling; futility in beginning again follows, and it is continually burning.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paratra nirapekṣatvādapradīpanahetukaḥ | punarārambhavaiyarthyaṃ nityadīptaḥ prasajyate || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Desiring to make clear this very meaning, he says:

Thus it is stated: Here, that by which something is kindled is the kindling, the fuel. The cause of kindling is that of which kindling is the cause; without a cause of kindling is without a cause of kindling. If fire were other than fuel, then it would be independent of fuel. For that which is other than something is seen independent of that thing, as a cloth and the like [are independent] of a pot. And from that, because it is independent of another, it would be without a cause of kindling, for fire dependent on fuel would be extinguished in the absence of that [fuel]. But when it is independent of fuel, because the condition for its extinguishing is lacking, it would indeed be ever burning. And when fire is ever burning, the bringing together of fuel and the like for the sake of not extinguishing the fire would be wholly pointless. And, this being so, fire would be an agent without an action. But an agent without an existing action is not an agent, just as a barren woman's son is not. Therefore otherness of fire from fuel is not reasonable.
