MMK 5.1

nākāśaṃ vidyate kiṃcitpūrvamākāśalakṣaṇāt
alakṣaṇaṃ prasajyeta syātpūrvaṃ yadi lakṣaṇāt || 1 ||

Nothing that is space is found before the characteristic of space. If it were before the characteristic, it would follow that it was without a characteristic.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nākāśaṃ vidyate kiṃcitpūrvamākāśalakṣaṇāt | alakṣaṇaṃ prasajyeta syātpūrvaṃ yadi lakṣaṇāt || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: “The elements exist, because they have not been refuted. And the Blessed One said, ‘This person, great king, consists of six elements,’ and so on. Therefore, from the recitation of the Teaching, just as the elements exist, so too the aggregates and sense-fields exist.” Reply: The aggregates and sense-fields would exist if the elements existed. How? He says:

“Nothing that is space is found before the characteristic of space. If it were before the characteristic, it would follow that it was without a characteristic.”

Here the six elements are named earth, water, fire, wind, space, and consciousness. Among these, with reference to space, the refutation is stated by examining its own nature. Here non-obstruction is said to be the characteristic of space. If space, as the characterized thing, were prior to the characteristic of non-obstruction, then, because the characteristic is applied, space in the form of the characterized thing is not prior to the characteristic of non-obstruction. When it is so, he says:

“Nothing that is space is found before the characteristic of space. If it were before the characteristic, it would follow that it was without a characteristic.”
