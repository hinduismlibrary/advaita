MMK 5.8

astitvaṃ ye tu paśyanti nāstitvaṃ cālpabuddhayaḥ
bhāvānāṃ te na paśyanti draṣṭavyopaśamaṃ śivam || 8 ||

Those of limited understanding who see existence and nonexistence do not see the auspicious peace of phenomena.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

astitvaṃ ye tu paśyanti nāstitvaṃ cālpabuddhayaḥ | bhāvānāṃ te na paśyanti draṣṭavyopaśamaṃ śivam || 8 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Those who see things as existing or non-existing are of limited understanding. They do not see the auspicious pacification of what is to be seen: the ending of the whole network of concepts, where knowing and known are no longer established as intrinsically real. Candrakīrti describes this as the peaceful, unaging, undying, unelaborated reality of nirvāṇa, whose nature is emptiness.

He cites the *Ratnāvalī*: one who holds nihilism goes to a bad state, while one who avoids nihilism goes to a good state; liberation rests on knowing things as they are, without duality. The *Samādhirājasūtra* further says that “it exists” and “it does not exist,” like “pure” and “impure,” are both extremes. The wise abandon both and do not settle in a middle position. Disputing about existence or purity does not quiet suffering; freedom from such dispute brings suffering to an end. Nirvāṇa is not reached by treating it as a destination along an intrinsically real worldly path.
