MMK 4.3

rūpeṇa tu vinirmuktaṃ yadi syād rūpakāraṇam
akāryakaṃ kāraṇaṃ syān nāstyakāryaṃ ca kāraṇam || 3 ||

If the cause of form were separate from form, the cause would be without an effect; and a cause without an effect does not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rūpeṇa tu vinirmuktaṃ yadi syād rūpakāraṇam | akāryakaṃ kāraṇaṃ syān nāstyakāryaṃ ca kāraṇam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, showing in the same way that a cause of form does not exist apart from form, he says: “If a cause of form were separate from form”—if a cause of form were separate from the effect-form—then, just as a bowl, established separately from a pot, is not a cause of the pot, so a cause accepted as separate from its effect

“would be a cause without an effect; it would be without a cause. And a cause without an effect does not exist.”

For the cause’s being a cause depends on the effect’s coming about; and, apart from dependence on the effect, the coming about of a cause established separately from it does not exist. And a cause that has no effect, because it is without a cause, does not exist at all, like the horns of a man, a snake, or a horse. Thus he says: “And a cause without an effect does not exist.”
