MMK 4.9

vyākhyāne ya upālambhaṃ kṛte śūnyatayā vadet
sarvaṃ tasyānupālabdhaṃ samaṃ sādhyena jāyate || 9 ||

If, in an explanation, someone should make an objection by means of emptiness, all of that objection of his becomes unobjectionable and equal to what is to be established.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

vyākhyāne ya upālambhaṃ kṛte śūnyatayā vadet | sarvaṃ tasyānupālabdhaṃ samaṃ sādhyena jāyate || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And just as this method was stated in refuting the opposing position, so too at the time of explanation, he says:

“If, in an explanation, someone should make an objection by means of emptiness, all of that objection of his becomes unobjectionable and equal to what is to be established.”

At the time of explanation too, any objection that a student being taught might make is to be understood, as before, as equal to what is to be established. As has been said:

“Whoever sees the emptiness of one entity is remembered as one who sees the emptiness of all. Whatever is the emptiness of one, that same is the emptiness of all.”

And also in the Noble Gaganagañja-samādhi-sūtra:

“Through one dharma he follows all dharmas, like an illusion and a mirage: ungraspable, empty, without core, and not eternal. He quickly goes to the seat of awakening.”

And also in the Samādhirāja-sūtra:

“As the conception of self is known, just so understanding is extended everywhere. And all dharmas are of that nature, pure, like the sky.

“By one he knows all; by one he sees all. Though he cultivates much, pride does not arise in him.”

Thus, in the Prasannapadā, the Madhyamaka commentary composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti, the fourth chapter, called ‘Examination of the Aggregates,’ is complete.
