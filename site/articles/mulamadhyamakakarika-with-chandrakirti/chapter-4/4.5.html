MMK 4.5

niṣkāraṇaṃ punā rūpaṃ naiva naivopapadyate
tattvadarśī yogī rūpagatān kāṃścinna vikalpān vikalpayet || 5 ||

Again, form without a cause is certainly not at all possible. Therefore, a yogi who sees reality should not construct any concepts concerning form.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

niṣkāraṇaṃ punā rūpaṃ naiva naivopapadyate | tattvadarśī yogī rūpagatān kāṃścinna vikalpān vikalpayet || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone might say: “Although a cause of form is not possible in this way, nevertheless the effect, form, exists; because it exists, its cause too will exist.” It would be so if form, the effect, existed. But it does not exist, because:

“Again, form without a cause is certainly not at all possible.”

It has been stated in this way that a cause of form does not exist. When there is no cause, how could effect-form be without a cause? By the two emphatic negations in “certainly not at all,” he shows that, because it would be causeless, this is wholly undesirable. And since form, when examined in this way, is not possible in any respect,

“therefore, a yogi who sees reality should not construct any concepts concerning form.”

That is the meaning: with form as their object, one should not construct any of the concepts of resistant and non-resistant, manifest and non-manifest, past, future, blue, yellow, and the like.
