MMK 8.4

hetāvasati kāryaṃ ca kāraṇaṃ ca na vidyate
tadabhāve kriyā kartā karaṇaṃ ca na vidyate || 4 ||

When there is no cause, neither effect nor cause exists. In their absence, action, actor, and instrument do not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetāvasati kāryaṃ ca kāraṇaṃ ca na vidyate | tadabhāve kriyā kartā karaṇaṃ ca na vidyate || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And if the doctrine of causelessness is accepted, all effects and causes would be denied, he says:

“When there is no cause, neither effect nor cause exists.”

When a cause is accepted, what is produced by the cause is reasonably called its effect, and that which produces it is its cause. For example, clay is the cause of a pot; the pot is its effect; and the wheel and the rest are its cooperative causes. But if the doctrine of causelessness is accepted, because it does not depend on a cause, this pot could not exist, like a pot made from the hair on a frog’s head. When the pot does not exist, where would its cause come from? Thus:

“When there is no cause, neither effect nor cause exists.”

And further:

“In their absence, action, actor, and instrument do not exist.”

When effect and cause are absent, what activity could there be for someone doing something? In what action would the potter have agency by his own power? Nor is it reasonable that clay, through activity proceeding as identical with it, be an instrument as the most effective means. Thus:

“In their absence, action, actor, and instrument do not exist.”
