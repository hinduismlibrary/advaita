MMK 8.1

Investigation of Act and Actor

MMK 8.1

sadbhūtaḥ kārakaḥ karma sadbhūtaṃ na karotyayam
kārako nāpyasadbhūtaḥ karmāsadbhūtamīhate || 1 ||

An existing actor does not perform an existing action; nor does an actor perform an action that is nonexistent.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sadbhūtaḥ kārakaḥ karma sadbhūtaṃ na karotyayam | kārako nāpyasadbhūtaḥ karmāsadbhūtamīhate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Consciousness and the rest certainly exist as conditioned things because the actor and action that produce them exist. For the Blessed One said: ‘Monks, this person, followed by ignorance, forms meritorious formations, also unmeritorious ones, and also formations that are imperturbable.’ By this and the rest, an actor of actions has been designated, and consciousness and the rest have been taught as the fruit of that action. What has an actor exists, as for example a pot; what does not exist has no actor, as for example a cloak made of tortoise hair.” Reply: Consciousness and the rest would be conditioned things if they had an actor and action that produced them. But they do not, because:

“An existing actor does not perform an existing action; nor does an actor perform an action that is nonexistent.”

Here, the one who does is an actor. The designation “actor” applies only to one who is doing something, not to one who is not doing it. And that doing—is it conceived for an existent actor, a nonexistent actor, or one that is both existent and nonexistent? “Action” is what is most desired by the actor because it is done. That too is threefold: existent, nonexistent, and both existent and nonexistent. Of these, one proposition is that an existent actor, endowed with activity, does not perform an existent action, endowed with activity. Now the other proposition is that a nonexistent actor, without activity and without the designation “actor,” does not perform a nonexistent action, without activity.
