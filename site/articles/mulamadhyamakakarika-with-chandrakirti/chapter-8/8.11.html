Investigation of Act and Actor

MMK 8.11

karoti sadasadbhūto na sannāsacca kārakaḥ
karma tattu vijānīyātpūrvoktaireva hetubhiḥ || 11 ||

An actor who is both existent and nonexistent does not perform an action that is neither existent nor nonexistent, nor one that is both; understand this by the reasons stated earlier.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

karoti sadasadbhūto na sannāsacca kārakaḥ | karma tattu vijānīyātpūrvoktaireva hetubhiḥ || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now he shows how an actor who is both existent and nonexistent, being one yet of both natures, does not perform an action that is distinct and of both natures:

“An actor who is both existent and nonexistent does not perform an action that is neither existent nor nonexistent, nor one that is both; understand this by the reasons stated earlier.”

Because of the statement “For how could what is mutually opposed, existent and nonexistent, be one?,” an actor who is both existent and nonexistent does not perform it. An existent action is not performed because:

“For an existing action there is no activity, and the agent would be without an action.”

A nonexistent action too is not performed because of the faults stated beginning with “the action would be without a cause,” and “when there is no cause, neither effect nor cause exists.” Since, in every way, the establishment of actor and action is unreasonable in both the uniform and mixed positions, the statement—“consciousness and the rest certainly exist as conditioned things because actors and actions that produce them exist”—is unreasonable.
