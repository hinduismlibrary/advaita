MMK 8.13

Investigation of Act and Actor

MMK 8.13

evaṃ vidyādupādānaṃ vyutsargāditi karmaṇaḥ
kartuśca karmakartṛbhyāṃ śeṣān bhāvān vibhāvayet || 13 ||

Thus, from the negation of action and agent, one should understand appropriation; and through agent and action one should analyze the remaining entities.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ vidyādupādānaṃ vyutsargāditi karmaṇaḥ | kartuśca karmakartṛbhyāṃ śeṣān bhāvān vibhāvayet || 13 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And just as there is mutual establishment of action and actor, so too for other entities, he indicates:

“Thus, from the negation of action and agent, one should understand appropriation; and through agent and action one should analyze the remaining entities.”

“Thus” indicates the preceding designation of action and actor. Appropriation means taking up. By this he refers to the act of taking up; and that act brings near its own means, the taker as agent, and the thing taken as action. The establishment of those two, the thing to be taken and the taker, depends on one another, just like action and actor; it is not inherent. Why is it not inherent? “From the negation of action and agent.” The meaning is: by the very reasons with which we stated the negation of action and actor, the taker and the thing to be taken should be understood as refuted. And not only is their mutual establishment to be understood through the negation of these two, but:

“Through agent and action one should analyze the remaining entities.”

The remainder of entities, apart from action, actor, appropriated thing, and appropriator: things produced and producers, goers and going, seen and seeing, characterized and characteristic, produced and producer, as well as parts and wholes, qualities and possessors of qualities, measures and objects measured, and all entities without remainder—by examining action and actor, one should reject their existence by own-nature and understand their establishment as only mutually dependent. A wise person, wishing for liberation from the bonds of birth, aging, and death, should analyze them in this way. The detailed examination of these is to be understood from the Madhyamakāvatāra and other works.

Someone says: “Since ‘one should analyze the remaining entities’ already includes the taker and the thing taken, the separate mention of appropriation and appropriator is unreasonable.” This is true. Nevertheless, appropriation and appropriator are mentioned to show their prominence in examining reality. For in the chapters that follow, the examination of just these two will occur extensively. For this very reason, the Blessed One said in the Noble Upāli’s Questions:

“I showed the terror of hell; many thousands of beings were shaken. But there is no being here who, having fallen, goes to the terrible lower realm.

“There are no actors or causes by which black swords are made. Through the power of conception one sees the bodies there falling into the lower realm, pierced by swords.

“Beautiful and delightful, adorned with flowers, golden palaces shine pleasingly. There is no actor in them either; they too have been established through the power of conception.

“Through the power of conception the world is conceived; the fool is conceived through the grasping of signs. But that grasping is no grasping, not real; for conceptions are like illusion and mirage.”

Thus, in the Prasannapadā, the Madhyamaka commentary composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti, the eighth chapter, called “Examination of Action and Actor,” is complete.
