MMK 1.12

athāsad api tat tebhyaḥ pratyayebhyaḥ pravartate
apratyayebhyo ’pi kasmān nābhipravartate phalam

If an effect that does not exist arises from these conditions, why does an effect not also arise from non-conditions?

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone might intend: “Even though [the result] does not exist, it proceeds from those conditions.” [Nāgārjuna asks:] “Then why does the result not also proceed from non-conditions?” The result is absent among non-conditions too. Why then does cloth not proceed even from non-conditions, such as vīraṇa grass? Thus the occurrence of a result is not established by intrinsic nature.

Here someone says: “If the result were different and the conditions were different, then there could be the question whether the result exists in the conditions or does not exist in them. But the result is not separate. Rather, it consists of the conditions themselves.” We reply:

Original Sanskrit
athāsadapi tattebhyaḥ pratyayebhyaḥ pravartate | ityabhiprāyaḥ syāt- apratyayebhyo 'pi kasmātphalaṃ nābhipravartate || 14 || apratyayeṣvapi nāsti phalamiti | apratyayebhyo 'pi vīraṇādibhyaḥ kasmānnābhipravartate paṭa iti nāsti phalapravṛttiḥ svarūpataḥ || 14 || atrāha- yadi anyat phalaṃ syādanye ca pratyayāḥ, tadā kiṃ pratyayeṣu phalamasti nāstīti cintā syāt | nāsti tu vyatiriktaṃ phalam, kiṃ tarhi pratyayamayameveti? ucyate-
