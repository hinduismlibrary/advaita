MMK 1.7

na san nāsan na sad asan dharmo nirvartate yadā
kathaṃ nirvartako hetur evaṃ sati hi yujyate

When a phenomenon is neither existent, non-existent, nor both, how can a productive cause be tenable? It cannot.

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here 'producer' means 'one that brings about [something].' If a phenomenon that was to be produced were actually produced, a cause that produces could produce it. But it is not produced, because there is no object of production that is existent, nonexistent, or both. An existent object is not produced because it already exists; a nonexistent object also is not produced because it does not exist; an object that is both existent and nonexistent is not produced, because mutually contradictory properties cannot belong to the same thing and because the faults stated for both alternatives apply.

Since there is thus no arising of an effect, a causal condition does not exist either. Accordingly, the claim that a causal condition exists because its defining characteristic is established is untenable. [Nāgārjuna] now speaks to refute the object-condition.

Original Sanskrit
na sannāsanna sadasan dharmo nirvartate yadā | kathaṃ nirvartako heturevaṃ sati hi yujyate || 9 || tatra nirvartaka utpādakaḥ | yadi nirvartyo dharmo nirvarteta, tamutpādako heturutpādayet | na tu nirvartate, sadasadubhayarūpasya nirvartyasyābhāvāt | tatra sanna nirvartate vidyamānatvāt | asannapi, avidyamānatvāt | sadasannapi, parasparaviruddhasyaikārthasyābhāvāt, ubhayapakṣābhihitadoṣatvācca | yata evaṃ kāryasyotpattirnāsti, hetupratyayo 'pyato nāsti | tataśca yaduktaṃ lakṣaṇasaṃbhavādvidyate hetupratyaya iti, tadevaṃ sati na yujyate || 9 || idānīmālambanapratyayaniṣedhārthamāha-
