MMK 1.13

phalaṃ ca pratyayamayaṃ pratyayāś cāsvayaṃmayāḥ
phalam asvamayebhyo yat tat pratyayamayaṃ katham

The effect is made of conditions, while the conditions are not made of themselves. How can an effect made from conditions that are not made of themselves be made of conditions?

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If it is established that the result consists of conditions, that it is a transformation of the conditions, this is not reasonable. For those conditions themselves do not consist of themselves, meaning that they do not possess the intrinsic nature of conditions. Thus one says, “Cloth consists of threads.” Cloth would be [so constituted] if the threads themselves were established by intrinsic nature. But they consist of fibres, are transformations of fibres, and are not established by intrinsic nature. Consequently, how could the effect called cloth, coming from these conditions that do not consist of themselves and are devoid of intrinsic nature, consist of threads?

As it has been said: “Cloth is established from a cause; the cause is established from another. How can that which lacks establishment from itself give rise to another?”
