MMK 1.5

utpadyate pratītyemān itīme pratyayāḥ kila
yāvan notpadyata ime tāvan nāpratyayāḥ katham

Since something arises in dependence on these, they are called “conditions.” As long as it has not arisen, why are these not non-conditions?

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If consciousness arises depending on conditions such as the eye, and for that reason these are called its conditions, then before the effect called consciousness has arisen, how are the eye and the others not non-conditions? The meaning is that they certainly are non-conditions. And there is no arising from non-conditions, just as oil does not arise from grains of sand.

Suppose it is thought that, although previously non-conditions, they attain the status of conditions in dependence on some other condition. This too is unreasonable. For that further condition, which is imagined to make the non-condition a condition, would become its condition only on having the status of a condition itself. The same question arises in that case too, so this is not reasonable.

Moreover, when the eye and the other factors are imagined as conditions of consciousness, are they imagined as conditions of a consciousness that exists, or one that does not exist? In either case it does not hold, as he says:
