MMK 1.2

catvāraḥ pratyayā hetur ārambaṇam anantaram
tathaivādhipateyaṃ ca pratyayo nāsti pañcamaḥ

There are four conditions: a cause, an object, an immediately preceding condition, and a dominant condition. There is no fifth.

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here, according to the definition “a cause is that which brings about [an effect],” whatever brings about a particular thing while existing in the form of a seed is its causal condition. The object in dependence upon which an arising phenomenon arises is its object-condition. The immediately preceding cessation of a cause is a condition for the arising of the effect; for example, the immediately preceding cessation of the seed is a condition for the arising of the sprout. That on whose presence another thing occurs is its dominant condition. These are the four conditions.

Other [conditions], such as those arising before, arising simultaneously, and arising later, are included within these very four. But Īśvara and the like cannot even be conditions, and for precisely this reason the statement is restrictive: “There is no fifth condition.” Therefore, there is arising of things from conditions that are other than them: this is arising from another. Here we reply: things do not arise from conditions that are other than them. For:
