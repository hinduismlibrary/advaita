MMK 20.1

hetośca pratyayānāṃ ca sāmagryā jāyate yadi
phalamasti ca sāmagryāṃ sāmagryā jāyate katham || 1 ||

If, in the collection of cause and conditions, the fruit exists, how is it produced from the collection?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetośca pratyayānāṃ ca sāmagryā jāyate yadi | phalamasti ca sāmagryāṃ sāmagryā jāyate katham || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The twentieth chapter, called “Examination of the Collection.” An opponent says: “Time exists, because it is a cooperating cause in the occurrence of a result. What does not exist does not function as a cooperating cause, like the son of a barren woman. Therefore time exists, because it is a cooperating cause. Here, this sprout arises depending on the collection of causes and conditions called seed, earth, water, fire, wind, and space. Although the collection of conditions such as seed exists, it does not arise because a particular season is not present. And so it is with internal things too. As the Blessed One said:

‘Actions are not destroyed even through hundreds of crores of eons; when they have obtained the collection and the time, they indeed bear fruit for embodied beings.’

“And because dependence on time exists in this way, that time exists, which is a cooperating cause in the occurrence of a sprout and the like.” It is replied: Time would be a cooperating cause if the occurrence of the fruit, the sprout and the like, existed. But it does not. How so? Here, when the arising of the fruit, the sprout and the like, from a collection of causes and conditions such as seed is imagined, is the arising of a result imagined when the result is already established in the collection, or when it is not established? What follows? If it is imagined to be already established, that is not fitting. Showing this, he says:

“If, in the collection of cause and conditions, the fruit exists, how is it produced from the collection?” || 1 ||

If, according to your view, the fruit exists in the collection of causes and conditions, then, since it exists in the collection, how is it produced by that collection? Yogurt existing in a pot is not produced by the pot. Moreover, what exists is already completed; like a completed pot standing before one, it does not again depend on birth. If it is said that manifestation or grossness is produced in what exists in a subtle form, then the answer to that position was already stated earlier:

“Fire does not come from elsewhere; fire is not found in the fuel.” || 1 ||
