MMK 20.8

pūrvameva ca sāmagryāḥ phalaṃ prādurbhavedyadi
hetupratyayanirmuktaṃ phalamāhetukaṃ bhavet || 8 ||

If the fruit were to appear before the collection, the fruit, being free from causes and conditions, would be without a cause.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pūrvameva ca sāmagryāḥ phalaṃ prādurbhavedyadi | hetupratyayanirmuktaṃ phalamāhetukaṃ bhavet || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Some others explain: “Things are not properly produced after not having existed, because the consequence would be that they arose without cause. Therefore the fruit, before the collection of causes and conditions, is already established in a future state, with a future nature. Its present state is produced by the collection of causes and conditions, but its substance is already established.” To them it is said:

“If the fruit were to appear before the collection, the fruit, being free from causes and conditions, would be without a cause.” || 8 ||

If, as you desire, the fruit existed in its own form even before the collection, it would be independent of causes and conditions, and then it would be without a cause. But it is not reasonable that causeless entities exist, since that would entail the existence of even a horse’s, bull’s, or snake’s horns. And since that which was already established before would again be without purpose in dependence on causes and conditions, this is not reasonable. || 8 ||
