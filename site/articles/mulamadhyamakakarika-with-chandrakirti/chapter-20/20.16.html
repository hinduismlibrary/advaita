MMK 20.16

hetuḥ phalena śūnyaścetkathaṃ janayate phalam
hetuḥ phalenāśūnyaścetkathaṃ janayate phalam || 16 ||

If a cause is empty of a result, how could it produce one? If it is not empty of a result, how could it produce one?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetuḥ phalena śūnyaścetkathaṃ janayate phalam | hetuḥ phalenāśūnyaścetkathaṃ janayate phalam || 16 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

If the cause is empty of the fruit, it has no fruit to produce and would be no better than a noncause. If it is not empty of the fruit, then the fruit already exists and does not need to arise.

Candrakīrti rules out both ways of treating the cause as a container with a fixed relation to its effect. Causality cannot be grounded in either an absent or a pre-existing fruit.
