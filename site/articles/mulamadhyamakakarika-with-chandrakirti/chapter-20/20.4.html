MMK 20.4

hetośca pratyayānāṃ ca sāmagryāṃ nāsti cetphalam
hetavaḥ pratyayāśca syurahetupratyayaiḥ samāḥ || 4 ||

If the fruit does not exist in the collection of cause and conditions, then causes and conditions would be equal to non-causes and non-conditions.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetośca pratyayānāṃ ca sāmagryāṃ nāsti cetphalam | hetavaḥ pratyayāśca syurahetupratyayaiḥ samāḥ || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If you think: “The fruit does not indeed exist in the collection,” even so:

“If the fruit does not exist in the collection of cause and conditions, then causes and conditions would be equal to non-causes and non-conditions.” || 4 ||

For example, since a sprout does not exist in flames, coals, and the like, they are not its causes and conditions. In the same way, since a sprout does not exist in the seeds and the like that are intended, they would not be causes and conditions. And an occurrence of a fruit from non-causes and non-conditions is not fitting. Therefore the occurrence of a fruit by its own-nature does not exist.

An opponent says: “The collection itself certainly does not have the capacity to produce the fruit; therefore this question might arise: ‘Does the fruit exist in the collection, or does it not exist?’ Rather, the cause has the capacity to produce the fruit. The collection merely assists the cause. The cause gives a causal factor for the arising of the fruit and ceases; and the fruit, assisted by that cause, arises.” It is replied: A cause does not assist a fruit that has not arisen. Nor is it possible for anyone to do anything at all to what has not arisen, just as to the son of a barren woman. This conception is not reasonable. || 4 ||
