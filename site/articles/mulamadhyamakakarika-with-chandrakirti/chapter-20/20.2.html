MMK 20.2

If a fruit is born from the combination of cause and conditions and does not exist in the combination, how can it be born from the combination itself?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetośca pratyayānāṃ ca sāmagryā jāyate yadi | phalaṃ nāsti ca sāmagryāṃ sāmagryā jāyate katham || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If the fruit is entirely absent from the combination, Candrakīrti asks how that combination can produce it. Something wholly nonexistent in its causes would be like oil supposedly extracted from sand.

The analysis rejects both a fruit already established in the conditions and a fruit with its own nature absent from them. It does not deny that results are spoken of conventionally; it denies an independently existing result that conditions can manufacture.
