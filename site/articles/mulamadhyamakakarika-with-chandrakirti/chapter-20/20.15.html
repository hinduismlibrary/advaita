MMK 20.15

satyāṃ vā saṃgatau hetuḥ kathaṃ janayate phalam || 15 ||

When there is no connection, how does a cause produce a fruit?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

satyāṃ vā saṃgatau hetuḥ kathaṃ janayate phalam || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And when there is no connection between cause and fruit in any way, then:

“When there is no connection, how does a cause produce a fruit?” || 15 ||

The intention is: a cause does not produce a fruit at all, because connection does not exist, just as a barren woman’s son does not.

An opponent might say: “A cause produces a fruit only when connection exists.” That too is not reasonable, because connection is not apprehended in any of the three times. And even if, somehow, a connection between cause and fruit is imagined, even so:

“When connection exists, how does a cause produce a fruit?” || 15 ||

The intention is: because the fruit is already near, another birth of it would be purposeless; and because connection between things that are not joined is not reasonable. || 15 ||
