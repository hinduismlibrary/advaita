MMK 20.24

asti pratyayasāmagrī kuta eva phalaṃ vinā || 24 ||

If the collection of conditions exists, whence indeed is a fruit without the collection?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

asti pratyayasāmagrī kuta eva phalaṃ vinā || 24 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Therefore “a fruit is not made by a collection.” If someone might say: “If a fruit made by a collection is not possible, then it will be made without a collection,” it is replied: “A fruit is not made without a collection.” When a fruit made by a collection is not possible, how could the utterly contrary, a fruit made without a collection, exist? A fruit made without a collection is not possible.

An opponent might say: “Although a fruit does not exist by its own-nature, nevertheless a collection of causes and conditions exists. And a collection of causes and conditions is not possible without a fruit; therefore a fruit too will be possible.” It is replied: A collection of causes and conditions would exist if the fruit itself existed. But when, by the reasoning stated, the fruit itself does not exist, then:

“If the collection of conditions exists, whence indeed is a fruit without the collection?” || 24 ||

The intention is: when there is no fruit, a collection of causes and conditions that would be without a cause does not exist either. For it is said in the Ārya-Lalitavistara Sūtra:

“Depending on the throat, lips, palate, and tongue that moves, syllables sound; yet no single letter is found in the throat, nor in the palate. And that speech, arising in dependence on a collection, issues forth under the control of mind and intellect. Mind and speech, whose form is unseen, are not apprehended outside or inside. The wise person, seeing the arising and passing away of speech, sound, noise, and tone, then sees all speech as momentary, controlled, and like an echo.”

And it was said by the Blessed One in the Ārya-Upāli-Paripṛcchā:

“Having gone forth in this most excellent teaching, having abandoned the marks of a householder, you will become fruitful, excellent ones, when instructed in these by the compassionate one. Having gone forth and abandoned the marks of a householder, there will be attainment of every fruit. Again, having weighed the nature of Dharma, there will be attainment of every fruit and fruition. They obtained the fruit and then the attainment; wonder arises again for them. Ah, the human lion is exceedingly compassionate; the Victor has well taught the reasoning.”

And further, in the Āryāṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā:

“Therefore, Kauśika, a bodhisattva mahāsattva, armed with the great armor, should not abide in form; should not abide in feeling, perception, formations, or consciousness. He should not abide in the fruit of stream-entry, in the fruit of once-returning, in the fruit of non-returning, in arhatship, in pratyekabuddhahood, or in perfect Buddhahood.”

Thus ends the twentieth chapter, called “Examination of the Collection,” in the Madhyamaka Commentary, the Clear Words, composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti.
