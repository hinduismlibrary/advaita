MMK 20.11

na hyadṛṣṭvā vā dṛṣṭvā vā heturjanayate phalam || 11 ||

And if it is not connected with the fruit, what fruit would that cause produce? A cause does not produce a fruit either unseen or seen.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na hyadṛṣṭvā vā dṛṣṭvā vā heturjanayate phalam || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, if you think: “Because it is not possible to produce an existing fruit again, the cause, not covered and not connected with the fruit, will produce the fruit,” this too is not reasonable. He says:

“And if it is not connected with the fruit, what fruit would that cause produce?” The intention is: if it is accepted that a cause is not connected with the fruit, then what fruit would it produce now? Since it is unconnected, it would either produce every fruit or produce nothing at all.

And further, if a cause produces a fruit, does it produce the fruit having seen it or without seeing it? In either way it is not reasonable, he says:

“A cause does not produce a fruit either unseen or seen.” || 11 ||

In this matter, if it is imagined that a cause produces after seeing, this is not reasonable, because only what exists can be seen, not what does not exist. If it exists, it is not produced, because it already exists. Thus the cause does not produce the fruit after seeing it; nor does it produce it without seeing it, for the consequence would be that the cause produces every fruit.

If asked, “What is this seeing, and what is non-seeing?” it is replied: “This is established in the world: apprehension is seeing.”

Someone might say: “This does not apply to seeds and the like, which have no senses.” Let it apply, or let it not apply; this is not our question, but the question of the one who asserts production. A proponent of production who might say “It produces after seeing” should be told: “That seed and the like are seen is not observed in the world.” Therefore this conception is not reasonable. If he imagines that it produces without seeing, then whatever unseen thing there may be would produce everything; but it does not produce everything. Therefore it does not produce without seeing either. For we examine another’s conception by the consequence that is not desired, like a chain of pain crowded with the suffering of the wilderness of saṃsāra, its remote tracts, mountain caverns, and precipices. And the Jainas, who accept a world whose cause is a person as a conscious maker, do not escape the consequence, since they accept a seed and the like as having a single sense faculty. Therefore there is no fault. || 11 ||
