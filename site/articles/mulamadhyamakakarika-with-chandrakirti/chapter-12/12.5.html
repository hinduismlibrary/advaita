MMK 12.5

parapudgalajaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi yasmai pradīyate
pareṇa kṛtvā tad duḥkhaṃ sa duḥkhena vinā kutaḥ || 5 ||

If suffering were born from another person, for whom it is given, having been made by another, where is that person without suffering?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

parapudgalajaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi yasmai pradīyate | pareṇa kṛtvā tad duḥkhaṃ sa duḥkhena vinā kutaḥ || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Who says that suffering is made by one’s own person? Rather, suffering is born from another person. The human person is indeed other than the suffering of a god. And the human person, having made the suffering of a god, gives it to that god-person. And that god-person is designated by that suffering of a god. Therefore that suffering of that person is indeed born from another person. The response:

If suffering were born from another person, and that suffering, having been made by another, is given to the one to whom it is given, where is that person without suffering? If the suffering of a god is made by a human person, and that suffering, made by that human person, is given to another, a god-person, then where is that god-person, free from the suffering of the god, to whom it would be given? Thus, for suffering not born from one’s own person, there is no recipient at all.
