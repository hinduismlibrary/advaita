MMK 12.8

na tāvat svakṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ na hi tenaiva tat kṛtam
paro nātmakṛtaś cet syād duḥkhaṃ parakṛtaṃ katham || 8 ||

Suffering is not made by oneself, for it is not made by that very one. If the other is not made by itself, how could suffering be made by another?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na tāvat svakṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ na hi tenaiva tat kṛtam | paro nātmakṛtaś cet syād duḥkhaṃ parakṛtaṃ katham || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, also by a different method, showing that the two positions do not occur, he says:

For this reason too, the making of suffering by oneself or by another is not reasonable, because suffering is not made by oneself at all. What is the reason? Because it is not made by that very one, owing to the contradiction of its occurring in oneself. Therefore it is not made by oneself. Nor is it made by another either, because that which is imagined as the other who makes is not itself made by the self, not brought into being by the self, since it too depends on another cause. And that which is not brought into being by itself, being of a nonexistent nature, how will it make another? This is not reasonable.
