MMK 12.9

syād ubhābhyāṃ kṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ syād ekaikakṛtaṃ yadi
parākārāsvayaṃkāraṃ duḥkham ahetukaṃ kutaḥ || 9 ||

If suffering were made by both, it would be made by each separately. How could suffering be without a cause, neither made by another nor made by itself?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

syād ubhābhyāṃ kṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ syād ekaikakṛtaṃ yadi | parākārāsvayaṃkāraṃ duḥkham ahetukaṃ kutaḥ || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now he says that suffering made by both also does not exist:

Suffering would be made by both if it were made by each one separately. For if suffering were made by each one separately, then it would be suffering made by both. But it is not made by each one separately, because of the faults stated. And it is not seen that when the taking of life is done by two, it is designated as done by both.

Now, showing how suffering without a cause also does not exist, he says:

What occasion is there for suffering to be without a cause, when it is neither done by another nor done by itself? “Not done by another” is what has non-doing by another; “not done by itself” is what has no doing by itself. If suffering is not made by itself, and is not made by another either, according to the reasoning stated, then how indeed will it be without a cause, like the fragrance of a sky-flower? Because suffering does not exist, from where is the self, its support?
