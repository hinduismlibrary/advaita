MMK 12.1

svayaṃ kṛtaṃ parakṛtaṃ dvābhyāṃ kṛtam ahetukam
duḥkham ity eka icchanti tac ca kāryaṃ na yujyate || 1 ||

Some say that suffering is made by itself, made by another, made by both, or without a cause; but that effect is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svayaṃ kṛtaṃ parakṛtaṃ dvābhyāṃ kṛtam ahetukam | duḥkham ity eka icchanti tac ca kāryaṃ na yujyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: The self indeed exists because suffering related to it exists. For here the five appropriated aggregates are called suffering; and that exists. And that suffering must belong to someone, since it is not without a support. Therefore the support of suffering indeed exists, and that is the self. The response: The self would exist if suffering itself existed. For that would be made by oneself, or made by another, or made by both, or without a cause. Showing that in every way the effect that is desired does not at all exist, he says:

There, some disputants have accepted that suffering is made by itself; others, in turn, [accept] that it is made by another; others [accept] that it is made by both; some have accepted that suffering is arisen without a cause. And in every way, when that suffering is accepted, the effect that is to be made is not reasonable. This is merely an assertion: this is the meaning.
