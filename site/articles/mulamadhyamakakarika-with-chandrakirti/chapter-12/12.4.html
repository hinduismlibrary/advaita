MMK 12.4

svapudgalakṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi duḥkhaṃ punar vinā
svapudgalaḥ sa katamo yena duḥkhaṃ svayaṃ kṛtam || 4 ||

If suffering were made by one’s own person, then without suffering, what is that own person by whom suffering was made by itself?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svapudgalakṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi duḥkhaṃ punar vinā | svapudgalaḥ sa katamo yena duḥkhaṃ svayaṃ kṛtam || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Then it may be said: We do not say, “Because suffering itself made suffering, therefore it is made by itself.” What then? We say “suffering made by itself” because it is made by one’s own person, since it is made by oneself, not made by another and given. The response:

That human suffering, characterized by the five appropriated aggregates, is imagined to be made by a person himself. Let that person by whom the suffering was made by himself be posited. If the very suffering by which a person is designated as one’s own person is what he made, then let it be stated separately: “This is that suffering; this is its maker.” Or, if the person, through the appropriation of human suffering, made that very suffering, then it is not made by his own person; it would be made by another person’s person. Or, if the non-difference of the person is accepted even though the appropriation is different, this is not so, because it is impossible to show a different person apart from the appropriation. Thus, for the present, suffering made by one’s own person does not occur.
