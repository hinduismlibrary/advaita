MMK 12.4

sva-pudgala-kṛtam = made by oneself; duḥkham = suffering; yadi = if; duḥkham = suffering; punaḥ = again; vinā = without; sva-pudgalaḥ = person oneself; saḥ = that; katamaḥ = which; yena = by whom; duḥkham = suffering; svayam = oneself; kṛtam = made

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svapudgalakṛtaṃ duḥkhaṃ yadi duḥkhaṃ punar vinā | svapudgalaḥ sa katamo yena duḥkhaṃ svayaṃ kṛtam || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent clarifies that “self-made” means made by one’s own person, rather than suffering making itself or someone else making it. Candrakīrti asks us to identify this person apart from suffering: if the person is designated through the very suffering in question, the proposed maker and what is made must be shown as distinct.

If a person based on a different set of aggregates made this suffering, it would be made by another person, not by one’s own self. If the person is said to remain the same despite a different basis, that claim still fails: no separate person can be demonstrated apart from the aggregates on which the designation depends. Suffering, therefore, is not established as self-made.
