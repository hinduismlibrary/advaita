MMK 9.1

Investigation of the Presence of Something Prior

MMK 9.1

darśana-śravaṇa-ādīni = seeing, hearing, and the rest; vedanā-ādīni = feeling and the rest; ca = and; api = also; atha = then; bhavanti = exist; yasya = for whom; prāk = before; ebhyaḥ = these; saḥ = he; asti = exists; iti = thus; eke = some; vadanti = say; uta = indeed

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha | bhavanti yasya prāgebhyaḥ so 'stītyeke vadantyuta || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Some Buddhists claim that an appropriator—a person who sees, hears, feels and so on—exists before the senses and experiences that are appropriated. Candrakīrti presents their reason: how could seeing and the other experiences belong to something nonexistent? The person must already be established, they say, before the senses arise.
