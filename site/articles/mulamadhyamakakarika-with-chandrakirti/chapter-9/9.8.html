MMK 9.8

draṣṭā sa eva sa śrotā sa eva yadi vedakaḥ
ekaikasmād bhavet pūrvam evaṃ caitan na yujyate || 8 ||

If the seer is the same as the hearer, and the knower the same, he would be prior to each one. But this is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

draṣṭā sa eva sa śrotā sa eva yadi vedakaḥ | ekaikasmād bhavet pūrvam evaṃ caitan na yujyate || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And it is not reasonable for this reason either, because:

“If the seer is the same as the hearer, and the knower the same, he would be prior to each one. But this is not reasonable.”

It is not reasonable to say that the seer is the same as the hearer. If it were so, then the hearer would be a seer even without the activity of seeing, and the seer would be a hearer even without the activity of hearing. But it is not seen that one is a seer without the activity of seeing or a hearer without the activity of hearing. Therefore he says: “But this is not reasonable.”

Ācārya Buddhapālita explains: “If the self is one, the consequence would be that a person moves to another sense faculty, as if going to another window.” Ācārya Bhāviveka says that this is a refutation: “Since the self is all-pervading, the consequence that it moves to another sense faculty is unreasonable.” This is not right, because the subject here is the refutation of the personal-entity theory conceived by fellow Buddhists, and its all-pervading nature is not accepted. Therefore the consequence is indeed appropriate.
