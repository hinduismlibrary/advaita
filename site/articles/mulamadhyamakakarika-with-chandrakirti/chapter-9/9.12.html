MMK 9.12

prāk ca yo darśanādibhyaḥ sāṃprataṃ cordhvameva ca
na vidyate 'sti nāstīti nivṛttāstatra kalpanāḥ || 12 ||

The one who is said to exist before seeing and the rest, now, or afterward is not found. Conceptions of existence and nonexistence therefore cease.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

prāk ca yo darśanādibhyaḥ sāṃprataṃ cordhvameva ca | na vidyate 'sti nāstīti nivṛttāstatra kalpanāḥ || 12 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

A person established before seeing and the other experiences is not found; neither is one established together with them as an independent entity, nor one established afterward. Since such a person cannot be found in the past, present or future relative to the senses, a wise person does not impose either “it exists” or “it does not exist.” The appropriator and what is appropriated are established only in dependence on one another, as actor and action are.

The commentary quotes the Samādhirāja: all phenomena are like a dream; no person is found being born or dying. They are like foam, a plantain trunk, an illusion, lightning, the moon reflected in water and a mirage. No person passes from this world to another, yet deeds do not vanish: wholesome and unwholesome actions ripen as results. There is neither permanence nor annihilation, no fixed stock of karma or independent abiding; neither the same self nor another self takes up an intrinsically existing deed. There is no absolute existence or nonexistence. The unarisen, peaceful and signless is the Buddhas’ domain.
