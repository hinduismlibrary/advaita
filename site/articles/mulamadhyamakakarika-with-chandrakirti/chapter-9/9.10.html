MMK 9.10

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha
bhavanti yebhyasteṣveṣa bhūteṣvapi na vidyate || 10 ||

The senses and feelings arise from those elements; but this one is not found even in those elements.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha | bhavanti yebhyasteṣveṣa bhūteṣvapi na vidyate || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “The self certainly exists before all seeing and the rest. Or, if one asks, ‘If it exists, by what is it designated?,’ before seeing and the rest, during the state of name and form, there are the four great elements, from which, in sequence, the six sense-bases arise, with name and form as condition; hence seeing, hearing, and the rest arise. Therefore, before seeing and the rest, there is the appropriation of the four great elements.” Even so:

“The senses and feelings arise from those elements; but this one is not found even in those elements.”

The meaning is that this one, even if dependent on the four great elements from which seeing and the rest arise, is not reasonable for the very reason stated earlier. In this regard, just as it was said earlier, “Without something, how can someone exist? Without someone, how can something exist?,” so too it is to be said here. A self that is first established through appropriation of the great elements would exist depending on the great elements. But that is not so, because it would be without a cause. And how would one who does not exist appropriate the great elements? The refutation of appropriation of the elements has already been stated, just like the appropriation of seeing, so it is not repeated.
