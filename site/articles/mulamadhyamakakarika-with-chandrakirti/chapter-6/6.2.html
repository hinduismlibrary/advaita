MMK 6.2

rakte 'sati puna rāgaḥ kuta eva bhaviṣyati
sati vāsati vā rāge rakte 'pyeṣa samaḥ kramaḥ || 2 ||

If the passionate person is absent, from where again would passion arise? Whether passion exists or does not exist, the same reasoning applies to the passionate person.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rakte 'sati puna rāgaḥ kuta eva bhaviṣyati | sati vāsati vā rāge rakte 'pyeṣa samaḥ kramaḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If, in this way, passion does not exist when the passionate person exists, then let passion exist when the passionate person does not exist. He says that this too is unreasonable:

“If the passionate person is absent, from where again would passion arise?”

When passion does not exist while the passionate person exists, how would passion without a support be established when the passionate person does not exist? For when the fruit is absent, its ripening is not possible.

Someone says: “Although passion has been denied by you, nevertheless the passionate person exists, because he has not been denied. And the passionate person is not reasonable without passion; therefore this too exists.” Reply: Passion would exist if the passionate person existed. For when this passionate person is accepted, is he posited when passion exists or when it does not exist? Neither way is possible, he says:

“Whether passion exists or does not exist, the same reasoning applies to the passionate person.”

If the passionate person is posited when passion exists, the same sequence just stated, showing that passion is not possible in the passionate person, applies to the passionate person too: “If passion existed first, a passionate person excluded from passion,” and so on. If the passionate person is accepted when passion does not exist, this too is unreasonable, because:

“If passion is absent, from where again would the passionate person arise?”

Therefore, the passionate person too does not exist. And because passion and the passionate person do not exist, the aggregates and the rest do not exist either.
