MMK 6.2

Investigation of Desire and the Desirous One

← MMK 6.1Chapter 6MMK 6.3 →

MMK 6.2

rakte 'sati puna rāgaḥ kuta eva bhaviṣyati
sati vāsati vā rāge rakte 'pyeṣa samaḥ kramaḥ || 2 ||

If the passionate person did not exist, where could passion be? Whether passion exists or does not exist, the same analysis applies to the passionate person.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rakte 'sati puna rāgaḥ kuta eva bhaviṣyati | sati vāsati vā rāge rakte 'pyeṣa samaḥ kramaḥ || 2 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

If there is no desirous person, where could desire be? Desire cannot exist without a basis. The verse then applies the same reasoning to the desirous person: whether desire is present or absent, the person cannot be established independently. If desire is present, the relation between desire and the desirous person still has to be explained; if it is absent, there is no basis for calling the person desirous.

An opponent says that although desire has been refuted, the desirous person remains, since that person has not been separately refuted; and a desirous person cannot exist without desire. Candrakīrti answers that the same analysis applies whichever is taken first. If the person is said to exist when desire exists, the question of how desire and person are related remains. If desire is absent, how can a desirous person be established? Neither is found independently of the other.
