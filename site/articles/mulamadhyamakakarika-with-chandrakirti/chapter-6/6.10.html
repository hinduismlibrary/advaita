MMK 6.10

Investigation of Desire and the Desirous One

← MMK 6.9Chapter 6MMK 7.1 →

MMK 6.10

evaṃ raktena rāgasya siddhirna saha nāsaha
rāgavatsarvadharmāṇāṃ siddhirna saha nāsaha || 10 ||

Thus passion and the passionate person are established neither together nor separately. As with passion, so it is with all phenomena.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ raktena rāgasya siddhirna saha nāsaha | rāgavatsarvadharmāṇāṃ siddhirna saha nāsaha || 10 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Candrakīrti concludes that desire and the desirous person are established neither as co-existent nor as non-co-existent. The same analysis applies to all phenomena, just as it did to desire and the person affected by desire; hatred, the person who hates, delusion, and the deluded person are not established through either relation.

Candrakīrti cites a teaching: one who is said to desire, hate, or be deluded does not find the thing, place, or means by which that affliction is supposed to occur. Not finding it, that person is called free from desire, hatred, and delusion, with an undistorted mind, concentrated, crossed over, secure, fearless, free of the taints, and liberated in mind and wisdom. The closing verse praises those who know that desire, hatred, pride, and delusion arise from conceptual construction and function falsely; they do not reify these states—or even reify the idea of dispassion—and are freed from habitual becoming. Candrakīrti then closes Chapter 6, “Examination of Desire and the Desirous Person.”
