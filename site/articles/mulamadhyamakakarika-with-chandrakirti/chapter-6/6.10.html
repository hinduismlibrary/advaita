MMK 6.10

evaṃ raktena rāgasya siddhirna saha nāsaha
rāgavatsarvadharmāṇāṃ siddhirna saha nāsaha || 10 ||

Thus, the establishment of passion by the passionate person is neither with coexistence nor without coexistence. As with passion, the establishment of all dharmas is neither with coexistence nor without coexistence.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ raktena rāgasya siddhirna saha nāsaha | rāgavatsarvadharmāṇāṃ siddhirna saha nāsaha || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, concluding that passion and the passionate person are not established through the analysis just stated, he says:

“Thus the establishment of passion by the passionate person is neither with coexistence nor without coexistence.”

And just as passion and the passionate person are not established either in sequence or by coexistence, so too are all entities. Indicating this extension, he says:

“As with passion, the establishment of all dharmas is neither with coexistence nor without coexistence.”

As with passion and the passionate person, the non-establishment of hatred and the hater, delusion and the deluded, and the rest is to be applied.

For this very reason, the Blessed One said:

“Whoever is impassioned, or is impassioned in regard to something, or is impassioned through something; whoever is angered, or is angered in regard to something, or is angered through something; whoever is deluded, or is deluded in regard to something, or is deluded through something, does not directly see that dharma; he does not apprehend that dharma. Not directly seeing and not apprehending that dharma, he is said to be without passion, without hatred, undeluded, with mind not inverted, and concentrated. He is said to have crossed over, to have gone to the far shore; to have reached safety; to have reached fearlessness; he is said to have exhausted the taints; to be without afflictions, masterful, with mind well liberated, with wisdom well liberated, noble, greatly fortunate, with the task accomplished, having done what was to be done, having laid down the burden, having attained his own good, having exhausted the bonds of existence, with mind well liberated by right knowledge, having reached the highest perfection of mastery over all mental states—he is called an ascetic.” This is the detailed passage.

And likewise:

“Those who, having known passion, fault, pride, and delusion as having a nature produced by conceptualization and proceeding falsely, do not conceptualize and also do not become dispassionate here, for those quickly illumined with all the states of becoming.”

Thus, in the Prasannapadā, the Madhyamaka commentary composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti, the sixth chapter, called “Examination of Passion and the Passionate Person,” is complete.
