MMK 6.4

Investigation of Desire and the Desirous One

MMK 6.4

na = not; ekatve = in identity; sahabhāvaḥ = coexistence; asti = exists; na = not; tena = with that; eva = indeed; hi = for; tat = that; saha = together; pṛthaktve = in difference; sahabhāvaḥ = coexistence; atha = then; kutaḥ = from where; eva = indeed; bhaviṣyati = would be

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

naikatve sahabhāvo 'sti na tenaiva hi tatsaha | pṛthaktve sahabhāvo 'tha kuta eva bhaviṣyati || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Could desire and the desirous person be one? A thing is not “together with itself”; if they are identical, there is no relation of co-existence between two things. Could they instead be different? If wholly distinct, how could they be together? Candrakīrti notes that co-existence is not found between things such as light and darkness or saṃsāra and nirvāṇa.

The argument therefore rejects both identity and difference as the basis for co-existence. If they are one, there are not two things to be together; if they are separate, no relation of being together is established.
