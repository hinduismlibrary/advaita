MMK 6.1

Investigation of Desire and the Desirous One

← MMK 5.8Chapter 6MMK 6.2 →

MMK 6.1

rāga-ādi = desire and the rest; yadi = if; bhavet = were; pūrvam = before; raktaḥ = the desirous one; rāga-tiraskṛtaḥ = apart from desire; tam = that; pratītya = depending on; bhavet = would be; rāgaḥ = desire; rakte = when the desirous one; rāgaḥ = desire; bhavet = would be; sati = present

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rāgādyadi bhavetpūrvaṃ rakto rāgatiraskṛtaḥ | taṃ pratītya bhavedrāgo rakte rāgo bhavetsati || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that the aggregates, sense bases, and elements exist because the afflictions that depend on them are observed. The Buddha teaches that an ordinary, unlearned person sees agreeable forms with the eye, becomes attached to them, and then produces actions rooted in desire, hatred, and delusion. Candrakīrti replies that those bases would be established only if the afflictions themselves were established. He therefore examines desire and the person who is desirous.

Could desire exist before the desirous person, or only in dependence on that person? If a person called “desirous” existed before desire, that person would then be without desire. But a person without desire cannot be established as desirous; otherwise even an arhat, who is without desire, would have to be called desirous. Desire therefore cannot be established as arising in dependence on a previously existing desirous person.
