MMK 27.10

yadi hy ayaṃ bhaved anyaḥ pratyākhyāyāpi taṃ bhavet
tathaiva ca sa saṃtiṣṭhet tatra jāyeta cāmṛtaḥ || 10 ||

If this one were different, then, even after rejecting that one, this one would exist; and that one would remain there just so, or would be born or die there.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi hy ayaṃ bhaved anyaḥ pratyākhyāyāpi taṃ bhavet | tathaiva ca sa saṃtiṣṭhet tatra jāyeta cāmṛtaḥ || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If this self were different from the former self, what fault would there be? It is said:

“If this one were different, then, even after rejecting that one, this one would exist.”

If this present self were different from the former self, then, having rejected and left behind that former self, this one would exist independent of it, without being caused by it.

Further, if this self were different from the former self, then that one would remain just so there. Because of the difference, just as when a pot is produced a cloth is not destroyed, even while this self is arising there would be no cessation of the former self. Because it had not ceased, it would remain there just as before—where it was previously born in the births of gods, humans, and the like, and appearing in the form, arrangement, and so forth in which it was previously apprehended. Therefore the view “I did not exist in the past” is not reasonable.
