MMK 27.1

abhūm atītam adhvānaṃ nābhūvam iti dṛṣṭayaḥ
yās tāḥ śāśvatalokādyāḥ pūrvāntaṃ samupāśritāḥ || 1 ||

The views “I existed in the past” and “I did not exist,” and those beginning with “the world is eternal,” rely on the former limit.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

abhūm atītam adhvānaṃ nābhūvam iti dṛṣṭayaḥ | yās tāḥ śāśvatalokādyāḥ pūrvāntaṃ samupāśritāḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

It is read in the sūtra: “Whoever sees dependent arising correctly, as it is, does not turn back toward the former limit, nor does he turn back toward the later limit,” and so forth. What is the former limit? What is the later limit? How does one not turn back toward them? For the explanation of this, this [chapter] is begun.

There, with reference to the present existence of self, past existences of self are called the “former limit.” For the former portion of the sequence of births is the former limit. One does not turn back toward it; one does not take it as an object through views. Because one sees the truth of dependent arising as it is established, one does not adhere to an entity established otherwise as though it were otherwise.

There are eight views that proceed otherwise, taking the former limit as their object. They are:

“The views ‘I existed in the past’ and ‘I did not exist,’ and those beginning with ‘the world is eternal,’ rely on the former limit.”

There, the word “and” has the meaning “and the rest.” Or, the mentioning of two views indicates the rest. These are four views: “What indeed was I in the past? Was I not in the past? Was I both and not both? Was I neither?” These four views rely on the former limit.

He says that there are four others as well:

“Those beginning with ‘the world is eternal,’ previously explained in connection [with this topic], also rely on the former limit.”

Although the four views that follow differ little from the four views based on the former limit, they are taken separately on the basis of just that much difference; we will explain it below. There, the view “the world is eternal” does not differ much from “I existed in the past.” Or the difference is this: the view “the world is eternal” generally relies on the former limit, but “I existed in the past” proceeds by considering the former limit of the self alone, not generally. The difference is to be stated in the same way for the other views too. Thus these eight views rely on the former limit.
