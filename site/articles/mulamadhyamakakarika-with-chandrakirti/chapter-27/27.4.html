MMK 27.4

saḥ = that; eva = same; ātmā = self; iti = thus; tu = but; bhavet = would be; upādānam = appropriation; viśiṣyate = is distinguished; upādāna = appropriation; vinirmuktaḥ = apart from; ātmā = self; te = for you; katamaḥ = which; punaḥ = again

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sa evātmeti tu bhaved upādānaṃ viśiṣyate | upādānavinirmukta ātmā te katamaḥ punaḥ || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If the self of a former life were exactly the self of the present life, the appropriated five aggregates would not differ, since the appropriator would not differ, just as in the former state. But the appropriation does differ, owing to differences in action and agency. Thus the assertion “that very self became me” cannot be maintained.

One might say that the appropriated aggregates change while the self remains one. But then what is this self apart from the appropriation? It cannot be identified separately as “this self” and “these aggregates belonging to it.” If the self is intrinsically defined by the appropriation, it would be causeless and separately apprehensible; neither follows.
