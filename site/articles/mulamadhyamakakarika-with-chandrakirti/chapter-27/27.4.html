MMK 27.4

sa evātmeti tu bhaved upādānaṃ viśiṣyate
upādānavinirmukta ātmā te katamaḥ punaḥ || 4 ||

If that very one were the self, appropriation would differ; then what self, apart from appropriation, is yours?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sa evātmeti tu bhaved upādānaṃ viśiṣyate | upādānavinirmukta ātmā te katamaḥ punaḥ || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

It is further said:

“‘If that very one were the self, appropriation would differ.’”

If the former self itself were this one now, then there would be no difference in appropriation, which is characterized by the five aggregates, because the appropriator would not differ, just as in the former state. But this self’s appropriation is not without difference; rather, the appropriation is indeed differentiated, because the karma and the agent differ. Therefore, because appropriation differs, it is not reasonable that this is that very self.

If you think: “Let the appropriation differ, but the self alone is one. Therefore, because the self does not differ, the statement ‘I existed in the past’ will indeed hold,” it is said:

“Then what self, apart from appropriation, is yours?”

If appropriation were one thing and self another, then even though appropriation differs, the self’s non-difference could be so. But it is not possible to show them separately, saying, “This is the self; this is its appropriation.” For the self’s nature is distinguished by appropriation; otherwise it would follow that the self is uncaused, and it would follow that it is apprehended separately. Since in this way a self apart from appropriation cannot be shown, it is not possible to conceive that the self does not differ even though appropriation differs.
