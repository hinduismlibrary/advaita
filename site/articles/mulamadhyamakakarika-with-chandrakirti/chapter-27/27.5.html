MMK 27.5

upādānavinirmukto nāsty ātmeti kṛte sati
syād upādānam evātmā nāsti cātmeti vaḥ punaḥ || 5 ||

If there is no self apart from appropriation, then the self would be appropriation; yet you also say that no self exists.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

upādānavinirmukto nāsty ātmeti kṛte sati | syād upādānam evātmā nāsti cātmeti vaḥ punaḥ || 5 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Someone may concede that a self free from the aggregates cannot be found, but claim that the appropriation itself is the self. Candrakīrti rejects that as well. If the self is asserted to have no existence apart from appropriation, then the five appropriated aggregates themselves would be the self; the verse points out that this leaves no self of the kind the opponent intends. The next argument shows why the aggregates cannot serve as that self.
