MMK 27.2

dṛṣṭayo na bhaviṣyāmi kim anyo 'nāgate 'dhvani
bhaviṣyāmīti cāntādyā aparāntaṃ samāśritāḥ || 2 ||

The views “Will I not exist?” and “What else will I be in the future?” and those beginning with “finite,” rely on the later limit.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dṛṣṭayo na bhaviṣyāmi kim anyo 'nāgate 'dhvani | bhaviṣyāmīti cāntādyā aparāntaṃ samāśritāḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The former limit has been stated together with the views that take it as their object. Now the later limit is stated.

There, with reference to the present existence of self, future existences of self are called the “later limit.” For the later portion of the sequence of births is the later limit. One does not turn back toward it; one does not take it as an object through views. Because one sees the truth of dependent arising as it is established, one does not adhere to an entity established otherwise as though it were otherwise.

There are eight views that proceed otherwise, taking the later limit as their object. They are:

“The views ‘Will I not exist? What else will I be in the future?’ and those beginning with ‘finite,’ rely on the later limit.”

Here too, the mention of two views indicates the rest. These are four views: “What indeed will I be in the future? Will I not be? Will I both be and not be? Will I neither be nor not be in the future?” These four views rely on the later limit.

Are only these four views based on the later limit? He says no: there are four others, those beginning with “finite,” which rely on the later limit. There, the four views beginning with “finite” proceed by relying on the later limit in general; but the views “What will I be in the future?” and the rest proceed by relying on the later limit of the self alone. This is to be understood as the difference between the four sets of views.
