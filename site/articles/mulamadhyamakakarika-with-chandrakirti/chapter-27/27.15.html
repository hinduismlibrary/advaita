MMK 27.15

sa devaḥ sa manuṣyaś ced evaṃ bhavati śāśvatam
anutpannaś ca devaḥ syāj jāyate na hi śāśvatam || 15 ||

If a god were that very human, permanence would follow; and an unborn god would be born, for what is permanent is not born.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sa devaḥ sa manuṣyaś ced evaṃ bhavati śāśvatam | anutpannaś ca devaḥ syāj jāyate na hi śāśvatam || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, for the purpose of refuting the four views beginning with “eternal,” which rely on the former limit, he says:

“If a god were that very human, permanence would follow; and an unborn god would be born, for what is permanent is not born.”

Here, someone established in the destiny of humans performs wholesome karma and goes to a divine destiny. If there were identity between the two, in this way: “That very human is that very god,” then permanence would follow. But it is not so that the human itself becomes the god. Therefore nothing is permanent.

Further, if the view of permanence held, the god would be unborn. Why? For what is permanent does not arise, because it already exists. Thus the god would be unborn; an unborn god is not reasonable. Thus, in this way, permanence is not reasonable.
