MMK 27.6

na = not; ca = and; upādānam = appropriation; eva = indeed; ātmā = self; vyeti = passes away; tat = that; samudeti = arises; ca = and; katham = how; hi = indeed; nāma = indeed; upādānam = appropriation; upādātā = appropriator; bhaviṣyati = will be

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na copādānam evātmā vyeti tat samudeti ca | kathaṃ hi nāmopādānam upādātā bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The appropriated aggregates themselves cannot be the self. They arise and perish from moment to moment, whereas the self is not ordinarily asserted to arise and perish in that way. Calling the aggregates the self would therefore force either permanence or annihilation, both rejected extremes.

There is a further difficulty: appropriation is an action, and the appropriator is its agent. If the aggregates were themselves the appropriator, agent and action would collapse into one. Then a cutter and what is cut, a pot and its maker, or fire and its fuel would also have to be identical. That is neither observed nor reasonable.
