MMK 27.6

na copādānam evātmā vyeti tat samudeti ca
kathaṃ hi nāmopādānam upādātā bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

And appropriation itself is not the self; it passes away and arises. How indeed could appropriation be the appropriator?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na copādānam evātmā vyeti tat samudeti ca | kathaṃ hi nāmopādānam upādātā bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Showing how appropriation itself cannot be the self, he says:

“And appropriation itself is not the self; it passes away and arises. How indeed could appropriation be the appropriator?”

There, this appropriation called the five aggregates of appropriation arises and perishes in each moment. But the self does not arise and perish in each moment in this way. Nor can the self be said to be identical with or different from the aggregates, and so forth, or to be permanent or impermanent, because many faults would follow. If the self were permanent, the view of permanence would follow; if impermanent, the view of annihilation would follow. Since both, called permanence and annihilation, bring great harm, they are not to be accepted. Therefore, in the first place, it is not reasonable that appropriation itself is the self.

And further:

“How indeed could appropriation be the appropriator?”

Here, appropriation is the action, because it is appropriated. An appropriator, one who acquires it, must necessarily exist for that action. If appropriation itself is accepted as the self, then appropriation itself would also be the appropriator. And when agent and action are identical, the cutter and what is to be cut, the pot and the potter, fire and fuel, and the like would also be identical. To show that this is neither observed nor reasonable, he says: “How indeed could appropriation be the appropriator?” The intended meaning is that this position is utterly impossible.
