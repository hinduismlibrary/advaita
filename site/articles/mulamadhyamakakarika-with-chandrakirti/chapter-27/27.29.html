MMK 27.29

atha vā sarvabhāvānāṃ śūnyatvāc chāśvatādayaḥ
kva kasya katamāḥ kasmāt saṃbhaviṣyanti dṛṣṭayaḥ || 29 ||

Or, because all entities are empty, where, for whom, which, and from what would views such as “eternal” arise?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

atha vā sarvabhāvānāṃ śūnyatvāc chāśvatādayaḥ | kva kasya katamāḥ kasmāt saṃbhaviṣyanti dṛṣṭayaḥ || 29 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus far, even while accepting the appropriator and appropriation as conventionally having the nature of reflections, the impossibility of views beginning with “eternal” has been shown. Now, desiring to show the impossibility of views beginning with “eternal” through the non-apprehension in every way of an own-nature of entities, like the dark and fair complexion of a barren woman’s son, he says:

“Or, because all entities are empty, where, for whom, which, and from what would views arise?”

Because all entities are dependently arisen, their emptiness has been shown throughout the whole treatise. And because all entities are empty, what views beginning with “eternal,” which are outside all entities, could there be for anyone to grasp, such that we would undertake to refute them?

Moreover, what basis, included among all entities, would there be on which these views could arise, and in which we would prevent these views? What entity or person, outside all entities, would there be in whom these views arise, whom we would prevent from holding the views? Or what cause of arising, object, or sign for the views, outside all entities, would there be, arising from which sign we could prevent views beginning with “eternal”?

For all entities are empty, because all things are included among entities; and because they are empty, all those things are not apprehended. Thus:

“Where, for whom, which, and from what would views arise?”

The intended meaning is: no views at all would arise, nowhere, for anyone, in any manner. And when they are impossible, even the conception of them does not arise. Therefore these views are unreasonable.
