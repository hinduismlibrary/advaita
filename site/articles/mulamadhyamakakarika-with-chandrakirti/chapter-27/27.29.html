MMK 27.29

atha vā sarvabhāvānāṃ śūnyatvāc chāśvatādayaḥ
kva kasya katamāḥ kasmāt saṃbhaviṣyanti dṛṣṭayaḥ || 29 ||

When all things are empty, where, for whom, which, and from what could views such as permanence arise?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

atha vā sarvabhāvānāṃ śūnyatvāc chāśvatādayaḥ | kva kasya katamāḥ kasmāt saṃbhaviṣyanti dṛṣṭayaḥ || 29 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

The preceding arguments provisionally treated the appropriator and the aggregates as conventionally appearing like reflections. In the final analysis, however, all things are empty of intrinsic nature. Since every entity is dependently arisen, no independently existing thing can be found. Where, then, could views such as permanence or annihilation arise? There is no view-holder, object, or cause for such views outside the range of all things.

Accordingly, no view exists anywhere, for anyone, in any form. When no such view can arise, there is nothing to imagine or establish; these positions are therefore unreasonable.
