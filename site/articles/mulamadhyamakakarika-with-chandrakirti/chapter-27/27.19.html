MMK 27.19

kutaścit = from somewhere; āgataḥ = come; kaścit = someone; kiṃcit = something; gacchet = would go; punaḥ = again; kvacit = somewhere; yadi = if; tasmāt = therefore; anādiḥ = beginningless; tu = but; saṃsāraḥ = cyclic existence; syāt = would be; na = not; ca = and; asti = exists; saḥ = that

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kutaścid āgataḥ kaścit kiṃcid gacchet punaḥ kva cit | yadi tasmād anādis tu saṃsāraḥ syān na cāsti saḥ || 19 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone might argue that the stream of saṃsāra has continued through beginningless births and deaths, so there must be some permanent self still moving through it. Candrakīrti asks what could actually travel: if something came from somewhere and went somewhere, there would have to be a traveler. But neither an eternal thing nor a momentary one can make such a journey. No self is found arriving from another realm or departing to one. A very long series whose beginning is not seen does not, by itself, prove that saṃsāra is beginningless or that a permanent traveler exists.
