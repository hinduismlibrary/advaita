MMK 27.3

abhūm atītam adhvānam ity etan nopapadyate
yo hi janmasu pūrveṣu sa eva na bhavaty ayam || 3 ||

The view “I existed in the past” is not reasonable; for this one is not that very one who existed in former births.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

abhūm atītam adhvānam ity etan nopapadyate | yo hi janmasu pūrveṣu sa eva na bhavaty ayam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

There, he states how the first four views, which take the former limit as their object, are not possible:

“The view ‘I existed in the past’ is not reasonable; for this one is not that very one who existed in former births.”

There, if the very one who existed in former births were this one now, then it would be reasonable for him to apprehend: “I existed in the past.” But this is not possible, because permanence would follow, and a permanent thing cannot transmigrate; and even while remaining in one destiny, it would follow that it is included in many destinies.

For example, if someone formerly belonging to a destiny such as hell, now, because of the variety of karma, were born among humans, it would not be reasonable for him to conceive: “I was that very hell-being.” How could one who is a human be a hell-being and the like?

Then what is to be understood by this passage read in a sūtra: “At that time, at that moment, I myself was a universal monarch named Māndhātṛ”? It is to be understood that this statement is directed to the denial of another [person], not to establishing identity. For this very reason it is not read: “Another was he at that time, at that moment.”

If, then, this very one and the former one were identical, what fault would there be? The fault has already been stated here: permanence would follow.
