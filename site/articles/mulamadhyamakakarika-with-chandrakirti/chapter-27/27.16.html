MMK 27.16

devād anyo manuṣyaś ced aśāśvatam ato bhavet
devād anyo manuṣyaś cet saṃtatir nopapadyate || 16 ||

If a human were different from a god, non-eternity would follow; if a human is different from a god, continuity is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

devād anyo manuṣyaś ced aśāśvatam ato bhavet | devād anyo manuṣyaś cet saṃtatir nopapadyate || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now he states how non-eternity too is not possible:

“If a human were different from a god, non-eternity would follow; if a human is different from a god, continuity is not reasonable.”

If the god were one and the human another, then, because the former human self had perished there and another alone had arisen here, the former human self would have been destroyed there, and thus non-eternity would follow.

If it is said that it is not non-eternity because the continuum continues, it is said in reply:

“If a human is different from a god, continuity is not reasonable.”

If the human were different from the god, then the god would not belong to the same continuum as the human, just as a mango tree does not belong to the continuum of a neem tree. Then, because the former one had perished, non-eternity alone would follow.

Alternatively, if the human were different from the god, continuity would not be reasonable. But this continuity does exist: the human and the god belong to a single continuum. Therefore, because the consequence would be that there is no continuity, the human is not different from the god. And because this is so, non-eternity too does not exist.
