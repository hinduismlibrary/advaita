MMK 27.25

antavān ekadeśaś ced ekadeśas tv anantavān
syād antavān anantaś ca lokas tac ca na yujyate || 25 ||

If one part were finite and another part infinite, the world would be finite and infinite; and that is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

antavān ekadeśaś ced ekadeśas tv anantavān | syād antavān anantaś ca lokas tac ca na yujyate || 25 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now he states the impossibility of the third position, that the world is both finite and infinite:

“If one part were finite and another part infinite, the world would be finite and infinite; and that is not reasonable.”

If the destruction of some part and the passage of another part to a different destiny existed, then the world would be both finite and infinite. But it is not possible that one part is destroyed and another part is not destroyed. Therefore, the world being both finite and infinite is not reasonable.
