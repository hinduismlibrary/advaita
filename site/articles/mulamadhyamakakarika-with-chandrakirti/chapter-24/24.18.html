MMK 24.18

yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe
sā prajñaptir upādāya pratipat saiva madhyamā || 18 ||

We call whatever is dependent arising emptiness. That is dependent designation; just that is the middle way.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe | sā prajñaptir upādāya pratipat saiva madhyamā || 18 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

But for us, who say that the own-nature of things is empty, all this is possible. Why? Because we say:

The arising of sprouts, consciousness, and the rest through causes and conditions is not arising by own-nature. And the non-arising of things by own-nature is emptiness. As the Blessed One said:

“That which arises through conditions is indeed unborn; it has no arising by own-nature. That which depends on conditions is said to be empty. Whoever knows emptiness is not heedless.”

And also in the noble Laṅkāvatāra: “Great-minded one, with reference to non-arising by own-nature, I have taught that all dharmas are empty.” This is stated at length. In the Two-Hundred-and-Fifty [Sūtra] too: “All dharmas are empty through connection with having no own-nature.”

And this emptiness of own-nature is a dependent designation; that same emptiness is established as a designation in dependence. Depending on wheels and the rest, the parts of a chariot, a chariot is designated. That designation of it in dependence on its own parts is non-arising by own-nature. And non-arising by own-nature is emptiness. That very emptiness, whose characteristic is non-arising by own-nature, is established as the middle way.

For that which does not arise by own-nature, existence is absent; and, because that which does not arise by own-nature does not cease, nonexistence is absent. Therefore emptiness, whose characteristic is the non-arising by own-nature of all things, is free from the two extremes of existence and nonexistence, and so is called the middle way, the middle path. Thus these are special names for dependent arising itself: emptiness, dependent designation, and the middle way.
