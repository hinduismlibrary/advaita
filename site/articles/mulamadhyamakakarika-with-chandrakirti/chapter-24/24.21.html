MMK 24.21

apratītya samutpannaṃ kuto duḥkhaṃ bhaviṣyati
anityam uktaṃ duḥkhaṃ hi tat svābhāvye na vidyate || 21 ||

Without dependent arising, whence could suffering exist? For suffering is said to be impermanent, and that is not found when things have own-nature.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

apratītya samutpannaṃ kuto duḥkhaṃ bhaviṣyati | anityam uktaṃ duḥkhaṃ hi tat svābhāvye na vidyate || 21 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Why? Because:

What has own-nature does not arise in dependence. And what has not arisen in dependence is not impermanent. For an absent sky-flower is not impermanent. And the Blessed One said, “What is impermanent is suffering.” As it is also said in the Śataka:

“What is impermanent is surely affliction; what is affliction is not happiness. Therefore, from the fact that all that is impermanent is suffering, this is understood.”

But when things are accepted as having own-nature, that which is impermanent does not exist.
