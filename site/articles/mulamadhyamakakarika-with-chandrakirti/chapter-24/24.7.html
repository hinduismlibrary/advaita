MMK 24.7

atra brūmaḥ śūnyatāyāṃ na tvaṃ vetsi prayojanam
śūnyatāṃ śūnyatārthaṃ ca tata evaṃ vihanyase || 7 ||

Here we say: you do not know the purpose of emptiness, emptiness, or the meaning of emptiness; therefore you are confused in this way.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

atra brūmaḥ śūnyatāyāṃ na tvaṃ vetsi prayojanam | śūnyatāṃ śūnyatārthaṃ ca tata evaṃ vihanyase || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here we reply:

You, having superimposed through your own conception the mistaken idea that the meaning of emptiness is nonexistence, and saying, “If all this is empty, there is no arising and no passing away,” and the rest, bring great distress upon us through your reproach; you are exceedingly confused, struck down by various false conceptions. The meaning of emptiness that you have grasped is not what we have explained in this treatise. Not knowing the meaning of emptiness, you do not know emptiness; nor do you know what the purpose of emptiness is. Thus, because you do not understand the nature of things as they stand, you have described many unreasonable things, disconnected from our explanation.

What, then, is the purpose of emptiness? That very thing was stated in the Analysis of the Self:

“Liberation comes from the destruction of action and afflictions; action and afflictions are from conceptual thought. They are from elaboration, and elaboration ceases in emptiness.”

Therefore emptiness is taught for the sake of pacifying all elaboration without remainder. Thus the purpose of emptiness is the pacification of all elaboration. But you, imagining nonexistence to be the meaning of emptiness, increase the very net of elaboration and do not know the purpose of emptiness.

And what is emptiness? That too was stated there:

“Not dependent on another, peaceful, not elaborated by elaborations, free from conceptual construction, and not many in meaning: this is the characteristic of reality.”

Therefore, since emptiness has the nature of the cessation of elaboration, how could its meaning be nonexistence? You do not even know emptiness. And here we shall explain the meaning on which the word “emptiness” is based:

“Whatever is dependent arising, that we call emptiness. That is a dependent designation; just that is the middle way.”

And, as the Blessed One’s verse says: “That which arises through conditions is indeed unborn; it has no arising by own-nature. That which depends on conditions is said to be empty. Whoever knows emptiness is not heedless.”

Thus, whatever is the meaning of the expression “dependent arising,” that is the meaning of the expression “emptiness.” But the meaning of the word “nonexistence” is not the meaning of the word “emptiness.” You superimpose the meaning of the word “nonexistence” as the meaning of emptiness and reproach us. Therefore you do not know the meaning of the word “emptiness”; and, not knowing it, you are certainly confused when you make such a reproach.
