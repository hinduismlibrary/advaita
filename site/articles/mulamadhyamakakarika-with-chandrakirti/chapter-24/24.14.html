MMK 24.14

sarvaṃ ca yujyate tasya śūnyatā yasya yujyate
sarvaṃ na yujyate tasya śūnyaṃ yasya na yujyate || 14 ||

Everything is reasonable for the one for whom emptiness is reasonable. Nothing is reasonable for the one for whom emptiness is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sarvaṃ ca yujyate tasya śūnyatā yasya yujyate | sarvaṃ na yujyate tasya śūnyaṃ yasya na yujyate || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Not only does the sequence of faults stated above not apply to our position; rather, he says, the whole arrangement of truths and the rest is much more reasonable:

For the one for whom this emptiness of the own-nature of all things is reasonable, all this as described is reasonable. How so? We explain dependent arising as emptiness, as the verse says:

“That which arises through conditions is indeed unborn; it has no arising by own-nature. That which depends on conditions is said to be empty. Whoever knows emptiness is not heedless.”

This is also stated in the Perfection of Wisdom: “All dharmas are empty through connection with having no own-nature.”

Therefore, for the one who accepts this emptiness, dependent arising is reasonable. For the one who accepts dependent arising, the four noble truths are reasonable. How so? Suffering is arisen in dependence; it is not arisen without dependence. And because it has no own-nature, it is empty. When suffering exists, the origin of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering are reasonable. Therefore full understanding of suffering, abandonment of origin, direct realization of cessation, and cultivation of the path are reasonable. When full understanding of the truths of suffering and the rest, and the rest, exist, the fruits are reasonable. When the fruits exist, those established in the fruits are reasonable. When those established in the fruits exist, those proceeding toward the fruits are reasonable. When those proceeding toward the fruits and those established in the fruits exist, the Sangha is reasonable. When the noble truths exist, the true Dharma too is reasonable. When the true Dharma and Sangha exist, the Buddha too is reasonable. Thus all three Jewels are reasonable. All worldly and supramundane things, all attainments of special qualities, Dharma and non-Dharma and their results, good and bad destinies, and all worldly conventions are reasonable.

Thus: “Everything is reasonable for the one for whom emptiness is reasonable.” The meaning is: for the one for whom the emptiness of the own-nature of all things is reasonable, all this as stated is reasonable and is accomplished. But for the one for whom emptiness as stated is not reasonable, because dependent arising does not exist for that person, nothing is reasonable. How it is not reasonable will be explained at length.
