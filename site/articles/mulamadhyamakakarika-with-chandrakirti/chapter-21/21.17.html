MMK 21.17

sadbhāvasya svabhāvena nāsadbhāvaśca yujyate
nirvāṇakāle cocchedaḥ praśamādbhavasaṃtateḥ || 17 ||

If existence is by own-nature, non-existence is not fitting; and at nirvāṇa, annihilation occurs because the continuity of existence is pacified.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sadbhāvasya svabhāvena nāsadbhāvaśca yujyate | nirvāṇakāle cocchedaḥ praśamādbhavasaṃtateḥ || 17 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, having raised the consequence of the view of annihilation—because the cause is not produced again—for one who accepts an entity, he now raises the consequence of the fault in the doctrine of permanence:

“If existence is by own-nature, non-existence is not fitting; and at nirvāṇa, annihilation occurs because the continuity of existence is pacified.” || 17 ||

If the existence of the cause were by own-nature, its non-existence afterward would not be possible, because own-nature does not depart. Then the consequence of the view of permanence would remain just as it was.

And further: “At the time of nirvāṇa, annihilation occurs because the continuity of existence is pacified.” Although the consequence of the views of permanence and annihilation is avoided by the occurrence of the continuity of arising and passing away of cause and fruit, nevertheless, where this continuity no longer proceeds, at nirvāṇa, the view of annihilation necessarily follows. And the Blessed One said that the view of annihilation is to be abandoned. If it is said: “There will not be an annihilation of this kind,” then why would another kind occur for you? For it has the cessation of an entity as its object, just as the supposed annihilation at the time of nirvāṇa has the cessation of an entity as its object. Such is the intention.

And what was said: “Existence is the continuity of the arising and passing away of cause and fruit”—that too is not reasonable. How so? Here, the final existence has the character of cessation; the first existence has the character of reconnection with a destination. There, the final existence, as it ceases, remains in the role of a cause; but the first existence, characterized by rebirth, is established in the form of a fruit. The designation “saṃsāra” is made for these two existences. || 17 ||
