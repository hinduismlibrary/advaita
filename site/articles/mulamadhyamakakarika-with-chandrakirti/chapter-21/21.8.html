MMK 21.8

Investigation of Rising and Passing

MMK 21.8

saṃbhavo vibhavaścaiva vinā bhāvaṃ na vidyate
saṃbhavaṃ vibhavaṃ caiva vinā bhāvo na vidyate || 8 ||

Arising and passing away are not found without an existent; an existent is not found without arising and passing away.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

saṃbhavo vibhavaścaiva vinā bhāvaṃ na vidyate | saṃbhavaṃ vibhavaṃ caiva vinā bhāvo na vidyate || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent says: “Arising and passing away of entities indeed exist, because there are entities that are their supporting subjects. Arising and passing away have entities for their support, and that entity exists. Because it exists, the two properties supported by it will also exist.” It is replied: These two properties would be supported by an entity if an entity existed. But when an entity does not exist, then:

“Arising and passing away are not found without an existent; an existent is not found without arising and passing away.” || 8 ||

Arising and passing away are the defining characteristics of an entity, and those two have been denied as existing by their own-nature. When they are denied as actually existing, without the defining characteristics of an entity, arising and passing away, whence would an entity different from those defining characteristics come to be? And arising and passing away do not exist without an entity.

Others, however, explain the first half by reversing it: “Arising and passing away do exist, because they are properties of entities. Here, what does not exist does not have properties of entities, like a crest-jewel on a frog’s hair. But arising and passing away are properties of entities; therefore they do exist.” If there were arising and passing away of anything ultimately, it would be reasonable to call that an entity. But they do not exist. Therefore:

“An existent is not found without arising and passing away.” The meaning is: because arising and passing away of an entity would exist, it would exist; but they do not. And when those do not exist, the purpose of the reason is not established. Likewise: “Arising and passing away are not found without an existent.” The intention is that, because the support does not exist, that which depends on it is not established. || 8 ||
