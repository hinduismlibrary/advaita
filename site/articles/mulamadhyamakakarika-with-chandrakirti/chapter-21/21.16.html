MMK 21.16

udayavyayasaṃtānaḥ phalahetvorbhavaḥ sa cet
vyayasyāpunarutpatterhetūcchedaḥ prasajyate || 16 ||

If existence is the continuity of the arising and passing away of cause and fruit, then from the non-arising again of what has passed away, annihilation of the cause follows.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

udayavyayasaṃtānaḥ phalahetvorbhavaḥ sa cet | vyayasyāpunarutpatterhetūcchedaḥ prasajyate || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

It is replied:

“If existence is the continuity of the arising and passing away of cause and fruit, then from the non-arising again of what has passed away, annihilation of the cause follows.” || 16 ||

A causal moment, having accepted the state of being a cause for the arising of a fruit, ceases. Surely, from the fact that this causal moment, which has passed away, does not arise again, the view of annihilation follows. If one asks, “How is this not your fault?”: this is a fault for one who accepts an entity. But I do not accept an entity, because all dharmas are not arisen by their own-nature. Even where I said:

“Whatever arises in dependence on this or that is not that very thing, nor is it other than that; therefore it is neither annihilation nor permanence,”

there too, by this reasoning, the absence of own-nature of entities alone was shown. For otherwise, if the nature of entities existed, how would seed and sprout not be different? Therefore this consequence does not refute us. || 16 ||
