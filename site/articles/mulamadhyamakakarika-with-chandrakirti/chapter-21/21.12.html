MMK 21.12

na bhāvājjāyate bhāvo bhāvo 'bhāvānna jāyate
nābhāvājjāyate 'bhāvo 'bhāvo bhāvānna jāyate || 12 ||

An existent does not arise from an existent; an existent does not arise from non-existence. Non-existence does not arise from non-existence; non-existence does not arise from an existent.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na bhāvājjāyate bhāvo bhāvo 'bhāvānna jāyate | nābhāvājjāyate 'bhāvo 'bhāvo bhāvānna jāyate || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“An existent does not arise from an existent; an existent does not arise from non-existence. Non-existence does not arise from non-existence; non-existence does not arise from an existent.” || 12 ||

First, the arising of an existent, called arising, from an existent, also called arising, does not exist, because cause and effect do not exist simultaneously, and because it is purposeless for what has already obtained birth to be born again. An existent does not arise from non-existence either. For what reason? Non-existence is passing away, destruction, and that is opposed to an existent. Therefore how could an existent come from what is opposed to an existent? If it did, then the son of a barren woman too would have a son. But this is not so. Therefore an existent does not arise from non-existence either.

Now non-existence does not arise from non-existence either. For non-existence is of the nature of the cessation of an existent; whence would it have the capacity to produce? If it did, nirvāṇa too would have the capacity to produce. And if non-existence arose from non-existence, then the son of a barren woman too would have a son. But this is not so. Therefore non-existence does not arise from non-existence either.

Now non-existence does not arise from an existent either. For non-existence is opposed to an existent; how could it arise from an existent? If it did, darkness would arise from a lamp. And since, when examined in this way, arising and passing away do not exist, they are to be understood as seen by people in the world only through delusion.

Or there is another preliminary position: “Here, if arising and passing away existed, they would have an existent or a non-existent as their support. But both an existent and a non-existent, when examined in every way, do not come about. Then whence would arising and passing away, which have no support, exist?” Therefore: “Arising and passing away are seen only through delusion.” And just as an existent and non-existence do not come about, he shows how they do not:

“An existent does not arise from an existent; an existent does not arise from non-existence. Non-existence does not arise from non-existence; non-existence does not arise from an existent.” The meaning is as before. || 12 ||
