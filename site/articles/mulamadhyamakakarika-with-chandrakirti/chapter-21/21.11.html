MMK 21.11

dṛśyate saṃbhavaścaiva mohādvibhava eva ca || 11 ||

Arising and passing away are seen only through delusion.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dṛśyate saṃbhavaścaiva mohādvibhava eva ca || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent might say: “What is the use of this subtle scrutiny? Since people, from cowherds to women, see arising and passing away, therefore arising and passing away exist. For the son of a barren woman, which does not exist, cannot be seen.” Even so:

“Arising and passing away are seen only through delusion.” || 11 ||

It is inconclusive that everything perceived by people in the world exists. For people from cowherds to women see a city of gandharvas, illusions, dreams, a wheel of firebrands, and water in a mirage, though those things do not exist, because of impairment of the senses. In this way, they see these two, arising and passing away, though they do not exist, through delusion alone. He says: “Arising and passing away are seen only through delusion.”

Now, how is it determined that these two, arising and passing away, do not exist in their own nature, and are seen by the people of the world through delusion alone? It is determined by reasoning. What is the reasoning here? If there were some existent, it would necessarily arise either from an existent or from a non-existent. Likewise, if there were some non-existence, it too would arise either from an existent or from a non-existent. Neither way is possible for either of them, he says:
