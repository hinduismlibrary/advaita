MMK 21.21

Investigation of Rising and Passing

MMK 21.21

evaṃ triṣvapi kāleṣu na yuktā bhavadabhimatā bhavasaṃtatiḥ
triṣu kāleṣu yā nāsti sā kathaṃ bhavasaṃtatiḥ || 21 ||

Thus, in all three times, the continuity of existence that you accept is not reasonable. How is that continuity of existence which is absent in the three times?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ triṣvapi kāleṣu na yuktā bhavadabhimatā bhavasaṃtatiḥ | triṣu kāleṣu yā nāsti sā kathaṃ bhavasaṃtatiḥ || 21 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“Thus, in all three times, the continuity of existence that you accept is not reasonable.” Since the first existence does not arise when the final existence has ceased, has not ceased, or is ceasing, therefore continuity of existence does not exist in any of the three times.

“How is that continuity of existence which is absent in the three times?” That continuity of existence, which now does not exist in the three times, how will it exist through some other nature? Thus, in every way, the continuity of existence accepted by you does not exist. And therefore what was said:

“Existence is the continuity of the arising and passing away of cause and fruit,”

is not reasonable. And then, if entities are accepted, the consequence of the doctrine of permanence and annihilation is hard to avoid for you. Therefore it is established that entities do not arise by their own-nature. As it was said in the noble Samādhirāja:

“As from an existing seed a sprout [arises], yet what is the seed is not itself the sprout; nor is it other than that. Thus the nature is neither annihilation nor permanence. An impression is seen from a seal, but no transmission of the seal is apprehended; it is neither that nor other than that. Thus formations are neither annihilated nor permanent.”

Therefore the venerable Nāgārjuna said:

“Recitation, a lamp, a seal, a mirror, sound, a sun-stone, seed, and mango: the rebirth-linking of aggregates without transmigration is to be understood by the wise.”

And the Blessed One said:

“He is born and also dies, yet there is neither birth nor death. For one who knows this, this concentration is not difficult to obtain.”

And further:

“Those people in the world are always very happy by whom the inconceivable dharmas are known. No discrimination of dharma and non-dharma; all mental proliferation has been fully understood. One discriminates knowledge as existence and non-existence, thinks all things, and imagines all things as existent; but those fools who follow the power of thought are miserable through hundreds of millions of existences. Even one who thinks of empty dharmas is a fool who has entered a bad path. The dharmas called empty are called syllables, and they are said to be not syllables. The person who thinks of peaceful and very peaceful dharmas has never existed as a mind. All conceptual thought of mind is proliferation; know the dharmas as subtle and inconceivable.” || 21 ||

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter, called “Examination of Arising and Passing Away,” in the Madhyamaka Commentary, the Clear Words, composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti.
