MMK 21.7

Investigation of Rising and Passing

← MMK 21.6Chapter 21MMK 21.8 →

MMK 21.7

kṣayasya saṃbhavo nāsti nākṣayasyāpi saṃbhavaḥ
kṣayasya vibhavo nāsti vibhavo nākṣayasya ca || 7 ||

Arising does not exist for the perishable, nor does arising exist for the imperishable; passing away does not exist for the perishable, nor for the imperishable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kṣayasya saṃbhavo nāsti nākṣayasyāpi saṃbhavaḥ | kṣayasya vibhavo nāsti vibhavo nākṣayasya ca || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And further, if these arising and passing away are imagined here, would they be imagined for an entity whose nature is perishable, or for one whose nature is imperishable? Neither way is reasonable, he says:

“Arising does not exist for the perishable, nor does arising exist for the imperishable; passing away does not exist for the perishable, nor for the imperishable.” || 7 ||

Here, arising is not reasonable for an entity characterized by perishability, because it has the opposing property. Arising is not reasonable for an imperishable entity either, because it is separate from the characteristics of an entity, like a hare’s horn. In the same way, passing away does not exist for the perishable: what has the property of perishing is itself absent, and its passing away without a support is not reasonable. Nor does passing away exist for the imperishable: the property of imperishability is different from the characteristics of existence and non-existence; whence would passing away exist for that which does not exist? And those arising and passing away that do not occur for an entity either perishable or imperishable do not occur. Thus arising and passing away do not exist. || 7 ||
