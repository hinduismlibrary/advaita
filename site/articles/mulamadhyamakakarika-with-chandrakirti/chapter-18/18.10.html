MMK 18.10

pratītya yadyadbhavati na hi tāvattadeva tat
na cānyadapi tattasmānnocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 10 ||

Whatever arises in dependence on this or that is not identical with it, nor is it entirely other. Therefore it is neither annihilation nor permanence.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya yadyadbhavati na hi tāvattadeva tat | na cānyadapi tattasmānnocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 10 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Candrakīrti illustrates dependent arising with a rice seed and its sprout. The sprout is not identical with the seed; otherwise cause and product would be the same. It is not wholly different either, or it could arise without the seed.

Causal continuity therefore requires neither a permanent substance passing unchanged through time nor a total break between cause and result. This avoids both permanence and annihilation while preserving the conventional working of actions and their effects.
