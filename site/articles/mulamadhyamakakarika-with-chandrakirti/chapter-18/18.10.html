MMK 18.10

pratītya yadyadbhavati na hi tāvattadeva tat
na cānyadapi tattasmānnocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 10 ||

Whatever arises depending on whatever is not that very thing, nor is it other than that. Therefore it is neither cut off nor eternal.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya yadyadbhavati na hi tāvattadeva tat | na cānyadapi tattasmānnocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus far, the mark of reality for the noble ones, who have accomplished the task of completely exhausting birth, aging, death, and saṃsāra. But, concerning the mark of reality for worldly people, it is said:

“Whatever arises depending on whatever is not that very thing, nor is it other than that. Therefore it is neither cut off nor eternal.” || 10 ||

Whatever effect arises depending on whatever cause—as, for example, a rice-shoot arises depending on a rice-seed and a collection of earth and the other conditions—it cannot be said, then, that it is that very thing. The seed itself is certainly not the shoot, because the consequence would be that producer and produced are one. And then father and son too would be one. And, because they were not different, in the state of the shoot the seed too would be apprehended just like the shoot, and the shoot too would be apprehended just like the seed. And thus the seed would be permanent, because its non-destruction is accepted. Then, as a consequence of the doctrine of permanence, a great mass of faults would follow, because of the consequence that there would be no karma, result, and so forth. Thus, for now, it is not fitting that just what is the seed is the shoot.

Nor is it also other than that. Nor is the shoot other than the seed, since the consequence would be that the shoot could arise even without the seed. Because of the statement: “If one thing could arise as another from another, even apart from another,” there would be the consequence that the seed is not cut off even in the state of the shoot; and then there would be the fault of the doctrine that the effect exists [in the cause]. Since it is thus, the effect that arises depending on a cause is not that cause, and that effect is not other than that cause. Therefore, it cannot be established that the cause is cut off, nor that it is permanent. As it was said by the noble Āryadeva:

“Because an existent proceeds, annihilation does not arise through it; because an existent ceases, permanence does not arise through it.”

And it was said in the Ārya-Lalitavistara Sūtra:

“As a sprout [arises] when a seed exists, yet the seed is not itself that sprout; nor is it other than that; thus the nature is not annihilation and not permanence.” || 10 ||
