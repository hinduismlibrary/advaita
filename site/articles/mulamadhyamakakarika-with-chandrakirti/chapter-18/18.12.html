MMK 18.12

Investigation of Self and Things

← MMK 18.11Chapter 18MMK 19.1 →

MMK 18.12

saṃbuddhānāmanutpāde śrāvakāṇāṃ punaḥkṣaye
jñānaṃ pratyekabuddhānāmasaṃsargātpravartate || 12 ||

When the fully awakened Buddhas do not arise and the disciples have subsequently disappeared, the knowledge of the pratyekabuddhas proceeds through nonassociation.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

saṃbuddhānāmanutpāde śrāvakāṇāṃ punaḥkṣaye | jñānaṃ pratyekabuddhānāmasaṃsargātpravartate || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And even if somehow:

“When the fully awakened Buddhas do not arise and the disciples have subsequently disappeared,”

there being a lack of the condition of a good spiritual friend who teaches the noble path, there would be no attainment of the nectar of the reality of Dharma; nevertheless, for the pratyekabuddhas who are independent of instruction in this present world and whose conditions have come about merely through resort to seclusion, through the force of the cause of hearing the reality of Dharma in a former birth, their self-arisen

“knowledge proceeds through nonassociation.” || 12 ||

Seclusion of body and mind is nonassociation; or alternatively, it is not seeking a good spiritual friend. Since, through the cause of nonassociation, the attainment of the reality of Dharma surely does arise for pratyekabuddhas even at a time when a perfectly awakened Buddha is absent, it is to be understood that the accomplishment of this medicine—the nectar of the reality of the True Dharma prescribed by the great physician king, the perfectly awakened Buddha—is not fruitless. And since this is so, a wise person ought to seek the reality of the True Dharma, even giving up life. As it was said by the Blessed One in the noble Blessed Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines:

The elder Subhūti asked the Blessed One: “And how, Blessed One, was this perfection of wisdom sought by the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sadāprarudita?” Thus addressed, the Blessed One said this to the elder Subhūti: “Formerly, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sadāprarudita sought the perfection of wisdom while not caring for his body, not regarding his life, and not relying on gain, honor, or fame. While he was seeking the perfection of wisdom, having gone to a forest, a sound was heard from the sky: ‘Go, son of good family, to the eastern direction. There you will hear the perfection of wisdom. And go in such a way that you do not produce attention to bodily fatigue; do not produce attention to drowsiness and sleep; do not produce attention to food; and do not direct the mind anywhere, inwardly or outwardly. And, son of good family, do not go looking to the left, to the right, to the east, to the west, to the north, upward, downward, or in the intermediate directions. And go in such a way that you do not waver from self, from the view of a personal self, from form, from feeling, from perception, from formations, or from consciousness. For whoever wavers from this is displaced. From what is he displaced? He is displaced from the Buddha-dharmas. Whoever is displaced from the Buddha-dharmas moves in saṃsāra. Whoever moves in saṃsāra does not move in the perfection of wisdom and does not attain it.’”

When the evil Māra had made the water disappear, this occurred to him: “What if I were to pierce my own body and sprinkle this ground with blood? For what reason? This ground is raised up with dust; let the element of dust from here not fall on the body of the bodhisattva mahāsattva Dharmodgata. What shall I do with embodied existence, which is certain to be subject to breaking apart? It would indeed be better if, by such an act, the destruction of my embodied existence were brought about, and not by an act without such power. For many thousands of my embodied existences, for the sake of desire and because of desire, have repeatedly been broken while I wandered in saṃsāra, but not in places such as this ...” Then the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sadāprarudita, having taken a sharp weapon, pierced himself all around and sprinkled that ground all around with his own blood, and so forth.

Then the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sadāprarudita, immediately upon seeing the bodhisattva mahāsattva Dharmodgata, experienced such happiness as a monk who has entered the first meditative absorption and whose attention is one-pointed. Thereupon this was the teaching of the perfection of wisdom by the bodhisattva mahāsattva Dharmodgata: namely, the equality of the perfection of wisdom through the equality of all things; the seclusion of the perfection of wisdom through the secludedness of all things; the immovability of the perfection of wisdom through the immovability of all things; the non-consideration of the perfection of wisdom through the non-consideration of all things; the non-fixity of the perfection of wisdom through the non-fixity of all things; the one taste of the perfection of wisdom through the one taste of all things; the limitlessness of the perfection of wisdom through the limitlessness of all things; the non-arising of the perfection of wisdom through the non-arising of all things; the non-cessation of the perfection of wisdom through the non-cessation of all things; the limitlessness of the perfection of wisdom through the limitlessness of space; up to the non-separation of the perfection of wisdom through the non-separation of all things; the non-apprehension of the perfection of wisdom through the non-apprehension of all things; the equality of the overcoming of the perfection of wisdom through the equality of the overcoming of all things; the inactivity of the perfection of wisdom through the inactivity of all things; and the inconceivability of the perfection of wisdom through the inconceivability of all things. || 12 ||

Thus ends the eighteenth chapter, called “Examination of Self,” in the Madhyamaka Commentary, the Clear Words, composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti.
