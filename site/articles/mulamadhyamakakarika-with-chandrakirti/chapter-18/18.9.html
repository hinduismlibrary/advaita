MMK 18.9

aparapratyayaṃ śāntaṃ prapañcairaprapañcitam
nirvikalpamanānārtham etattattvasya lakṣaṇam || 9 ||

Not dependent on another, peaceful, not made proliferate by proliferations, without conceptualization, not having many meanings: this is the mark of reality.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

aparapratyayaṃ śāntaṃ prapañcairaprapañcitam | nirvikalpamanānārtham etattattvasya lakṣaṇam || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

What, then, is the mark of that reality, for the sake of entry into which the Blessed Ones teach these teachings? This has been said by us: “The expressible has ceased, when the domain of mind has ceased.” When this is so, what else is being asked? Even so, in accordance with conventional truth, just as worldly truth and the like are accepted, let its mark also be stated by imputation. It is said:

“Not dependent on another, peaceful, not made proliferate by proliferations, without conceptualization, not having many meanings: this is the mark of reality.” || 9 ||

Here, because there is no reliance on another in regard to it, it is “not dependent on another”; it is not to be reached through another’s instruction. The meaning is: it is to be realized by oneself alone. For example, people with eye-disease, seeing a false appearance of hair, gnats, flies, and the like, are not able—even through the instruction of one without eye-disease—to realize, in the manner of one who sees, the nature of hairs as it really stands. What, then, do they understand? From the instruction of one without eye-disease, they understand only this much: “This is false.” But when their eyes of understanding have been anointed with the collyrium of the nonerroneous vision of emptiness that removes eye-disease, and they have become those in whom knowledge of reality has arisen, then they themselves realize that reality, by the means of their own realization. Thus the nature of things, which is not dependent on another, is reality.

And this has a peaceful nature: that means, it is devoid of own-nature, like the non-seeing of hairs by one without eye-disease. For that very reason it is “not made proliferate by proliferations.” For proliferation is speech, because it proliferates objects; “not made proliferate by proliferations” means not spoken by words.

And it is without conceptualization. Conceptualization is the activity of mind. Because it is free from that, reality is without conceptualization. As it is said in a sūtra: “What is ultimate truth? That in which even knowledge does not operate; what need to speak of syllables?” Thus it is without conceptualization.

“That whose meaning is not many” is “not having many meanings”: having a non-different meaning. As it is said in the Ārya-Satyadvayāvatāra Sūtra:

The son of the gods said: “What, then, is right application, Mañjuśrī?” Mañjuśrī said: “Son of the gods, just as suchness, the dharma-element, and utter birthlessness are equal in ultimate truth, so the five immediate [karmic] offenses are equal in ultimate truth. Just as the five immediate offenses are equal, so are the views that have been made; just as the views that have been made are equal, so are the dharmas of ordinary persons; just as the dharmas of ordinary persons are equal, so are the dharmas of trainees; just as the dharmas of trainees are equal, so are the dharmas of those beyond training; just as the dharmas of those beyond training are equal, so are the dharmas of perfectly awakened Buddhas; just as the dharmas of perfectly awakened Buddhas are equal, so is nirvāṇa; just as nirvāṇa is equal, so is saṃsāra; just as saṃsāra is equal, so is defilement in ultimate truth; just as defilement in ultimate truth is equal, so is purification in ultimate truth; just as purification in ultimate truth is equal, so are all things in ultimate truth. Son of the gods, a monk engaged in the equality of all things in ultimate truth is called rightly engaged.”

The son of the gods said: “By what equality, Mañjuśrī, are all things equal in ultimate truth, from purification onward?” Mañjuśrī said: “All things are equal by the equality of the non-arising of all things in ultimate truth, by the equality of the utter birthlessness of all things in ultimate truth. For what reason? Son of the gods, all things are not differentiated from nirvāṇa in ultimate truth, because they have utter non-arising as their basis. For example, son of the gods, the space within a clay vessel and the space within a jewel vessel—this is just the element of space. Ultimately there is no differentiation at all. In just this way, son of the gods, whatever is defilement is, in ultimate truth, utter non-arising; whatever is purification is also, in ultimate truth, utter non-arising. Saṃsāra too is, in ultimate truth, utter non-arising, and even nirvāṇa is, in ultimate truth, utter non-arising. Here there is no differentiation at all in ultimate truth. For what reason? Because all things are, in ultimate truth, utterly non-arisen.”

Thus the non-multiplicity of reality is to be understood as its mark, because it has the one taste of emptiness. Here the subsequent explanation is also to be understood. || 9 ||
