MMK 18.3

nirmamo nirahaṃkāro yaśca so 'pi na vidyate
nirmamaṃ nirahaṃkāraṃ yaḥ paśyati na paśyati || 3 ||

That one who is without mine and without I does not exist either. Whoever sees one without mine and without I does not see.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nirmamo nirahaṃkāro yaśca so 'pi na vidyate | nirmamaṃ nirahaṃkāraṃ yaḥ paśyati na paśyati || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: But that yogi who becomes without mine and without I exists. And when he exists, self and aggregates are established. It is not so. For:

“That one who is without mine and without I does not exist either. Whoever sees one without mine and without I does not see.”

When self and aggregates are not found in any way as having own-nature, where would there be another entity apart from those, which is without mine and without I? And whoever sees this one, whose own-nature does not exist, as without mine and without I, it should be understood that he does not see reality. As stated by the Blessed One:

“See the inner as empty; see the outer as empty. That one who cultivates emptiness does not exist either.”

And likewise:

“Whoever thinks of empty dharmas is a child who has entered a bad path. Empty dharmas are said to be imperishable, and they are said to be imperishable.”

“Whoever thinks of peaceful, completely peaceful dharmas—his mind has never come to be. All proliferations are thought by the thinker of mind; understand the dharmas as unthinkable.”

And likewise:

“The aggregates are empty and detached by nature; awakening is empty and detached by nature. Whoever practices is also empty by nature, but this is for one who knows, not for a child.”
