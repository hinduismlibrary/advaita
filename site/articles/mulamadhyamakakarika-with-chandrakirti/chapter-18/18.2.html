MMK 18.2

ātmanyasati cātmīyaṃ kuta eva bhaviṣyati
nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ śamādātmātmanīnayoḥ || 2 ||

When self is absent, how could what belongs to self exist? From the cessation of self and what belongs to self, one is without mine and without I.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmanyasati cātmīyaṃ kuta eva bhaviṣyati | nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ śamādātmātmanīnayoḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Therefore, because self and what belongs to self are not found, the yogi, who is close to seeing ultimate truth, certainly becomes:

“When self is absent, how could what belongs to self exist? From the cessation of self and what belongs to self, one is without mine and without I.”

“What is beneficial to self” is “what belongs to self”; it means the five aggregates. Because of the cessation, the non-arising, through not finding the entity that is self, the object of making “I,” and the entity such as the aggregates that belong to self, the object of making “mine,” the yogi becomes without mine and without I.
