MMK 18.2

ātmanyasati cātmīyaṃ kuta eva bhaviṣyati
nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ śamādātmātmanīnayoḥ || 2 ||

When there is no self, how could there be anything belonging to a self? Through the quieting of ‘I’ and ‘mine,’ possessiveness and egoism cease.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmanyasati cātmīyaṃ kuta eva bhaviṣyati | nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ śamādātmātmanīnayoḥ || 2 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

The first half asks where anything “belonging to me” could exist if the self were absent. Candrakīrti explains that what is called “mine” means the five aggregates considered in relation to an imagined owner; it cannot stand independently of that designation.

The verse then describes the yogin as free of possessiveness and ego through the quieting of self and what belongs to self. There is no hidden ownerless possession left over: both sides of the relation are designated together and lose their imagined footing together.
