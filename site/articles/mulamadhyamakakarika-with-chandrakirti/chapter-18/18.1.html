MMK 18.1

ātmā skandhā yadi bhavedudayavyayabhāgbhavet
skandhebhyo 'nyo yadi bhavedbhavedaskandhalakṣaṇaḥ || 1 ||

If the self were the aggregates, it would partake in arising and ceasing. If it were other than the aggregates, it would have the characteristic of being other than the aggregates.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmā skandhā yadi bhavedudayavyayabhāgbhavet | skandhebhyo 'nyo yadi bhavedbhavedaskandhalakṣaṇaḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: If afflictions, actions, bodies, agents, and fruits are all not reality, and only what is not reality appears to fools in the form of reality, like a city of gandharvas and the rest, then what is reality here? And how is there an entry into reality? The response: through not finding all things, internal and external, the complete exhaustion in every way of making “I” and making “mine,” internally and externally, is reality here. Entry into reality, then, is to be understood at length from the Madhyamakāvatāra, beginning with:

“Surveying in understanding all the afflictions and faults without remainder that arise from the view of the existent group, the yogi, having awakened, makes only the negation of self and its object.”

Seeing saṃsāra as rooted in the view of the body, and seeing the abandonment of the view of the existent group through the non-finding of self, and through its abandonment seeing the turning back of all afflictions, he first examines self itself: “What is this self?”—that which is the object of making “I.” And this object of making “I,” when conceived, would either be of the nature of the aggregates or different from the aggregates. Since the positions of support, supported, and possessing them are also included in the positions of identity and difference, and since only these are intended concisely, wishing to begin the negation of self through just the negation of the two positions, identity and difference, the Teacher says:

“If the self were the aggregates, it would partake in arising and ceasing. If it were other than the aggregates, it would have the characteristic of being other than the aggregates.”

There, if the aggregates are conceived as self, then self would follow as partaking in arising and ceasing, as arising and perishing, because the aggregates partake in arising and ceasing. But this is not accepted, because the consequence of the fault of there being many selves would follow. For he will say:

Why, then, were five positions stated elsewhere, in the examination of the Tathāgata and the examination of fire and fuel, but here only two positions? The response: Because the five positions were stated in those two chapters, they are not stated again here. But the two positions are stated concisely.

“Nor would there be the fault of something arising after not having been. For self would be made, or else it would arise without a cause.”

And likewise:

“Nor does appropriation itself go and arise as self. For how indeed will appropriation be the appropriator?”

Moreover:

“If the aggregates were self, then, because of their multiplicity, there would be many selves; and those aggregates too are many. And self would be a substance, and in such a substance there would be no reversal in its activity.

“There would necessarily be annihilation of self at nirvāṇa, since destruction and arising occur in the moments before nirvāṇa. Because the agent is destroyed, there would only be absence of that fruit; and one person would experience action accumulated by another.”

From the examination made at length in the Madhyamakāvatāra, beginning with these and the rest, this position is also to be understood. Therefore no further elaboration is undertaken here.
