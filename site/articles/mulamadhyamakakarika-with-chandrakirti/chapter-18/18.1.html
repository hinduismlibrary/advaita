MMK 18.1

ātmā skandhā yadi bhavedudayavyayabhāgbhavet
skandhebhyo 'nyo yadi bhavedbhavedaskandhalakṣaṇaḥ || 1 ||

If the self were the aggregates, it would be subject to arising and ceasing. If it were other than the aggregates, it would have none of their defining characteristics.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmā skandhā yadi bhavedudayavyayabhāgbhavet | skandhebhyo 'nyo yadi bhavedbhavedaskandhalakṣaṇaḥ || 1 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Candrakīrti examines the two basic possibilities for the self: it is either the aggregates or something distinct from them. If it were identical with the aggregates, it would arise and perish with them, and the many aggregates would imply many selves. The imagined self, however, is treated as a lasting owner.

A wholly separate self would have no aggregate characteristics and could not be found apart from body, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. Claims that such a self exists by name do not establish it as an independently real thing. The sense of “I” is designated in dependence on the aggregates, like a reflection that depends on a mirror; neither the owner nor what is called “mine” is found with its own nature.
