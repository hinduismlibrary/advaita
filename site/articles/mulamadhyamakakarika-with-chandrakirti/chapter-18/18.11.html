MMK 18.11

anekārthamanānārthamanucchedamaśāśvatam
etattallokanāthānāṃ buddhānāṃ śāsanāmṛtam || 11 ||

Not one in meaning, nor many in meaning; not cut off, nor eternal: this is the nectar of the teaching of those Buddhas, the protectors of the world.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anekārthamanānārthamanucchedamaśāśvatam | etattallokanāthānāṃ buddhānāṃ śāsanāmṛtam || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, by the reasoning described, it is said:

“Not one in meaning, nor many in meaning; not cut off, nor eternal: this is the nectar of the teaching of those Buddhas, the protectors of the world.” || 11 ||

That nectar of the True Dharma of those great nāga-like Buddhas, perfectly awakened, protectors of the whole world, lords of the world, lords of those without a lord, protectors beyond the worldly and protectors of those without protection, whose sphere of directions, extending as far as the element of space, is continuously covered by the canopy of the great cloud of great compassion and skill in means; who are intent on pacifying the burning heat of the fire of the suffering of birth, aging, and death of the world, heated by the extremely sharp sun of the outbreak of the host of afflictions such as attachment; who, by the uninterrupted fall of streams of nectar—the teaching of the True Dharma, the antidote, in accordance with each one's conduct—wish to help the wholesome-root crops, medicinal herbs, fruits, and flowering vines of the people to be trained, in accordance with each one's disposition, arise, grow, and increase: that nectar of the True Dharma of all the protectors of the world is to be understood, by the reasoning described, as having the nature of the exhaustion of the suffering of becoming throughout the entire three realms, free from identity and difference, and free from the doctrines of permanence and annihilation.

For the hearers who have entered this nectar of the reality of the True Dharma, proceeding by the sequence of hearing, reflection, and meditation, through partaking of the taste of the nectar of the three aggregates consisting of morality, concentration, and wisdom, the attainment of nirvāṇa, whose nature is the exhaustion of aging and death, is certain. And even if, somehow, because their wholesome roots are not yet matured here, they do not attain liberation in this very life, although they have heard this nectar of the True Dharma, nevertheless, in another birth as well, their certain accomplishment comes about through the force of the former cause. As it is said in the Śataka:

“Although here the knower of reality does not attain nirvāṇa, surely, without effort, he attains it in another birth, as with karma.” || 11 ||
