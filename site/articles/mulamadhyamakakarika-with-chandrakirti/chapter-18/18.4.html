MMK 18.4

mametyahamiti kṣīṇe bahirdhādhyātmameva ca
nirudhyata upādānaṃ tatkṣayājjanmanaḥ kṣayaḥ || 4 ||

When ‘mine’ and ‘I’ have ceased, both externally and internally, appropriation ceases; with its cessation, birth ceases.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

mametyahamiti kṣīṇe bahirdhādhyātmameva ca | nirudhyata upādānaṃ tatkṣayājjanmanaḥ kṣayaḥ || 4 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Candrakīrti traces all afflictions to the view of a personal identity. When self and what belongs to self are no longer apprehended, that view is abandoned. The four forms of appropriation then cease: attachment to sensual pleasure, to views, to rites and observances, and to doctrines of self.

With appropriation ended, the conditions for renewed becoming are absent. Since birth depends on becoming, the cessation of appropriation brings the cessation of birth.
