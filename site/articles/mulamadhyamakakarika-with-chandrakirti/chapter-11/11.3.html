MMK 11.3

pūrvaṃ jātiryadi bhavejjarāmaraṇamuttaram
nirjarāmaraṇā jātirbhavejjāyeta cāmṛtaḥ || 3 ||

If birth were first and aging and death later, birth would be without aging and death, and an immortal would be born.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pūrvaṃ jātiryadi bhavejjarāmaraṇamuttaram | nirjarāmaraṇā jātirbhavejjāyeta cāmṛtaḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And showing how they do not obtain, he says:

If birth were first, it would then be accompanied by death. And birth without aging and the like is not reasonable, because the consequence of its being unconditioned would follow. If the birth of an entity devoid of aging and death were supposed, then the first birth here would be supposed for an immortal Devadatta alone, apart from that. And then saṃsāra would have a beginning, and the fault of being causeless would follow. And the conception of the former limit, “I existed in the past,” and so on, would not occur; and that which previously did not exist would arise here afterward.

If it should be said: Just as the first arising of mangoes and the like is seen earlier, even without their association with aging and death, so too with selves. It is not so, because it is the same as what is to be proved. For mangoes and the like also arise when their own seed ceases; arising elsewhere for things that have not perished is the same as before.

If it should be said: The seed is indeed different from the tree; therefore the arising of the tree is preceded by non-destruction elsewhere. It is not so, because the otherness of cause and effect is not established. And thus he will say:

“Whatever arises in dependence, that indeed is not that; nor is it other than that. Therefore it is neither annihilated nor eternal.”

And the tree is not other than the seed. Therefore this is the same as what is to be proved. And because there is no arising here from elsewhere of an immortal, birth first is not to be accepted.
