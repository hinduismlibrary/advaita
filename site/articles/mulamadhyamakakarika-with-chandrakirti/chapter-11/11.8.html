MMK 11.8

Investigation of Extremes of Before and After

MMK 11.8

pūrvā = first; na vidyate = is not found; koṭiḥ = limit; saṃsārasya = of cyclic existence; na = not; kevalam = only; sarveṣām = of all; api = also; bhāvānām = entities; pūrvā = first; koṭiḥ = limit; na vidyate = is not found

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pūrvā na vidyate koṭiḥ saṃsārasya na kevalam | sarveṣāmapi bhāvānāṃ pūrvā koṭirna vidyate || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

No phenomenon has an intrinsically established first limit. Candrakīrti extends the analysis to all things that can be named: cause and effect, a mark and what it marks, feeling and the feeler, knowledge and what is known, means and ends, parts and wholes, qualities and their bearers. None has an independent first point from which the other begins.

The cited verses praise the Buddha for revealing that phenomena are beginningless, unarisen and naturally at peace. All are empty, like space or an illusion, without self or beings. Conventionally, actions and their results continue, and beings appear in higher or lower states; yet these phenomena are empty by nature. The teaching points beyond both the claim that things exist inherently and the claim that nothing exists at all.
