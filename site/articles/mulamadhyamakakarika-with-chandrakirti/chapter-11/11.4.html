MMK 11.4

paścājjātiryadi bhavejjarāmaraṇamāditaḥ
ahetukamajātasya syājjaraṃ maraṇaṃ katham || 4 ||

If birth were later and aging and death first, how would aging and death of one not yet born be without a cause?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paścājjātiryadi bhavejjarāmaraṇamāditaḥ | ahetukamajātasya syājjaraṃ maraṇaṃ katham || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If aging and death were first, and birth afterward, even so:

Because it is said, “Aging and death have birth as their condition,” aging and death are said to have birth as their cause. If these were first, then they would be without a cause. Therefore this is not reasonable. And because it is also said:

“Just as, when a lump is thrown up, there is a cause in the throwing up; in the falling there is no other cause than the cause of the throwing up,”

Just as here the throwing up is the cause of falling, and no other, so here too we describe birth itself as the cause of destruction, and no other. Therefore destruction is not without a cause. And because its arising has birth as its cause, considering that very arising to be the cause of destruction, this verse too is well directed:

“Thus these conditioned phenomena arise with causes. That alone is the being of phenomena: that they are destroyed when arisen.”
