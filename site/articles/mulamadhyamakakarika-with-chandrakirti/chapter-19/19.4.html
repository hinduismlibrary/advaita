MMK 19.4

etenaivāvaśiṣṭau dvau krameṇa parivartakau
uttamādhamamadhyādīnekatvādīṃśca lakṣayet || 4 ||

By this very method, let the remaining two turnings be applied in order; and let one discern the highest, lowest, middle, and so on, and oneness and so on.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

etenaivāvaśiṣṭau dvau krameṇa parivartakau | uttamādhamamadhyādīnekatvādīṃśca lakṣayet || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And since, in this way, the present and future are not established either in dependence on the past or independently, when it is accepted that the past and future are not established either in dependence on the present or independently, and that the present and past are not established either in dependence on the future or independently, he indicates the equality of the faults by extending to those very same sequences of non-establishment through dependence on, or non-dependence on, the past, and says:

“By this very method, let the remaining two turnings be applied in order; and let one discern the highest, lowest, middle, and so on, and oneness and so on.” || 4 ||

How is this done?

“If past and future depend on the present, the past and future times would exist in present time. If the past and future times do not exist there, how could they depend on it? If established without dependence, neither the past nor the future is found in the present. Therefore the so-called past and future times do not exist.” This, for now, is one turning of time.

“If past and present depend on the unborn, the past and present times would exist in unborn time. If the past and present times do not exist there, how could they depend on it? If established without dependence, neither the past nor the present is found in the unborn. Therefore the so-called past and present times do not exist.” This is the second turning of time. These are explanatory verses. In this way the two turnings of time are to be understood.

And since, when examined in this way, the three times do not exist, therefore time does not exist; and from the non-existence of time, it is established that the existence of entities too does not exist.

And just as these three times have been examined, so one should discern the highest, lowest, middle, and so on, and oneness and so on.

By the word “and so on” after “highest, lowest, middle,” all entities arranged in relation to three are included: the wholesome, unwholesome, and undetermined; arising, abiding, and destruction; the former end, later end, and middle end; the realms of desire, form, and formlessness; those in training, those beyond training, and those neither in training nor beyond training; and all other such entities. By “and so on” after “oneness and so on,” duality and multiplicity are included. Those very highest and so forth, and one and so forth, are to be understood as explained through the exposition of the three times. || 4 ||
