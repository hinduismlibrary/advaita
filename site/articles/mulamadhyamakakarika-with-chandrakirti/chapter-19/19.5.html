MMK 19.5

nāsthito gṛhyate kālaḥ sthitaḥ kālo na vidyate
yo gṛhyetāgṛhītaśca kālaḥ prajñapyate katham || 5 ||

Time is not apprehended when it is not established, and established time is not found. How, then, can time be designated as apprehended or unapprehended?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nāsthito gṛhyate kālaḥ sthitaḥ kālo na vidyate | yo gṛhyetāgṛhītaśca kālaḥ prajñapyate katham || 5 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

An opponent argues that time exists because it can be measured in moments, minutes, days, and years. Candrakīrti asks whether time is a fixed entity apart from those units. If such an abiding time existed, the units could apprehend it; yet no separate, stable time is found.

Time that is not established cannot be apprehended, and something unapprehended cannot be given an intrinsic identity by designation. Time is spoken of through temporal units, not found as an independent substance behind them.
