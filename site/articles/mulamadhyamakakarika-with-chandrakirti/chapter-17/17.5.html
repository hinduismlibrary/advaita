MMK 17.5

paribhogānvayaṃ puṇyam apuṇyaṃ ca tathāvidham
cetanā ceti saptaite dharmāḥ karmāñjanāḥ smṛtāḥ || 5 ||

Speech and bodily movement, and non-abstinences called non-indications; other non-indications are remembered as abstinences likewise.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paribhogānvayaṃ puṇyam apuṇyaṃ ca tathāvidham | cetanā ceti saptaite dharmāḥ karmāñjanāḥ smṛtāḥ || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“Merit in connection with use” means wholesome. It is in connection with use; therefore it is called “in connection with use.” Use is the use by the Saṅgha and others of an object that has been given away. Connection is following. The meaning is: an increase of the wholesome arising in the continuum of the giver.

“And demerit of that kind,” that is, in connection with use. For example, establishing a shrine and the like where living beings are killed. For, to the extent that living beings are killed because of its fame, to that same extent, from using that shrine and the like, demerit in connection with use arises in the continuum of those who made it, even though they are not doing it. Thus demerit too becomes of that kind.

“And volition,” characterized as the mental action of constructing thought.

In brief, this action is sevenfold: wholesome or unwholesome speech; wholesome or unwholesome movement; wholesome non-indication; unwholesome non-indication; merit in connection with use; demerit in connection with use; and volition.

And these seven dharmas, manifested as action, are remembered as marks of action.
