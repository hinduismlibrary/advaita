MMK 17.20

śūnyatā ca na cocchedaḥ saṃsāraś ca na śāśvatam
karmaṇo 'vipraṇāśaś ca dharmo buddhena deśitaḥ || 20 ||

Emptiness, yet not annihilation; saṃsāra, yet not permanence; and non-destruction of action: this dharma was taught by the Buddha.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

śūnyatā ca na cocchedaḥ saṃsāraś ca na śāśvatam | karmaṇo 'vipraṇāśaś ca dharmo buddhena deśitaḥ || 20 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus:

“Emptiness, yet not annihilation; saṃsāra, yet not permanence; and non-destruction of action: this dharma was taught by the Buddha.”

Because action, having been done, ceases and does not remain by its own nature, emptiness of action is reasonable. And there is no consequence that, because action does not remain in this way, there is a view of annihilation, because the ripening of action exists through the acceptance of non-destruction. For if there were no ripening, there would be a view of annihilation of action.

Because the dharma of non-destruction exists, and because there is no conception that it is similar to a continuum of seeds, diverse saṃsāra of the five destinies is established, differentiated by various destinies, births, kinds, and realms. And there is no consequence of the view of permanence, because it is not accepted that action remains in its own form. And because non-destruction exists, there is non-destruction of actions.

Thus, because this dharma was taught by the Blessed One, awakened through the complete removal of the sleep of ignorance, what the other side said before—“If action remains until the time of ripening, it would become permanent; if it has ceased, having ceased, what fruit will it produce?”—does not apply to our position. Therefore the conception explained by us alone is reasonable.
