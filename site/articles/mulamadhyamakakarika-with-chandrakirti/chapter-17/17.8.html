MMK 17.8

bījāc ca yasmāt saṃtānaḥ saṃtānāc ca phalodbhavaḥ
bījapūrvaṃ phalaṃ tasmān nocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 8 ||

Because a continuum is from a seed and the arising of fruit is from a continuum, the fruit preceded by the seed is neither cut off nor permanent.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bījāc ca yasmāt saṃtānaḥ saṃtānāc ca phalodbhavaḥ | bījapūrvaṃ phalaṃ tasmān nocchinnaṃ nāpi śāśvatam || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus:

“Because a continuum is from a seed and the arising of fruit is from a continuum, the fruit preceded by the seed is neither cut off nor permanent.”

If here the seed, without producing the continuum of sprout and the rest, were to cease because of the presence of opposing conditions such as flame and coals, then, because the continuation of the effect would not be seen there, there would be the view of annihilation. And if the seed did not cease, while the continuum of sprout and the rest proceeded, then, because non-cessation of the seed was accepted, there would be the view of permanence. But this is not so. Therefore there is no consequence of the two views, permanence and annihilation, with regard to the seed.
