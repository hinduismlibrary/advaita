MMK 17.11

dharmasya sādhanopāyāḥ śuklāḥ karmapathā daśa
phalaṃ kāmaguṇāḥ pañca dharmasya pretya ceha ca || 11 ||

The ten bright paths of action are means for accomplishing dharma. The five strands of sensual pleasure are dharma’s fruit, after death and here.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dharmasya sādhanopāyāḥ śuklāḥ karmapathā daśa | phalaṃ kāmaguṇāḥ pañca dharmasya pretya ceha ca || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

In this explanation of the divisions of action stated above, the ten wholesome paths of action have been explained. They are:

“The ten bright paths of action are means for accomplishing dharma. The five strands of sensual pleasure are dharma’s fruit, after death and here.”

The meaning is that these ten wholesome paths of action are means for accomplishing dharma, causes for its completion.

What then is the dharma, apart from the wholesome paths of action, for which these are established as means for accomplishing it? The response: some particular mind alone has been stated by the word “dharma,” by the verse:

“Whatever mind is self-restraining, helpful to others, and friendly—that is dharma.”

Or alternatively, these ten wholesome paths of action, in their completed form, are expressed by the word “dharma”; when in the form of being done, they are expressed by the words “wholesome paths of action.” These ten wholesome paths of action, having the characteristic of this dharma as stated, are established as causes for its completion. How, then, in the division of action undertaken here, are there ten wholesome paths of action? The response:

By “the movement of speech, and the non-abstinences called non-indications,” and so on, the paths of action based on the body and the four verbal ones have been explained. By “and volition,” the mental ones, based on what are called covetousness, ill will, and right view, have been explained. Thus all ten wholesome paths of action have been explained here. And they are causes for the completion of the dharma stated above. And the five strands of sensual pleasure, characterized by form, sound, smell, taste, and touch, are the fruit of this dharma. “After death” means in another, unseen world, and “here” means in this world; the fruit is enjoyed.
