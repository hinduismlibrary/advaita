MMK 17.25

tadvipakvavipākaṃ ca punar eva vipakṣyati
karma vyavasthitaṃ yasmāt tasmāt svābhāvikaṃ yadi || 25 ||

And an action whose fruit has ripened would ripen again, since, if action were inherent, it would remain established.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tadvipakvavipākaṃ ca punar eva vipakṣyati | karma vyavasthitaṃ yasmāt tasmāt svābhāvikaṃ yadi || 25 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover:

“An action whose fruit has ripened would ripen again, since, if action were inherent, it would remain established.”

It would follow that an action whose fruit has ripened would give ripening again, because it had not departed from its own form, as in the state when its fruit had not ripened. Thus, if you think that action is inherent, then, because that action is established, the faults described would undoubtedly follow if it had own-nature. Therefore action is without own-nature. And because action is without own-nature, the consequence of the views of permanence and annihilation does not follow for us, who explain it in this way.
