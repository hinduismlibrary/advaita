MMK 17.32

tathā nirmitakākāraḥ kartā karma ca tatkṛtam
tadyathā nirmitenānyo nirmitako nirmitas tathā || 32 ||

Likewise, the agent is like an emanation, and the action is done by him, just as one emanation is created by another emanation.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tathā nirmitakākāraḥ kartā karma ca tatkṛtam | tadyathā nirmitenānyo nirmitako nirmitas tathā || 32 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“Likewise, the agent is like an emanation, and the action is done by him, just as one emanation is created by another emanation.”

For whoever here is the agent of action is like an emanation, empty of own-nature. And whatever action is done by that agent, empty of own-nature, is also empty of own-nature. It is to be understood just as one emanation is created by another emanation. As stated in the authoritative text:

“When one is speaking, all the emanations speak; when one has become silent, all indeed become silent.”

Therefore, where is the wrong view for the Madhyamikas who speak of nonduality? And it is said in the noble Samādhirāja:

“When the Sugata, the protector, speaks a discourse, taking pity on the people who have gone along the road, the Jina creates emanations there and moves among them, teaching the excellent Buddha-dharmas.

“Hundreds of thousands of living beings, having heard him, made aspirations for the highest and supreme Buddha-knowledge. ‘When will we obtain knowledge of this kind?’ Knowing their intention, the Jina prophesies to them.

“The Dharma King emitted immeasurable hundreds of thousands of rays from the soles of his feet. All the hells become cool; having put away suffering, they experience happiness.

“In the dharma spoken here by the Ten-Powered One, the eye of gods and humans becomes pure.” And so on.

“Some there at that time generate longing. Supreme and inconceivable are the gains obtained by those who invited the Jina, the king of men; for them the offering has no limit.” And so on, in detail.

And likewise in the noble Vimalakīrtinirdeśa:

“By that emanated bodhisattva, food left over after it had been used by the Tathāgata Samantabhadra was brought from the world-realm Gandhasugandha. It was accompanied by various dishes, chewables, and the like, each distinct and of diverse tastes. With one meal, he satisfied all that people—the assembly of hearers and bodhisattvas, kings, royal ministers, priests, women of the inner chambers, gatekeepers, caravan leaders, and others—and caused them to attain the great samādhi called Joyful Appearance.”

And it is recited in the Vinaya:

“A monk who is an unsuitable model for a bad monk was created by the Blessed One. By his words, communion in a pure vow was declared even for one who is virtuous.”
