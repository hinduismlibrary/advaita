MMK 17.7

yo 'ṅkuraprabhṛtir bījāt saṃtāno 'bhipravartate
tataḥ phalam ṛte bījāt sa ca nābhipravartate || 7 ||

The continuum beginning with a sprout proceeds from the seed; from it, fruit. Without the seed that continuum does not proceed.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yo 'ṅkuraprabhṛtir bījāt saṃtāno 'bhipravartate | tataḥ phalam ṛte bījāt sa ca nābhipravartate || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

There, some of another school explain an answer: Since formations perish immediately after arising, the fault of impermanence does not apply to us. And to what was also said, “If it has ceased, having ceased, what fruit will it produce?”—here too we state an answer:

“The continuum beginning with a sprout proceeds from the seed; from it, fruit. Without the seed that continuum does not proceed.”

Here, the seed, although momentary, ceases after accepting the state of cause for just that continuum endowed with a particular power to produce a particular future fruit of its own kind, called sprout, stalk, stem, leaf, and so on. And this continuum beginning with the sprout and the rest proceeds from the seed. From that, gradually, when the cooperating causes are not lacking, a great accumulation of fruit arises even from a small cause. Apart from the seed, without the seed, that continuum beginning with sprout and the rest does not proceed. Thus it is shown that the continuum of sprout and the rest, and the fruit, are caused by the seed, through being caused when that is present and not being caused when that is absent.
