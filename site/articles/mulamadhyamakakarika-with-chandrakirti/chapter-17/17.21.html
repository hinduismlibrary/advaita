MMK 17.21

karma notpadyate kasmān niḥsvabhāvaṃ yataḥ
yasmāc ca tadanutpannaṃ na tasmād vipraṇaśyati || 21 ||

Since it is not arisen, it does not perish on that account.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

karma notpadyate kasmān niḥsvabhāvaṃ yataḥ | yasmāc ca tadanutpannaṃ na tasmād vipraṇaśyati || 21 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Why are you so greatly distressed here, fearing the collapse of the rampart of a city of gandharvas and undertaking the toil and labor of protecting it, when you dispute about the fruit of action that is not possible? If action were to arise by its own nature, then, because it remains until ripening, it would be permanent; because it is destroyed, it would be annihilated. But when action does not arise at all, because it is empty of own-nature, where would its remaining or destruction be, on account of which there would be this concern? Here the opponent says:

“Why does action not arise?”

The Teacher says:

“Because it is without own-nature.”

Because action does not arise, being without own-nature, how then is this stated by the Blessed One?

“Actions do not perish even in hundreds of millions of eons. Having met with the assemblage and the time, they indeed ripen for embodied ones.”

The response:

“Since it has not arisen, it does not perish on that account.”

This is the intention of the Blessed One. Therefore this rule is not a refutation of us.
