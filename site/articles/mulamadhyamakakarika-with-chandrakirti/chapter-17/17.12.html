MMK 17.12

bahavaś ca mahāntaś ca doṣāḥ syur api kalpanā
yad eṣā tena naivaiṣā kalpanātropapadyate || 12 ||

Many and great faults would occur in the conception; therefore this conception is not appropriate here.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bahavaś ca mahāntaś ca doṣāḥ syur api kalpanā | yad eṣā tena naivaiṣā kalpanātropapadyate || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus, when the answer to the objection has been explained by those of one school, others, raising a fault against them, explain another answer to the objection, saying:

“Many and great faults would occur in the conception; therefore this conception is not appropriate here.”

If, through the similarity of seed and sprout, the fault of the two views of permanence and annihilation were avoided in the continuum of mind, then many faults by number and great faults because of contradiction with what is seen and unseen would accrue to the position of the other side. How so? If, by the example of the continuum of seed, only a continuum of sprout and the rest of the same kind proceeds from a rice seed, and not of a different kind, and from the continuum of rice sprout and the rest only a rice fruit arises, and not a fruit of the bael tree, because it is of a different kind, then here too, from a wholesome mind only a continuum of wholesome mind would exist, because it is of the same kind, and not a continuum of unwholesome or indeterminate mind, because it is of a different kind. In the same way, from unwholesome or indeterminate mind only a continuum of unwholesome or indeterminate mind would exist, and no other, because it is of a different kind.

From minds moving in the desire realm, form realm, formless realm, and the uncontaminated, only the arising of minds similar to them would occur, not of those of a different kind. From a human mind there would be only a human mind, not another mind such as a god, hell-being, animal, and so on. And then a god would only be a god, a human only a human, and so on. And then, for gods and humans doing even unwholesome actions, there would be no diversity of destiny, womb, color, intellect, faculties, strength, form, enjoyment, and the rest, nor would there be falling into states of woe. But all this is accepted. Since many and great faults of this kind follow in the conception of a similarity to the continuum of a seed, therefore this conception is not appropriate here.
