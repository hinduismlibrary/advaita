MMK 17.28

avidyānivṛto jantus tṛṣṇāsaṃyojanaś ca saḥ
sa bhoktā sa ca na kartur anyo na ca sa eva saḥ || 28 ||

A being is veiled by ignorance and bound by craving. That one is the experiencer, and is neither other than the agent nor that very one.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

avidyānivṛto jantus tṛṣṇāsaṃyojanaś ca saḥ | sa bhoktā sa ca na kartur anyo na ca sa eva saḥ || 28 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Action indeed exists by its own nature, because an experiencer of its fruit exists. What does not exist has no experiencer of its fruit, for example, the fruit of a sky-mango. And an experiencer of the fruit of action exists:

“A being is veiled by ignorance and bound by craving. That one is the experiencer, and is neither other than the agent nor that very one.”

There, “ignorance,” “non-knowledge,” “darkness,” and “confusion” are synonyms. Veiled means covered by ignorance. “Being” is one who is born again and again in the saṃsāra of the five destinies. “Sentient being,” “person,” and “living creature” are synonyms for that same one. “Craving,” “passion,” “attachment,” and “clinging” are synonyms. Bondage is a fetter. Because craving is his fetter, he is bound by craving; this is the meaning. As stated in the sūtra: “Beings are veiled by ignorance and bound by craving.”

Further, this evil action itself is done by oneself, and its ripening is to be experienced by oneself, as stated. And he is the experiencer of the fruit of action. And he is neither other than the agent nor that very same one, because identity and difference are inexpressible. Therefore action indeed exists because an experiencer of the fruit exists.
