MMK 17.14

patraṃ yathāvipraṇāśas tatharṇam iva karma ca
caturvidho dhātutaḥ sa prakṛtyāvyākṛtaś ca saḥ || 14 ||

Like a promissory note is non-destruction, and action is like a debt. It is fourfold by realm and indeterminate by nature.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

patraṃ yathāvipraṇāśas tatharṇam iva karma ca | caturvidho dhātutaḥ sa prakṛtyāvyākṛtaś ca saḥ || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here, a wholesome action that has been done ceases immediately after arising, and there is no consequence that fruit is absent when it has ceased. For, at the very time that action is produced, a dissociated dharma called “non-destruction” arises in the continuum of the agent, like a promissory note. Thus it is to be understood as a note; and the action for which this dharma called “non-destruction” arises is to be understood as a debt.

And just as, because a note remains, even when the money lent has been spent, the creditor does not lose the money, but at a later time is connected with a further amount of money, so too, even when the action has perished, because another dharma called “non-destruction” remains, the agent is indeed connected with the fruit that has it as its cause.

And just as a note, after bringing the giver the money, is released, and whether it exists or does not exist, it is not capable of bringing the money again, so too non-destruction, once it has given ripening, whether it exists or does not exist, cannot again make the agent connected with ripening, like a released note.

And this non-destruction that we have stated, spoken of in another sūtra, is fourfold according to realm, through the divisions of moving in the desire realm, moving in the form realm, moving in the formless realm, and uncontaminated. And it is indeterminate by nature. Because it is not determined as wholesome or unwholesome, non-destruction is indeterminate. If it were unwholesome for unwholesome actions, then it would not exist for those free from passion. And if it were wholesome for wholesome actions, it would not exist for those whose wholesome roots have been cut off. Therefore it is indeterminate by nature.
