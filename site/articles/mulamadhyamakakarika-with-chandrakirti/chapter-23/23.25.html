MMK 23.25

yadi = if; abhūtāḥ = nonexistent; sva-bhāvena = by own-nature; kleśāḥ = afflictions; kecit = some; hi = indeed; kasyacit = for anyone; katham = how; nāma = indeed; prahīyeran = would be abandoned; kaḥ = who; asat-bhāvam = nonexistence; prahāsyati = will abandon

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yady abhūtāḥ svabhāvena kleśāḥ kecid dhi kasyacit | kathaṃ nāma prahīyeran ko 'sadbhāvaṃ prahāsyati || 25 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If afflictions were absolutely nonexistent by their own nature, there would be nothing to abandon and no one who could abandon it—just as one cannot remove a coldness that was never present in fire.

Candrakīrti rejects both inherent existence and absolute nonexistence. He closes with scriptural examples of the grasper, object, and act of grasping as unfindable, while ordinary teaching and practice continue through conventional language.
