MMK 23.2

Whatever arises in dependence upon the pleasant, the unpleasant and confusion, (whatever arises in dependence on confusion about the pleasant and unpleasant) they have no own-nature, therefore, afflictions do not really exist (do not exist in themselves).

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

śubhāśubhaviparyāsān saṃbhavanti pratītya ye | te svabhāvān na vidyante tasmāt kleśā na tattvataḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Desire, hatred, and confusion arise in dependence on what is judged pleasant or unpleasant and on mistaken views. If the afflictions existed by their own nature, they would not need to arise through these conditions.

Because they do depend on conditions, they are empty of intrinsic nature and are not ultimately established. Their conventional appearance does not make them self-existing.
