MMK 23.23

evaṃ nirudhyate 'vidyā viparyayanirodhanāt
avidyāyāṃ niruddhāyāṃ saṃskārādyaṃ nirudhyate || 23 ||

In this way ignorance ceases through the cessation of mistaken views. When ignorance has ceased, formations and the rest cease.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ nirudhyate 'vidyā viparyayanirodhanāt | avidyāyāṃ niruddhāyāṃ saṃskārādyaṃ nirudhyate || 23 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Showing the great purpose of this analysis of mistaken views as it is explained above, because it is the cause for the abandonment of ignorance and the rest, he says:

When this yogin, by the reasoning stated above, does not apprehend mistaken views, then, through this non-apprehension of mistaken views, ignorance, which has them as its cause, ceases. And from its cessation, formations and the other dharmas that have ignorance as their cause cease, down to aging and death, grief, lamentation, suffering, unhappiness, and distress.

For ignorance is the cause of the entire mass of afflictions and of suffering beginning with birth. Just as all material sense faculties, which have the body faculty as their cause, cease when the body faculty ceases, so the factors of existence beginning with formations, which arise and proceed having ignorance as their cause, certainly cease when ignorance has ceased. To explain this, he says:

“When ignorance has ceased, formations and the rest cease.”
