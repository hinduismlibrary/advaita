MMK 23.15

yena gṛhṇāti yo grāho grahītā yac ca gṛhyate
upaśāntāni sarvāṇi tasmād grāho na vidyate || 15 ||

That by which one grasps, the grasping, the grasper, and that which is grasped: all are pacified. Therefore grasping does not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yena gṛhṇāti yo grāho grahītā yac ca gṛhyate | upaśāntāni sarvāṇi tasmād grāho na vidyate || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here an opponent says: “Although the mistaken view of permanence in what is impermanent does not occur, at least this grasping exists. Grasping is something that has the nature of an existent thing. It must necessarily have an instrument, an effective means—such as permanence and the like—and it must have an agent, an independent permanent self or mind. It must also have an object, the thing most desired by the agent, such as form and the rest. And if the existences of existent thing, instrument, agent, and action are established, then all that we wish for will be established.”

We reply: this hope is false. As has been explained by the reasoning set out above:

Here, some agent grasps some object, such as form and sound, by some particular means, such as permanence, which functions as an instrument. Just as that is not possible, it was explained earlier. How so? It was shown through “If grasping permanence in what is impermanent is an inversion” and the rest that permanence and the like, as instruments, are not possible. That the grasper too does not exist was shown through “The existence and nonexistence of the self are in no way established.” And that which is grasped also does not exist was shown through “Forms, sounds, tastes, touches, smells, and mental objects alone.” When in this way agent, instrument, and action are not established, whence could there be grasping without a cause? Therefore:

“That by which one grasps, the grasping, the grasper, and that which is grasped are all pacified.” “Pacified” means that all are extinguished because they are not arisen by own-nature. And because they are all pacified in this way:

“Therefore grasping does not exist.”

Or alternatively: through the chapters beginning with the Analysis of Conditions, the non-arising in every way of all instruments, agents, and actions has been shown. Therefore all these are pacified, being devoid of the nature of an existent thing. For this reason grasping does not exist.
