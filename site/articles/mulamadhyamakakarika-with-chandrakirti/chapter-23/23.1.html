MMK 23.1

saṃkalpa = conception; prabhavaḥ = source; rāgaḥ = desire; dveṣaḥ = aversion; mohaḥ = confusion; ca = and; kathyate = is called; śubha = attractive; aśubha = unattractive; viparyāsān = inversions; saṃbhavanti = arise; pratītya = depending on; hi = indeed

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

saṃkalpaprabhavo rāgo dveṣo mohaś ca kathyate | śubhāśubhaviparyāsān saṃbhavanti pratītya hi || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that the continuity of rebirth must exist because its causes—the afflictions—exist. Candrakīrti begins with the three principal afflictions: desire, hatred, and confusion. Desire arises by taking an appearance as pleasant, hatred by taking one as unpleasant, and confusion through distorted conception; all depend on conceptual thought.

The verse’s point is that these afflictions arise in dependence on appearances and mistaken judgments. What depends on such conditions is not established by an intrinsic nature.
