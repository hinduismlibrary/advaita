MMK 23.7

Colour/shape, sound, taste, tactile sensation, smell and dharmas: these six are conceived as the basis of desire, hatred and stupidity.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rūpaśabdarasasparśā gandhā dharmāś ca ṣaḍvidham | vastu rāgasya doṣasya mohasya ca vikalpyate || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent points to six kinds of objects—forms, sounds, smells, tastes, tangible things, and mental objects—as supports for desire, hatred, and confusion. Candrakīrti explains how they are treated as objects: desire follows a pleasing appearance, hatred an unpleasant one, and confusion a projection of permanence or self.

These are objects as conceived and classified by the mind. The verse lists the six fields without claiming that they provide an independent ground for the afflictions.
