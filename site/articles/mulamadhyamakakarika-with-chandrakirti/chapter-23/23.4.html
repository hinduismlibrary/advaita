MMK 23.4

kasya = for whom; cit = anyone; hi = indeed; bhavanti = exist; ime = these; kleśāḥ = afflictions; saḥ = that one; ca = and; na = not; sidhyati = is established; kaścit = anyone; āho = or; vinā = without; kaṃcit = anyone; santi = exist; kleśāḥ = afflictions; na = not; kasyacit = for anyone

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kasya cid dhi bhavantīme kleśāḥ sa ca na sidhyati | kaścid āho vinā kaṃcit santi kleśā na kasyacit || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Afflictions, like a painting that needs a surface, seem to require a basis. That basis might be proposed as a self or as a mind, but neither has been established as an independent foundation.

If afflictions belong to someone, that person must be found; if they exist without anyone, they would be ownerless entities. Candrakīrti finds neither alternative coherent under analysis.
