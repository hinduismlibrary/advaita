MMK 23.14

anitye nityam ity evaṃ yadi grāho viparyayaḥ
anityam ity api grāhaḥ śūnye kiṃ na viparyayaḥ || 14 ||

If grasping permanence in what is impermanent is an inversion, why is grasping “impermanent” in what is empty not an inversion?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anitye nityam ity evaṃ yadi grāho viparyayaḥ | anityam ity api grāhaḥ śūnye kiṃ na viparyayaḥ || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Suppose, then, that because an entity is absent by own-nature, the grasping “impermanent” is not possible either. Why is that not also established as an inversion? To explain this, he says:

When both permanence and impermanence are reversed, there is no third thing, apart from those two, that could be non-inverted. And when what is non-inverted does not exist, with reference to what could there be an inversion? Therefore, by this reasoning too, no inversion exists. And the same is to be applied to the possibility or impossibility of the remaining inversions. For this very reason, the Blessed One said in the Questions of Ārya Dṛḍhāśaya:

“The Blessed One said: ‘What happens, noble youth, to the one who seeks escape by the path? Noble youth, the Tathāgata has not designated the abandonment of passion by abandoning dharmas that are to be desired. In the same way, the Tathāgata has not designated the abandonment of aversion and delusion by abandoning dharmas that are to be hated or that cause delusion. For what reason? Noble youth, the Tathāgatas teach the Dharma neither for relinquishing nor for acquiring any dharma, neither for full knowledge, nor for abandoning, nor for directly realizing, nor for fully understanding, nor for entering into saṃsāra, nor for going to nirvāṇa, nor for casting away, nor for distinguishing. For, noble youth, the nature of the Tathāgatas’ Dharma is not governed by duality. Those who dwell in duality are not rightly engaged; they are to be called wrongly engaged. And what is the duality, noble youth? “I shall abandon passion”—this is a duality. “I shall abandon aversion”—this is a duality. “I shall abandon delusion”—this is a duality. Those who are engaged in this way are not rightly engaged; they are to be understood as wrongly engaged.’”

“For example, noble youth, if a certain man, while a magician’s performance was taking place, were to see a woman created by the magician and produce a mind of passion, then, with his mind overcome by passion, he would rise from his seat and leave, fearing shame before the assembly. Having left, he would direct his mind to that very woman as impure, as impermanent, as suffering, as empty, and as not-self. The Blessed One said: ‘In just this way, noble youth, certain monks, nuns, male lay followers, and female lay followers are to be seen: they direct their minds to unborn, unproduced dharmas as impure, as impermanent, as suffering, and as not-self. I do not say that those deluded people cultivate the path. They are to be understood as wrongly engaged.’”

“For example, noble youth, if a certain man, asleep, were to see in a dream within his own house the king’s wife, and were to arrange a bed with her, then, because his memory was confused, he would think, ‘I have transgressed.’ Frightened and terrified, he would flee, thinking, ‘May the king not pierce me; may he not deprive me of life.’ What do you think, noble youth? Would that man, frightened and terrified as he fled, be freed from the fear arising from the king’s wife?” He said: “No, Blessed One.” “For what reason?” “For, Blessed One, that man produced the recognition of a woman where there was no woman, and imagined what was unreal.” The Blessed One said: “In just this way, noble youth, certain monks, nuns, male lay followers, and female lay followers are to be seen: having produced the recognition of passion where there is no passion, they are frightened by the fear of passion and seek escape from passion. In the same way, having produced the recognition of aversion where there is no aversion, they are frightened by the fear of aversion and seek escape from aversion. Having produced the recognition of delusion where there is no delusion, they are frightened by the fear of delusion and seek escape from delusion. I do not say that those deluded people cultivate the path. They are to be understood as wrongly engaged.’”

“For example, noble youth, if that man were to produce the recognition of fear in what is fearless through a false imputation, in just this way all childish ordinary people, not understanding the boundary of passion and the boundary of dispassion, frightened by the boundary of passion, seek escape in the boundary of dispassion; not understanding the boundary of aversion and the boundary of nothingness, frightened by the boundary of aversion, seek escape in the boundary of nothingness; not understanding the boundary of delusion and the boundary of emptiness, frightened by the boundary of delusion, seek escape in the boundary of emptiness. I do not say, noble youth, that those deluded people cultivate the path. They are to be understood as wrongly engaged.” And so forth at length.
