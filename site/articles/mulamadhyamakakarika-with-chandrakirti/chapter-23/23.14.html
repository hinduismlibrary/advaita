MMK 23.14

anitye nityam ity evaṃ yadi grāho viparyayaḥ
anityam ity api grāhaḥ śūnye kiṃ na viparyayaḥ || 14 ||

If taking the impermanent to be permanent is a distortion, why would taking the impermanent to be impermanent not also be a distortion, when things are empty?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anitye nityam ity evaṃ yadi grāho viparyayaḥ | anityam ity api grāhaḥ śūnye kiṃ na viparyayaḥ || 14 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

An opponent asks whether the perception of impermanence must also count as an error, since impermanence is no more intrinsically established than permanence. Candrakīrti says no third, self-existing position can be found between the two extremes.

Once permanence and impermanence are not fixed realities, neither can be used as an ultimate standard to define an intrinsically real error. This does not make ordinary discernment useless; it removes the demand for an independent basis.
