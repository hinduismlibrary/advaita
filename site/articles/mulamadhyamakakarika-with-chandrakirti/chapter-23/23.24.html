MMK 23.24

yadi bhūtāḥ svabhāvena kleśāḥ kecid dhi kasyacit
kathaṃ nāma prahīyeran kaḥ svabhāvaṃ prahāsyati || 24 ||

If some afflictions were real by own-nature for someone, how could they be abandoned? Who would abandon own-nature?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi bhūtāḥ svabhāvena kleśāḥ kecid dhi kasyacit | kathaṃ nāma prahīyeran kaḥ svabhāvaṃ prahāsyati || 24 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further, if the abandonment of afflictions is imagined, it must be either of afflictions that exist in reality or of afflictions that do not exist. What follows from this? If the abandonment of afflictions that exist by their own form is accepted, that is not reasonable. For what reason? Because:

If some afflictions were real by own-nature for someone, how could they be abandoned? Who would abandon own-nature?

The own-nature of things that exist by own-nature cannot be made to disappear. For the nature of earth and the rest, such as hardness, does not disappear. In the same way, if these afflictions existed by own-nature—“some” means ignorance and the rest; “for someone” means for a person—how could they be abandoned? They could never be abandoned by anyone in any way. Why are they not abandoned? He says: “Who would abandon own-nature?” The meaning is: because own-nature cannot be made to disappear, just as it is not possible to make the obstruction of space disappear.
