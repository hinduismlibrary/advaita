MMK 23.22

nātmā ca śuci nityaṃ ca sukhaṃ ca yadi vidyate
anātmā 'śucy anityaṃ ca naiva duḥkhaṃ ca vidyate || 22 ||

If self, purity, permanence, and happiness do not exist, then not-self, impurity, impermanence, and suffering do not exist either.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nātmā ca śuci nityaṃ ca sukhaṃ ca yadi vidyate | anātmā 'śucy anityaṃ ca naiva duḥkhaṃ ca vidyate || 22 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Suppose they do not exist. Then, because these [four] are nonexistent, not only are they not mistaken views; he also explains that, because their opposites are absent, the existence of not-self and the rest—the non-mistaken views—is absent too. He says:

If you think that self, purity, permanence, and happiness do not exist, because mistaken views are not possible, then, since self and the rest are absent, if not-self and the rest are grasped as non-mistaken views, those too should be abandoned. When what is to be denied does not exist, there is no denial. And when not-self and the rest are not possible, since they too are nonexistent in their own form, like self and the rest, how would they not also be mistaken views? Therefore those seeking liberation from the bonds of the prison-house of saṃsāra—birth, aging, and death—should abandon all eight of these mistaken views.
