MMK 23.17

na cāpi viparītasya saṃbhavanti viparyayāḥ
na cāpy aviparītasya saṃbhavanti viparyayāḥ || 17 ||

Mistaken views do not occur in one who is mistaken; mistaken views do not occur in one who is not mistaken.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na cāpi viparītasya saṃbhavanti viparyayāḥ | na cāpy aviparītasya saṃbhavanti viparyayāḥ || 17 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further, if these mistaken views are accepted as belonging to someone, would they be conceived in one who is mistaken, in one who is not mistaken, or in one who is becoming mistaken? In every way they are not reasonable. To explain this, he says:

The mistaken views do not occur in one who is mistaken. Why? Because one who is mistaken is already mistaken. What further connection with mistaken views could be made for him? It would be purposeless. Mistaken views are not reasonable in one who is not mistaken either, because it would follow that even Buddhas, whose eyes of understanding have awakened and from whom the darkness of the sleep of ignorance has ceased, are subject to mistaken views.

Mistaken views do not occur in one who is becoming mistaken either, because no entity that is becoming mistaken exists. What other entity, free from being mistaken and from not being mistaken, would be called “becoming mistaken”? Suppose one says, “One who is half mistaken is becoming mistaken.” But “half mistaken” means one for whom something is mistaken and something is not mistaken. There, the mistaken view does not make that part of him which is mistaken mistaken, because it is already mistaken; and the mistaken view does not make that part which is not mistaken mistaken, because it is not mistaken. Therefore mistaken views do not occur in anyone who is becoming mistaken either.

And when, in this way, one who is mistaken, one who is not mistaken, and one who is becoming mistaken are not possible, then, standing impartial with wisdom, examine for yourself: for whom do mistaken views occur? Thus, because there is no support, mistaken views do not exist.
