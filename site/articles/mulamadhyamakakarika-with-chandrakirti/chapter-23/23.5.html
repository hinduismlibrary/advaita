MMK 23.5

svakāyadṛṣṭivat kleśāḥ kliṣṭe santi na pañcadhā
svakāyadṛṣṭivat kliṣṭaṃ kleśeṣv api na pañcadhā || 5 ||

As with the view of one’s own body, afflictions are not found in the afflicted in five ways. As with the view of one’s own body, the afflicted is not found in the afflictions in five ways.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

svakāyadṛṣṭivat kleśāḥ kliṣṭe santi na pañcadhā | svakāyadṛṣṭivat kliṣṭaṃ kleśeṣv api na pañcadhā || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here an opponent says: “We do not accept that any support of the afflictions was established beforehand. And there is no self at all that could be set out as their support, since it would be causeless, like a mango tree in the sky. Rather, the afflictions arise in dependence on an afflicted mind, and that mind arises together with the afflictions.” To say that this too is not reasonable, he says:

“Like the view of one’s own body, afflictions are not found in the afflicted in five ways; like the view of one’s own body, the afflicted is not found in the afflictions in five ways.”

“One’s own body” is a collection characterized by form and the rest. “The view of one’s own body” is the view of self in one’s own body, proceeding by grasping the appearance of what belongs to oneself. Just as this [view], when examined in five ways, is not possible in one’s own body, as in the statement, “The aggregates are not [the Tathāgata], nor is he other than the aggregates; the aggregates are not in him, nor is he in them; the Tathāgata does not possess the aggregates. Which one here is the Tathāgata?”—in the same way, the afflictions, when examined in five ways, are not possible in the afflicted.

There, the afflictions are so called because they afflict; “the afflicted” is that which is afflicted. It is not reasonable that precisely what is afflicted is the afflictions, because it would follow that the burner and the thing burned are one. Nor is it reasonable that one thing is afflicted and the afflictions are another, because the one would be independent of the other, and it would follow that afflictions have a non-afflicted cause. For this very reason, because identity and difference are absent, and because the positions of support, supported, and possession are absent, the afflicted is not in the afflictions, the afflictions are not in the afflicted, and the afflicted is not one who possesses afflictions. Thus, when the afflictions are examined in five ways in the afflicted, they are not possible.

Just as afflictions having the afflicted as their cause are not possible, so too the afflicted, which has the afflictions as its cause, is not possible when examined in five ways in relation to the afflictions. For the afflictions themselves are not the afflicted, since it would follow that agent and action are one; the afflictions are not other and the afflicted another, since it would follow that they are independent; nor are the afflictions in the afflicted, nor the afflicted in the afflictions, nor are the afflictions possessors of the afflicted. Thus, just as the view of one’s own body is not present in one’s own body in five ways, so the afflicted too is not present in the afflictions in five ways. And because this is so, the afflictions and the unafflicted are not established even through dependence upon one another.
