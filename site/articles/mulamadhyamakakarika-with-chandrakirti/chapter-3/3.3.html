MMK 3.3

na = not; paryāptaḥ = sufficient; agni-dṛṣṭāntaḥ = fire example; darśanasya = for seeing; prasiddhaye = establishment; sa-darśanaḥ = together with seeing; saḥ = it; pratyuktaḥ = answered; gamyamāna-gata-āgataiḥ = by being-gone-over, gone, and not-gone

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na paryāpto 'gnidṛṣṭānto darśanasya prasiddhaye | sadarśanaḥ sa pratyukto gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent objects that seeing may fail to see itself yet still see other things, just as fire burns other things without burning itself. Candrakīrti says the fire example is inadequate. The same analysis used for going applies to burning and seeing: what has already been burned, what has not been burned, and what is being burned cannot establish a separate act of burning; likewise, what has been seen, not seen, or is being seen cannot establish seeing.

The example also fails because the relation between fire and burning is no clearer than the relation between a goer and going. Candrakīrti directs the reader to apply the same reasoning: a burner does not burn, just as a goer does not go; and seeing does not become established merely by invoking fire. The point follows: just as it does not see itself, seeing cannot be shown to see anything else.
