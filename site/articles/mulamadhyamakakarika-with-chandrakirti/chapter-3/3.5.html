MMK 3.5

paśyati darśanaṃ naiva naiva paśyatyadarśanam
vyākhyāto darśanenaiva draṣṭā cāpyupagamyatām || 5 ||

Seeing does not see what is seen; nonseeing does not see it either. Through the analysis of seeing, the seer too is explained.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paśyati darśanaṃ naiva naiva paśyatyadarśanam | vyākhyāto darśanenaiva draṣṭā cāpyupagamyatām || 5 ||

Commentary note on Candrakīrti

Candrakīrti asks whether the eye is related to an act of seeing as something whose nature is seeing, or as something whose nature is not seeing. If the eye already has the nature of seeing, joining it to a further act of seeing entails two acts of seeing and two faculties. If it has no such nature, it cannot see—just as a fingertip cannot see. Thus neither “seeing” nor “non-seeing” establishes the claim that seeing sees.

The repeated question is: if seeing does not see, and non-seeing does not see, how can it be coherent to say “seeing sees”? The verse’s second point is that the seer is to be examined by the same reasoning as seeing itself.

Some argue that a mere phenomenon arises without activity or agency and that “seeing does not see” only means there is no action. Candrakīrti replies that if activity were absent even in everyday convention, the supposed phenomenon would not be established either, like a sky-flower. At the level of conventional truth, activity is accepted along with phenomena. Under ultimate analysis, however, neither activity nor the phenomenon is established. He cites the *Śatakasāstra*: the permanent has no activity, and the all-pervasive has no activity; a wholly inactive thing is no more real than a non-entity—so why would selflessness not be acceptable?

The opponent reframes seeing as an instrument: it is not the agent who sees, but “that by means of which one sees.” The one who sees by this instrument is the seer—consciousness or a self—and, since the agent exists, the instrument of seeing must exist too. Candrakīrti replies that the seer is refuted by the very analysis already given for seeing; the next verses apply that analysis to the seer.
