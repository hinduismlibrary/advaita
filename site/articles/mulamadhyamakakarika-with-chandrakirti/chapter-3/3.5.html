MMK 3.5

paśyati darśanaṃ naiva naiva paśyatyadarśanam
vyākhyāto darśanenaiva draṣṭā cāpyupagamyatām || 5 ||

Seeing does not see; non-seeing does not see. The seer, too, is to be understood as explained by seeing alone.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paśyati darśanaṃ naiva naiva paśyatyadarśanam | vyākhyāto darśanenaiva draṣṭā cāpyupagamyatām || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Further: when it is said, “Seeing sees,” a connection might be conceived either between the act of seeing and the eye whose nature is seeing, or between the act of seeing and the eye whose nature is non-seeing. In neither way is it reasonable, he says:

“Seeing does not see; non-seeing does not see.”

For the eye whose nature is seeing and which is connected with the act of seeing, a further connection with “sees” is not reasonable, because it would entail two acts of seeing and two seeings. Non-seeing also does not see, because it is without the act of seeing, like a fingertip. When seeing does not see and non-seeing does not see, then:

“How is it reasonable to say, ‘Seeing sees’?”

Some, however, think: “This mere entity, arising, arises without activity. Since there is no action, no one sees an object. Thus it is established that seeing does not see.” To this we reply: If activity, which is a component of worldly convention, did not exist, then a mere entity would not exist either, because it would be devoid of activity, like a sky-flower. How, then, could there be a mere entity devoid of activity? Therefore, if you accept a mere entity as conventional truth, accept activity as well. If you are examining reality, then accept that a mere entity, like activity, does not exist. As it is said in the Śataka:

“One who acts is not eternal; there is no action in what is all-pervading. What is inactive is not nonexistent, nor equal to nonexistence. Why is selflessness not dear to you?”

Therefore this rule neither refutes the opponent nor incurs for us the fault of proving what is already established.

Here someone says: “‘Seeing’ is not accepted as an agent-derivation meaning ‘it sees’; rather, ‘seeing’ is an instrument-derivation meaning ‘by this one sees,’ and therefore the stated faults do not follow. The one who sees by this seeing as an instrument is the seer; and this exists as consciousness or self. Since an agent exists, seeing too is established.” In reply:

“The seer, too, is to be understood as explained by seeing alone.”
