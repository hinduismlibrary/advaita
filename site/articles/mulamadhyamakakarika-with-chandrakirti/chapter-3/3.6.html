MMK 3.6

Without letting go of [seeing] a seer does not exist; in letting go of seeing, there is also [no seer]. If there is no seer, where can there be what-is-seen and seeing?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tiraskṛtya draṣṭā nāstyatiraskṛtya ca darśanam | draṣṭavyaṃ darśanaṃ caiva draṣṭaryasati te kutaḥ || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Just as seeing does not see itself, the seer does not see itself through seeing. If the seer cannot see itself, how could it see others? Thus the seer is not established any more than seeing is.

An opponent says the seer exists because it has an instrument (seeing) and an object (what is seen), as a cutter exists with a tool and something cut. Candrakīrti answers that seeing and what is seen depend on a seer. But if the seer is examined, it cannot be found either with seeing or apart from seeing. If the seer is not established, what could establish seeing and what is seen? Without a seer, these two are groundless; so they cannot in turn prove the seer.
