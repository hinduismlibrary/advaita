Traditional Sarasvatī Vidyārambha prayer

Show Sanskrit
सरस्वति नमस्तुभ्यं वरदे कामरूपिणि ।
विद्यारम्भं करिष्यामि सिद्धिर्भवतु मे सदा ॥

O Goddess Sarasvatī, I bow to you. You are the giver of boons and the fulfiller of wishes. As I begin my studies, please bless me so that I may attain success always.

Before turning to varṇa in Sanātana Dharma, this inquiry begins with the means by which claims may be established in Hindu thought. It considers scriptural and relevant external evidence without assuming the conclusion.

Bhagavad Gītā 16.23–24

Show Sanskrit
यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः । न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥ तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ । ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥

The Supreme Lord said, “He who, discarding scriptural procedure, acts according to his own wishes does not attain success, happiness, or the highest goal. Scripture is therefore your basis for determining what should and should not be done. Having understood the scriptural injunction, perform your duty in this world.”

One should explain the Vedas with the Itihāsas and Purāṇas. The Vedas fear that a person of little learning will misinterpret them.

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā 232

Show Sanskrit

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Yoga Vāsiṣṭha 2.18.3

Show Sanskrit
युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि। अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना॥ ३॥

A reasonable statement should be accepted even when spoken by a child. An unreasonable statement should be discarded like a piece of straw, even if it was spoken by lotus-born Brahmā himself.

Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2

Show Sanskrit
यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि। नो इतराणि॥ यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि। नो इतराणि॥

Only those actions of ours that are unobjectionable should be followed, not the others. Only those good deeds of ours should be emulated, not the others

Show Remark 1

Thus, with the support of pramāṇas as well as sound reasoning one may conclude that the consensus amongst ācāryas is no proof of a scriptural stance. The statements, conduct and conclusions of all ācāryas are to be subjected to

  1. Logic and reasoning
  2. Śāstra

Objection. But ācāryas dedicate many years to studying the Śāstras, and follow a strict paramparā. Hence the consensus of the paramparās is to be taken with confidence as one should interpret the scripture with the stance of an ācārya based on the fact that an ācārya is entirely based on the scripture.

Reply. This is not so for many issues. If consensus / agreement of the currently existing paramparās is to be taken as a valid stance in its own regard we reject this for many gurus have gone against the Paramparic consensus to establish their sampradaya. Śaṅkarācārya, Rāmānuja, Madhva etc. This objection will also be in conflict with the previously established pramāṇas that one should verify an ācāryas claim using the scriptures, THERE IS NO STATEMENT anywhere saying the consensus of ācāryas on a topic pertains to an established fact on that topic.

Objection. This is in regards to theological issues which we take the ācāryas to be infallible on, and not philosophical

Reply. This is not so, we reject this for the very obvious reason that Varṇa has direct philosophical implications meaning it is to be addressed by the scope of philosophy as well. This is because the only way to judge if a theological claim is consistent with other theological claims is through some metaphysical or logical laws. Also, theological claims that directly entail certain behaviours and actions in society crosses directly over into ethics, meta-ethics and epistemology. It is impossible to imagine a theological claim entirely devoid of interaction with philosophy or empirical data and is relevant to day to day life. Another obvious reason to reject the current statement is the basic fact that ācāryas conflict on matters of theology (and philosophy) all the time, including varṇa.

Show Remark 2

The notion that one needs a paramparā to interpret scripture also may fall into a circular chain of reasoning

  1. Our sect is authoritative because the śāstras support it
  2. The guru paramparā tells us how the śāstra is to be interpreted, including the passages establishing its own authority

Śāstra validates a paramparā → Paramparā determines how to interpret śāstra → The prior interpretation validates the paramparā

The statements stated above are no actual issue in and of themselves as no system can prove its own fundamental foundations without some pre-existing assumptions within the particular system.

Hence it is proven that any notion of an infallible parmapara is simply a faith based claim from which their system is developed but there is no reason for any one else to accept this. Hence we should validate any claim using the maxim:

“One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.”
