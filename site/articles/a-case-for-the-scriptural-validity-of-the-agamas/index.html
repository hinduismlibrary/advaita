Introduction

Purpose and Method

The Vedas are recognized as Śruti, the highest scriptural authority. The Āgamas have sometimes been rejected as non-Vedic, delusory, or deceitful on the basis of isolated passages.

This paper argues for the authority of the Āgamas across Hindu traditions.

It begins with Śruti, then considers evidence from Itihāsa, Purāṇa, and Smṛti. It follows the Mīmāṃsā principle of samanvaya, or reconciliation, when addressing apparent contradictions.

Scriptural Authority and the Scope of Revelation

Expansion of Valid Authority Beyond the Four Vedas

First, let us examine the claim that the only valid scripture for Hindus is the Vedas. This premise is proven incorrect by the Vedas themselves.

Extension to Smṛti

In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā of the Yajurveda (2.2.10.2), Manusmṛti is explicitly referenced:

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 2.2.10.2

यद् वै मनुर् अवदत् तद् भेषजम्

Whatever Manu said is medicine.

Because the authority and validity of the Manusmṛti is established directly by the Veda, anyone rejecting the Manusmṛti must simultaneously reject the validity of the Veda itself. This is how the category of Śāstra (scripture) is extended to authoritative texts outside of Śruti.

Extension to Itihāsa and Purāṇa

In the Chandogya Upaniṣad (7.1.4), it is stated:

Chandogya Upaniṣad 7.1.4

नाम वा ऋग्वेदो यजुर्वेदः सामवेद आथर्वणश् चतुर्थ इतिहास-पुराणः पञ्चमो वेदानां वेदः पित्र्यो राशिर् दैवो निधिर् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्या ब्रह्मविद्या भूतविद्या क्षत्रविद्या नक्षत्रविद्या सर्पदेवजनविद्या नामैवैतन्नामोपास्स्वेति ॥ ७.१.४ ॥

Name indeed is the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, the Atharvaveda as the fourth, the Itihāsa-Purāṇa as the fifth among the Vedas...

Therefore, the category of the Veda-and Śruti-level authority by extension-includes the Itihāsas (such as the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and Śiva Rahasya Itihāsa) as well as the 36 Purāṇas codified by Vedavyasa (consisting of the 18 Maha-Purāṇas and 18 Upa-Purāṇas).

Validation of Smṛtis through Itihāsa and Purāṇa The validity of the Smṛtis is further established across multiple Purāṇas and Itihāsas. For instance, the Mahābhārata (Book 14, Ashvamedhika Parva, Chapter 96) enumerates an extensive lineage of authoritative Smṛtis:

  • Manu, Vāsiṣṭha, Kashyapa, Gargya, Gautama, and Gopalaka: Codes of law authored by these ancient sages.
  • Parashara, Maitreya, Umā, Maheśvara, and Nandi: Ancient teachings, including the holy doctrines of Umā and Maheśvara and the purifying laws of Nandi.
  • Brahma, Sanatkumāra (Kaumāra), Dhumāyana, and Kāṇḍa-Vaishvanara: Divine laws and teachings spoken by Brahma and other exalted figures.
  • Bhargava, Yājñavalkya, Markandeya, Bharadvaja, and Brhaspati: Authoritative law codes composed by these prominent rishis.
  • Kuni, Kunibahu, Viśvāmitra, Sumāntu, Jaimini, and Shakuneya: Dharmic codes authored across various sage traditions.
  • Pulastya, Pulaha, Agni (Pavakiya), Agastya, Mudgala, Shandilya, and Shalabhayana: Sacred laws sung by primary sages and divine figures.
  • Valakhilyas, Saptarshis (Seven Sages), Āpastamba, Śaṅkha, and Likhita: Essential legal and moral frameworks codified by these revered groups and seers.
  • Prajapati, Yama, Mahendra, Vyaghra, Vyāsa, and Vibhandaka: Traditions handed down through divine and patriarchal lineages.
  • Narada, Kapota, Vidura, Bhrigu, and Angiras: Wise sayings, dharmic traditions, and foundational moral codes.
  • Kraunca, Mrdanga, Surya (Solar Traditions), Harita, Pishanga, Katapaya, and Suvalaka: Ancient sung traditions and specialized legal lineages.
  • Uddalaka, Ushanas (Shukrācārya), and Vaishampayana: Laws and teachings passed down through major classical lineages.

Furthermore, the Parashara Smṛti-one of the Smṛtis explicitly mentioned in the Mahābhārata-enumerates additional canonical texts in verses 1.1.12-1.1.15:

Parāśara Smṛti 1.1.12-1.1.15; Mahābhārata 1.1.12-1.1.15

धर्मं कथय मे तात अनुग्राह्यो ह्यहं तव ।
श्रुता मे मानवा धर्मा वासिष्ठा काश्यपास्तथा ॥ १.१२ ॥
गर्गेया गौतमीयाश्च तथा चोशनसा स्मृताः ।
अत्रेर्विष्णोश्च संवर्ताद्दक्षादङ्गिरसस्तथा ॥ १.१३ ॥
शातातपाच्च हारीताद्याज्ञवल्क्यात्तथैव च ।
आपस्तम्बकृता धर्मा शङ्खस्य लिखितस्य च ॥ १.१४ ॥
कात्यायनकृताश्चैव तथा प्राचेतसान्मुनेः ।
श्रुता ह्येते भवत्प्रोक्ताः श्रुत्यर्था मे न विस्मृताः ॥ १.१५ ॥

O Father, please tell me about Dharma (righteousness/duty), for I deserve your grace. I have already heard of the codes of law (dharmas) authored by Manu, Vasiṣṭha, and Kashyapa;

...As well as those remembered from Gargya, Gautama, Ushanas (Śukrācārya), Atri, Viṣṇu, Samvarta, Daksha, and Angiras;

...And those proclaimed by Shatatapa, Harita, Yājñavalkya, Āpastamba, Śaṅkha, and Likhita;

...As well as those composed by Kātyāyana and the sage Prachetas. I have heard all these as taught by you, and the teachings of the Vedic texts have not been forgotten by me.

Because several of these Smṛtis cross-reference even more text lineages, this canonical list is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

In the Annapurna Upaniṣad (Chapter 3, Verse 21)-which is Śruti, belongs to the Atharvaveda, and is part of the Muktika Canon of 108 Upaniṣads-the Śaiva Āgamas are not subordinated, but are explicitly identified as the ultimate conclusion of all Śāstras, including the Vedas themselves:

Annapurna Upaniṣad, Chapter 3, Verse 21

शिवः शैवागमस्थाना कालः कालैकवादिनाम् । यत्सर्वशास्त्रसिद्धान्तं यत्सर्वहृदयानुगमम् ॥ २१ ॥

What Śiva is to the followers of the Śaiva Āgamas, and as Time to those who uphold Time as the ultimate truth. It is the ultimate conclusion of all scriptures and that which resonates within every heart.

Furthermore, the Chandogya Upaniṣad (7.1.2) references the Āgamas under another traditional designation, Ekayana:

ऋग्वेदं भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदम् आथर्वणं चतुर्थम् इतिहास-पुराणं पञ्चमं वेदानां वेदं पित्र्यं राशिं दैवं निधिम् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्यां ब्रह्मविद्यां भूतविद्यां क्षत्रविद्यां नक्षत्रविद्यां सर्पदेवजनविद्याम् एतद् भगवोऽध्येमि ॥ ७.१.२ ॥

In his work Āgama Pramanya, Yamunācārya explicitly identifies Ekayana as referring to the Kashmir-Āgamas, affirming them as apaurusheya (authorless/eternal) and treating them as Śruti when examining this verse:

Āgama Pramanya

एकायनशाखाया अपौरुषेयत्वं तथा काश्मीरागमप्रामाण्ये प्रपञ्चितम् इति नेह प्रस्तूयते.

...The eternality/authoritativeness of the Ekayana Shakha has been detailed by me in [the work] Kashmira-Āgama-Pramanya, so it is not discussed here.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Ekayana Shakha only refers to Vaiṣṇava or Pancaratra works, not other Āgamas.

Refutation and Validation: The Āgamas are explicitly acknowledged and validated by Vedavyasa himself in the Mahābhārata (Shanti Parva, Mokshadharma Parva, Chapter 349, Verse 64). Vyāsa asserts that speculative reasoning (hetu) cannot be used to invalidate either the Pāśupata or Pancaratra texts along with the Vedas, as they possess intrinsic validity (atmapramana):

Mahābhārata, Shanti Parva, Mokshadharma Parva, Chapter 349, Verse 64

साङ्ख्यं योगः पाञ्चरात्रं वेदाः पाशुपतं तथा । आत्मप्रमाणान्य् एतानि न हन्तव्यानि हेतुभिः ॥

Sankhya, Yoga, Pancaratra, the Vedas, and the Pāśupata-these are self-evident authorities (atmapramana) and should not be invalidated by logical arguments.

Furthermore, prominent Ācāryas within Yamunācārya's own Shri Vaiṣṇava lineage confirm that the scope of authoritative Āgamas extends beyond Vaiṣṇava texts. Vedānta Deshika, in his Pancaratra Raksha, explicitly accepts the validity of the Pāśupata Āgamas while commenting on the aforementioned Mahābhārata verse:

Vedānta Deśika, Pāñcarātra Rakṣā (on Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 349.64)

अत एव भगवतापि शिवादिरूपान्तरैर् यथार्हाधिकारिफलविशेषार्थिभ्यः तदुपदेशाः कृताः.

Accordingly, the Lord (as Śiva) revealed the Sastra for the sake of those who sought particular fruits (phala-vishesharthibhyah.) And in accordance with their respective fitness.

Vedānta Deśika, Pāñcarātra Rakṣā (on Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 349.64)

ततश् च साङ्ख्यादीनां तत्र तत्र पृथगुपदिष्टफलविशेषप्राप्तिपरतया प्रामाण्यम् इति सिद्धम्.

...It follows, therefore, that the Sankhya and other systems are valid for those who seek the particular results (phala-visheshah.) Mentioned in them.

To reinforce this point, Vedānta Deshika cites Lord Śiva directly from the Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā (12.4-5):

Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā 12.4-5

साङ्ख्यं योगं तथैवान्यच्छास्त्रं यत् परमं तपः । विस्तरेण हि तस्योक्तं मयैव विदुरेति वै ॥

Sankhya, Yoga, and other systems which are supreme austerity - these were spoken at length by Me alone, O Vidura.

Thus, non-Vaiṣṇava Āgamas are explicitly acknowledged by Shri Vaiṣṇava Ācāryas as part of the broader Agamic tradition identified with the Ekayana Shakha.

In the Śiva Purāṇa (Kailasa Saṃhitā, Chapter 21, Verses 9-10), the Śaiva Āgamas are explicitly prescribed by Lord Śiva through a Vidhi-Vakya (injunction) for aspirants seeking spiritual realization:

Śiva Purāṇa, Kailāsa Saṃhitā 21.9

अधैर्यचित्तो यः कश्चित् समाधिं न च विन्दति । तदुपायं प्रवक्ष्यामि सावधानतया शृणु ॥ ९ ॥

If there is such a person devoid of concentration who does not attain Samadhi, I shall mention the means of remedy for him. Listen attentively.

Śiva Purāṇa, Kailāsa Saṃhitā 21.10

त्रिपदार्थपरिज्ञानं वेदान्तागमवाक्यजम् । श्रुत्वा गुरोर्मुखाद् योगं अभ्यसेत् स यमादिकम् ॥ १० ॥

On hearing from his preceptor the lore of Vedānta and Āgamas along with the explanation, he shall practise Yoga by means of Yama, Niyama, etc.

Even if one attempts to dismiss references to the Śaiva Āgamas within the Śiva Purāṇa under the pretext of sectarian categorization, the Padma Purāṇa (Bhumi-Khanda, Chapter 69, Verse 1) independently designates the Shivadharma Āgamas as Uttama (supreme/excellent):

Śiva Purāṇa, Bhumi-Khanda, Chapter 69, Verse 1; Padma Purāṇa, Bhumi-Khanda, Chapter 69, Verse 1

अथ धर्माः शिवेनोक्ताः शिवधर्मागमोत्तमाः । ज्ञेया बहुविधास् ते च कर्मयोगप्रभेदतः ॥ हिंसादिदोषनिर्मुक्ताः क्लेशायासविवर्जिताः । सर्वभूतहिताः शुद्धाः सूक्ष्मायासा महत्फलाः ॥ १ ॥

Now, the supreme righteous duties (dharmas) spoken by Lord Śiva in the excellent Shivadharma Āgamas are to be known in their many variations according to the divisions of Karma Yoga. They are free from faults such as violence (hisa), devoid of affliction and extreme exertion, beneficial to all living beings, pure, requiring subtle effort, yet yielding immense fruits.

In the Skanda Purāṇa (Suta Saṃhitā, Yajña-vaibhava Khanda, Chapter 22), the Bhairava, Śaiva, and Pāśupata Āgamas-along with the Vaiṣṇava Āgamas (referring to the Pancaratra Āgamas) and even the Jain and Bauddha Āgamas-are declared to possess validity (pramanya) as principal Āgamas:

Recognition of the Āgamas across Itihāsa and Purāṇa

Skanda Purāṇa, Sūta Saṃhitā, Yajñavaibhava Khaṇḍa 22

अथातः सम्प्रवक्ष्यामि मार्गप्रामाण्यनिर्णयम् । श्रद्धया सहिता यूयं शृणुध्व मुनिसत्तमाः ॥ वेदाश् च धर्मशास्त्राणि पुराण-भारतं तथा । वेदाङ्गाद्युपवेदाश् च कामिकाद्यागमान् अपि ॥ कापालं नाकुलम् चैव तथोपादानं द्विजर्षभाः । तथा पाशुपतं सौम्यं भैरवप्रमुखान् आगमान् ॥ तेषाम् एवोपभेदाश् च शतशोऽथ सहस्रशः । विष्ण्वागमांस् तथा बाह्यान् बुद्धार्हदागमान् अपि ॥ लोकायतं तर्कशास्त्रं बहुविस्तारसयुतम् । मीमांसामतिगम्भीराः साङ्ख्ययोगौ तथैव च ॥... तान्येव विस्तरेणैव सङ्ग्रहेण वा पुनः । कुर्वन्ति तानि नामानि कथितानि महर्षिभिः । अधिकारिविभेदेन नैकरूपा हि सदा द्विजाः । तर्कैरेते हि मार्गास्तु न हन्तव्या मनीषिभिः ॥

Now I shall explain the decision regarding the authoritativeness of the paths (marga). O best of sages, listen with faith. The Vedas, Dharma-śāstras, Purāṇas, the Bharata (Mahābhārata), the Vedāṅgas, Upavedas, the Kamika and other Āgamas, the Kapala, Nakula, Upadana, the Pāśupata, Saumya, and Bhairava-these major Āgamas, along with their sub-sects by the hundreds and thousands; the Vaiṣṇava Āgamas, and even the external Buddhist and Jaina Āgamas, the Lokayata system, Logic (Tarka-śāstra), the deep Mīmāṃsā, and Sankhya and Yoga-the great sages have given names to these paths and taught them in detail or in brief according to the varying eligibility (adhikāra) of seekers. These paths should not be invalidated by wise men through logical arguments.

Specific emphasis is laid on the principle that each of these Agamic systems is articulated according to the varying adhikāra (competence/qualification) of seekers. This framework of Adhikāra-Bheda is central to addressing passages in certain Purāṇas that designate particular Āgamas as mohaka or delusory.

Purva Paksha Objection: This verse from the Skanda Purāṇa is merely Arthavada (praise/exaggeration) and cannot be taken literally.

Refutation: The notion that statements within the Purāṇas can be casually dismissed as Arthavada is explicitly rejected in scripture. The Narada Purāṇa (Purva-Khanda, Chapter 1, Verses 57-58) strictly prohibits reading Arthavada into the Purāṇas:

Nārada Purāṇa, Purva-Khanda, Chapter 1, Verses 57-58

पुराणेष्व् अर्थवादत्वं ये वदन्ति नराधमाः ॥ १.५७ ॥ तैर् अर्जितानि पुण्यानि क्षयं यान्ति द्विजोत्तमाः । समस्तकर्मनिर्मूलसाधनानि नराधमाः ॥ १.५८ ॥ पुराणान्य् अर्थवादेन ब्रुवन्न् अरकम् अश्नुते ॥

Those lowest among men who claim that the Purāṇas contain Arthavada-all their accumulated merit and virtue are destroyed, O best of dvijas. By declaring the Purāṇas to be Arthavada, such wretched men go to hell.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Skanda Purāṇa is classified as a Tāmasika Purāṇa (e.g., in the Padma Purāṇa) and its statements are therefore non-authoritative.

Refutation: This line of objection creates a mutual contradiction. While the Padma Purāṇa classifies the Skanda Purāṇa as Tāmasika, the Skanda Purāṇa itself (Śaṅkara Saṃhitā, Daksha Khanda) conversely classifies the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas-including the Padma Purāṇa-as Tāmasika:

Padma Purāṇa; Skanda Purāṇa

वैष्णवानि च चत्वारि तामसानि मनीश्वराः.

O foremost of sages. The four Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas are considered Tamasic. In them, the duties of the Kṣatriyas (warrior class) are heard, and Hari (Viṣṇu) is their presiding deity.

Relying on one text to invalidate another when both make reciprocal claims results in Atmashraya (circular reasoning/self-dependence), which is an unacceptable defect in classical hermeneutics. According to Purva Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics, scriptures must be interpreted through inherent authority rather than selective rejection. As stated in the Mīmāṃsā Sūtras (1.1.5):

Mīmāṃsā Sūtras 1.1.5

औत्पत्तिकस् तु शब्दस्यार्थेन सम्बन्धः । तस्य ज्ञानम् उपदेशो ऽव्यतिरेकश् चार्थे ऽनुपलब्धे । तत्प्रमाणं बादरायणस्यानपेक्षत्वात् ॥ ५ ॥

On the other hand, the relation of the word with its meaning is inborn and eternal; consequently, injunction is the means of knowing dharma, and it is unfailing in regard to objects not perceived by other means of knowledge. It is authoritative, according to Badarayana, especially as it is independent and self-sufficient in its authority.

Therefore, discarding explicit scriptural statements by labeling them as "absurd" or Tāmasika violates the foundational principle of Anapekshatva (independent self-sufficiency of scriptural authority).

Purva Paksha Objection: The Śaṅkara Saṃhitā and Suta Saṃhitā are not legitimate parts of the Skanda Purāṇa.

Context of the Objection: It is true that standard modern print editions (such as those from Gītā Press) structure the Skanda Purāṇa into seven Khaṇḍas totaling 81,000 verses. This structure

Is referenced in the Narada Purāṇa (Chapter 104, Verse 3), where Lord Brahma states:

Nārada Purāṇa, Chapter 104, Verse 3

स्कन्दाह्वयस् तत्र खण्डाः सप्तैव परिकल्पिताः । एकाशीतिसहस्रं तु स्कान्दं सर्वोघकृन्तनम् ॥ ३ ॥

In that Purāṇa named Skanda, seven sections (khaṇḍas) have been designed. The Skanda Purāṇa consists of eightyone thousand (ekashitisahasram) verses, and it destroys the entire accumulation of sins.

Refutation and Textual Evidence: Lord Brahma's statement in the Narada Purāṇa enumerates a specific recension of 81,000 verses, but nowhere does it state that this represents the absolute limit or entirety of the Skanda Purāṇa corpus. The internal testimony of the Skanda Purāṇa's own Saṃhitā tradition affirms a larger, expanded recension of 100,000 verses across six Saṃhitās:

Nārada Purāṇa

स्वतन्त्रम् एकम् अद्वैतं प्रवदन्ति निरङ्कुशम् । तत्र स्कान्दं प्रवक्ष्यामि पुराणेषु त्रयोदशम् ॥ ४४ ॥ सहिताभिः तथा षड्भिः लक्षैर् ग्रन्थैर् विराजितम् । दश चैव पुराणानि सात्त्विकानि विदुर् बुधाः ॥ ४५ ॥

They declare Him to be independent, one without a second (non-dual), and completely unrestricted. Among those Purāṇas, I shall now narrate the Skanda Purāṇa, which is the thirteenth. It shines brilliantly with six Saṃhitās and contains one hundred thousand verses. Wise scholars know that ten of the Purāṇas are Sāttvika.

Combining the verse counts of the extant Saṃhitās (the Suta Saṃhitā and Śaṅkara Saṃhitā) with the seven Khaṇḍas yields precisely 100,000 verses, matching the internal verse count specified within the Saṃhitā text itself. Furthermore, this 100,000-verse recension is explicitly recognized and validated by other major Purāṇas. The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa (Brahma Parva, 1.1.103-105) explicitly states:

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, Brahmaparvan 1.1.103–105

सर्वाण्य् एव पुराणानि संज्ञेयानि नरर्षभ । द्वादशैव सहस्राणि प्रोक्तानीह मनीषिभिः ॥ १०३ ॥ पुनर्वृद्धिं गतानीह आख्यानैर् विविधैर् वृष । यथा स्कान्दं तथा चेदं भविष्यं कुरुनन्दन ॥ १०४ ॥ स्कान्दं शतसहस्रं तु लोकानां ज्ञातम् एव हि । भविष्यम् एतद् ऋषिणा लक्षार्धं संख्यया कृतम् ॥ १०५ ॥

All the Purāṇas were originally known to consist of twelve thousand verses by the wise, O best of men. Later, they expanded through various narratives-just like the Skanda Purāṇa and this Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, O descendant of Kuru. The Skanda Purāṇa consists of one hundred thousand (shatasahasram) verses; indeed, this is well known among the people.

Historical transmission further corroborates the antiquity and authority of the Saṃhitā tradition. The Kāṇḍa Puranam, authored in the 11th century CE by the Tamil Śaiva Ācārya Kacchiyappa Shivachariyar, is a direct adaptation of the Śaṅkara and Suta Saṃhitās of the Skanda Purāṇa. This precedes the earliest citations of the Khanda recension found in the works of 13th-century commentators such as Ramanujācārya and Madhvācārya. Consequently, both the Khanda and Saṃhitā recensions are authentic, historically transmitted branches of the Skanda Purāṇa corpus, and both possess full scriptural authority (pramanya).

Purva Paksha Objection: Vedavyasa's Brahma Sūtras mandate the complete rejection of the Pancaratra and Pāśupata Āgamas.

Refutation and Contextual Analysis: The text of the Brahma Sūtras itself does not state that the Āgamas are to be rejected as a whole. Rather, Brahma Sūtra 2.2.42 specifically addresses a single cosmological point regarding origin:

Brahma Sūtra 2.2.42

उत्पत्त्यसम्भवात् ॥ ४२ ॥

On account of the impossibility of origination.

The sūtra targets a literal reading wherein the individual soul (Jiva) is generated or created-a notion contradicted by Śruti. While Adi Śaṅkarācārya's commentary on the Brahma Sūtras evaluates philosophical positions of the Pāśupata and Pancaratra schools, Śaṅkarācārya explicitly upholds the devotional and ritual validity (Pancakala Kriya) prescribed in the Pancaratra Āgamas:

Brahma Sūtras

...इति, तन् न निराक्रियते, “स एकधा भवति त्रिधा भवति” इत्यादिश्रुतिभ्यः परमात्मनोऽनेकधाभावस्याधिगतत्वात् । यद् अपि तस्य भगवतोऽभिगमनादिलक्षणम् आराधनम् अजस्रम् अनन्यचित्ततयाभिप्रेयते, तद् अपि न प्रतिषिध्यते, श्रुतिस्मृत्योर् ईश्वरप्रणिधानस्य प्रसिद्धत्वात् । यत् पुनर् इदम् उच्यते—वासुदेवात् सङ्कर्षण उत्पद्यते, सङ्कर्षणाच् च प्रद्युम्नः, प्रद्युम्नाच् चानिरुद्ध इति...

...That is not refuted, from scriptural texts (Śruti) such as 'He becomes one-fold, he becomes three-fold...' (Chandogya Upaniṣad 7.26.2), because the state of the Supreme Self (Paramatman) existing in manifold forms is well understood. Even though worship of the Lord characterized by approaching Him continuously with undivided focus is intended, that too is not prohibited, because devotion to the Supreme Lord (Ishvara-pranidhana) is well-established in both Śruti and Smṛti scriptures. As for what is further stated-that Sankarshana is born from Vasudeva, Pradyumna from Sankarshana, and Aniruddha from Pradyumna...

Vedānta objections and hermeneutical context

Śaṅkarācārya explicitly confirms that practical devotion, ritual methodology, and approach (abhigamana) prescribed by the Āgamas are valid and non-prohibited, as they align with Śruti and Smṛti. Furthermore, Adi Śaṅkarācārya directly acknowledges the tradition of the 64 Bhairava Āgamas in Verse 31 of the Saundarya Lahari:

Saundarya Laharī 31

चतुःषष्ट्या तन्त्रैः सकलम् अतिसन्धाय भुवनं स्थितस् तत्तत् सिद्धिप्रसवपरतन्त्रैः पशुपतिः । पुनस् त्वन्निर्बन्धाद् अखिलपुरुषार्थैकघटना-स्वतन्त्रं ते तन्त्रं क्षितितलम् अवातीतरद् इदम् ॥ ३१ ॥

Pasupati (Lord Śiva), having deluded the entire world by means of the sixty-four Tantras which yield their respective mystical powers, brought down to earth at Your insistence this independent Tantra of Yours, which alone fulfills all four humān goals (Purusharthas).

Rather than condemning these Agamic and Tantric corpora, Śaṅkarācārya integrates their existence and functional purpose into the broader framework of traditional practice and spiritual realization, all while labelling them as a Mohaka, which begs the question: - Is Mohaka really even a negative term to begin with? Purva Paksha Objection: The Brahma Sūtras declare the Pāśupata and Śaiva Āgamas to be non-Vedic (Avaidika) and completely invalid.

Refutation and Hermeneutical Context: To properly address this objection, one must distinguish between the direct text of the Brahma Sūtras and the later commentary traditions. The sūtra frequently cited in this debate is Brahma Sūtra 2.2.45:

Brahma Sūtra 2.2.45

विप्रतिषेधाच् च ॥ ४५ ॥

And on account of contradictions.

When read in its proper contextual sequence (prakarana) alongside the preceding sūtras (Brahma Sūtras 2.2.42-44: utpattyasambhavat, na ca kartuh. Karanam, and vijnanadibhave va tadapratishedhah.), this discussion addresses specific metaphysical formulations (such as the origination of the Jiva) rather than issuing a total rejection of the Agamic corpus itself.

This contextual interpretation aligns directly with the hermeneutical principles of Purva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.2.24:

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.2.24

प्रकरणे सम्भवन्न् अपकर्षो न कल्प्येत, विध्यानर्थक्यं हि सम्प्रति ॥ २४ ॥

In the case of that which is compatible with its own context, detachment or removal should not be assumed; for otherwise, the injunction itself would become meaningless.

Even in commentators who vigorously debate rival metaphysical positions, the validity of Agamic worship and practice remains intact. As Śaṅkarācārya himself notes in his commentary on the adjacent sūtras:

Śaṅkara, Brahmasūtrabhāṣya 2.2.45

तन्न निराक्रियते

That is not refuted.

Furthermore, responding to apparent philosophical dualisms between Śruti and Āgama, the Śaiva Ācārya Jayaratha, in his commentary Tantralokaviveka on Abhinavagupta's Tantraloka, expounds on the secret nature of Brahman:

Jayaratha, Tantrālokaviveka on Abhinavagupta’s Tantrāloka

नहि परस्यां संविदि तदुभयस्य स्थूलसूक्ष्मरूपस्य कश्चिद्विरोधः

In the Supreme Consciousness, there is no contradiction whatsoever between those two forms-the gross and the subtle.

The dialectical description of the Supreme Being as transcending standard logical categories is not a defect or contradiction, but a foundational feature of Brahman. In the Bhagavad Gītā (Chapter 13, Verse 13), Lord Krshna Himself defines the nature of Brahman using these exact paradoxes:

Bhagavad Gītā, Chapter 13, Verse 13

ज्ञेयं यत् तत् प्रवक्ष्यामि यज् ज्ञात्वामृतम् अश्नुते । अनादि मत्-परं ब्रह्म न सत् तन् नासद् उच्यते ॥ १३ ॥

I shall now describe that which is to be known, knowing which one attains immortality. It is the beginningless Supreme Brahman, which is said to be neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat).

Similarly, the Kalika Purāṇa (Chapter 1, Verses 19-20) employs identical language to describe the transcendent and immanent nature of the Supreme Reality:

Kālikā Purāṇa 1.19–20

व्यक्ताव्यक्तस्वरूपाय सदसद्व्यक्तरूपिणे । स्थूलाय सूक्ष्मरूपाय विश्वरूपाय वेधसे ॥ १९ ॥ नित्याय नित्यज्ञानाय निर्विकाराय चेतसे । विद्याऽविद्यास्वरूपाय कलारूपाय वै नमः ॥ २० ॥

Salutations to the Creator, whose nature is both manifest (vyakta) and unmanifest (avyakta), who embodies both being (sat) and non-being (asat); who is gross (sthula) yet subtle (sukshma), and whose form is the entire universe; who is eternal, possessing eternal knowledge, unchanging consciousness, and who embodies both knowledge (vidya) and ignorance (avidya) as well as all creative arts (kala).

Far from indicating incoherence, these descriptions express the absolute transcendence of Brahman/Shakti beyond dualistic conceptual frameworks. They affirm that empirical logic based on mundane categories cannot invalidate the self-evident authority (svatahpramanya) of divine revelation across the Śruti, Purāṇa, and Āgama literature.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Pāśupata doctrine is inherently non-Vedic (Avaidika) and encourages practices opposed to Vedic orthodoxy.

Refutation and Canonical Evidence: This objection fails to account for the foundational texts of the Pāśupata tradition itself, which explicitly uphold the authority of the Vedas and the traditional Āśrama system. In the Pāśupata Sūtras (4.20), accompanied by Kaundinya's commentary (Pancartha Bhashya), the trajectory of liberation for a Vedic practitioner is unambiguously defined:

Pāśupata Sūtra 4.20

न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनरावर्तते ॥ २० ॥

No Brāhmaṇa whatsoever returns again [to mundane existence].

In his authoritative commentary, Kaundinya details the exact scope and Vedic alignment of this sūtra:

Kauṇḍinya, Pañcārthabhāṣya on Pāśupata Sūtra 4.20

अत्र नकारोपदेशोऽन्याचरणप्रतिपत्तिप्रतिषेधार्थः — कश्चिद् इति गृहस्थाद्यः — स्थानमात्रवैलक्षण्यदर्शनाद् ब्राह्मणेष्व् एव कश्चिच्छब्दः — गृहस्थो ब्रह्मचारी वानप्रस्थो भिक्षुर् एकवेदो द्विवेदस् त्रिवेदश् चतुर्वेदश् च गायत्रीमात्रसारो वानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं प्राप्तः सन् न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनर् आवर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — ब्राह्मणग्रहणं ब्राह्मण्यावधारणार्थम्, ब्राह्मण एव नान्य इत्य् अर्थः — क्षेत्रज्ञे च ब्राह्मणसंज्ञा — कस्मात्? उपचयजन्मयोगात् संस्कारयोगात्, श्रुतियोगाच् च ब्राह्मणः — पुनःशब्दः पुनरावृत्तिप्रतिषेधे — यथा पूर्वं संज्ञानादिभिर् गत्वा आवर्तते पुनः पुनः, तथानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं गत्वा न सकृद् आवर्तते — पुनः पुनः सर्वथापि नावर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — आङ् इति स्वशास्त्रोक्थमर्यादम् अधिकुरुते, अभिविध्यर्थं च — ये चानेन विधिना क्षपिताज्ञानकलुषपापमायादयः क्षीणास् ते पुनः पुनर् आवर्तन्ते — न तैः सह संयोगो भवति, न चापरं जन्म प्रतिपद्यत इत्य् अर्थः ॥ २० ॥

Here, the instruction of the negative particle (na) is intended to prohibit the adoption of any other conduct. The word kashcid ('anyone') refers to householders and others. Because the distinction is seen only in their stage of life (Āśrama), the word kashcid applies exclusively among the Brāhmaṇas. The meaning is that a householder (grhastha), a celibate student (brahmacari), a forest-dweller (vanaprastha), or a mendicant (bhikshu)-whether he knows one Veda, two Vedas, three Vedas, four Vedas, or has only the Gayatri mantra as his sole essence-having attained the proximity of Lord Rudra by this prescribed method, no such Brāhmaṇa returns. The use of the word Brāhmaṇa is to firmly restrict it to Brāhmaṇahood; meaning, a Brāhmaṇa alone and no one else. Furthermore, the designation Brāhmaṇa is applied to the Kshetrajna (the conscious soul). Why? Because one becomes a Brāhmaṇa through the association with noble birth, the association with purifying sacraments (saskaras), and the association with Vedic learning (śruti). The word punah. ('Again') is used to prohibit rebirth. Just as previously, having departed through mere worldly knowledge, one returns again and again, similarly, having approached Lord Rudra by this method, one does not return even once. Under no circumstances does one return again. The prefix An denotes the limit stated in one's own scriptures and also signifies inclusive extension. Those whose ignorance, impurities, and maya have been exhausted by this prescribed method do not return again. There is no further association with them, nor do they attain another birth. This is the meaning.

This foundational text proves that the Pāśupata system explicitly integrates Vedic learning (śruti-yoga), sacraments (saskara-yoga), and traditional Āśrama stages as valid bases for spiritual practice and final liberation (duhkhanta). Consequently, labeling the Pāśupata doctrine as fundamentally anti-Vedic contradicts its own primary canonical source.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Varaha Purāṇa declares the Āgamas to be Mohaka (delusory scriptures) produced to mislead people away from the Vedas.

Contextual Analysis of the Objection: This passage from the Varaha Purāṇa (Chapter 70, Verses 36-37, 42-44) is frequently cited by dualistic commentators-such as Madhvācārya in his Brahma Sūtra Bhashya (1.1.1)-to argue for the total rejection of non-Vaiṣṇava

Purāṇic objections and interpretation

Agamic texts:

Varāha Purāṇa 70.36–37, 70.42–44

त्वं च रुद्र महाबाहो मोहशास्त्राणि कारय । अल्पायासं दर्शयित्वा फलं दीर्घं प्रदर्शय ॥ ३६ ॥ कुहकश् चेन्द्रजालानि विरुद्धाचरणानि च । दर्शयित्वा जनं सर्वं मोहयाशु महेश्वर ॥ ३७ ॥ ये वेदमार्गानिर्मुक्तास् तेषां मोहार्थम् एव च । नयसिद्धान्तसंज्ञाभिर् मया शास्त्रं तु दर्शितम् ॥ ४२ ॥ पाशोऽयं पशुभावस् तु स यदा पतितो भवेत् । तदा पाशुपतं शास्त्रं जायते वेदसंज्ञितम् ॥ ४३ ॥ वेदमूर्तिर् अहं विप्र नान्यशास्त्रार्थवादिभिः । ज्ञायते मत्स्वरूपं तु मुक्त्वा वेदम् अनादिमत् । वेदवेद्योऽस्मि विप्रर्षे… ॥ ४४ ॥

'O mighty-armed Rudra, create scriptures that delude [the unworthy]. Show them paths that require little effort yet promise great rewards. O Maheśvara, delude all people quickly by exhibiting tricks, magic, and contradictory practices... Indeed, for the delusion of those who have strayed from the path of the Vedas, scriptures under the names of logic and established doctrines (naya-siddhanta) were presented by me. This bond (pasha) is the state of animallike existence (pashubhava); when that falls away, then the Pāśupata scripture becomes known as equal to the Vedas... O Brāhmaṇa, I am the embodiment of the Veda. My true nature is not known by proponents of other scriptures, except through the eternal, beginningless Veda. O sage among Brāhmaṇas, I am that which is to be known through the Vedas...'

The core argument posed by Vaiṣṇava critics rests on a specific deductive chain: Viṣṇu commands Rudra to produce deluding scriptures (moha-śāstras) for those outside the Vedic fold; because the Pāśupata doctrine appears within this discourse, the entire Śaiva/Pāśupata Agamic corpus is inferred to be mohaka.

Refutation and Hermeneutical Resolution: This conclusion is untenable when subjected to rigorous textual and Mimasaka analysis: 1. Specificity vs. Universal Invalidation: The text explicitly uses terms such as moha-shastrani, kuhaka, indrajalani, and viruddhacarana. Nowhere does the text make a blanket assertion that the 28 canonical Śaiva Āgamas broadly constitute these moha-śāstras. 2. Exegesis of Verse 70.43 and Adhikāra-Bheda: A close reading of Verse 70.43 reveals the core intent of the passage:

तदा पाशुपतं शास्त्रं जायते वेदसंज्ञितम् ॥ ४३ ॥

The term patita refers to an individual who has fallen or degraded from the standard Vedic social and religious order. For such a person who is outside ordinary Vedic eligibility, the Pāśupata Śāstra assumes a Veda-like function (Veda-sajnita). This provides primary Purāṇic evidence for the principle of Adhikāra- Bheda (differential qualification), demonstrating that Agamic revelation accommodates seekers who are otherwise disconnected from direct Vedic injunctions. 3. Mīmāṃsā Interpretation of Designation: The term Vedasajnita cannot be casually discarded as empty praise or figure of speech. As established in Purva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.4.2:

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.4.2

अपि वा नामधेयं स्याद् यदुत्पत्ताव् अपूर्वम् अविधायकत्वात् ॥ २ ॥

Rather, it should be recognized as a proper name/designation (namadheya), since it applies to an original subject and does not function as an injunction.

Because Veda-sajnita serves as a formal designation (namadheya) within the verse, it possesses direct interpretative value and cannot be dismissed as mere arthavada-a position further fortified by the Narada Purāṇa's explicit prohibition against reading arthavada into Purāṇic statements. Thus, far from disproving Agamic validity, the Varaha Purāṇa affirms that the Pāśupata doctrine functions with Veda-like authority for its intended practitioners, perfectly aligned with the framework of Adhikāra-Bheda.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Kurma Purāṇa classifies all Āgamas/Tantras as Mohaka, where Bhairava, Pancaratra, and Pāśupata Āgamas are labeled as Mohaka.

Analysis of the Objection: The Kurma Purāṇa contains passages that explicitly designate various Agamic traditions as Mohaka (delusory):

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 111

तस्माद् धि वेदबाह्यानां रक्षणार्थाय पापिनाम् । विमोहनाय शास्त्राणि करिष्यामो वृषध्वज ॥ १११ ॥

Therefore, for the protection of those outside the Vedic fold, and for their delusion, we shall compose śāstras, O Vrshadhvaja.

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 112

एवं स बोधितो रुद्रो माधवेन मुरारिणा । चकार मोहशास्त्राणि केशवोऽपि शिवेरितः ॥ ११२ ॥

Thus addressed by Madhava, Rudra composed the deluding śāstras, and Keshava too, prompted by Śiva, [composed them].

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 113

कापालं नाकुलं वामं भैरवं पूर्वपश्चिमम् । पाञ्चरात्रं पाशुपतं तथान्यानि सहस्रशः ॥ ११३ ॥

Kapala, Nakula, Vāmā, Bhairava, Purvapashcima, Pancaratra, Pāśupata, and thousands of others [are among them].

Refutation and Hermeneutical Re-interpretation: However, in the same passage, the purpose of Lord Viṣṇu and Lord Śiva creating these scriptures is explicitly explained:

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 111

तस्माद् वै वेदबाह्यानां रक्षणार्थाय पापिनाम् । विमोहनाय शास्त्राणि करिष्यामो वृषध्वज.

Therefore, for the protection of sinful people who are outside the Veda, we shall create scriptures for delusion (mohaka)...

Here, Mohaka does not mean malicious delusion; rather, Mohaka can also signify enchantment or allure. The correct interpretation of these verses is that Lord Śiva and Lord Viṣṇu, through their grace and mercy, created the Āgamas to allure people back toward the Vedas (Veda-marga). This underlying harmony is expressed in the Āgamas themselves, such as in the Sarvollasa Tantra (Chapter 7):

Sarvollāsa Tantra, Chapter 7, verses 34–36

शृणु देवि प्रवक्ष्यामि सुराशुद्धिं यथाक्रमम् । मकाराणां च सर्वेषां शोधनं सिद्धिवर्धनम् ॥ ३४ ॥ वेदो निष्कलशब्दो वै वेदार्थोऽस्त्य् आगमः शिवे । वेदार्थागमतत्त्वज्ञैः सुरा सेव्या मुमुक्षुभिः ॥ ३५ ॥ न वेदागमयोर् भेदः सर्वथेति विनिश्चयः । शास्त्राणां परमेशानि भ्रान्तिः कार्या न कौलिकैः ॥ ३६ ॥

'Listen, O Goddess. I shall explain, step-by-step, the purification of wine (sura) and the purification/sanctification of all the five elements (makaras), which enhances spiritual achievements (siddhis). -- 34 --' 'O Shive. The Veda is indeed the flawless, partless sound (nishkala śabda), and the essence/meaning of the Veda is the Āgama (scripture). Those who seek liberation (mumukshu) and understand the underlying truths of both the Vedas and the Āgamas may partake in the ritual offering. -- 35 --' 'There is absolutely no difference between the Vedas and the Āgamas-this is a certainty. O Supreme Goddess, practitioners of the Kaula tradition (kaulikas) should not entertain any confusion or delusion regarding the scriptures. -- 36 --'

If Mohaka does not mean enchantment and indeed means delusion, Śiva and Viṣṇu creating Āgamas as Mohaka-Śāstras does not make logical sense, as the creation of these very Śāstras is explicitly referred to as a form of Ishvara's protection (rakshana). Moreover, the same passage states:

Sarvollāsa Tantra, Chapter 7

जायन्तो मानुषे लोके क्षीणपापचयास् ततः । ईश्वराराधनबलाद् गच्छध्वं सुकृतां गतिम्.

Having been born in the humān world, with their accumulation of sin diminished, by the power of worship of Ishvara, go to the destination of the righteous.

Sarvollāsa Tantra, Chapter 7

वर्तध्वं मत्प्रसादेन नान्यथा निष्कृतिर् हि वः.

Act according to my grace; there is no other means of deliverance for you.

Furthermore, the passage goes on to say that Lord Śiva himself incarnates to "mohayant" or enchant the world for their own benefit:

Sarvollāsa Tantra, Chapter 7

मोहयन्त इमं लोकम् अवतीर्य महीतले । चकार शङ्करो भिक्षां हितायैषा द्विजैः सह.

Having descended to the surface of the earth, enchanting this world, Śaṅkara went about begging for alms along with the twice-born (Dvijas) for their benefit.

It would not make sense to delude someone to hell for their own benefit. The text explicitly frames this mohaka as rakshana (protection).

Purva Paksha Objection: The Kurma Purāṇa asserts that Tantrikas attain Naraka for many kalpas.

Analysis of the Objection Verse: The objection relies on Verse 114 of the Kurma Purāṇa:

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 114

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ कुर्वाणाः शास्त्रचोदितम् । पतन्तो निरये घोरे बहून् कल्पान् पुनः पुनः ॥ ११४ ॥

Having created them, the two gods said: 'Those who act according to the injunctions of these śāstras, falling into terrible hell, [remain there] for many kalpas again and again.'

Refutation and Hermeneutical Re-interpretation:

1. Semantic Re-evaluation via Ashtanga Hrdaya Consider this alternative textual reading (patha-antara): in medical treatises

Such as the Ashtanga Hrdaya, the phrase bahu-kalpa does not denote "many kalpas" (cosmic time cycles), but rather "of many methods" or therapeutic procedures. As is well established, the Āgamas function as an upaya (expedient means) and consist of diverse methods-including Pancaratra, Śaiva, Pāśupata, etc. This usage is attested in Verse 28:

बहुकल्पं बहुगुणं सम्पन्नं योग्यम् औषधम् ॥ २८ ॥

2. Re-ordering of Syntax (Patha-krama) via Mīmāṃsā Rules We can re-arrange the word order of Verse 114 in accordance with Purva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 5.4.1:

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 5.4.1

क्रमकोपोऽर्थशब्दानां श्रुतिविशेषाद् अर्थपरत्वाच् च ॥ ५.४.१ ॥

There is a disruption (modification) of the textual sequence of action-denoting words/mantras, because of explicit scriptural statement and because these words exist primarily to serve the execution of the action.

However, a modification of textual sequence (patha-krama) can only be invoked if there is a logical impossibility (anupapatti) in the literal reading of the verse. In Chapter 6, Verse 12 of the Harita Smṛti, it is stated:

Harita Smṛti 6.12

महाभागवतो विप्रस् तान्त्रिकः सर्वकर्मसु ॥ १२ ॥

A most exalted devotee of the Lord, a learned scholar, and is a Tantric adept in all actions.

Furthermore, Chapter 1, Verse 26 states:

Harita Smṛti 1.26

अचक्रधारी यो विप्रो बहुवेदश्रुतोऽपि वा । स जीवन्नेव चण्डालो मृतो निरयम् आप्नुयात् ॥ २६ ॥

Whether a learned scholar or well-versed in many Vedas, if a person does not wear the mark of the Cakra (discus), they live as an outcast and, upon death, attain hell.

The practice of wearing the Cakra (Cakra-dharana) is exclusive to the Pancaratra Āgamas. If Verse 114 from the Kurma Purāṇa were taken literally to suggest that following the Pancaratra Āgamas leads to hell, it would create a direct contradiction with the Harita Smṛti. Therefore, the re-arranged and correct patha-krama of this verse is:

Kūrma Purāṇa, verse 114 (reordered reading)

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ — पुनः पुनः घोरे निरये पतन्तः [जनाः], शास्त्रचोदितम् कुर्वाणाः बहून् कल्पान् [लभन्ते] —

Having created them, the two gods declared: 'For those people who fall again and again into the terrible hell [of worldly suffering], performing that which is enjoined by these scriptures provides many therapeutic methods (bahun kalpan).'

3. Resolution via Smṛti Superiority Even if this contextual re-interpretation of the Kurma Purāṇa is not accepted, Vyāsa Smṛti (Chapter 1, Verse 4) explicitly rules that when a conflict arises between a Purāṇa and a Smṛti, the statements of the Smṛti take precedence:

Vyāsa Smṛti, Chapter 1, Verse 4

श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानां विरोधो यत्र दृश्यते । तत्र श्रौतं प्रमाणं तु तयोर् द्वैधे स्मृतिर् वरा ॥ ४ ॥

Where a contradiction is observed among Śruti, Smṛti, and Purāṇa, the Śruti is the supreme authority; in a conflict between Smṛti and Purāṇa, the Smṛti is superior.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Āgamas are not the Vedas, and therefore they lack scriptural authority (pramanya).

Establishing Śabda-Pramāṇa for the Āgamas: To establish the validity of the Āgamas, we must first examine the nature of valid knowledge (jnana) as the sole means to liberation, and then demonstrate how the Āgamas qualify as valid verbal testimony (śabda-pramāṇa).

Jnana as the Sole Means to Liberation: In the Atmabodha (Verse 2), Adi Śaṅkarācārya defines jnana as the direct and indispensable cause of liberation:

Ātmabodha 2

बोधोऽन्यसाधनेभ्यो हि साक्षान् मोक्षैकसाधनम् । पाकस्य वह्नितज्ज्ञानं विना मोक्षो न सिध्यति ॥ २ ॥

Compared with all other forms of discipline, Knowledge of the Self (bodha) is the one direct means for liberation. Just as cooking cannot be accomplished without fire, so Liberation cannot be attained without wisdom.

In Verse 3, he further clarifies the inherent limitations of ritual action (karma) in destroying fundamental ignorance (avidya):

Ātmabodha 3

अविरोधितया कर्म नाविद्यां विनिवर्तयेत् । विद्याविद्यां निहन्त्य् एव तेजस्तिमिरसङ्घवत् ॥ ३ ॥

Action cannot destroy ignorance, for it is not in conflict with or opposed to ignorance. Knowledge (vidya) alone destroys ignorance, just as light destroys deep darkness.

Epistemological Framework of Nyaya: The Nyaya system defines valid knowledge through four foundational means of cognition (pramāṇas). As stated in Nyaya Sūtra 1.1.3:

The Āgamas as Valid Testimony and the Reliability of Śiva

Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.3

प्रत्यक्षानुमानोपमानशब्दाः प्रमाणानि ॥ १.१.३ ॥

Perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), comparison (upamana), and verbal testimony (śabda) are the valid means of knowledge.

Nyaya Sūtra 1.1.7 specifically defines the nature of shabdapramana (which governs authoritative verbal testimony and revealed scriptures):

Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.7

आप्तोपदेशः शब्दः ॥ १.१.७ ॥

The instruction of an apta (a trustworthy/reliable authority) is śabda.

Ācārya Vatsyayana, in his foundational commentary (Bhashya) on this sūtra, explicitly defines the characteristics of an apta:

Vātsyāyana, Nyāyabhāṣya on Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.7

यः साक्षात्कृतधर्मा यथादृष्टस्यार्थस्य चिख्यापयिषया प्रयुक्त उपदेष्टा स आप्तः.

That person is called apta (reliable) who possesses direct and immediate perception of reality (sakshatkrtadharma), who is moved by the desire to impart the truth exactly as perceived, and who is fully capable of communicating it.

Furthermore, according to Nyaya Sūtra 1.1.8 (drshtadrshtarthatvat), śabda-pramāṇa applies to both perceptible (drshta) and imperceptible/transcendent (adrshta) entities. Because the Āgamas originate directly from the Supreme Being (Ishvara)-the ultimate, omniscient Apta-their verbal instructions constitute an infallible Śabda-Pramāṇa capable of bestowing liberating knowledge.

The Aptatva of Lord Śiva: To establish the Aptatva (absolute reliability and trustworthiness) of Lord Śiva as the author of the Āgamas within the Nyaya framework, three criteria must be fulfilled: complete knowledge of reality (jnana), freedom from error or deception, and the benevolent desire to communicate truth (iccha-shakti).

1. Jnana: Does the Deity Know Reality As It Is? In the Kailasa Saṃhitā of the Śiva Purāṇa (Chapter 9, Verse 21), it is established that Sadāśiva possesses direct, effortless, and omniscient perception of all existence:

Śiva Purāṇa, Kailāsa Saṃhitā 9.21

यद्यथावस्थितं वस्तु तत्तथैव सदाशिवः । अयत्नेनैव जानाति तस्मात् सर्वज्ञ उच्यते.

Whatever object exists, exactly in whichever way it is situated, Sadāśiva knows it precisely in that very manner without any effort; therefore, He is called 'Omniscient' (sarvajna).

This passage directly aligns with Ācārya Vatsyayana's criterion of yathadrshtasyarthasya (cognizing an object precisely as it exists in reality).

2. Freedom from Deception (Akhanda-Yatharthatva): Purva Paksha Objection: Omniscience alone is insufficient. Why assume Lord Śiva's statements are free from error, bias, or intentional deception? To establish absolute truthfulness (akhanda-yatharthatva), the source must be completely free from avidya (ignorance) and delusion. The Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad (4.18) defines Śiva as pure, untainted consciousness beyond all dualities and darkness:

Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad 4.18

यदा तमस् तन् न दिवा न रात्रिर् न सन् न चासच् छिव एव केवलः । तद् अक्षरं तत् सवितुर् वरेण्यं प्रज्ञा च तस्मात् प्रसृता पुराणी ॥ १८ ॥

When there is no darkness [ignorance], there is neither day nor night, neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat), but Śiva alone, absolute and pure (kevalah.). That is the Imperishable (aksharam), that is the adorable light of Savitr., and from That issued forth ancient Wisdom (prajna).

Because Lord Śiva is the pure, imperishable source of primal wisdom (prajna) devoid of tamas (ignorance), his words are inherently free from error, delusion, or intent to deceive.

3. Iccha-Shakti: Śiva's Desire to Reveal Truth: The desire to make known the truth as perceived (cikhyapayisha) is demonstrated through Lord Śiva's Iccha-Shakti (will/desire to illuminate). As revealed in the Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad (3.18):

Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad 3.18

यो ब्रह्माणं विदधाति पूर्वं यो वै वेदांश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै । तं ह देवम् आत्मबुद्धिप्रकाशं मुमुक्षुर् वै शरणम् अहं प्रपद्ये ॥ १८ ॥

To Him who at the beginning of creation brought forth Brahma, and who verily delivered (revealed) the Vedas unto him, to that Lord, the illuminator of self-knowledge, I, desirous of liberation (mumukshu), fly for refuge.

The compound atma-buddhi-prakasham specifically characterizes Lord Śiva as the illuminator of Self-knowledge (atmabuddhi) through revealed scriptures. If Lord Śiva is recognized as the ultimate revealer of atma-buddhi who imparted the Vedas, there is no logical ground to deny that His secondary revelations-such as the Śaiva, Pāśupata, or Bhairava Āgamas-likewise convey atma-buddhi. Given that illuminating spiritual knowledge is an essential attribute of Lord Śiva, and given that the Vedic corpus itself acknowledges the authority of Agamic revelations, the Āgamas stand validated as self-authoritative (svatah-pramāṇa) through the divine Aptatva of Lord Śiva.

4. Upadeshtrtva (Communication) and Application of Danda-pupa Nyaya: Purva Paksha Objection: Even if Lord Śiva possesses complete knowledge (jnana) and freedom from deception, He cannot be considered an Apta for the Āgamas if He lacks the capacity to communicate (upadeshtrtva) or transmit scripture effectively to humān beings.

Refutation and Hermeneutical Resolution: This objection is directly refuted by the Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad (3.18), which explicitly affirms Lord Śiva's active role in transmitting supreme revelation:

Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad 3.18

यो वै वेदाश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै.

Who indeed revealed and sent forth the Vedas unto Him (Brahma).

Because the Upaniṣad establishes Lord Śiva as the original revealer and communicator of the entire Vedic corpus, His capacity as an active upadeshta (teacher/communicator) is indisputable. To establish the authority of the Āgamas from this premise, we apply the classical maxim of Danda-pupa Nyaya (the Maxim of the Stick and the Cake): Danda-pupa Nyaya dictates that if a stick and a cake are bound together, and a mouse is known to have eaten the hard stick, it is naturally and inevitably inferred that the softer cake tied to it has also been eaten. In this context, the vast, highly complex, and eternal Veda represents the danda (the greater/harder task), while the specialized Āgamas represent the pupa (the secondary/easier task). Since it is authoritatively established that Lord Śiva revealed the monumental Vedic revelation-which is universally accepted as authoritative Śabda-Pramāṇa-it follows a

Fortiori (with much less effort) that His secondary, specialized revelations, such as the Śaiva and Pāśupata Āgamas, are also authoritative. If the primary and greater work (Veda) is recognized as His infallible utterance, the secondary and auxiliary work (Āgama) originating from the exact same Apta cannot be logically rejected.

5. Identity of the Revealers (Abheda) and Extension of Aptatva to Śākta and Vaiṣṇava Āgamas: The Aptatva of Devi and the authority of the Śākta Āgamas need not be established independently, as the Śiva Purāṇa (Vayaviya Saṃhitā, Uttarabhaga, Chapter 4, Verse 9) explicitly defines the complete non-difference (*abheda*) between Śiva and Devi:

Śiva Purāṇa, Vayaviya Saṃhitā, Uttarabhaga, Chapter 4, Verse 9

यथा शिवस् तथा देवी यथा देवी तथा शिवः । नानयोर् अन्तरं विद्याच् चन्द्रचन्द्रिकयोर् इव ॥ ९ ॥

As is Śiva, so is the Goddess (Devi); as is the Goddess, so is Śiva. One should perceive no difference between them, just as there is no separation between the moon and the moonlight.

Because there is absolute identity between Śiva and Devi, the Aptatva established for Lord Śiva applies inherently and identically to Devi, thereby validating the Śākta Āgamas. Furthermore, the Rudra-hrdaya Upaniṣad (Verse 11) declares:

Rudrahṛdaya Upaniṣad 11

उमाशङ्करयोगो यः स योगो विष्णुर् उच्यते । यस्तु तस्मै नमस्कारं कुर्याद् भक्तिसमन्वितः ॥ ११ ॥

The union of Umā and Śaṅkara is that very union called Viṣṇu. One should offer bowful respects to Him with devotion.

To establish the Aptatva of Lord Viṣṇu from this scriptural premise, we apply the classical maxim of Yatha-Karana- Guṇa-Tatha-Karya-Guṇa Nyaya (The Maxim that the Qualities of the Cause Manifest in the Effect): This Nyaya dictates that the qualities (guṇas) inherent in the constituent causes must necessarily manifest in the resulting union or effect. Since the constituent principles of this divine union-Śiva and Umā-are established Aptas characterized by absolute omniscience (sarvajnatva) and freedom from error, the non-different union itself (Lord Viṣṇu) inherently possesses the complete quality of Aptatva. Consequently, the Vaiṣṇava Āgamas (Pancaratra) revealed by Lord Viṣṇu possess the exact same self-evident authority (atma-pramanya) as the Vedas and the Pāśupata Āgamas. This non-difference among manifestations extends equally to the fierce aspect of Bhairava. As stated in the Padma Purāṇa (Uttara-khanda, Chapter 18, Verse 128):

Padma Purāṇa, Uttara-khanda, Chapter 18, Verse 128

स्वयं च भैरवरूपं कृत्वा तन्मध्यगः पपौ । तीक्ष्णदंष्ट्रा महाकायास् तदासन् योगिनीगणाः ॥ १२८ ॥

He Himself assumed the form of Bhairava, stood among them, and drank. The groups of Yoginis then became large-bodied with sharp fangs.

Therefore, in all matters concerning Aptatva and scriptural revelation, complete abheda (non-difference) must be recognized among the divine forms (Śiva, Devi, Viṣṇu, Bhairava) who revealed the Āgama-śāstras.

Mangalacarana and Conclusion To conclude this defense of Sarva-Āgama-Pramanya, we offer our humble obeisances unto the Supreme Divine Manifestations and the Sacred Revealed Texts through the following concluding Mangalacarana:

Concluding Maṅgalācaraṇam

नमः शिवायाभयदाय नित्यं देव्यै च तस्यै जगदीश्वरायै । श्री-भैरवायात्म-विशुद्धि-दात्रे नमोऽस्तु सर्वागमपूजिताय ॥ १ ॥

Salutations forever unto Śiva, the bestower of fearlessness; unto the Goddess (Devi), the Supreme Controller of the universe; unto Shri Bhairava, the purifier of the Self; and salutations unto the holy Āgamas worshipped by all.

Thus, the Āgamas stand united with the Vedas as eternal, infallible, and self-authoritative (svatah-pramāṇa) guideposts leading all seeking souls (mumukshus) toward ultimate liberation (moksha).

॥ इति श्री-सर्वागम-प्रामाण्य-वादः सम्पूर्णम् ॥
