VP 1.8.1

कथितस्तामसः सर्गो ब्रह्मणस्ते महामुने । रुद्रसर्गं प्रवक्ष्यामि तन्मे निगदतः शृणु ॥१॥

The Tamas creation of Brahmā has been described to you, O great sage. I shall now speak of Rudra’s creation; listen to me as I tell it.

Śrīdhara translation

After the lineage of Adharma, situated in intelligence and opposed to Dharma, had been stated as a cause of continual dissolution, its tāmasa lineage was described. The account now turns to the creation of Nīlalohita Rudra, husband of Dakṣa’s two daughters, to continue their story. Although Khyāti would fittingly be mentioned first in the sequence Khyāti, Satyā, and Sambhūti, her story is extensive and will be told later. Alternatively, this is a sāttvika Rudra creation included in the Kumāra creation, distinct from the tāmasa Rudra creation described earlier. “Hear this” gives the connection.

VP 1.8.2

कल्पादावात्मनस्तुल्यं सुतं प्रध्यायतस्ततः । प्रादुरासीत्प्रभोरङ्के कुमारो नीललोहितः ॥२॥

Then, at the beginning of the Kalpa, as [Brahmā] meditated on a son equal to himself, the youth Nīlalohita appeared on the Lord’s lap.

Śrīdhara translation

Rudra appeared crying and running; this is the etymology of the name Rudra. The Vāyu says: “They cried and ran all around; from crying and running they became known as Rudras.”

VP 1.8.5

ततोऽन्यानि ददौ तस्मै सप्त नामानि वै प्रभुः । स्थानानि चैषामष्टानां पत्नीः पुत्रांश्च स प्रभुः ॥५॥

Then the Lord gave him seven other names, and [gave] the stations, wives, and sons of these eight [forms].

Śrīdhara translation

Even though he is the Supreme Lord, crying and the like is a display of infancy, meant to gather the world and indicate dependence on a father. Because it is wondrous, crying serves as the basis for the etymology of his name; since the names are distinguished by the designation of crying and the like, he is said to have eight names.

She, the mother of the world, Śrī of Viṣṇu, never separated, is eternal. As Viṣṇu is all-pervading, so also is she, O best of twice-born.

Śrīdhara translation

Here is the meaning of the answer: a contradiction would arise if Śrī were subject to birth. She is eternal, all-pervading, and the power of Viṣṇu, universal in nature. According to the work to be done, she merely manifests as Viṣṇu does. Therefore, there is no contradiction in her appearing first as Bhṛgu’s daughter, disappearing through Durvāsas’s curse, and appearing again in the Milk Ocean. This continues through the end of the next chapter, beginning “She is eternal.”

Śrīdhara translation

After the lineage of Adharma, situated in intelligence and opposed to Dharma, had been stated as a cause of continual dissolution, its tāmasa lineage was described. The account now turns to the creation of Nīlalohita Rudra, husband of Dakṣa’s two daughters, to continue their story. Although Khyāti would fittingly be mentioned first in the sequence Khyāti, Satyā, and Sambhūti, her story is extensive and will be told later. Alternatively, this is a sāttvika Rudra creation included in the Kumāra creation, distinct from the tāmasa Rudra creation described earlier. “Hear this” gives the connection.
