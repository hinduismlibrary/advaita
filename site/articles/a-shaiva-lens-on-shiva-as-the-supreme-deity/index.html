Opening Salutation

शिवपरमत्वसाधनम् अथ शिवस्य परमेश्वरत्वं निरूप्यते।

Invocation

सदाशिवमजं नित्यं विश्वोत्तीर्णं निरञ्जनम् । आदिरूपं महादेवं वन्दे शिवपरत्वसिद्धये ॥

I salute Sadāśiva — the unborn, eternal, transcendent of the universe, the stainless, the primordial form, Mahādeva — for the establishment of Śiva's supremacy.

1. Preface

1.1 Introduction

Questions about the supreme Brahman have long shaped Hindu philosophical debate. Hari-Tattva-Vimarśa presents the Vaiṣṇava case. This paper presents the Śaiva position that Sadāśiva, or Maheśvara, is the ultimate ground of existence and that Viṣṇu is a functional manifestation. It follows the opposing paper’s scriptural method while arguing that the texts support Śiva as the Para-Tattva.

1.2 Methodology

This paper follows the same hierarchy of scriptural authority. Śruti, including the Vedas and Upaniṣads, takes precedence, followed by Smṛti, Itihāsa, Purāṇa, and Dharmaśāstra. It applies the Mīmāṃsā principle of samanvaya, or reconciliation, and does not dismiss a source as an interpolation for convenience.

The first point of disagreement is the guṇa-based classification of the Purāṇas. This paper argues that the classification is internally inconsistent and cannot serve as a neutral standard between Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva traditions.

2. On Guṇa-Based Purāṇa Classification

Hari-Tattva-Vimarśa relies heavily on classifying the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas as sāttvika and the Śaiva Purāṇas as tāmasa. That classification shapes how each text is read, because it can discount statements in Śaiva Purāṇas before their verses are considered.

This is a serious argument, but it has several problems.

2.1 Padma Purāṇa’s Contradiction

The Padma Puran, Uttarakhanda, Adhyaya 236 does say that the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas are Sāttvika. However, the Padma Puran itself in a different section presents the full picture.

In the Svarga Khanda, Chapter 62, Verse 2-7, the sage Suta describes how the eighteen Purāṇas are the body parts of Bhagavān Sri Hari himself.

सूत उवाच । एवं यन्महिमा लोके लोकनिस्तारकारणम् । तस्य विष्णोः परेशस्य नानाविग्रहधारिणः ॥ १ ॥

Sūta said: Thus, the single Purāṇic form belongs to that Supreme Lord Viṣṇu, who assumes various forms and whose majesty in the world is the cause of liberation for all beings. (1)

एकं पुराणं रूपं वै तत्र पाद्मं परं महत् । ब्राह्मं मूर्धा हरेरेव हृदयं पद्मसंज्ञितम् ॥ २ ॥

Among those, the Padma Purāṇa is supreme and great. The Brahma Purāṇa is indeed the head of Hari, and the one named Padma is His heart. (2)

वैष्णवं दक्षिणो बाहुः शैवं वामो महेशितुः । ऊरू भागवतं प्रोक्तं नाभिः स्यान्नारदीयकम् ॥ ३ ॥

The Viṣṇu Purāna is His right arm, and the Śiva Purāna is the left arm of the Great Lord. The Bhāgavata Purāna is said to be His thighs, and the Nāradīya Purāna is His navel. (3)

मार्कण्डेयं च दक्षांघ्रिर्वामो ह्याग्नेयमुच्यते । भविष्यं दक्षिणो जानुर्विष्णोरेव महात्मनः ॥ ४ ॥

The Mārkaṇḍeya Purāna is His right foot, and the Āgneya (Agni) Purāna is called His left foot. The Bhaviṣya Purāna is the right knee of the high-souled Viṣṇu. (4)

ब्रह्मवैवर्तसंज्ञं तु वामजानुरुदाहृतः । लैङ्गं ह गुल्फकं दक्षं वाराहं वामगुल्फकम् ॥ ५ ॥

The one known as Brahmavaivarta is declared to be His left knee. The Liṅga Purāna is His right ankle, and the Varāha Purāna is His left ankle. (5)

स्काान्दं पुराणं लोमानि त्वगस्य वामनं स्मृतम् । कौर्मं पृष्ठं समाख्यातं मात्स्यं मेदः प्रकीर्तितम् ॥ ६ ॥

The Skanda Purāna is the hair on His body, and the Vāmana Purāna is remembered as His skin. The Kūrma Purāna is described as His back, and the Matsya Purāna is celebrated as His fat. (6)

मज्जा तु गारुडं प्रोक्तं ब्रह्माण्डमस्थि गीयते । एवमेवाभवद्विष्णुः पुराणावयवो हरिः ॥ ७ ॥

The Garuḍa Purāna is said to be His marrow, and the Brahmāṇḍa Purāna is sung of as His bones. In this very manner, Lord Hari/Viṣṇu manifested with the Purāṇas as His body parts. (7)

हृदयं तत्र वै पाद्मं यच्छ्रुत्वामृतमश्नुते । पाद्ममेतत्पुराणं तु स्वयं देवोभवद्धरिः ॥ ८ ॥

Among those, the Padma is the heart, by hearing which one experiences immortality. This Padma Purāna is indeed Lord Hari Himself manifested. (8)

According to this passage, the Śiva Puran is the LEFT ARM of Bhagavān Hari himself. The Liṅga Puran is the RIGHT ANKLE of Sri Hari. The Skanda Puran is the HAIR on the body of Bhagavān Hari. The Kurma Puran is the BACK of Sri Hari. The Matsya Puran is the STOMACH of Lord Hari.

To dismiss the Śaiva Purāṇas just because they are “Tamasic” is to dismiss a body part of Bhagavān Hari himself for even Vaiṣṇavas. The Padma Puran that states this is not a peripheral text to Vaishnavism, but the very heart of Bhagavān Viṣṇu.

2.2 Garuḍa Purāṇa’s Contradiction

If you read the passage of Padma Puran, Uttarakhanda carefully, Garud Puran, which is classified as a Sattvic Puran, praises a Tāmasika Puran as greater than the other Purāṇas, including the Sattvic Purāṇas. In Brahmakhanda, Chapter 1, Verse 50 it says:

Tṛtīyāṃśa-śravaṇād arthatash ca puṇyaṃ āhuḥ paṭhato vai daśāṃśam | tato varaṃ matsya-purāṇam āhuḥ tato varaṃ kūrma-purāṇam āhuḥ

They say that by hearing a third part [of the Purāṇa], or by understanding its meaning, one attains merit; and by reading it, one attains a tenth part of that merit. They say the Matsya Purāṇa is better than that, and they say the Kūrma Purāṇa is even better than that.

Here, a Sattvic Purāṇa has established the supremacy of the Matsya Puran and Kurma Puran, which are considered “Tamasic” Purāṇas.

The Matsya Puran has an explanation for this:

In Chapter 53, Verses 67–68, it says: "The glory of Hari is greater in sāttvika Purāṇas; the glory of Brahmā is greater in rājasa Purāṇas; and that of Agni and Śiva is greater in tāmasa Purāṇas. In mixed Purāṇas, the glory of Sarasvatī and the Pitṛs is said to be greater."

2.3 Supremacy of Śiva in Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas

In the Padma Puran only, which classifies the Śaiva Purāṇas as “Tamasic”, in the 33rd Chapter of Svarga Khanda says, in verses 57-59, Narad Muni establishes Śiva’s supremacy.

यथानारायणः श्रेष्ठो देवानां पुरुषोत्तमः । यथेश्वराणां गिरिशः स्थानानामेतदुत्तमम् ॥ ५८ ॥

Just as Nārāyaṇa, the Puruṣottama, is the greatest among the Devas, and just as Giriśa (Śiva) is the greatest among the lords (Īśvaras), so is this place (Avimukta) the highest among all sacred places.

Note that the identity of Narayan is tied to that of a Devata, while Śiva is identified as Ishvara.

Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtras (specifically the Devatā-adhikāraṇa, 9.1.6–10), Maharṣi Jaimini and the commentator Śabara Svāmī define a deity strictly through the lens of ritual grammar.

The Rule: Tyāgoddheśyatvaṁ devatātvam

त्यागोद्देश्यत्वं देवतात्वम्

The Meaning: "Deity-hood consists solely in being the intended recipient of a ritual relinquishment."

Mīmāṃsā strips deities of physical forms (vigraha) or personal, independent wealth in the empirical sense. Instead, a Devatā is a functional, structural placeholder within a sacrifice (Yajña). They are defined entirely by the fact that a sacrificial offering (Havis) is poured into the fire for them while their name is uttered in the dative case (e.g., Agnaye svāhā—"To Agni, svāhā."). Thus, a Devatā is inherently bound to a cooperative, cosmic-ritual economy.

In contrast, the word Īśvara does not originate from a ritual-functional category, but from an intrinsic, verbal action in Sanskrit grammar.

The Root: It is derived from the Pāṇinian Dhātu īś (īśa aiśvarye —

ईश ऐश्वर्ये

The Meaning: "To possess absolute lordship, sovereignty, or to rule unconditionally."

An Īśvara is an independent master who commands, governs, and exercises unchecked autonomy. While a Devatā’s power is relational and structural (dependent on the Veda, the mantra, and the ritual space), an Īśvara's power is ontological and absolute.

In Srimad Bhagavat Puran, the core Vaiṣṇava text of Vaishnavism, Śiva is described as Supreme again:

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 3.14.25–26

श्मशानचक्रानिलधूलिधूम्र- विकीर्णविद्योतजटाकलाप: । भस्मावगुण्ठामलरुक्‍मदेहो देवस्त्रिभि: पश्यति देवरस्ते ॥ २५ ॥ न यस्य लोके स्वजन: परो वा नात्याद‍ृतो नोत कश्चिद्विगर्ह्य: । वयं व्रतैर्यच्चरणापविद्धा- माशास्महेऽजां बत भुक्तभोगाम् ॥ २६ ॥

Your brother-in-law, God Rudra, whose tawny matted hair is scattered by the winds of the cremation ground, and whose golden body is smeared with ashes--sees everything with his three eyes (the Sun, Moon, and Fire). Thus, nothing remains hidden from him. He has no bias--no one is his particular relative or outsider, nor does he show special preference or contempt for anyone. Though we perform various vows hoping for the powers of Māyā that he once possessed and renounced, wise people instead glorify his detached way of life. He has none equal or superior to him. He is the ultimate refuge of saintly beings, yet he chooses to live the austere life of a piśāca

Even in Bhagavat Puran, Śiva is described as beyond dualities.

3. Viṣṇu as Tāmasic and Śiva as Sāttvic

3.1 Śiva Purāṇa on the True Nature of Viṣṇu

For most of this document up until now has only taken references from Sattvic Purāṇas from the Vaiṣṇava perspective. Even though Śiva’s supremacy is established even while being called “tamasic” in Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas, it is actually Viṣṇu who is Tamasic and Śiva as Sattvic from a Śaiva perspective:

Let us understand this from the perspective of the Śiva Puran.

In Chapter 16 of the Rudra Saṃhitā, Brahma explains the true nature of Śiva and Viṣṇu:

Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā 16.36

वामांगो यस्य वैकुंठो दक्षिणांगोऽहमेव च। रुद्रो हृदयजो यस्य त्रिविधस्तु शिव: स्मृत:॥ ३६ ॥

Lord Śiva is known in three forms: He whose left side is Vaikuntha (Viṣṇu), whose right side is myself (Brahma), and Rudra is born from His heart.

Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā 16.37

अहं विष्णुश्च रुद्रश्च गुणास्त्रय उदाहृता:। स्वयं सदा निर्गुणश्च परब्रह्माव्यय: शिव:॥ ३७ ॥

I (Brahma), Viṣṇu, and Rudra are cited as the examples of the three Guṇas (cosmic attributes). However, Supreme Brahman, the imperishable Lord Śiva Himself, always remains completely beyond these attributes (Nirguna).

Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā 16.38

विष्णु: सत्त्वं रजोऽहं च तमो रुद्र उदाहृत:। लोकाचारात इत्येवं नामतो वस्तुतोऽन्यथा॥ ३८ ॥

Viṣṇu is designated as Sattva, I (Brahma) as Rajas, and Rudra is cited as Tamas. This distinction is made nominally in worldly convention, but in reality (the ultimate truth), it is entirely different.

Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā 16.39

अंतस्तमो बहि: सत्त्वो विष्णू रुद्रस्तथा मत:। अंत: सत्त्वस्तमो बाह्यो रजोऽहं सर्वथा मुने॥ ३९ ॥

O Sage. Viṣṇu is considered to have Tamas internally and Sattva externally; whereas Rudra has Sattva internally and Tamas externally. As for me (Brahma), I am entirely filled with Rajas both internally and externally.

Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra Saṃhitā 16.40

राजसी च सुरा देवी सत्त्वरूपा तु सा सती। लक्ष्मीस्तमोमयी ज्ञेया त्रिरूपा च शिवा परा॥ ४० ॥

Similarly, Goddess Sura (Sarasvatī) is Rajasic, Goddess Sati is the embodiment of Sattva, and Goddess Lakshmi should be known as Tamasic. In this manner, the Supreme Goddess (Paramba) should also be understood in three forms.

3.2 Tāmasic Nature of Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas

Furthermore, Skanda Puran, Maheśvara Khanda, Daksha Khanda, Chapter 29, the true nature of Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas as Tamasic is revealed:

आगमान्त गिरो नित्यं अमृतं प्रवदन्ति हि। तद्वदेव पुराणानि शिवं साम्बं त्रियम्बकम्॥ ४३ ॥

The words of the Āgamas and Upaniṣads eternally proclaim the immortal truth. In the same manner, the Purāṇas praise Lord Śiva, who is accompanied by the Divine Mother (Amba) and is three-eyed.

स्वतन्त्रमेकमद्वैतं प्रवदन्ति निरङ्कुशम्। तत्र स्कान्दं प्रवक्ष्यामि पुराणेषु त्रयोदशम्॥ ४४ ॥

They declare Him to be independent, one without a second (non-dual), and completely unrestricted. Among those Purāṇas, I shall now narrate the Skanda Purāṇa, which is the thirteenth.

संहिताभिः तथा षड्भिः लक्षैर्ग्रन्थैर्विराजितम्। दश चैव पुराणानि सात्त्विकानि विदुर्बुधाः॥ ४५ ॥

It shines brilliantly with six Saṃhitās and contains one hundred thousand verses. The wise scholars know that ten of the Purāṇas are Sattvic.

श्रद्धेयानि द्विजवरैः तेषां धर्मास्तु तत्रयत्। सत्त्वं शुक्लं समादिष्टं सुखज्ञानपदं तु यत्॥ ४६ ॥

These are worthy of faith by the foremost of the twice-born (Brāhmaṇas), and their duties are established therein. Sattva is the source of happiness and spiritual knowledge.

विद्योपदेष्टा योगिभ्यः शुद्धस्फटिक संनिभः। न निद्राति शिवः क्वापि ब्राह्मणाधिपतिः श्रुतः॥ ४७ ॥

Lord Śiva, who resembles a pure crystal, is the instructor of supreme knowledge to the yogis. He never sleeps anywhere and is heard of as the lord and master of the Brāhmaṇas.

व्रतत्येव पुराणानि ब्राह्मणानां कथाः शुभाः। दश चैव पुराणानि हिंसा दोष पराङ्मुखम्॥ ४८ ॥

These Purāṇas indeed contain auspicious narratives and vows meant for Brāhmaṇas. These ten Purāṇas are completely averse to the defects of violence (Himsa).

वैष्णवानि च चत्वारि तामसानि मुनीश्वराः।

O foremost of sages. The four Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas are considered Tamasic. In them, the duties of the Kṣatriyas (warrior class) are heard, and Hari (Viṣṇu) is their presiding deity.

क्षत्रियाणां श्रुता धर्माः तेषु तद्देवता हरिः॥ ४९ ॥

तमः कृष्णमुदासीनं कूटकृत्य विशारदम्। निद्रालस्य प्रमादाद्याः तद्गुणाः परिकीर्तिताः॥ ५० ॥

Tamas is described as black, indifferent, and skilled in illusory or deceptive actions. Sleep, laziness, and delusion are proclaimed to be its inherent attributes.

कृष्णो विष्णुः स्मृतः शेष शायी भक्त विमोहकः। तेषु च क्षत्रियाणां च धर्मा विप्रैरुदाहृताः॥ ५१ ॥

Viṣṇu is known as Kṛṣṇa (or dark-complexioned), who reclines on the serpent Shesha and deludes his devotees. In those texts, the duties of the Kṣatriyas are illustrated by the wise sages.

ब्राह्मेतु राजसे वैश्य सेव्ये सर्वत्र संमते। दुःखास्पदं रक्तवर्णं चञ्चलं च रजो मतम्॥ ५२ ॥

The Brahma Purāṇas are Rajasic, which are universally accepted as fit to be followed by the Vaiśyas (merchant class). Rajas are considered to be red in color, unstable/restless, and the source of misery.

4. Double Standards on Relative and Absolute Supremacy

Even setting aside the classification framework, the Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha applies a revealing double standard in its interpretation of passages. This methodological inconsistency runs through the entire document and must be named explicitly.

When Vedic Brāhmaṇa passages praise Viṣṇu — such as the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa's 'Agni is lowest, Viṣṇu is highest' — the opposing paper reads these as statements of ontological supremacy. When identical language is used for Rudra — such as the Shvetashvatara's 'Eko hi Rudro na dvitiyaya tasthe' (Rudra is one, there is no second to him) — these are suddenly reinterpreted as relative superiority within a domain.

The Shvetashvatara is a PRINCIPAL Upaniṣad in the jnana-kāṇḍa — the section the opposing paper itself designates as 'the actual doctrinally decisive section of the Vedas.' The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa is karma-kāṇḍa — the ritual section the same paper admits is 'doctrinally indecisive.' On the opposing paper's own methodology, the Shvetashvatara should outrank the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa. Yet the hermeneutic works in the opposite direction.

The Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha uses a sophisticated move to resolve Viṣṇu's worship of Śiva: Viṣṇu, as the antaryamin of Rudra, worships himself when he appears to worship Śiva. This is structurally identical to what Śaiva ācāryas say about Viṣṇu — that he is Sadāśiva's Sāttvika manifestation, and Sadāśiva is his antaratman.

The opposing paper itself cites (from the Brahma Saṃhitā) the milk-and-curd analogy: just as milk becomes curd but not vice versa, Viṣṇu enters Rudra but Rudra is not the source of Viṣṇu. The Śaiva inverts this: Sadāśiva (milk) manifests as the Sāttvika Viṣṇu (curd). Which came first — milk or curd? The Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad and the Śiva Purāṇa are unambiguous: Sadāśiva is the source. The Mahābhārat 13.14, where Kṛṣṇa himself says this, is addressed below.

4.1 Kṛṣṇa on Śiva's Supremacy in Mahābhārat

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.180

त्रिभुवनराज्यविभूतिरप्यनिष्टा ॥ १८० ॥

Even the sovereignty over the three worlds is undesirable without Śiva. || 180 ||

By the command of Lord Pashupati (Śiva), I would happily and instantly become an insect or a tree with many branches. However, if I am offered the royal grandeur and wealth of all the three worlds through the boon or favor of anyone other than Lord Śiva, it is completely undesirable to me.

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.181

जन्म श्वपाकमध्येऽपि मेऽस्तु हरचरणवन्दनरतस्य । मा वाणीश्वरभक्तो भवानि भवनेऽपि शक्रस्य ॥ १८१ ॥

Let my birth be even among the outcastes, as long as I am devoted to adoring Hara's feet. Let me not be devoid of devotion to the Lord, O Bhavani, even if I were to reside in the palace of Indra. || 181 ||

If I get the opportunity to remain continuously engaged in praising and worshiping the lotus feet of Lord Śaṅkara, I would gladly accept being born even among the lowest outcastes (Chandalas). But, if I am devoid of exclusive devotion to Lord Śiva, I do not wish to have a place even in the palace of Indra (the king of gods).

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.182

वाय्वम्बुभुजोऽपि सतो नरस्य दुःखक्षयः कुतस्तस्य । भवति हि सुरासुरगुरौ यस्य न विश्वेश्वरे भक्तिः ॥ १८२ ॥

Even for a man subsisting only on air and water, how can there be an end to his miseries, if he lacks devotion to Vishveshwara (Lord of the Universe), the Lord of both gods and demons? || 182 ||

Even if a person performs severe penance by surviving solely on water or air, how can his sufferings ever come to an end if he does not possess devotion toward Lord Vishwanath (Śiva), who is the supreme guru of both the deities (Suras) and the demons (Asuras)?

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.186, 187

अपि कीटः पतङ्गो वा भवेयं शङ्कराज्ञया । न तु शक्र त्वया दत्तं त्रैलोक्यमपि कामये ॥ १८६ ॥ श्वापि महेश्वरवचनाद् भवामि स हि नः परः कामः । त्रिदशगणराज्यमपि खलु नेच्छाम्यमहेश्वराज्ञप्तम् ॥ १८७ ॥

By the command of Śaṅkara, I would even become an insect or a moth. But, O Shakra (Indra), I do not desire even the three worlds if given by you. || 186 || Even if I become a dog by the word of Maheśvara, that indeed is my highest desire. Truly, I do not wish for sovereignty over the hosts of gods if it is not commanded by Maheśvara. || 187 ||

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.188

न नाकपृष्ठं न च देवराज्यं न ब्रह्मलोकं न च निष्कलत्वम् । न सर्वकामानखिलान् वृणोमि हरस्य दासत्वमहं वृणोमि ॥ १८८ ॥

Neither do I choose the vault of heaven, nor the kingdom of the gods, neither the realm of Brahma, nor final liberation (formless absorption). I do not choose the fulfillment of all worldly desires combined; I only choose servitude to Hara (Lord Śiva). || 188 ||

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.189

यावच्छशाङ्कधवलामलबाद्धमौलि- र्न प्रीयते पशुपतिर्भगवान् ममेशः । तावज्जरामरणजन्मशताभिघातै- र्दुःखानि देहविहितानि समुद्वहामि ॥ १८९ ॥

As long as my Lord Bhagavān Paśupati, whose crown is adorned with a bright, spotless crescent moon, is not pleased, I bear the bodily sufferings caused by the hundreds of blows of birth, old age, and death.

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva 13.14.98, PDF verse 190

दिवसकरशशाङ्कवह्निदीप्तं त्रिभुवनसारमपारमाद्यमेकम् । अजरममरमप्रसाद्य रुद्रं जगति पुमानिह को लभेत शान्तिम् ॥

He who shines with the splendor of His eyes—the Sun, the Moon, and the Fire; who is the essence of the three worlds, beyond whom there is no higher essence; who is the primeval cause, the peerless, the ageless, and the immortal— without pleasing that Rudra, what man in this world can ever attain true peace? || 190 ||

5. Evidence from Śruti on the Supremacy of Śiva

5.1 Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad and Ādi Śaṅkarācārya on the Supremacy of Śiva

The Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha relies heavily on the argument that Viṣṇu is the Antaryamin (indwelling soul) of Śiva, claiming that "Rudra is not actually the supreme lord" but merely a vessel for Viṣṇu.

However, in his commentary on Verse 1.12 of Shvestahsvara Upaniṣad, Adi Śaṅkarācārya identifies the "Impeller" (Prerita)—the indwelling ruler of both the individual soul (Bhokta) and the material world (Bhogya)—specifically as the Parameshvara described in this Upaniṣad. Since the Shvetashvatara identifies this Lord as Rudra (Verse 3.2), the logic of indwelling is flipped: Śiva is the Impeller (Prerita) and Indweller (Antaryamin) of Viṣṇu.

If Viṣṇu is the "Sāttvika manifestation" used for preservation, he is part of the "triad" (Trividham) that is impelled by the supreme Prerita (Śiva). Viṣṇu is "Tamasic internally" because he is "deluded" into sleep on Shesha, whereas Śiva is the "Sattvic internal" instructor of yogis who "never sleeps".

Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad 1.12:

Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 1.12

एतज्ज्ञेयं नित्यमेवात्मसंस्थं नातः परं वेदितव्यं हि किञ्चित् ।

This is to be known as eternally existing within oneself; indeed, there is nothing higher than this to be known.

Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 1.12

भोक्ता भोग्यं प्रेरितारं च मत्वा सर्वं प्रोक्तं त्रिविधं ब्रह्ममेतत् ॥ १२ ॥

By understanding the enjoyer (the individual soul), the object of enjoyment (the world), and the ruler (the Supreme Lord), all this threefold reality is declared to be Brahman.

Adi Śaṅkarācārya’s commentary on Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad 1.12:

Śaṅkara’s Commentary (Sanskrit Bhashya)

एतत्प्रकृतं केवलात्मकाशमङ्गरूपं नित्यं नियमेन ज्ञेयम् ।

This subject under discussion (Rudra), which is the pure light of the Self and the very form of one's own nature, must eternally and invariably be known.

किमन्यसंस्थं न खात्मसंस्थं ज्ञेयं नानात्मनि बाह्ये ।

Is it established in something else? No, it is established in one's own Self; it is to be known there, not in the external non-Self.

श्रूयते च—

And the Śruti (Scripture) states: "The wise who perceive Him existing within their own Self, to them belongs eternal peace, and to no others." (Katha Upaniṣad 2.2.12).

कथमेतज्ज्ञेयम्? इत्याह—

How is this to be known? To this, the verse says:

भोक्ता जीवो भोग्यमितरत्सर्वं प्रेरितान्तर्यामी परमेश्वरः ।

The 'enjoyer' is the individual soul, the 'object of enjoyment' is everything else (the objective world), and the 'ruler' is the inner controller, the Supreme Lord.

तदेतत्रिविधं प्रोक्तं ब्रह्मैवेति ।

This threefold reality is declared to be Brahman alone.

भोक्त्राद्यशेषभेदप्रपञ्चविलापनेनै वनिर्विशेषं ब्रह्मात्मानं जानीयादित्यर्थः ।

The meaning is that by dissolving the entire phenomenal universe of differences—such as the enjoyer, etc.—one should realize the attribute-less Brahman as one's own Self.

By identifying the Prerita (the Lord of this Upaniṣad) as the source of this "dissolution," Śaṅkara establishes that Śiva is not a "domain-specific" deity but the Absolute Brahman in whom the entire "triad" of existence is resolved. This confirms the Śaiva document’s point: the Shvetashvatara, as a Principal Upaniṣad, must outrank the ritual Brāhmaṇas used by Vaiṣṇavas to claim Viṣṇu's supremacy.

The Vaiṣṇava case uses the Brahma Saṃhitā to argue that Viṣṇu is "milk" and Śiva is "curd" (a transformed product). However, Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad and Adi Śaṅkarācārya in Verse 1.12 and its commentary prove the opposite. Śaṅkara explains that the Prerita (Śiva) is the Parameshvara who is "eternal and residing in the Self" (nityam-eva-atmasamstham).

This establishes Śiva as the Abhinna-Nimitta-Upadana Karana (the non-different material and efficient cause).

- Śiva is the Milk: The eternal, unchanging ground (Atmasamstham). - Viṣṇu is the Curd: The functional manifestation (Bhogya/Bhokta triad) that arises for the purpose of the Lila.

This aligns with the Śiva Purāṇa’s statement that Viṣṇu is merely the "left side" of Śiva, a functional form born for the sake of the universe.

The Vaiṣṇava document claims that the word "Brahman" is used "principally for Viṣṇu" and only as a "courtesy" for Śiva. Śaṅkara’s commentary on 1.12 explicitly contradicts this by stating:

Ādi Śaṅkarācārya, Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad Bhāṣya 1.12

तदेतत्रिविधं प्रोक्तं ब्रह्मैवेति ।

This triad [including the Impeller] is proclaimed as Brahman indeed.

By identifying Rudra/Śiva as the Prerita (the Ruler and source of the triad), Śaṅkara confirms that the term "Brahman" refers primarily and factually to Śiva. As Kṛṣṇa himself admits in the Mahābhārata, he would rather be an insect by Śiva's command than rule the three worlds by any other's favor, because "there is no higher essence" than the one who is the "primeval cause”.

5.2 The Vaiṣṇava Claim: "Relative Superiority" and "Courtesy"

Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha argues that Vedic praises of Rudra, such as "Rudra is one, there is no second to him," must be interpreted as "relative superiority" within a specific domain, similar to praises given to Indra. It further asserts that titles like "Brahman" and "Bhagavān" primarily and factually refer only to Viṣṇu, while their application to Śiva is a mere "secondary courtesy" or for the purpose of worship. Finally, it claims that the core scholarly work of Adi Śaṅkarācārya is "unequivocally Vaiṣṇava" at the pragmatic (Vyāvahārika) level.

While the Hari Paratva Vimarsha uses ritualistic Karma-kāṇḍa texts (like the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa) to claim Viṣṇu's "absolute" supremacy, they dismiss the Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad—a Principal Upaniṣad in the doctrinally decisive Jnana-kāṇḍa—as "relative". On the Vaiṣṇava's own methodological grounds, a principal Upaniṣad must outrank ritual sections.

Verse 3.2 of Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad says:

Verse 3.2 (Sanskrit)

Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.2

एको हि रुद्रो न द्वितीयाय तस्थु- र्य इमाँल्लोकानीशत ईशनीभिः । प्रत्यङ् जनांस्तिष्ठति संचुकोचान्तकाले संसृज्य विश्वा भुवनानि गोपाः ॥ २ ॥

Because there is only one Rudra, the knowers of Brahman (the ultimate truth) do not recognize any second entity beside Him. He rules all these worlds with His ruling powers (Ishanibhih). He is the indwelling Self of all beings, appearing in every form. Having created all the worlds and being their protector, He withdraws them into Himself at the time of dissolution.

Here is Adi Śaṅkarācārya’s commentary on the verse:

एको हीति । हिशब्दो यस्मादर्थे ।

He is indeed one..." (Quoting the Upaniṣadic verse). The word hi (indeed/for) is used in the sense of "because".

यस्मादेक एव रुद्रः स्वतो न द्वितीयाय वस्त्वन्तराय तस्थुर्ब्रह्मविदः परमार्थदर्शिनः ।

Because Rudra (the Divine) is inherently one without a second; He does not stand for the existence of any second entity. This is the view of the knowers of Brahman, the seers of the Ultimate Truth.

उक्तं च—एको रुद्रो न द्वितीयाय तस्थुरिति ।

As it has been stated (in the scripture): "Rudra is truly one; they do not stand for a second.

य इमाँल्लोकानीशते नियमयतीशनीभिः ।

He who rules over and regulates all these worlds by means of His divine, governing powers (īśanīs).

सर्वांश्च जनान्प्रत्यन्तरः प्रतिपुरुषमवस्थितः ।

And He exists as the innermost Self (pratyantara) within all living beings, residing inside every single person.

रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूवेत्यर्थः ।

The meaning is: "He assumes a form corresponding to every form" (He reflects Himself in every individual body).

किञ्च, संचुकोच अन्तकाले प्रलयकाले किं कृत्वा? संसृज्य विश्वा भुवनानि गोपा गोप्ता भूत्वा ।

Furthermore, at the end of time—at the time of cosmic dissolution (pralaya)—what does He do? Having projected (saṁsṛjya) all the worlds, and having been their protector (gopā/goptā), He withdraws (dissolves) them into Himself.

एतदुक्तं भवति—अद्वितीयः परमात्मा, न चासौ कुम्भकारवदात्मानं केवलं मृत्पिण्डस्थानीयमुपादानकारणमुपादत्ते ।

The core meaning intended here is this: The Supreme Self (Paramātmā) is non-dual. He does not act like a potter, who takes a separate lump of clay to use as the material cause (upādāna-kāraṇa) to make a pot.

किं तर्हि? स्वशक्तिविक्षेपं कुर्वन्स्रष्टा नियन्ता चाभिधीयत इति ।

What then is the case? By projecting His own inherent power (sva-śakti-vikṣepa), He is designated as both the Creator and the Ruler of the universe.

उत्तरो मन्त्रस्तस्यैव विराडात्मनावस्थानं तत्स्रष्टृत्वं प्रतिपादयति ॥ २ ॥

The subsequent verse establishes His existence as the cosmic physical reality (Virāṭ-ātmā) and demonstrates His nature as the Creator of that reality.

Adi Śaṅkarācārya himself states:

Ādi Śaṅkarācārya on Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.2

यस्मादेक एव रुद्रः स्वतो न द्वितीयाय वस्त्वन्तराय तस्थुर्ब्रह्मविदः परमार्थदर्शिनः ।

Because Rudra (the Divine) is inherently one without a second; He does not stand for the existence of any second entity. This is the view of the knowers of Brahman, the seers of the Ultimate Truth.

So this idea that Adi Śaṅkarācārya was “unequivocally a Vaiṣṇava” is false.

In the language of Vedānta, Paramārtha refers to the Absolute Reality, as opposed to Vyavahāra (relative reality). By identifying the realization of Rudra’s non-duality with the "ultimate truth," Śaṅkara confirms that this is not "courtesy" praise but a statement of Para-Tattva.

By describing Rudra as Paramārtha, and Viṣṇu as supreme in Vyavahāra, Adi Śaṅkarācārya actually accepted Rudra as the milk and Viṣṇu as the curd, in terms of the analogy made by Caligayla Ji in Hari Paratattva Vimarsha.

If, as Śaṅkara says, the Paramarthadarshins recognize no second besides Rudra, then the Vaiṣṇava claim that "Nārāyaṇa alone was in the beginning" must be reconciled to Śiva as the ultimate Antaryamin (indwelling soul).

By Śaṅkara’s own logic in this principal text, Śiva is the "primeval cause" and the "highest essence" beyond whom there is no other. Viṣṇu, as the Sāttvika manifestation used for the preservation of the world, is thus a functional form of the absolute Sadāśiva, who remains "completely beyond all attributes (Nirguna)".

Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha argues that terms like "Brahman" and "Bhagavān" belong primarily and factually only to Viṣṇu. It dismisses their application to Śiva as a "secondary courtesy" or merely for the purpose of worship. However, the Śaiva position, supported by the doctrinally decisive Jnana-kāṇḍa, uses etymology not as a "courtesy," but ontologically.

5.3 "Hara" as the Destroyer of the Root of Bondage

In Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha, Śiva is reduced to a functional "destroyer" of the physical universe, a role subordinate to the "creator", Viṣṇu. Adi Śaṅkara’s commentary on Verse 1.10 destroys this reductionist view:

He defines "Hara" as the Parameshvara because He "removes" or "destroys" (harana) the very root of material bondage Ignorance (Avidya).

Śaṅkara explicitly identifies this "Hara" as the "non-dual Supreme Self, who is Consciousness-Existence-Bliss" (chitsadanandad-vitiyah paramatma).

If "Hara" is the one who removes the Avidya that keeps the soul in the cycle of birth and death, he is not a "functional manifestation" within the world; he is the Absolute Ground that precedes and dissolves the play of the world (Lila).

Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad Verse 1.10

क्षरं प्रधानममृताक्षरं हरः

Perishable is primeval matter (Prakriti), and immortal and imperishable is Hara (the Lord).

क्षरात्मानावीशते देव एकः ।

The one divine Lord rules over both the perishable matter and the individual soul.

तस्याभिध्यानाद्योजनात्तत्त्व भावात् भूयश्चान्ते विश्वमायानिवृत्तिः ॥ १० ॥

By meditating upon Him, by uniting with Him, and by entering into His true being, there is in the end a complete cessation of the cosmic illusion (Maya).

Śaṅkara’s Commentary (Sanskrit Bhashya)

क्षरं प्रधानममृताक्षरं हर इति ।

Perishable is primeval matter, and immortal and imperishable is Hara"—this is the text.

अविद्यादेर्हरणात्परमेश्वरो हरः ।

Because He destroys (takes away) ignorance and its effects, the Supreme Lord is called Hara.

अमृतं च तदक्षरं चामृताक्षरममृतं ब्रह्मेश्वर इत्यर्थः ।

That which is both immortal and imperishable is amṛtākṣaram; the meaning is that the Lord is the immortal Brahman.

स ईश्वरः क्षरात्मानौ प्रधानपुरुषावीशत इष्टे देव एकश्चित्सदानन्दाद्वितीयः परमात्मा ।

That Lord rules over both the perishable matter and the individual soul (Pradhana and Puruṣa). He is the one Divine Being, the Supreme Self, who is non-dual Existence-Consciousness-Bliss (Cit-Sat-Ananda).

तस्य परमात्मनोऽभिध्यानात्, कथम्?

By meditating upon that Supreme Self—how?

योजनाज्जीवानां परमात्मसंयोजनात्तत्त्वभावात्

By "uniting"—meaning the joining of the individual souls with the Supreme Self through the realization of the absolute truth of the identity: "I am Brahman" (Aham Brahmasmi).

भूयश्चासकृदन्ते प्रारब्धकर्मान्ते

Furthermore, "in the end"—meaning repeatedly at the end of the fruits of one's past actions (Prarabdha Karma);

यद्वाऽऽत्मज्ञानोत्पत्तिरन्तस्तस्मिन्ना त्मज्ञानोदयवेलायां विश्वमायानिवृत्तिः ।

Or rather, "the end" refers to the dawn of Self-knowledge; at the very moment of the awakening of Self-knowledge, there is the cessation of the cosmic illusion (Visva-Maya-Nivritti).

सुख-दुःखमोहात्मकाशेषप्रपञ्चरूपमाया निर्वृत्तिः ॥ १० ॥

This means the complete termination of Maya, which manifests as the entire phenomenal universe consisting of pleasure, pain, and delusion.

The śāstras admit Viṣṇu uses "illusory or deceptive actions" (Maya) to "mystify His devotees" and labels his internal nature as Tamasic (the quality of sleep and delusion) while reclining on Shesha.

But the source of Maya itself, this power used by Viṣṇu is Rudra.

Verse 4.10 of Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad says:

मायां तु प्रकृतिं विद्यान्मायिनं तु महेश्वरम् ।

Know then that Māyā (illusion) is primeval matter (Prakṛti), and the Supreme Lord (Maheśvara) is the wielder of Māyā.

तस्यावयवभूतै स्तु व्याप्तं सर्वमिदं जगत् ॥ १० ॥

This entire moving universe is filled with beings that are like limbs (parts or manifestations) of Him.

मायां तु प्रकृतिं विद्यात् । मायेति नाम अव्याकृतस्य ।

Know Māyā to be primeval matter." Māyā is another name for the Unmanifested (Avyākṛta).

तु-शब्दोऽवधारणे । मायामेव प्रकृतिं विद्धि न तु प्रधानं साङ्ख्यपरिकल्पितं स्वातन्त्र्येण परिणामि ।

6.1 Lord Rāma on Supremacy of Śiva

The Hari-Paratva-Vimarsha argues that the deities and Rishis "accepted Viṣṇu as superior" following a functional dispute in the Bālakāṇḍa. The opposing document cites the Bālakāṇḍa (1.74) to claim Śiva was "made inert" by Viṣṇu's valor. This "defeat" is rendered doctrinally irrelevant by the words of Sri Rāma in the Yuddha Khanda (6.123.19). After achieving his greatest victory over Ravana, Rāma identifies the source of his success:

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha Kāṇḍa 6.123.19

स्कन्धावारनिवेशनम् || ६-१२३-१९ अत्र पूर्वं महादेवः प्रसादमकरोत्प्रभुः |

If Śiva were ontologically inferior or "stunned" by Viṣṇu, the "Sāttvika" Rāma would have no need for Śiva's Prasada. By crediting his victory to Śiva's grace, the Rāmāyaṇa establishes that the Bālakāṇḍa incident was merely a functional world-play (lila), while the Yuddha Khanda reveals the permanent truth: Śiva is the sovereign Giver, and the Avatar is the grateful Receiver.

A common Vaiṣṇava strategy to explain Rāma’s worship of Śiva is the claim that he does so only to "set an example for the world" and is actually worshipping himself as the Antaryamin of Śiva. This defense is inapplicable to the Yuddha Khanda verse. Rāma is speaking privately to Sita; he is not performing a public ritual for the benefit of subjects. He is stating a historical and spiritual fact: his victory was enabled by the grace of the Supreme Lord Śiva.

The Rāmāyaṇa proves that Śiva is the Sattvic Bestower of Grace who empowers the avatar to fulfill his mission. The "Sāttvika" Viṣṇu is a functional manifestation of the absolute Sadāśiva. Rāma’s recognition of Śiva as the Prabhu who gave him Prasada is not a "courtesy," but the ultimate recognition of the Para-Tattva from whom all avatars derive their power.

सर्वत्रात्र ह्यहंशब्दो बोधमात्रैकवाचकः। स भोक्तृप्रभुशब्दाभ्यां याज्ययष्टृतयोदितः ॥१३२॥

Here (in the Bhagavadgītā) the word 'I' aham, has been used in the sense of pure consciousness alone. It is He who has been referred to by the words 'enjoyer', bhoktṛ, and Lord, prabhu, in the sense of the object of the sacrifice as well as the agent of it by the words bearing those meanings respectively. (132)

Verse 132 in particular is the exact conclusion that Adi Śaṅkarācārya and Shvetashvara Upaniṣad itself made on the supremacy of Śiva.

While, from the Vaiṣṇava perspective, Kṛṣṇa is addressing himself as supreme, he is addressing his Prerita, as the Supreme Devata in the Bhagavad Gītā, above other anya-devatas, as we established as none other than Śiva/Rudra in Shvetashvara Upaniṣad.

However, Lord Kṛṣṇa himself backs up this claim in the Mahābhārat, Book 12/Shanti Parva, SECTION CCCXLII

Therefore, Lord Rudra is my soul, and I therefore worship Him.

If I do not worship Lord Śiva, the bestower of all auspicious boons,

It occurred to me that no one should worship himself.

तस्माद् रुद्रो हि मे ह्यात्मा ततः पूजां करोम्यहम् । यद्यहं नार्चयेयं वै ईशानं वरदं शिवम् ॥ आत्मानं नार्चयेत् कश्चिद् इति मे भावितं मनः ।

O best of the brāhmaṇas, I shall not offer my obeisances to any other demigod.

I worship myself alone, and therefore I worship Lord Śiva.

नाहं नमस्याम्यन्यस्मै देवाय द्विजसत्तम । आत्मानमेव पूज्यामि तस्माद् रुद्रं यजाम्यहम् ॥

Even if we accept the Vaiṣṇava Position: Mahāviṣṇu descends as the Kṛṣṇa Avatāra to give the Bhagavad Gītā, where He points to Himself and says: "Mattah parataram nānyat" (There is nothing superior to Me).

Mahādeva descends as the Śaṅkarācārya Avatāra to give the Prasthānatrayī Bhāṣyas. In his commentary on the core Vedic Upaniṣads (like the Śvetāśvatara), he points to Śiva and establishes Him as the absolute Paramārtha Reality.

In this passage, Mahādeva consoles the deities and sages about the onset of Kali Yuga, promising to descend to revive the true meaning of the Vedas:

Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa, Pūrva-khaṇḍa 27.25–26

कलिना ग्रसिते लोके विप्ररूपावृतः प्रभुः । करिष्यामि कलां भूमौ शङ्कराख्यां न संशयः ॥

When the world is swallowed by Kali Yuga, I, the Lord, concealing myself in the form of a Brāhmaṇa, will manifest a portion of myself on earth named Śaṅkara, without a doubt.

This text explicitly frames his birth as a direct tactical mission by Śiva to conquer misinterpretations of scripture:

Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa, Pūrva-khaṇḍa 27.25–26

शापानुग्रहशक्तो हि शङ्करो लोकशङ्करः । कलाववतीर्णो लोकेऽस्मिन् वेदमार्गप्रवर्तकः ॥

Śiva, the doer of good to the universe, capable of both cursing and blessing, descended in this world during the age of Kali to re-establish the path of the Vedas.

Śaktisaṅgama Tantra (Chinnamastā Khaṇḍa)

Our Vaiṣṇava brothers often emphasize the supreme authority of the Bhagavad Gītā on the grounds that it features the direct, unmediated words of the Supreme Absolute manifesting on the historical plane as Śrī Kṛṣṇa. However, an identical metaphysical symmetry exists within the Śaiva tradition regarding the structural interpretation of the Upaniṣads.

The Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa (Pūrva 27.25) and the Śaktisaṅgama Tantra explicitly reveal that Lord Śiva took a physical form in Kali Yuga as Adi Śaṅkarācārya to preserve the Vaidika Dharma. Therefore, when analyzing the Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad Bhāṣya, we are not merely reading the notes of a humān scholar; we are witnessing the transcendent Lord Śiva, operating through his Avataric intellect, defining Himself as the ultimate Paramārtha-Tattva.

A key example of Viṣṇu’s subordination to Lord Śiva is found in the Mahābhārat, where Viṣṇu is not only subordinate to Śiva, but also to Brahma, whom he salutes before the true Lord Śiva:

In Dronaparva, Chapter 69, of the Mahābhārat:

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 69.50

हृततेजोबलाः सर्वे तदा सेन्द्रा दिवौकसः | ब्रह्माणं शरणं जग्मुर्वृत्राद्भीता महासुरात् ||५०||

Having lost their energy and strength, all the dwellers of heaven, including Indra, went to Brahma for refuge, terrified of the great Asura Vritra.

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 69.52

अथ पार्श्वे स्थितं विष्णुं शक्रादींश्च सुरोत्तमान् | प्राह तथ्यमिदं वाक्यं विषण्णान्सुरसत्तमान् ||५२||

Then [Brahma] spoke these true words to the dejected superior deities, including Viṣṇu who was standing by his side, and Indra.

स तस्यैव प्रसादाद्वै हन्यादेव रिपुर्बली | नागत्वा शङ्करस्थानं भगवान्दृश्यते हरः ||५५|| दृष्ट्वा हनिष्यथ रिपुं क्षिप्रं गच्छत मन्दरम् | यत्रास्ते तपसां योनिर्दक्षयज्ञविनाशनः ||५६||

Through [Śiva's] grace alone can that powerful enemy be slain. Lord Hara cannot be seen without going to Shankar’s place. Having seen him, you will slay the enemy. Go quickly to Mount Mandara, where the source of all penance and the destroyer of Daksha’s sacrifice resides.)

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 69.57

ते गत्वा सहिता देवा ब्रह्मणा सह मन्दरम् | अपश्यंस्तेजसां राशिं सूर्यकोटिसमप्रभम् ||५७||

Those gods, accompanied by Brahma, went to Mandara and beheld a mass of energy equal in splendor to ten million suns.

In Chapter 173 of Drona Parva, Lord Kṛṣṇa and Ved Vyāsa both again admit the superiority of Śiva:

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.99

एष देवो महादेवो योऽसौ पार्थ तवाग्रतः । सङ्ग्रामे शात्रवान्निघ्नंस्त्वया दृष्टः पिनाकधृक् ॥९९॥

This God is Mahadeva, he who is before you, O Partha; the wielder of the Pinaka, whom you saw slaying enemies in battle.

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.100

एष वै भगवान्देवः सङ्ग्रामे याति तेऽग्रतः । येन दत्तानि तेऽस्त्राणि यैस्त्वया दानवा हताः ॥१००॥

This Lord God indeed goes before you in battle; it is he who gave you the weapons with which you have slain the Danavas.

At the conclusion of this revelation, Vyāsa reassures Arjuna that he is invincible because he has the ultimate support by his side:

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.106

गच्छ युध्यस्व कौन्तेय न तवास्ति पराजयः । यस्य मन्त्री च गोप्ता च पार्श्वतस्ते जनार्दनः ॥१०६॥

Go, fight, O son of Kunti; there is no defeat for you, since you have Janardana (Kṛṣṇa) as your counselor and protector by your side.

Vyāsa again re-iterates the identification of Śiva with the Supreme Deity in the Brāhmaṇas, Prajapati, in the Mahābhārat, Chapter 173:

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.9

प्रजापतीनां प्रथमं तैजसं पुरुषं विभुम् | भुवनं भूर्भुवं देवं सर्वलोकेश्वरं प्रभुम् ||९||

(Vyāsa said: You have seen) the first of Prajapatis, the effulgent Male, the Lord, the Deva of the world, of the earth and the atmosphere, the Lord and Master of all.

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.42

विव्याध कुपितो यज्ञं निर्भयस्तु भवस्तदा | धनुषा बाणमुत्सृज्य सघोषं विननाद च ||४२||

Then Bhava (Śiva), being fearless and angry, pierced the Sacrifice [personified] with an arrow from his bow and uttered a loud roar.

ते न शर्म कुतः शान्तिं लेभिरे स्म सुरास्तदा | विद्रुते सहसा यज्ञे कुपिते च महेश्वरे ||४३||

The gods then found no happiness, nor did they obtain any peace, for the Sacrifice had suddenly fled and Maheśvara was filled with wrath.)

Mahābhārata, Droṇa Parva 173.49

ततो निश्चक्रमुर्देवा वेपमाना नताः स्म तम् | पुनश्च संदधे दीप्तं देवानां निशितं शरम् ||४९||

Then the gods emerged trembling and bowed to him; yet he again aimed a blazing, sharp arrow at the deities.

रुद्रस्य यज्ञभागं च विशिष्टं ते न्वकल्पयन् | भयेन त्रिदशा राजञ्शरणं च प्रपेदिरे ||५०||

O King, out of fear, the three-times-ten [gods] sought refuge in him and assigned a distinguished share of the sacrifice to Rudra.)

ते न शर्म कुतः शान्तिं लेभिरे स्म सुरास्तदा | विद्रुते सहसा यज्ञे कुपिते च महेश्वरे ||४३||" "रुद्रस्य यज्ञभागं च विशिष्टं ते न्वकल्पयन् | भयेन त्रिदशा राजञ्शरणं च प्रपेदिरे ||५०||

Here the Tridashas have been described to flee in terror at sight of Śiva’s wrath, which is identified with the 33 Vishwadevatas of the Brāhmaṇas, who in Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa are also described as subordinate to Śiva/Prajapati.

The phrase "Tridasha" refers to the entire pantheon of deities. The Mahābhārat notes that these gods, driven by fear, "sought refuge" (sharanam) in Rudra and assigned him a "distinguished share" of the sacrifice to appease him. This implies that no deity in the assembly, including the "superior deities" like Viṣṇu mentioned elsewhere in similar crises, was capable of stopping Śiva's destruction of the rite.

Objections

7. Pūrva Pakṣa: Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad

Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, Nārāyaṇa Sūktam, PDF p. 43

नारायणः परं ब्रह्म तत्त्वं नारायणः परः । नारायणः परो ज्योतिरात्मा नारायणः परः ॥ यच्च किञ्चिज्जगत्यस्मिन् दृश्यते श्रूयतेऽपि वा । अन्तर्बहिश्च तत्सर्वं व्याप्य नारायणः स्थितः ॥

Nārāyaṇa is the Supreme Brahman; Nārāyaṇa is the Supreme Reality. Nārāyaṇa is the Supreme Light; Nārāyaṇa is the Supreme Self. Whatever there is in this world that is seen or heard, Nārāyaṇa stands pervading all of that, both internally and externally.

In Sanskrit grammar, proper nouns can be interpreted either as fixed names (Rūḍhi) or by their root etymological meanings (Yauga). Śaiva scholars argue that in the highest Vedantic contexts, words like "Nārāyaṇa" describe the qualities of the absolute consciousness, not a specific deity with a discus and conch.

Nāra means the collection of all souls/entities, and Ayana means the resting place or refuge.

Therefore, Nārāyaṇa literally means "The Ultimate Refuge of all living beings." Who is the ultimate resting place of the universe during cosmic dissolution (Pralaya)? The Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad explicitly states that during dissolution, only Śiva exists as the absolute substratum. Thus, "Nārāyaṇa" here is an epithet describing the all-pervasive, restful nature of Lord Śiva, not Lord Viṣṇu.

The Nārāyaṇa Sūktam does not exist in a vacuum; it is part of the larger Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad. Just a few sections later in the exact same text, the Upaniṣad explicitly identifies this Supreme Reality with names uniquely reserved for Śiva:

ऋतंसत्यंपरंब्रह्मपुरुषंकृष्णपिङ्गलम्।ऊर्ध्वरेतंविरूपाक्षंविश्वरूपायवैनमोनमः॥

(Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad 12.1)

This verse defines the Supreme Brahman as Virūpākṣa (the Three-Eyed One) and Kṛṣṇapiṅgalam (the dark and tawny-colored ascetic). These physical attributes belong exclusively to Lord Śiva. Therefore, the text itself clarifies that the all-pervading "Nārāyaṇa" consciousness is identical to Mahādeva.

Śiva Purāṇa, opening passage, verse 3, PDF numbering

Śiva Purāṇa, opening passage, verse 4, PDF numbering

4. Now tell me about the greatest means to achieve the most perfect weal, the holiest of the holy modes.

Even Vayu Puran, begins with a broader question about the world, and verse 1 invokes both Nara and Narayan, who are the anshas of Viṣṇu:

Verse 6 of Vayu Puran says:

Vāyu Purāṇa, opening passage, verses 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, PDF numbering

2-6. Desirous of knowing (more about) Purāṇas, legends, (etc.) I seek refuge in Lord Brahmā, the creator of the worlds, (who is) omniscient, unvanquished, the noble lord of (everything) past, present and future; God Brahmā, the lord of the universe in whom are inherent the four divine excellences, viz. Unparalleled knowledge, detachment, supremacy and virtue (dharma); (Lord Brahmā) who for ever visualizes all beings (irrespective of their being) manifest or unmanifest, which re-enter the Lord (Brahmā) for cessation of activities (at the time of the dissolution of the universe); (God Brahmā) the creator of the world, the knower of the reality of the world, the knower of the principles, who, resorting to Yoga, created all living beings mobile and immobile; the unborn lord, the creator of everything, the lord in the form of consciousness and the cosmic witness of the world.

9. Supremacy of Śiva in Dharmaśāstras

Because of the bogus claim that Śiva is not in Manusmriti, Hari-Paratattva-Vimarsha has accused Śiva from being absent in Dharmaśāstra literature. However, his absence in Manusmriti is isolated, and not reflective of Dharmaśāstra broadly which supports the supremacy of Śiva:

Laugākṣi Smṛti, Volume 6

त्र्यम्बकसमो नास्ति देव देव घृणानिधिः । प्रसादशीलः प्रीतश्च तथा पुत्रधनप्रदः ॥

Among all Gods, there is none who can equal Bhagavān Tryambaka, who is the ocean of compassion (Karuna Avataram). He is the granter of boons and the bestower of wealth and progeny.

तस्मात्सर्वं परित्यज्य त्र्यम्बकं तमुमापतिम् । त्रियम्बकेण मन्त्रेण पूजयेत्सुसमाहितः ॥

Therefore, leaving all else aside, one should worship Bhagavān Tryambaka, accompanied by Devi Umā, using the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya mantra with a deeply focused mind.

सर्वावस्थां गतो वाऽपि युक्तो वा सर्वपातकैः । ध्यात्वा शिवं कृतार्थःस्यात्सरुद्रश्च भवेच्छनैः ॥

Even those in the lowest of states or burdened with the greatest of sins will obtain the highest bliss, attain ultimate fulfillment, and gradually become like Rudra Himself by meditating on Śiva

Bhagavān Markandeya says in Markandeya Smṛti:

फलानि सर्वपापौघवारकाणि चिरन्तनैः । शैवं पाशुपतं शाक्तं सौरं वैनायकं तथा ।।

The fruits [of these paths] are the destroyers of the multitude of all sins since ancient times. Śaiva, Pāśupata, Śākta, Saura, and Vainayaka...

वैष्णवं चेति भिन्नानि दर्शनानि षड(ङ्वच) । (देव)भेदेन चोक्तानि देवस्त्वेको महेश्वरः

...As well as Vaiṣṇava—these are the six different philosophical systems (Darshanas). They are spoken of as different due to the diversity of deities, but the Divine is only one: Maheśvara.

शिवो नारायणो ब्रह्मा सूर्यः सोमोऽनिलोऽनलः । भैरवस्कन्दविघ्नेशाः लोकेशाश्च नवग्रहाः ।।

He is Śiva, Nārāyaṇa, Brahma, Surya (Sun), Soma (Moon), Anila (Wind), and Anala (Fire). He is Bhairava, Skanda, Vighnesha (Ganesha), the Lokapalas (world guardians), and the Navagrahas (nine planets).

भुवनानि तथा (देवी राधा) लक्ष्मी सरस्वती । दुर्गा स्वाहा स्वधा देवी शचीन्द्राणी महेश्वरी ।।

He is the cosmic worlds, as well as (Goddess Radha), Lakshmi, and Sarasvatī. He is Durgā, Svāhā, Svadhā, Devi Shachindrani (Indrani), and Māheśvarī.

सत्यं ज्ञानमनन्तं च कः किं यत्तन्महच्छिवम् । स्त्रीपुंसादिविभेदेन सर्वे शब्दा ममैव हि

He is Truth, Knowledge, and the Infinite; what else is that Great Śiva? Through distinctions of male, female, etc., all words are indeed indicators of Me alone...

......वाचका एव व्यवहारेषु ते परम् । निर्दिष्टा एव तेनैव तत्संकेतमहत्वतः ।।

...Merely designations used in worldly dealings. They are specified by that alone due to the cosmic importance of His indication.

भिन्नभिन्नतया भान्ति तन्मायापरिकल्पनात् । विचार्यमाणे ते सर्वे सूक्ष्मदृष्ट्या श्रु.......... ।।

They appear as separate and diverse due to the mental projection of His Maya (illusion). When analyzed with a subtle vision according to the Śruti (scriptures)...

......वाचका एव न तु तत्तन्निरूपकाः । एकस्य शंकरस्यास्य पूजनातसर्वनाकिनाम् ।।

...They are only expressive names and do not fully define that Reality on their own. By the worship of this one Lord Śaṅkara, all the denizens of heaven (all gods) stand worshipped.

The Vaiṣṇavas invoke Brihat Parashara Hora Śāstra, but Parashara says in Parashara Smṛti, Chapter 11, Verse 84, where the Nārāyaṇa Sūktam is explicitly identified by Parashara with Śiva, and not Viṣṇu:

शिखा तस्य तु रुद्रस्योत्तरनारायणं द्विजः । शिरः पुरुषसूक्तं च शिवसङ्कल्पकं च हृत् ॥१४७॥

O Twice-born (Dvija), the Shikha (crest/tuft of hair) of that Rudra is the Uttara-Nārāyaṇa hymn; His head is the Puruṣa Sukta, and His heart is the Śiva-Sankalpa hymn.

कवचं चाप्रतिरथं नेत्रं विभ्राट् बृहत्पिबन् । शतरुद्रीयमन्त्रेण देवस्यास्त्रं प्रकल्पयेत् ॥१४८॥

His Kavacha (protective armor) is the Apratiratha hymn, His Netra (eyes) are the Vibhrat hymn, and one should invoke the Astra (weapon) of the Lord using the Shatarudriya (Shri Rudram) mantra.

पञ्चाङ्गानि स्मरेदष्टप्रणवं च जपेद्विजः । उद्धृत्य प्रणवेशं विकिरिद्रं विमर्जयेत् ॥१४९॥

The twice-born practitioner should contemplate these five limbs (Panchanga-Nyasa) and chant the eight Pranavas (Om). Raising the Lord of Pranava, one should perform the ritual dismissals (Vimarjayet).
