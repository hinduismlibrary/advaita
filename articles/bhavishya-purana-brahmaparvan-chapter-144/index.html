Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 144 — The Origin of the Sun-Priests

BP 1.144.1

सुमन्तुरुवाच । अथाजगाम भगवान्व्यासो द्वारवतीं पुरीम् । द्रष्टुं नारायणं देवं शङ्खचक्रगदाधरम् ॥

Sumantu said: Then the blessed Vyāsa came to the city of Dvāravatī to see the god Nārāyaṇa, who bears the conch, discus, and mace.

BP 1.144.2

तमागतमृषिं दृष्ट्वा वासुदेवो विशांपते । अभ्युत्थाय महातेजाः पूजयामास भारत ॥

Having seen the seer who had come, Vāsudeva, O lord of peoples, the greatly radiant one, rose and worshipped him, O Bhārata.

BP 1.144.3

स्वयमेवासनं दत्त्वा पाद्यमर्घ्यं तथैव च । पप्रच्छ प्रयतो भूत्वा व्यासं सत्यवतीसुतम् ॥

Having himself offered a seat, water for washing the feet, and a water offering as well, he became attentive and questioned Vyāsa, the son of Satyavatī.

BP 1.144.4

य एते भोजका विप्रा आनीता मत्सुतेन वै । शाकद्वीपमितो गत्वा ज्ञानिनो मोक्षगामिनः ॥

These Sun-priest Brahmins have indeed been brought by my son, who went from here to Śākadvīpa; they are wise and journeying toward liberation.

BP 1.144.5

तान्दृष्ट्वा रूपतो विप्र प्रवेशात्कर्मतस्तथा । कौतूहलं समुत्पन्नं हर्षश्च परमो मम ॥

Seeing them in appearance, in initiation, and in conduct, O Brahmin, great curiosity and supreme joy arose in me.

BP 1.144.6

कथमेते क्षणमपि तिष्ठन्ते पृथिवीतले । येषां रविः सदा पूज्यस्तेषां मुक्तिः सदा वसेत् ॥

How can these remain for even a moment on the surface of the earth? For those who always worship the Sun, liberation ever dwells.

BP 1.144.7

नागत्वा भोजकत्वं हि मोक्षमाप्नोति कश्चन । इदं मे मनसो ब्रह्मन्सदा सम्प्रतिभाति वै ॥

Without becoming a Sun-priest, surely no one attains liberation. O Brahmin, this indeed always appears in my mind.

BP 1.144.8

व्यास उवाच । एवमेव यथात्थ त्वं शङ्खचक्रगदाधर । धन्या एते महात्मानो भोजका नात्र संशयः ॥

Vyāsa said: It is just as you say, O bearer of the conch, discus, and mace. These great-souled Sun-priests are blessed; of this there is no doubt.

BP 1.144.9

ये पूजयन्ति सततं भानुमन्तं दिवाकरम् । ज्ञानिनः कर्मनिष्ठाश्च सदा मोक्षगतिं गताः ॥

Those who constantly worship the radiant Sun, the maker of day, are knowers, steadfast in action, and have always attained the path of liberation.

BP 1.144.10

यजन्ते सततं भानुं बलिपुष्पफलैस्तथा । अन्नेनौषधिभिश्चैव आज्यहोमैश्च कृत्स्नशः ॥

They continually worship the Sun with offerings, flowers, fruits, food, medicinal herbs, and fire offerings of clarified butter, in every way.

BP 1.144.11

होमं च शाश्वतं कृत्वा परं होमं ततः श्रिताः । परहोमस्य करणात्पूतात्मानो ह्यकल्मषाः ॥

Having performed the eternal fire-offering, they then resort to the highest fire-offering. By performing that highest offering, they are purified in self, indeed without stain.

BP 1.144.12

विशन्ति परमां दिव्यां भास्करीं तैजसीं कलाम् । कर्मणः साधने चैका तत्र चाग्नौ प्रतिष्ठिता ॥

They enter the highest, divine, radiant portion of Bhāskara. One portion is established there in fire for the accomplishment of action.

BP 1.144.13

वायुमार्गस्थिता व्योम्नि द्वितीयान्तः प्रकाशिका । ततः परं तृतीया तु तत्स्मृतं सूर्यमण्डले ॥

The second, situated in the sky along the path of wind, shines within; beyond that, the third is remembered in the solar orb.

BP 1.144.14

मण्डलं तच्च सवितुर्दिव्यं ह्यजरमव्ययम् । तस्याऽसौ पुरुषो मध्ये योऽसौ सदसदात्मकः ॥

That divine disc of Savitṛ is indeed unaging and imperishable. In its middle is that person whose nature is being and non-being.

BP 1.144.15

क्षराक्षरस्तु विज्ञेयो महासूर्यस्तथैव च । निष्कलः सकलश्चापि द्वौ च तस्य प्रकल्पितौ ॥

The Great Sun is to be known as both perishable and imperishable; two forms are conceived for him: one partless, one with parts.

BP 1.144.16

अक्षरः सकलश्चैव सर्वभूतव्यवस्थितः । सतत्त्वः सकलः प्रोक्तस्तत्त्वहीनस्तु निष्कलः ॥

He is imperishable and with parts, situated in all beings. The one with true essence is said to have parts; the one devoid of essence is partless.

BP 1.144.17

तृणगुल्मलतावृक्षवृकसिंहद्विजाधिपान् । सुरसिद्धमनुष्यांश्च स्थलजाञ्जलजान्हरेत् ॥

He carries off grasses, shrubs, creepers, trees, wolves, lions, chiefs of birds, gods, Siddhas, humans, and those born on land and in water.

BP 1.144.18

व्यवस्थितः स सर्वत्र सर्वेषामन्तरात्मनि । यदा कल्पात्मकश्चैव द्वितीयां तनुमाश्रितः ॥

He is established everywhere, in the inner self of all. When he, whose nature is an aeon, has taken a second form,

BP 1.144.19

निष्कलस्तु सदा ज्ञेयः संस्थितस्तैजसीं कलाम् । हिमं घर्मं च वर्षं च त्रैलोक्ये कुरुते सदा ॥

the partless one is always to be known as established in the radiant portion. He always produces cold, heat, and rain in the three worlds.

BP 1.144.20

द्वितीया या तनुस्तस्य अक्षरं तत्परं पदम् । देवयानं तु पन्थानं कर्मयोगेन संस्थिता ॥

The second form of his is imperishable, the highest state. It is established in the path of the gods through the discipline of action.

BP 1.144.21

आदित्यसिद्धान्तरिताः साङ्ख्ययोगविदश्च ये । तेऽभिगच्छन्ति तत्स्थानं स मोक्षः परिकीर्तितः ॥

Those who have studied the Āditya Siddhānta and those who know Sāṃkhya and Yoga reach that place; that is declared to be liberation.

BP 1.144.22

निर्द्वन्द्वो निर्गमश्चैव तत्र गत्वा न शोचति । वेदेषु ब्रह्म वदन्ति ध्यायन्ते तत्त्ववेदिनः ॥

Free from the pairs of opposites and without departure, one who has gone there does not grieve. The knowers of truth speak of Brahman in the Vedas and meditate.

BP 1.144.23

अकारं तत्त्वतश्चापि ध्यायन्ते पुरुषोत्तम । त्र्यक्षरं च तमोंकारं सार्धमात्रात्रये स्थितम् ॥

They meditate also on the letter A in its true nature, O best of men, and on Om, the three-letter syllable, situated in three and a half measures.

BP 1.144.24

वदन्ति चार्धमात्रस्थं मकारं व्यञ्जनात्मकम् । ध्यायन्ति ये मकारीयं ज्ञानं ते हि सदात्मकम् ॥

They say that the letter M is situated in the half-measure and has the nature of a consonant. Those who meditate on this knowledge of M are indeed of the nature of existence.

BP 1.144.25

मकारो भगवान्देवो भास्करः परिकीर्तितः । मकारध्यानयोगाच्च मगा ह्येते प्रकीर्तिताः ॥

The M is proclaimed as the blessed deity, Bhāskara. Through the discipline of meditation on M, these are proclaimed as Māgas.

BP 1.144.26

धूपमाल्यैर्युतश्चापि उपहारैस्तथैव च । भोजयन्ति सहस्रांशुं तेन ते भोजकाः स्मृताः ॥

They feed the thousand-rayed one with incense, garlands, and offerings; therefore they are called Sun-priests.
