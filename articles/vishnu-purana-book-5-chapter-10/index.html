VP 5.10.1

तयोर् विहरतोर् एवं राम-केशवयोर् व्रजे ।
प्रावृड् व्यतीता विकसत्-सरोजा चाभवच् छरत् ॥१॥

As Rāma and Keśava thus sported in Vraja, the rainy season passed, and autumn arose, with lotuses blooming.

Śrīdhara translation

Describing the autumn activities, beginning with the carrying of Govardhana, he first describes autumn by comparisons bearing spiritual meaning. Having described Hari’s rainy-season sports, he next intends to describe autumn sports beginning with Govardhana worship and its lifting; first, he describes autumn, containing dispassion and knowledge, in ten verses beginning “As the two.” “Of those two,” thus. “In which lotuses bloom”: that is autumn.

VP 5.10.4

उत्सृज्य जल-सर्वस्वं विमलाः सित-मूर्तयः ।
तत्यजुश् चाम्बरं मेघा गृहं विज्ञानिनो यथा ॥४॥

The clouds, having abandoned all their water, stainless and white-bodied, left the sky, as the wise leave home.

Śrīdhara translation

“Having abandoned,” thus. Water means water; “all” is the inert matter beginning with ego. Having left that entirety, the stainless ones are free from impurity such as darkness and passion; the white-bodied are of bright form and pure in being; the wise are knowers of the self.

In the sky clear with stars the full-disc moon shone, as a yogin whose self is in the final body shines in the family of holy ones.

Śrīdhara translation

“In a sky clear with stars,” thus. In a clear sky marked by stars, the full disc—the full light—of the moon shone near the full moon. Just as the moon shines among stars, a yogin shines in the stainless family of good people through learning, conduct, and the like: a full manifestation of Brahman, one whose self is in the final body.

VP 5.10.15

प्राणायाम इवाम्भोभिः सरसां कृत-पूरकैः ।

The lakes practiced breath-control day after day with their waters as inhalations, by exhalations, retentions, and the like.

Śrīdhara translation

“Breath-control,” etc. Breath-control is practiced with the waters of lakes as inhalations, with exhalations, retentions, and the like. In the reading “is practiced,” it means “as if practiced.” Likewise, having filled [the fields] with water from canals and the like, as wind held in a pot, having kept it there for a time, then at the proper time it is released through channels for fields and the like; this is the similarity to breath-control. पर्जन्यः सर्वलोकस्य भवाय भुवि वर्षति ॥२३॥

This earthly water is milked by the Sun’s rays into clouds; Parjanya rains it upon the earth for the arising of the whole world.

Śrīdhara translation

“But is it not known that the sun draws earthly essence with its rays and rains it down, not Indra?” Here the answer begins with “earthly.” The water drawn up is poured into clouds; Parjanya, Indra, rains it, taken from the earth by the sun’s rays and spread into clouds, for the world’s arising. “By the rays with which the Sun burns, by those the rain-god rains,” says the Śruti.

On the mountain’s summit, Kṛṣṇa, embodied as the mountain and saying, “I am the mountain,” ate the abundant food brought by the best of the cowherds, O Brahmin.

Śrīdhara translation

“At the mountain’s summit,” thus. To distinguish himself from Indra, having shown himself in the form of the deity presiding over the mountain, saying “I am the mountain,” he ate the abundant food brought and offered by the best of the cowherds.
