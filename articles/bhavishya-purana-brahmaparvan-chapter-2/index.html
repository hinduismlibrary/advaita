Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 2: Creation, Time and the Varṇas

BP 1.2.1

सुमन्तुरुवाच
शृणुष्वेदं महाबाहो पुराणं पञ्चलक्षणम् ।
यच्छ्रुत्वा मुच्यते राजन्पुरुषो ब्रह्महत्यया ॥ १

Sumantu said: Hear this Purāṇa of five characteristics, O mighty-armed one. Hearing it, a person is freed from brahmahatyā, O king.

BP 1.2.2

पर्वाणि चात्र वै पञ्च कीर्तितानि स्वयम्भुवा ।
प्रथमं कथ्यते ब्राह्मं द्वितीयं वैष्णवं स्मृतम् ॥ २

Five parvans are proclaimed here by the Self-born. The first is called Brāhma; the second is remembered as Vaiṣṇava.

BP 1.2.3

तृतीयं शैवमाख्यातं चतुर्थं त्वाष्ट्रमुच्यते ।
पञ्चमं प्रतिसर्गाख्यं सर्वलोकैः सुपूजितम् ॥ ३

The third is called Śaiva, the fourth Tvāṣṭra, and the fifth Pratisarga, greatly honoured by all the worlds.

BP 1.2.4

एतानि तात पर्वाणि लक्षणानि निबोध मे ।
सर्गश्च प्रतिसर्गश्च वंशो मन्वन्तराणि च ॥ ४

My child, learn from me the marks of these parvans: creation, re-creation, lineage and the Manvantaras.

BP 1.2.5

वंशानुचरितं चैव पुराणं पञ्चलक्षणम् ।
चतुर्दशभिर्विद्याभिर्भूषितं कुरुनन्दन ॥ ५

Also the account of the lineages: a Purāṇa has five marks and is adorned with the fourteen branches of knowledge, O joy of the Kurus.

BP 1.2.6

अङ्गानि चतुरो वेदा मीमांसा न्यायविस्तरः ।
पुराणं धर्मशास्त्रं च विद्या ह्येताश्चतुर्दश ॥ ६

The six Vedāṅgas, the four Vedas, Mīmāṃsā, Nyāya, Purāṇa and Dharmaśāstra—these are the fourteen branches of knowledge.

BP 1.2.7

आयुर्वेदो धनुर्वेदो गान्धर्वश्चैव ते त्रयः ।
अर्थशास्त्रं चतुर्थं तु विद्या ह्यष्टादशैव ताः ॥ ७

Āyurveda, Dhanurveda and Gāndharvaveda are the three; Arthaśāstra is the fourth. These are the eighteen branches of knowledge.

BP 1.2.8

प्रथमं कथ्यते सर्गो भूतानामिह सर्वशः ।
यच्छ्रुत्वा पापनिर्मुक्तो याति शान्तिमनुत्तमाम् ॥ ८

First, the creation of all beings here is described. By hearing it, one is freed from sin and attains unsurpassed peace.

BP 1.2.9

जगदासीत्पुरा तात तमोभूतमलक्षणम् ।
अविज्ञेयमतर्क्यं च प्रसुप्तमिव सर्वशः ॥ ९

Formerly, my child, the universe was darkness—without distinguishing marks, unknowable, beyond reasoning, and as though entirely asleep.

BP 1.2.10

ततः स भगवानीशो ह्यव्यक्तो व्यञ्जयन्निदम् ।
महाभूतानि वृत्तौजाः प्रोत्थितस्तमनाशनः ॥.2.१०

Then the blessed Lord, the unmanifest sovereign, made this manifest. Possessing mighty elemental power, he arose as the destroyer of darkness.

BP 1.2.11

योऽसावतीन्द्रियोऽग्राह्यः सूक्ष्मोऽव्यक्तः सनातनः ।
सर्वभूतमयोऽचिन्त्यः स एष स्वयमुत्थितः ॥१

He is beyond the senses, imperceptible, subtle, unmanifest and eternal; consisting of all beings and inconceivable, he arose by himself.

BP 1.2.12

योऽसौ षड्विंशको लोके तथा यः पुरुषोत्तमः ।
भास्करश्च महाबाहो परं ब्रह्म च कथ्यते ॥२

He is known in the world as the twenty-sixth principle and as the Supreme Person; O mighty-armed king, he is also called Bhāskara and the supreme Brahman.

BP 1.2.13

सोऽभिध्याय शरीरात्स्वात्सिसृक्षुर्विविधाः प्रजाः ।
अत एव ससर्जादौ तासु वीर्यमवासृजत् ॥३

Desiring to create diverse living beings from his own body, he contemplated; at the beginning he created the waters and deposited his seed in them.

BP 1.2.14

यस्मादुत्पद्यते सर्वं सदेवासुरमानुषम् ।
बीजं शुक्रं तथा रेत उग्रं वीर्यं च कथ्यते ॥४

From that arises everything consisting of gods, demons and humans. It is called seed, generative fluid, semen, fierce potency and creative power.

BP 1.2.15

वीर्यस्यैतानि नामानि कथितानि स्वयम्भुवा ।
तदण्डमभवद्धैमं ज्वालामालाकुलं विभो ॥५

The Self-born Lord declared these names of creative power. From it came a golden egg encircled by a mass of flames, O mighty king.

BP 1.2.16

यस्मिञ्जज्ञे स्वयं ब्रह्मा सर्वलोकपितामहः ।
सुरज्येष्ठश्चतुर्वक्त्रः परमेष्ठी पितामहः ॥६

Within that egg Brahmā himself was born—the grandsire of all worlds, eldest of the gods, four-faced, supreme and ancestral lord.

BP 1.2.17

क्षेत्रज्ञः पुरुषो वेधाः शम्भुर्नारायणस्तथा ।
पर्यायवाचकैः शब्दैरेव ब्रह्मा प्रकीर्त्यते ॥७

Brahmā is praised through synonymous terms such as Kṣetrajña, Puruṣa, Vedhas, Śambhu and Nārāyaṇa.

BP 1.2.18

सदा मनीषिभिस्तात विरञ्चिः कञ्जजस्तथा२ ।
आपो नारा इति प्रोक्ता आपो वै नरसूनवः ॥८

The wise also always call him Virañci and Lotus-born, my child. The waters are called nārā, for the waters are said to be the offspring of Nara.

BP 1.2.19

ता यदस्यायनं पूर्वं तेन नारायणः स्मृतः ।
अरमित्येव शीघ्राय नियताः कविभिः कृताः ॥९

Because those waters were his first resting place, he is remembered as Nārāyaṇa. The sages established the expression in relation to swift movement and repose.

BP 1.2.20

आप एवार्णवीभूत्वा सुशीघ्रास्तेन ता नराः ।
यत्तत्कारणमव्यक्तं नित्यं सदसदात्मकम् ॥.2.२०

Those waters swiftly became an ocean and were therefore called naras. The causal principle is unmanifest, eternal, and of the nature of both being and non-being.

BP 1.2.21

तद्विसृष्टः स पुरुषो लोके ब्रह्मेति कीर्त्त्यते ।
एवं स भगवानण्डे तत्त्वमेव निरूप्य वै ॥२ १

Created from that causal principle, the Person is celebrated in the world as Brahmā. Thus the blessed Lord, within the egg, distinctly established the cosmic principle.

BP 1.2.22

ध्यानमास्थाय राजेन्द्र तदण्डमकरोद्द्विधा ।
शकलाभ्यां च राजेन्द्र दिवं भूमिं च निर्ममे ॥२

Entering meditation, O king, he divided that egg in two; from its two halves he fashioned heaven and earth.

BP 1.2.23

अन्तर्व्योम दिशश्चाष्टौ वारुणं स्थानमेव हि ।
ऊर्ध्वं महान्गतो राजन् समन्ताल्लोकभूतये ॥३

He made the intermediate sky, the eight directions and the watery realm. The great one extended above and all around, O king, so that the worlds might come into being.

BP 1.2.24

महतश्चाप्यहंकारस्तस्माच्च त्रिगुणा अपि ।
त्रिगुणा अतिसूक्ष्मास्तु बुद्धिगम्या हि भारत ॥४

From the Great Principle arose ego, and from it the three qualities. These three qualities are exceedingly subtle and can be grasped only by understanding, O descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.2.25

उत्पत्तिहेतुभूता वै भूतानां महतां नृप ।
तेषामेव गृहीतानि शनैः पञ्चेन्द्रियाणि तु ॥५

O king, they became the causes of the arising of the great elements; from those very principles the five senses were gradually formed.

BP 1.2.26

तथैवावयवाः सूक्ष्माः षण्णामप्यमितौजसाम् ॥६

Likewise there were the subtle constituent parts of all six principles of immeasurable power.

BP 1.2.27

संनिवेश्यात्ममात्रासु स राजन्भगवान्विभुः ।
भूतानि निर्ममे तात सर्वाणि विधिपूर्वकम् ॥७

Placing those subtle parts within their respective elemental measures, the all-powerful Lord methodically fashioned all beings, O king and my child.

BP 1.2.28

यन्मूर्त्यवयवाः सूक्ष्मास्तस्येमान्याश्रयाणि षट् ।
तस्माच्छरीरमित्याहुस्तस्य मूर्तिं मनीषिणः ॥८

The six subtle principles are the supports of the constituent parts of his manifest form; therefore the wise call that form of his the body.

BP 1.2.29

महान्ति तानि भूतानि आविशन्ति ततो विभुम् ।
कर्मणा सह राजेन्द्र सगुणाश्चापि वै गुणाः ॥९

Then those great elements enter the all-pervading Lord together with action, O king, and the qualities enter endowed with their attributes.

BP 1.2.30

तेषामिदं तु सप्तानां पुरुषाणां महौजसाम् ।
सूक्ष्माभ्यो मूर्तिमात्राभ्यः सम्भवत्यव्ययाद्वयम् ॥.2.३०

From the subtle form-elements of these seven exceedingly powerful principles, the perishable world arises from the imperishable.

BP 1.2.31

भूतादिमहतस्तात येन व्याप्तमिदं जगत् ।
तस्मादपि महाबाहो पुरुषाः पञ्च एव हि ॥१

My child, this universe is pervaded by the Great Principle beginning with the elemental source; therefore, O mighty-armed king, the principles are indeed five.

BP 1.2.32

केचिदेवं परां तात सृष्टिमिच्छन्ति पण्डिताः ।
अन्येऽप्येवं महाबाहो प्रवदन्ति मनीषिणः ॥२

Some learned people, my child, understand the supreme creation in this way; other wise teachers too describe it in this same manner, O mighty-armed king.

BP 1.2.33

योऽसावात्मा परस्तात कल्पादौ सृजते तनुम् ।
प्रजनश्च महाबाहो सिसृक्षुर्विविधाः प्रजाः ॥३

That supreme Self, my child, creates a body at the beginning of a kalpa; as progenitor, O mighty-armed king, he desires to create diverse living beings.

BP 1.2.34

तेन सृष्टः पुद्गलस्तु प्रधानं विशते नृप ।
प्रधानं क्षोभितं तेन विकारान्युजते बहून् ॥४

The embodied aggregate created by him enters Pradhāna, O king. Agitated by that presence, Pradhāna brings forth many transformations.

BP 1.2.35

उत्पद्यते महांस्तस्मात्ततो भूतादिरेव हि ।
उत्पद्यते विशालं च भूतादेः कुरुनन्दन ॥५

From it the Great Principle arises, and from that comes Bhūtādi; from Bhūtādi, O joy of the Kurus, Viśāla arises.

BP 1.2.36

विशालाच्च हरिस्तात हरेश्चापि वृकास्तथा ।
वृकैर्मुष्णन्ति च बुधास्तस्मात्सर्वं भवेन्नृप ॥६

From Viśāla comes Hari, my child, and from Hari the principles called Vṛkas. Through the Vṛkas the wise apprehend; from that, O king, the entire manifested order comes to be.

BP 1.2.37

तथैषामेव राजेन्द्र प्रादुर्भवति वेगतः ।
मात्राणां कुरुशार्दूल विबोधस्तदनन्तरम् ॥७

In this same sequence, O king, they rapidly become manifest; immediately afterward, O tiger among the Kurus, comes cognition of the subtle measures.

BP 1.2.38

तस्मादपि हृषीकाणि विविधानि नृपोत्तम ।
तथेयं सृष्टिराख्याताऽऽराध्यतः कुरुनन्दन ॥८

From that arise the various sense faculties, O best of kings. Thus, O joy of the Kurus, this creation has been described from its beginning.

BP 1.2.39

भूयो निबोध राजेन्द्र भूतानामिह विस्तरम् ।
गुणाधिकानि सर्वाणि भूतानि पृथिवीपते ॥९

Now learn further, O king, the detailed nature of the elements here. O lord of the earth, each successive element possesses an increasing number of qualities.

BP 1.2.40

आकाशमादितः कृत्वा उत्तरोत्तरमेव हि ।
एकं द्वौ च तथा त्रीणि चत्वारश्चापि पञ्च च ॥.2.४०

Beginning with space, each succeeding element has successively one, two, three, four and five qualities.

BP 1.2.41

ततः स भगवान्ब्रह्मा पद्मासनगतः प्रभुः ।
सर्वेषां तु स नामानि कर्माणि च पृथक्पृथक् ॥१

Then the blessed Lord Brahmā, seated upon the lotus, separately established the names and functions of them all.

BP 1.2.42

वेदशब्देभ्य एवादौ पृथक्संस्थाश्च निर्ममे ।
कर्मोद्भवानां देवानां सोऽसृजद्देहिनां प्रभुः ॥२

At the beginning he fashioned their distinct conditions from the words of the Veda; the Lord created the action-born gods and embodied beings.

BP 1.2.43

तुषितानां गणं राजन्यज्ञं चैव सनातनम् ।
दत्त्वा वीर समानेभ्यो गुह्यं ब्रह्म सनातनम् ॥३

O king, he created the host of Tuṣitas and the eternal sacrifice, and, O hero, bestowed the secret eternal Brahman upon those who were equal to receive it.

BP 1.2.44

दुदोह यज्ञसिद्धयर्थमृग्यजुः सामलक्षणम् ।
कालं कालविभक्तीश्च ग्रहानृतूंस्तथा नृप ॥४

For the accomplishment of sacrifice he drew forth the Ṛg, Yajus and Sāman; he also made time and its divisions, the planets and the seasons, O king.

BP 1.2.45

सरितः सागराञ्छैलान्समानि विषमाणि च ।
कामं क्रोधं तथा वाचं रतिं चापि कुरुद्वह ॥५

He created rivers, oceans and mountains, level and uneven regions, desire, anger, speech and also love, O descendant of Kuru.

BP 1.2.46

सृष्टिं ससर्ज राजेन्द्र सिसृक्षुर्विविधाः प्रजाः ।
धर्माधर्मौ विवेकाय कर्मणां च तथासृजत् ॥४ ६

Desiring to create diverse living beings, he produced the created order, O king; and for discrimination among actions he also created dharma and adharma.

BP 1.2.47

सुखदुःखादिभिर्द्वन्द्वैः प्रजाश्चेमा न्ययोजयत् ।
अण्व्यो मात्राविनाशिन्यो दशार्धानां तु याः स्मृताः ॥७

He joined these creatures with pairs such as pleasure and pain, and with the subtle, imperishable measures remembered as the five.

BP 1.2.48

ताभिः सर्वमिदं वीर सम्भवत्यनुपूर्वशः ।
यत्कृतं तु पुरा कर्म सन्नियुक्तेन वै नृप ॥८

Through those measures, O hero, this entire world arises in proper sequence. Whatever action was formerly performed by an appointed being, O king—

BP 1.2.49

स तदेव स्वयं भेजे सृज्यमानं पुनः पुनः ।
हिंस्राहिंस्रे मृदुक्रूरे धर्माधर्मे ऋतानृते ॥९

—that very action each being receives again and again when created: violent or nonviolent, gentle or cruel, dharmic or adharmic, true or false.

BP 1.2.50

यद्यथास्याभवत्सर्गे तत्तस्य स्वयमाविशत् ।
यथा च लिङ्गान्यृतवः स्वयमेवानुपर्यये ॥.2.५०

Whatever quality belonged to each being in the former creation entered that being of itself, just as the characteristic signs of the seasons return naturally in succession.

BP 1.2.51

स्वानि स्वान्यभिपद्यन्ते तथा कर्माणि देहिनः ।
लोकस्येह विवृद्ध्यर्थं मुखबाहूरुपादतः ॥१

In the same way embodied beings take up their respective actions. For the growth of the world here, he produced orders from the mouth, arms, thighs and feet.

BP 1.2.52

ब्रह्म क्षत्रं तथा चोभौ वैश्यशूद्रौ नृपोत्तम ।
मुखानि यानि चत्वारि तेभ्यो वेदा विनिःसृताः ॥२

O best of kings, these became the brāhmaṇa and kṣatriya, and likewise the vaiśya and śūdra. From Brahmā's four mouths the Vedas issued forth.

BP 1.2.53

ऋग्वेदसहिता तात वसिष्ठेन महात्मना ।
पूर्वान्मुखान्महाबाहो दक्षिणाच्चापि वै शृणु ॥३

My child, the Ṛgveda, together with the great-souled Vasiṣṭha, came from the eastern mouth. O mighty-armed king, now hear what came from the southern mouth.

BP 1.2.54

यजुर्वेदो महाराज याज्ञवल्क्येन वै सह ।
सामानि पश्चिमात्तात गौतमश्च महानृषिः ॥४

From the southern mouth came the Yajurveda with Yājñavalkya, O great king; from the western came the Sāman chants and the great sage Gautama, my child.

BP 1.2.55

अथर्ववेदो राजेन्द्र मुखाच्चाप्युत्तरान्नृप ।
ऋषिश्चापि तथा राजञ्छौनको लोकपूजितः ॥५

The Atharvaveda came from the northern mouth, O king, and from it also came the sage Śaunaka, honoured throughout the world.

BP 1.2.56

यत्तन्मुखं महाबाहो पञ्चमं लोकविश्रुतम् ।
अष्टादशपुराणानि सेतिहासानि भारत ॥६

O mighty-armed descendant of Bharata, from the renowned fifth mouth came the eighteen Purāṇas together with the histories.

BP 1.2.57

निर्गतानि ततस्तस्मान्मुखात्कुरुकुलोद्वह ।
तथान्याः स्मृतयश्चापि यमाद्या लोकपूजिताः ॥७

They issued from that mouth, O descendant of the Kuru line; so too did the other revered traditional codes, beginning with that of Yama.

BP 1.2.58

ततः स भगवान्देवो द्विधा देहमकारयत् ।
द्विधा कृत्वात्मनो देहमर्धेन पुरुषोऽभवत् ॥८

Then the blessed Lord caused his body to become twofold. Dividing his own body in two, he became male in one half.

BP 1.2.59

अर्धेन नारी तस्यां च विराजमसृजत्प्रभुः ।
तपस्तप्त्वासृजद्यं तु स स्वयं पुरुषो विराट् ॥९

With the other half he became female, and in her the Lord created Virāj. After performing austerity, Virāj himself brought forth creation.

BP 1.2.60

स चकार तपो राजन्सिसृक्षुर्विविधाः प्रजाः ।
पतीन्प्रजानामसृजन्महर्षीनादितो दश ॥.2.६०

Desiring to create diverse beings, he performed austerity, O king, and at the beginning created ten great seers as lords of creatures.

BP 1.2.61

नारदं च भृगुं तात कं प्रचेतसमेव हि ।
पुलहं क्रतुं पुलस्त्यं च अत्रिमङ्गिरसं तथा ॥१

They were Nārada, Bhṛgu, Ka, Pracetas, Pulaha, Kratu, Pulastya, Atri, and the great sage Aṅgiras.

BP 1.2.62

मरीचिं चापि राजेन्द्र योऽसावाद्यः प्रजापतिः ।
एतांश्चान्यांश्च राजेन्द्र असृजद् भूरितेजसः ॥२

Marīci was the first of the lords of creatures. These and other great seers, radiant with spiritual splendour, were brought forth.

BP 1.2.63

अथ देवानृषीन्दैत्यान्सोऽसृजत्कुरुनन्दन ।
यक्षरक्षः पिशाचांश्च गन्धर्वाप्सरसोऽसुरान् ॥३

He created gods, seers, Daityas, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas, Piśācas, Gandharvas, Apsarases, and Asuras.

BP 1.2.64

मनुष्याणां पितॄणां च सर्पाणां चैव भारत ।
नागानां च महाबाहो ससर्ज विविधान्गणान् ॥४

He also created human beings, the ancestors, serpents and Nāgas, together with the many other classes of living beings.

BP 1.2.65

क्षणरुचोऽशनिगणान्रोहितेन्द्रधनूंषि च ।
धूमकेतूंस्तथा चोल्का निर्याताञ्ज्योतिषां गणान् ॥५

He produced flashes of lightning, thunderbolts, rainbows, comets, meteors, and the various heavenly lights.

BP 1.2.66

मनुष्यान्किन्नरान्मत्स्यान्वराहांश्च विहङ्गमान् ।
गजानश्वानथ पशून्मृगान्व्यालांश्च भारत ॥६

He created humans and Kinnaras, fish and boars, birds, elephants, horses, cattle, deer, and the other beasts.

BP 1.2.67

कृमिकीटपतङ्गांश्च यूकालिक्षकमत्कुणान् ।
सर्वं च दंशमशकं स्थावरं१ च पृथग्विधम् ॥७

He likewise produced worms, insects, moths, lice, bugs, biting creatures, mosquitoes, and every kind of plant life.

BP 1.2.68

एवं स भास्करो देवः ससर्ज भुवनत्रयम् ।
येषां तु यादृशं कर्म भूतानामिह कीर्तितम् ॥८

Thus Bhāskara brought forth the three worlds with all their moving and unmoving beings, and assigned to them their respective modes of action.

BP 1.2.69

कथयिष्यामि तत्सर्वं क्रमयोगं च जन्मनि ।
गजा व्याला मृगास्तात पशवश्च पृथग्विधाः ॥९

I shall now declare in due order the manner of birth of all these beings. Elephants, beasts of prey, deer, cattle, and similar creatures are born from the womb.

BP 1.2.70

पिशाचा मानुषास्तात रक्षांसि च जरायुजाः ।
द्विजास्तु अण्डजाः सर्पा नक्रा मत्स्याः सकच्छपाः ॥.2.७०

Piśācas, human beings, and Rākṣasas are womb-born. Birds are born from eggs, as are snakes, crocodiles, fish, and tortoises.

BP 1.2.71

एवंविधानि यानीह स्थलजान्यौदकानि च ।
स्वेदजं दंशमशकं यूकालिक्षकमत्कुणाः ॥ ७१

Such creatures as are born on land or in water belong to these classes. Biting insects, mosquitoes, lice, flies, and bedbugs are moisture-born.

BP 1.2.72

ऊष्मणा चोपजायन्ते यच्चान्यत्किञ्चिदीदृशम् ।
उद्भिज्जाः स्थावराः सर्वे बीजकाण्डप्ररोहिणः ॥ ७२

They, and whatever else is of this kind, arise from heat. All immovable beings are sprout-born, growing from seeds or from cuttings.

BP 1.2.73

ओषध्यः फलपाकान्ता नानाविधफलोपगाः ।
अपुष्पाः फलवन्तो ये ते वनस्पतयः स्मृताः ॥ ७३

Herbs complete their life when their fruit ripens and occur with many kinds of fruit. Those that bear fruit without flowers are traditionally called forest-lords.

BP 1.2.74

पुष्पिणः फलिनश्चैव वृक्षास्तूभयतः स्मृताः ।
गुच्छगुल्मं तु विविधं तथैव तृणजातयः ॥ ७४

Those that bear both flowers and fruit are called trees. There are also the many kinds of bushes and shrubs, and likewise the species of grasses.

BP 1.2.75

बीजकाण्डरुहाण्येव प्रताना वल्ल्य एव च ।
तमसा बहुरूपेण वेष्टिताः कर्महेतुना ॥ ७५

There are plants growing from seeds and cuttings, spreading plants, and creepers. Through the cause of their karma, they are enveloped in darkness of many forms.

BP 1.2.76

अन्तःसंज्ञा भवन्त्येते सुखदुःखसमन्विताः ।
एतावत्यस्तु गतयः प्रोद्भूताः कुरुनन्दन ॥ ७६

These beings possess inward consciousness and experience pleasure and pain. Such are the courses of existence that have arisen, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.2.77

तस्माद्देवाद्दीप्तिमन्तो भास्कराच्य महात्मनः ।
घोरेऽस्मिंस्तात संसारे नित्यं सततयायिनि ॥ ७७

They arose from that radiant god—from the great-souled Bhāskara—in this terrible cycle of existence, my child, which moves onward unceasingly.

BP 1.2.78

एवं सर्वं स सृष्ट्वेदं राजँल्लोकगुरुं परम् ।
तिरोभूतः स भूतात्मा कालं कालेन पीडयन् ॥ ७८

Having thus created this entire world, O king, that supreme teacher of the worlds—the Self of all beings—became hidden, impelling time by means of time.

BP 1.2.79

यदा स देवो जागर्ति तदेदं चेष्टते जगत् ।
यदा स्वपिति शान्तात्मा तदा सर्वं निमीलति ॥ ७९

When that god awakens, this world becomes active; when the tranquil Self sleeps, then everything closes its eyes and becomes still.

BP 1.2.80

तस्मिन्स्वपिति राजेन्द्र जन्तवः कर्मबन्धनाः ।
स्वकर्मभ्यो निवर्तन्ते मनश्च ग्लानिमृच्छति ॥.2.८०

When he sleeps, O king of kings, creatures bound by karma cease from their respective activities, and the mind itself sinks into weariness.

BP 1.2.81

युगपत्तु प्रलीयन्ते यदा तस्मिन्महात्मनि ।
तदायं सर्वभूतात्मा सुखं स्वपिति भारत ॥ ८१

When they dissolve all at once into that great Self, then the Self of all beings sleeps in ease, O descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.2.82

तमो यदा समाश्रित्य चिरं तिष्ठति सेन्द्रियः ।
न नवं कुरुते कर्म तदोत्क्रामति मूर्तितः ॥ ८२

When an embodied being, together with its senses, takes refuge in darkness and remains there for a long time, it performs no new action and then departs from its bodily form.

BP 1.2.83

यदाहंमात्रिको भूत्वा बीजं स्थास्नु चरिष्णु च ।
समाविशति संसृष्टस्तदा मूतिं विमुञ्चति ॥ ८३

When, reduced to the principle of individuality, it enters the seed of what is stationary and what moves, and becomes united with it, it then relinquishes its former form.

BP 1.2.84

एवं स जाग्रत्स्वप्नाभ्यामिदं सर्वं जगत्प्रभुः ।
संजीवयति चाजस्रं प्रमापयति चाव्ययः ॥ ८४

Thus, through waking and sleep, the imperishable Lord continually animates this entire world and causes it to cease.

BP 1.2.85

कल्पादौ सृजते तात अन्ते कल्पस्य संहरेत् ।
दिनं तस्येह यत्तात कल्पान्तमिति कथ्यते ॥ ८५

At the beginning of a kalpa he creates, my child, and at the end of the kalpa he withdraws creation. What is his day here, my child, is called the duration ending with a kalpa.

BP 1.2.86

कालसंख्यां ततस्तस्य कल्पस्य शृणु भारत ।
निमेषा दश चाष्टौ च अक्ष्णः काष्ठा निगद्यते ॥ ८६

Now hear, O descendant of Bharata, the reckoning of the time belonging to that kalpa. Eighteen twinklings of the eye are declared to make one kāṣṭhā.

BP 1.2.87

त्रिंशत्काष्ठाः कलामाहुः क्षणस्त्रिंशत्कलाः स्मृताः ।
मुहूर्तमथ मौहूर्ता वदन्ति द्वादश क्षणम् ॥ ८७

Thirty kāṣṭhās are called one kalā, and thirty kalās are remembered as one kṣaṇa. Those who calculate time say that twelve kṣaṇas make one muhūrta.

BP 1.2.88

त्रिंशन्मुहूर्तमुद्दिष्टमहोरात्रं मनीषिभिः ।
मासस्त्रिंशदहोरात्रं द्वौ द्वौ मासावृतुः स्मृतः ॥ ८८

The wise declare thirty muhūrtas to be a day and night. Thirty days and nights make a month, and every two months are remembered as a season.

BP 1.2.89

ऋतुत्रयमप्ययनमयने द्वे तु वत्सरः ।
अहोरात्रे विभजते सूर्यो मानुषदैविके ॥ ८९

Three seasons constitute one solar course, and two such courses make a year. The sun divides days and nights for human beings and for the gods.

BP 1.2.90

रात्रिः स्वप्नाय भूतानां चेष्टायै कर्मणामहः ।
पित्र्ये रात्र्यहनी मासः प्रविभागस्तु पक्षयोः ॥.2.९०

For living beings, night is for sleep and day for the performance of actions. For the ancestors, a month forms a night and day, divided between its two fortnights.

BP 1.2.91

कर्म चेष्टास्वहः कृष्णः मुक्तः स्वप्नाय शर्वरी ।
दैवे रात्र्यहनी वर्षं प्रविभागस्तयोः पुनः ॥ ९१

For the ancestors, the bright fortnight is the day for ritual activity, while the dark fortnight is the night for sleep. For the gods, a year forms a night and day, again divided into two parts.

BP 1.2.92

अहस्तत्रोदगयनं रात्रिः स्याद्दक्षिणायनम् ।
ब्राह्मस्य तु क्षपाहस्य यत्प्रमाणं महीपते ॥ ९२

There the northern course of the sun is the day, and the southern course is the night. Now hear, O ruler of the earth, the measure of Brahmā’s night and day.

BP 1.2.93

एकैकशो युगानां तु क्रमशस्तन्तिबोध मे ।
चत्वार्याहुः सहस्राणि वर्षाणां तत्कृतं युगम् ॥ ९३

Understand from me in order the duration of each of the ages. They declare four thousand divine years to constitute the Kṛta age.

BP 1.2.94

तस्य तावच्छती सन्ध्या सन्ध्यांशश्च तथाविधः ।
त्रेता त्रीणि सहस्राणि वर्षाणि च विदुर्बुधाः ॥ ९४

Its dawn consists of four hundred years, and its twilight is of the same extent. The wise know the Tretā age to comprise three thousand years.

BP 1.2.95

शतानि षट् च राजेन्द्र सन्ध्यासन्ध्यांशयोः पृथक् ।
वर्षाणां द्वे सहस्रे तु द्वापरे परिकीर्तिते ॥ ९५

Its dawn and twilight each comprise six hundred years in all, O king of kings. The Dvāpara age is proclaimed to consist of two thousand years.

BP 1.2.96

चत्वारि च शतान्याहुः सन्ध्यासन्ध्यांशयोर्बुधः ।
सहस्रं कथितं तिष्ये शतद्वयसमन्वितम् ॥ ९६

The wise declare four hundred years for its dawn and twilight together. In the Tiṣya or Kali age, one thousand years are stated, accompanied by two hundred years of dawn and twilight.

BP 1.2.97

एषा चतुर्युगस्यापि संख्या प्रोक्ता नृपोत्तम ।
यदेतत्परिसंख्या तमादावेव चतुर्युगम् ॥ ९७

This reckoning of the four ages has been declared, O best of kings. The sum thus enumerated constitutes one complete cycle of four ages.

BP 1.2.98

एतद्द्वादशसाहस्रं देवानां युगमुच्यते ।
दैविकानां युगानां तु सहस्रपरिसंख्यया ॥ ९८

This total of twelve thousand is called an age of the gods. One thousand such divine ages are then reckoned together.

BP 1.2.99

ब्राह्ममेकमहर्ज्ञेयं तावती रात्रिरुच्यते ।
एतद्युगसहस्रान्तं ब्राह्मं पुण्यमहर्विदुः ॥ ९९

That is to be known as one day of Brahmā, and his night is said to be of equal length. The wise know Brahmā’s sacred day to end after this thousandfold cycle of ages.

BP 1.2.100

रात्रिं च तावतीमेव तेऽहोरात्रविदो जनाः ।
ततोऽसौ युगपर्यन्ते प्रसुप्तः प्रतिबुध्यते ॥.2.१००

Those who understand days and nights know his night to be of precisely the same duration. Then, at the end of that cycle of ages, he awakens from sleep.

BP 1.2.101

प्रतिबुद्धस्तु सृजति मनः सदसदात्मकम् ।
मनः सृष्टिं विकुरुते चोद्यमानं सिसृक्षया ॥०१

Having awakened, he creates mind, whose nature comprises both the manifest and the unmanifest. Impelled by the desire to create, mind then undergoes transformation and produces creation.

BP 1.2.102

विपुलं जायते तस्मात्तस्य शब्दं गुणं विदुः ।
विपुलात्तु विकुर्वाणात्सर्वगन्धवहः शुचिः ॥०२

From it arises the vast expanse, whose quality they know to be sound. From that vast expanse undergoing transformation comes the pure and mighty wind, carrier of every fragrance.

BP 1.2.103

बलवाञ्जायते वायुः स वै स्पर्शगुणो मतः ।
वायोरपि विकुर्वाणाद्विरोचिष्णु तमोनुदम् ॥०३

The mighty wind is born, and touch is considered its distinctive quality. From the wind also, as it undergoes transformation, arises the shining dispeller of darkness.

BP 1.2.104

उत्पद्यते विचित्रांशुस्तस्य रूपं गुणं विदुः ।
तस्मादपि विकुर्वाणादापो जाताः स्मृता बुधैः ॥०४

Radiant fire, adorned with varied rays, is produced; form is known as its quality. From that too, as it transforms, the waters are born, as the wise remember.

BP 1.2.105

तासां गुणो रसो ज्ञेयः सर्वलोकस्य भावनः ।
अद्भ्यो गन्धगुणा भूमिरित्येषा सृष्टिरादितः ॥०५

Taste should be known as the quality of the waters, which sustain the whole world. From the waters arose earth, whose quality is smell. Such was the creation at the beginning.

BP 1.2.106

यत्प्राग्द्वादशसाहस्रमुक्तं सौमनसं युगम् ।
तदेकसप्ततिगुणं मन्वन्तरमिहोच्यते ॥०६

The harmonious cycle of twelve thousand divine years mentioned earlier, multiplied seventy-one times, is here called a Manvantara.

BP 1.2.107

मन्वन्तराण्यसंख्यानि सर्गः संहार एव च ।
तथाप्यहे सदा ब्राह्मे मनवस्तु चतुर्दश ॥०७

The Manvantaras, creations, and dissolutions are without number. Nevertheless, in each day of Brahmā there are always fourteen Manus.

BP 1.2.108

कथ्यन्ते कुरुशार्दूल संख्यया पण्डितैः सदा ।
मनोः स्वायम्भुवस्येह षड्वंश्या मनवोऽपरे ॥०८

The learned always enumerate them so, O tiger among the Kurus. After Svāyambhuva Manu, six other Manus belonging to his succession are spoken of here.

BP 1.2.109

सृष्टवन्तः प्रजाः स्वाःस्वाः महात्मानो महौजसः ।
सावर्णेयस्तथा पञ्चभौत्यो रौच्यस्तथापरः ॥०९

Those great-souled and exceedingly powerful Manus created their respective races of beings. The future Manus also include Sāvarṇeya, Pāñcabhautya, and another named Raucya.

BP 1.2.110

एते भविष्या मनवः सप्त प्रोक्ता नृपोत्तम ।
स्वस्वेऽन्तरे सर्वमिदं पालयन्ति चराचरम् ॥.2.११०

These seven future Manus have been declared, O best of kings. During their respective intervals they protect this entire universe of moving and unmoving beings.

BP 1.2.111

एवंविधं दिनं तस्य विरिञ्चेस्तु महात्मनः ।
तस्यान्ते कुरुते सर्गं यथेदं कथितं तव ॥११

Such is the day of the great-souled Viriñci. At its conclusion he brings forth creation in the manner that has been related to you.

BP 1.2.112

क्रीडन्निवैतत्कुरुते परमेष्ठी नराधिप ।
चतुष्पात्सकलो धर्मः सत्यं चैव कृते युगे ॥१२

The Supreme Lord performs all this as though in play, O ruler of men. In the Kṛta age, dharma is complete and stands on four feet, and truth likewise prevails.

BP 1.2.113

नाधर्मेणागमः कश्चिन्मनुष्याणां प्रवर्तते ।
इतरेष्वागमास्तात धर्मश्च कुरुनन्दन ॥१३

No gain among human beings proceeds through unrighteousness in that age. In the other ages, my child, both accepted practice and dharma progressively change, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.2.114

यादृशाः परिहीयन्ते यथाह भगवान्मनुः ।
चौर्याच्चाप्यनृताद्राजन्मायाभिरमितद्युते ॥१४

Their condition declines in the manner declared by the blessed Manu. Through theft, falsehood, and deceit, O radiant king, that decline takes place.

BP 1.2.115

पादेन हीयते धर्मस्त्रेतादिषु युगेषु वै ।
अरोगाः सर्वसिद्धार्थाश्चतुर्वर्षशतायुषः ॥१५

In the ages beginning with Tretā, dharma is diminished by one foot at each transition. In the Kṛta age people are free from disease, accomplish every aim, and live four hundred years.

BP 1.2.116

कृतत्रेतादिषु त्वेषां वयो ह्रसति पादशः ।
वेदोक्तमायुराशीश्च मर्त्यानां कुरुनन्दन ॥१६

In Kṛta, Tretā, and the following ages, their lifespan decreases by a quarter at a time. So too do the Veda-ordained lifespan and blessings of mortals, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.2.117

कर्मणां तु फलं तात फलत्यनुयुगं सदा ।
प्रभावश्च तथा लोके फलत्येव शरीरिणाम् ॥१७

The fruit of actions, my child, always manifests in accordance with the age; and the power of embodied beings in the world likewise manifests accordingly.

BP 1.2.118

अन्ये कृतयुगे धर्मास्त्रेतायां द्वापरे परे ।
अन्ये कलियुगे नृणां युगधर्मानुरूपतः ॥१८

The duties are one kind in the Kṛta age, another in Tretā, another in Dvāpara, and still another for human beings in Kali, corresponding to the dharma of each age.

BP 1.2.119

तपः परं कृतयुगे त्रेतायां ज्ञानमुच्यते ।
द्वापरे यज्ञमित्याहुर्दानमेकं कलौ युगे ॥१९

Austerity is supreme in the Kṛta age; in Tretā it is said to be knowledge. In Dvāpara they declare sacrifice supreme, and in the Kali age, giving alone.

BP 1.2.120

सर्वस्य राजन्सर्गस्य गुप्त्यर्थं च महाद्युते ।
मुखबाहूरुपादानां पृथक्कर्माण्यकल्पयत् ॥.2.१२०

For the protection of this entire creation, O radiant king, he assigned distinct duties to those born from the mouth, arms, thighs, and feet.

BP 1.2.121

अध्यापनमध्ययनं यजनं याजनं तथा ।
दानं प्रतिग्रहं चैव ब्राह्मणानामकल्पयत् ॥२१

For brāhmaṇas he ordained teaching and study, performing sacrifice and officiating at sacrifice, giving gifts, and receiving gifts.

BP 1.2.122

प्रजानां पालनं राजन्दानमध्ययनं तथा ।
विषयेषु प्रसक्तिं च तथेज्यां क्षत्रियस्य तु ॥२२

For a kṣatriya he ordained the protection of subjects, generosity, study, proper engagement in worldly affairs, and the performance of sacrifice, O king.

BP 1.2.123

पशूनां रक्षणं दानमिज्याध्ययनमेव च ।
वणिक्पथं कुसीदं च वैश्यस्य कृषिरेव च ॥२३

For a vaiśya he ordained the tending of cattle, generosity, sacrifice, study, commerce, money-lending, and agriculture.

BP 1.2.124

एकमेव तु शूद्रस्य कर्म लोके प्रकीर्तितम् ।
एतेषामेव वर्णानां शुश्रूषामनुपूर्वशः ॥२४

Only one duty is proclaimed in the world for a śūdra: service, in due order, to these other varṇas.

BP 1.2.125

पुरुषस्य सदा श्रेष्ठं नाभेरूर्ध्वं नृपोत्तम ।
तस्मादपि शुचितरं मुखं तात स्वयम्भुवः ॥२५

The portion of a person above the navel is always considered superior, O best of kings; and of that, the mouth fashioned by the Self-born is still purer, my child.

BP 1.2.126

तस्मान्मुखाद्द्विजो जात इतीयं वैदिकी श्रुतिः ।
सर्वस्यैवास्य धर्मस्य धर्मतो ब्राह्मणः प्रभुः ॥२६

Therefore the twice-born was produced from the mouth—such is the Vedic revelation. By virtue of dharma, the brāhmaṇa is the guardian of this entire body of sacred law.

BP 1.2.127

स सृष्टो ब्रह्मणा पूर्वं तपस्तप्त्वा कुरूद्वह ।
हव्यानामिव कव्यानां सर्वस्यापि च गुप्तये ॥२७

After performing austerity, Brahmā created him first, O bearer of the Kuru line, for the preservation of offerings to the gods, offerings to the ancestors, and indeed of the whole order.

BP 1.2.128

अश्नन्ति च मुखेनास्य हव्यानि त्रिदिवौकसः ।
कव्यानि चैव पितरः किम्भूतमधिकं ततः ॥२८

Through his mouth the inhabitants of heaven consume sacrificial offerings, and the ancestors consume their ancestral offerings. What being, then, could be considered higher than he?

BP 1.2.129

भूतानां प्राणिनः श्रेष्ठाः प्राणिनां बुद्धिजीविनः ।
बुद्धिमत्सु नराः श्रेष्ठा नरेषु ब्राह्मणाः स्मृताः ॥२९

Among beings, living creatures are superior; among living creatures, those sustained by intelligence; among intelligent beings, humans are superior; and among humans, brāhmaṇas are traditionally regarded as superior.

BP 1.2.130

ब्राह्मणेषु च विद्वांसो विद्वत्सु कृतबुद्धयः ।
कृतबुद्धिषु कर्तारः कर्तृषु ब्रह्मवेदिनः ॥.2.१३०

Among brāhmaṇas, the learned are superior; among the learned, those of firm understanding; among those of firm understanding, those who act accordingly; and among the doers, those who know brahman.

BP 1.2.131

जन्म विप्रस्य राजेन्द्र धमार्थमिह कथ्यते ।
उत्पन्नः सर्वसिद्धयर्थं४ याति ब्रह्मसदो नृप ॥३१

The birth of a learned brāhmaṇa is said here to be for the sake of dharma, O king of kings. Born for the accomplishment of every good, he proceeds to Brahmā’s assembly, O king.

BP 1.2.132

स चापि जायमानस्तु पृथिव्यामिह जायते ।
भूतानां प्रभवायैव धर्मकोशस्य गुप्तये ॥३२

When such a person is born, he is born here upon the earth for the flourishing of beings and for the safeguarding of the treasury of dharma.

BP 1.2.133

सर्वं हि ब्राह्मणस्येदं यत्किञ्चित्पृथिवीगतम् ।
जन्मना चोत्तमेनेदं सर्वं वै ब्राह्मणोऽर्हति ॥३३

Whatever exists upon the earth is here declared to belong to the brāhmaṇa; by reason of his exalted birth, the brāhmaṇa is said to be entitled to all of it.

BP 1.2.134

स्वकीयं ब्राह्मणो भुङ्क्ते विदधाति च सुव्रत ।
करुणां कुर्वतस्तस्य भुञ्जन्ती हेतरे जनाः ॥३४

The brāhmaṇa consumes what is his own and distributes it, O keeper of good vows. Other people partake through the compassion that he extends to them.

BP 1.2.135

त्रयाणामिह वर्णानां भावाभावाय वै द्विजः ।
भवेद्राजन्न सन्देहस्तुष्टो भावाय वै द्विजः ॥३५

The twice-born is here said to possess the power to bring about the prosperity or decline of the other three classes, O king; there is no doubt that when pleased he promotes their prosperity.

BP 1.2.136

अभावाय भवेत्क्रुद्धस्तस्मात्पूज्यतमो हि सः ।
ब्राह्मणे सति नान्यस्य प्रभुत्वं विद्यते नृप । .।३६

When angered, he brings about their decline; therefore he is most worthy of honour. When a brāhmaṇa is present, no other has authority, O king.

BP 1.2.137

कामात्करोत्यसौ कर्म कामगश्च नृपोत्तम ।
तस्माद्वृन्दारकपुरी तस्मादपि महः पुनः ॥३७

He performs action according to his desire and moves wherever he wishes, O best of kings. From here he reaches the city of the gods, and beyond that the world called Mahas.

BP 1.2.138

महर्लोकाज्जनोलोकं ब्रह्मलोकं च गच्छति ।
ब्रह्मत्वं च महाबाहो याति विप्रो न संशयः ॥३८

From Maharloka he goes to Janaloka and then to Brahmaloka. A learned brāhmaṇa attains the state of brahman, O mighty-armed king; of this there is no doubt.

BP 1.2.139

शतानीक उवाच
ब्रह्मत्वं नाम दुष्प्रापं ब्रह्मलोकेषु सुव्रत ॥३९

Śatānīka said: ‘The state of brahman is said to be difficult to attain, O keeper of good vows, even among the worlds of Brahmā.’

BP 1.2.140

ब्रह्मत्वं कीदृशं विप्रो ब्रह्मलोकं च गच्छति ।
नाममात्रोऽथ किं विप्रो ब्रह्मत्वं ब्रह्मणः सदा ॥
याति ब्रह्मन्गुणाः के स्युर्ब्रह्मप्राप्तौ ममोच्यताम् ॥.2.१४०

‘What is this state of brahman, and how does a learned brāhmaṇa reach Brahmaloka? Is “vipra” merely a name, or does he truly attain Brahmā’s state? Tell me, O brāhmaṇa, what qualities lead to the attainment of brahman.’

BP 1.2.141

सुमन्तुरुवाच
साधुसाधु महाबाहो शृणु मे परमं वचः ॥४१

Sumantu said: ‘Well asked, well asked, O mighty-armed king. Hear from me this supreme teaching.’

BP 1.2.142

ये प्रोक्ता वेदशास्त्रेषु संस्कारा ब्राह्मणस्य तु ।
गर्भाधानादयो ये च संस्कारा यस्य पार्थिव ॥४२

‘The rites of consecration prescribed for a brāhmaṇa in the Vedic scriptures—those beginning with the rite of conception—must be understood, O ruler of the earth.’

BP 1.2.143

चत्वारिंशतथाष्टौ च निर्वृत्ताः शास्त्रतो नृप ।
स याति ब्रह्मणः स्थानं ब्राह्मणत्वं च मानद ॥
संस्काराः सर्वथा हेतुर्ब्रह्मत्वे नात्र संशयः ॥४३

‘When all forty-eight of these are duly completed according to scripture, O king, he reaches Brahmā’s abode and the state of a brāhmaṇa, O giver of honour. These consecrations are assuredly the cause of attaining the state of brahman; of this there is no doubt.’

BP 1.2.144

शतानीक उवाच
संस्काराः के मता ब्रह्मन्ब्रह्मत्वे ब्राह्मणस्य तु ।
शंस मे द्विजशार्दूलं कौतुकं हि महन्मम ॥४४

Śatānīka said: ‘What consecrations are held to lead a brāhmaṇa to the state of brahman? Tell me, O tiger among the twice-born, for my curiosity is very great.’

BP 1.2.145

सुमन्तुरुवाच
साधुसाधु महाबाहो शृणु मे परमं वचः ।
ये प्रोक्ता वेदशास्त्रेषु संस्कारा ब्राह्मणस्य तु ॥
मनीषिभिर्महाबाहो शृणु सर्वानशेषतः ॥४५

Sumantu said: ‘Well asked, well asked, O mighty-armed king. Hear my supreme teaching. Listen without omission to all the brāhmaṇa’s consecrations prescribed in the Vedic scriptures by the wise.’

BP 1.2.146

गर्भाधानं पुंसवनं सीमन्तोन्नयनं तथा ।
जातकर्मान्नाशनं च चूडोपनयनं नृप ॥४६

‘They are the rite of conception, the rite for begetting a son, the parting of the pregnant mother’s hair, the birth ceremony, the first feeding of solid food, the tonsure, and initiation into Vedic study, O king.’

BP 1.2.147

ब्रह्मव्रतानि चत्वारि स्नानं च तदनन्तरम् ।
सधर्मचारिणीयोगो यज्ञानां कर्म मानद ॥४७

‘Then come the four vows connected with Vedic study, the ceremonial bath upon completion, union with a wife who shares one’s dharma, and the prescribed performance of the five great sacrifices, O giver of honour.’

BP 1.2.148

पञ्चानां कार्यमित्याहुरात्मनः श्रेयसे नृप ।
देवपितृमनुष्याणां भूतानां ब्राह्मणस्तथा ॥४८

‘The wise declare that the five are to be performed for one’s own highest welfare, O king: offerings to the gods, the ancestors, human guests, living beings, and sacred learning.’

BP 1.2.149

एतेषां चाष्टकाकर्म पार्वणश्राद्धमेव हि ।
श्रावणी चाग्रहायणी चैत्री चाश्वयुजी तथा ॥४९

‘These are followed by the Aṣṭakā rite, the ancestral offering at the lunar transition, and the seasonal rites called Śrāvaṇī, Āgrahāyaṇī, Caitrī, and Āśvayujī.’

BP 1.2.150

पाकयज्ञास्तथा सप्त अग्न्याधानं च सत्क्रिया ।
अग्निहोत्रं तथा राजन्दर्शं च विधुसञ्क्षये ॥.2.१५०

‘These are the seven domestic offerings. Then come the establishment of the sacred fire with its proper ceremony, the Agnihotra, O king, and the Darśa offering at the moon’s disappearance.’

BP 1.2.151

पौर्णमासं च राजेन्द्र चातुर्मास्यानि चापि हि ।
निरूपणं पशुवधं तथा सौत्रामणीति च ॥५१

‘Also included are the Paurṇamāsa offering at the full moon, the Cāturmāsya rites, the prescribed animal offering, and the rite called Sautrāmaṇī, O king of kings.’

BP 1.2.152

हविर्यज्ञास्तथा सप्त तेषां चापि हि सत्क्रिया ।
अग्निष्टोमोऽत्यग्निष्टोमस्तथोक्थ्यः षोडशीं विदुः ॥१५२

‘These constitute the seven oblations of sacrificial food, each with its proper performance. The seven Soma rites begin with Agniṣṭoma and Atyagniṣṭoma, followed by Ukthya and Ṣoḍaśin.’

BP 1.2.153

वाजपेयोऽतिरात्रश्च आप्तोर्यामेति वै स्मृतः ।
संस्कारेषु स्थिताः सप्त सोमाः कुरुकुलोद्वह ॥५३

‘Vājapeya, Atirātra, and Āptoryāma are likewise remembered. These are the seven Soma sacrifices included among the consecratory rites, O bearer of the Kuru lineage.’

BP 1.2.154

इत्येते द्विजसंस्काराश्चत्वारिंशन्नृपोत्तम ।
अष्टौ चात्मगुणास्तात शृणु तानपि भारत ॥५४

‘These, O best of kings, are the forty consecratory rites of a twice-born person. There are also eight qualities of the self; hear of them too, my child, O descendant of Bharata.’

BP 1.2.155

अनसूया दया क्षान्तिरनायासं च मङ्गलम् ।
अकार्पण्यं तथा शौचमस्पृहा च कुरूद्वह ॥५५

‘They are freedom from envy, compassion, patience, freedom from strain, auspicious conduct, generosity, purity, and freedom from craving, O bearer of the Kuru line.’

BP 1.2.156

य एतेऽष्टगुणास्तात कीर्त्यन्ते वै मनीषिभिः ।
एतेषां लक्षणं वीर शृणु सर्वमशेषतः ॥५६

‘These eight qualities are celebrated by the wise, my child. Hear now, O hero, all their defining characteristics without omission.’

BP 1.2.157

न गुणान्गुणिनो हन्ति न स्तौत्यात्मगुणानपि ।
प्रहष्यन्ते नान्यदोषैरनसूया प्रकीर्तिता ॥५७

‘One who neither disparages the virtues of the virtuous, nor praises one’s own qualities, nor rejoices over the faults of others is said to possess freedom from envy.’

BP 1.2.158

अपरे बन्धुवर्गे वा मित्रे द्वेष्टरि वा सदा ।
आत्मवद्वर्तनं यत्स्यात्सा दया परिकीर्तिता ॥५८

‘Conducting oneself toward strangers, relatives, friends, and even enemies at all times as one would toward oneself is proclaimed to be compassion.’

BP 1.2.159

वाचा मनसि काये च दुःखेनोत्पादितेन च ।
न कुप्यति न चाप्रीतिः सा क्षमा परिकीर्तिता ॥५९

‘When pain is inflicted through speech, thought, or bodily action, not becoming angry or resentful is proclaimed to be patience.’

BP 1.2.160

अभक्ष्यपरिहारश्च संसर्गश्चाप्यनिन्दितैः ।
आचारे च व्यवस्थानं शौचमेतत्प्रकीर्तितम् ॥.2.१६०

‘Avoiding forbidden food, associating with people who are not blameworthy, and remaining firmly established in proper conduct—this is proclaimed to be purity.’

BP 1.2.161

शरीरं पीड्यते येन शुभेनापि च कर्मणा ।
अत्यन्तं तन्न कुर्वीत अनायासः स उच्यते ॥६१

‘One should not carry out to excess even a virtuous action that causes the body severe distress. This restraint is called freedom from strain.’

BP 1.2.162

प्रशस्ताचरणं नित्यमप्रशस्तविवर्जनम् ।
एतद्धि मङ्गलं प्रोक्तं मुनिभिर्ब्रह्मवादिभिः ॥६२

‘Constantly following praiseworthy conduct and avoiding what is blameworthy—this is declared to be auspicious conduct by sages who teach brahman.’

BP 1.2.163

स्तोकादपि प्रदातव्यमदीनेनान्तरात्मना ।
अहन्यहनि यत्किंचिदकार्पण्यं तदुच्यते ॥६३

‘Every day one should give something, even if only a little, with a heart free from dejection. This is called generosity.’

BP 1.2.164

यथोत्पन्नेन सन्तुष्टः स्वल्पेनाप्यथ वस्तुना ।
अहिंसया परस्वेषु साऽस्पृहा परिकीर्तिता ॥६४

‘Being content with whatever comes, even when the possession is small, and refraining from injury in relation to another’s property—this is proclaimed to be freedom from craving.’

BP 1.2.165

वपुर्यस्य तु इत्येतैः संस्कारैः संस्कृतं द्विजः ।
ब्रह्मत्वमिह सम्प्राप्य ब्रह्मलोकं च गच्छति ॥६५

‘A twice-born person whose body is refined by these consecrations attains the state of brahman here and then proceeds to Brahmaloka.’

BP 1.2.166

वैदिकैः कर्मभिः पुण्यैर्निषेकाद्यैर्द्विजन्मनाम् ।
कार्यः शरीरसंस्कारः पावनः प्रेत्य चेह च ॥६६

‘For the twice-born, bodily purification should be accomplished through meritorious Vedic rites beginning with conception; it brings purification both here and after death.’

BP 1.2.167

गर्भशुद्धिं ततः प्राप्य धर्मं चाश्रमलक्षणम् ।
याति मुक्तिं न सन्देहः पुराणेऽस्मिन्नृपोत्तम ॥६७

‘Having thereby obtained purity from the womb onward and the dharma characterized by the stages of life, one attains liberation—of this there is no doubt in this Purāṇa, O best of kings.’

BP 1.2.168

उशन्ति कुरुशार्दूल ब्राह्मणा नात्र संशयः ।
१आश्रितानां विशेषेण ये नित्यं स्वस्तिवादिनः ॥६८

‘Brāhmaṇas who continually pronounce blessings are especially valued by those who seek their protection, O tiger among the Kurus; of this there is no doubt.’

BP 1.2.169

निकटस्थान्द्विजान्हित्वा योऽन्यान्पूजयति द्विजान् ।
सिद्धं पापं तदपमानात्तद्वक्तुं नैव शक्यते ॥६९

‘One who passes over twice-born persons living nearby and honours others instead incurs a manifest sin through that disrespect—its extent cannot even be expressed.’

BP 1.2.170

तस्मात्सदा समीपस्थः सम्पूज्यो विधिवन्नृप ।
पूजयेदतिथींस्तद्वदन्नपानादिदानतः ॥.2.१७०

‘Therefore a person living nearby should always be duly honoured, O king. Guests should likewise be honoured by gifts of food, drink, and other necessities.’

BP 1.2.171

ब्राह्मणः सर्ववर्णानां ज्येष्ठः श्रेष्ठस्तथोत्तमः ।
एवमस्मिन्पुराणे तु संस्कारान्ब्राह्मणस्य तु ॥७१

The brāhmaṇa is eldest, best and foremost among all varṇas. Such are the brāhmaṇa’s saṃskāras in this Purāṇa.

BP 1.2.172

शृणोति यश्च जानाति यश्चापि पठते सदा ।
ऋद्धिं वृद्धिं तथा कीर्तिं प्राप्येह श्रियमुत्तमाम् ॥७२

Whoever hears these rites, understands them, or continually recites them obtains prosperity, increase, fame, and supreme good fortune here in this world.

BP 1.2.173

धनं धान्यं यशश्चापि पुत्रान्बन्धून्सुरूपताम् ।
सावित्रं लोकमासाद्य ब्रह्मलोकमवाप्नुयात् ॥७३

Obtaining wealth, grain, renown, children, relatives, and beauty, that person reaches the world of Savitṛ and thereafter attains Brahmaloka.
