VP 3.13.1

और्व उवाच— स-चैलस्य पितुः स्नानं जाते पुत्रे विधीयते।
जात-कर्म तदा कुर्याच् छ्राद्धम् अभ्युदये च यत्

When a son is born, the father is prescribed to bathe with his clothes on. He should then perform the birth-rite and the prosperity śrāddha as prescribed.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
सचेलस्य पितुः (sacelasya pituḥ) — of the clothed father
स्नानं (snānaṃ) — bathing
जाते पुत्रे (jāte putre) — when a son is born
विधीयते (vidhīyate) — is prescribed
जातकर्म (jātakarma) — the birth-rite
तथा कुर्यात् (tathā kuryāt) — he should likewise perform
श्राद्धम् अभ्युदयं (śrāddham abhyudayaṃ) — the prosperity-śrāddha
च यत् (ca yat) — and whatever [is prescribed]
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sa-cailasya pituḥ snānaṃ jāte putre vidhīyate|jāta-karma tadā kuryāc chrāddham abhyudaye ca yat

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

त्रयोदशेऽथ श्राद्धानि सनिमित्तान्यवर्णयत् । प्रेतक्रियास्तथा तासु यथार्हमधिकारिणः । पितुः पुत्रजन्मकाले सन्निहितस्य

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

In the thirteenth chapter he describes śrāddhas with their occasions, and the rites for the dead, together with the persons entitled to perform them; this concerns the father present when a son is born.

VP 3.13.2

युग्मान् देवांश् च पित्र्यांश् च सम्यक् सव्य-क्रमाद् द्विजान्।
पूजयेद् भोजयेच् चैव तन्-मना नान्य-मानसः

He should worship the gods and the ancestors in pairs, properly and in clockwise order, and feed the twice-born, keeping his mind fixed on that and on nothing else.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
युग्मान् (yugmān) — in pairs
देवांश् च (devāṃś ca) — the gods
पित्र्यांश् च (pitryāṃś ca) — the ancestors
सम्यक् (samyak) — properly
सव्यक्रमात् (savyakramāt) — by clockwise order
द्विजान् (dvijān) — the twice-born
पूजयेत् (pūjayet) — he should worship
भोजयेत् च एव (bhojayet ca eva) — he should also feed
तन्-मनाः (tan-manāḥ) — with mind fixed on that
न अन्यमानसः (na anyamānasaḥ) — not with mind directed elsewhere
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yugmān devāṃś ca pitryāṃś ca samyak savya-kramād dvijān|pūjayed bhojayec caiva tan-manā nānya-mānasaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सव्यक्रमात् प्रदक्षिणक्रमेण । नान्यमानसः अन्यस्मिन् उत्पन्नपुत्रादौ मानसं यस्य सः श्राद्धसमये तादृशो भवेदित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Savyakrama means the clockwise or pradakṣiṇa order. Nān-yamānasa means one whose mind is not directed elsewhere—especially toward a newly born child; at the time of the śrāddha he should be attentive in this way.

VP 3.13.5

कन्या-पुत्र-विवाहेषु प्रवेशेषु च वेश्मनः।
नाम-कर्मणि बालानां चूडाकर्मादिके तथा

At the marriage of a daughter or son, on entering a house, at the naming of children, at the tonsure rite, and likewise on such occasions, the same rite should be performed.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
कन्या-पुत्र-विवाहेषु (kanyā-putra-vivāheṣu) — at the marriage of daughter or son

नाम-कर्मणि बालानाम् (nāma-karmaṇi bālānām) — at the naming of children
चूडा-कर्म-आदिके (cūḍā-karma-ādike) — at the tonsure rite and the like

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kanyā-putra-vivāheṣu praveśeṣu ca veśmanaḥ|nāma-karmaṇi bālānāṃ cūḍākarmādike tathā

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

नान्दीमुखम् पितृगणम् (nāndīmukham pitṛgaṇam) — the Nandīmukha ancestors
पूजयेत् (pūjayet) — he should worship
प्रयतः गृही (prayataḥ gṛhī) — the attentive householder
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sīmantonnayane caiva putrādi-mukha-darśane|nāndīmukhaṃ pitṛ-gaṇaṃ pūjayet prayato gṛhī

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पुत्रस्यादिमुखदर्शने प्रथमतो दर्शने । तच्च जन्मकाले सन्निहितस्यैव पितुस्तथैवोक्तत्वात्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Putrasyādimukhadarśane means at the first seeing of the son’s face. This is stated because the father is present at the time of birth, as has been said.

Snāna means the means of bathing; those who bathe with their previously worn garments still on are called sacela.

Praveśa is at the time the cows enter, or when the stars become visible. Kaṭakarma means the rites connected with the corpse during the period of impurity; prastara means a bed of grass and the like.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Brāhmaṇa-feeding applies to Sapindas and Samanodakas. The distinction of Sapindas and the rest is stated in the Kūrma: Sapindatā ends in the seventh generation; the state of sharing the same water applies when the birth and name are not known.
