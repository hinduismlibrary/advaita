VP 3.4.1

आद्यो वेदश् चतुष्पादः शत-साहस्र-सम्मितः।
ततो दश-गुणः कृत्स्नो यज्ञोऽयं सर्व-काम-धुक्

The primordial Veda was fourfold and measured at one hundred thousand. From it proceeded this complete tenfold sacrifice, which fulfils every desire.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
आद्यः (ādyaḥ) — first
वेदः (vedaḥ) — the Veda
चतुष्पादः (catuṣpādaḥ) — fourfold, having four divisions
शतसाहस्रसम्मितः (śatasāhasrasammitaḥ) — measured in one hundred thousand
ततः (tataḥ) — from it, thereafter
दशगुणः (daśaguṇaḥ) — tenfold
कृत्स्नः (kṛtsnaḥ) — complete
यज्ञः अयम् (yajñaḥ ayam) — this sacrifice
सर्वकामधुक् (sarvakāmadhuk) — fulfiller of every desire
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ādyo vedaś catuṣpādaḥ śata-sāhasra-sammitaḥ|tato daśa-guṇaḥ kṛtsno yajño'yaṃ sarva-kāma-dhuk

Śrīdhara

aṣṭāviṃśe dvāpare’tra nānyatrāpi prasaṅgataḥ kṛṣṇadvaipāyanenaiva vedā vyastā itīryate । ādya īśvarād bhūtaścatuṣpāda ṛgādicaturbhedasamūharūpaḥ śatasāhasrasambhita ityarthaḥ । tataḥ pravṛtto’yaṃ kṛtsno jño daśaguṇaḥ daśavidhaḥ, agnihotradarśapaurṇamāsacāturmāsyapaśusomā iti pañcavidhaḥ, sa eva prakṛtivikṛtibhedena daśavidha iti । yadvā gṛhyoktaiḥ pañcayajñaiḥ saha daśavidhatvam

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अष्टाविंशे द्वापरेऽत्र नान्यत्रापि प्रसङ्गतः कृष्णद्वैपायनेनैव वेदा व्यस्ता इतीर्यते । आद्य ईश्वराद् भूतश्चतुष्पाद ऋगादिचतुर्भेदसमूहरूपः शतसाहस्रसम्भित इत्यर्थः । ततः प्रवृत्तोऽयं कृत्स्नो ज्ञो दशगुणः दशविधः, अग्निहोत्रदर्शपौर्णमासचातुर्मास्यपशुसोमा इति पञ्चविधः, स एव प्रकृतिविकृतिभेदेन दशविध इति । यद्वा गृह्योक्तैः पञ्चयज्ञैः सह दशविधत्वम्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains that the Vedas were divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana specifically in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara. The primordial Veda arose from the Lord and was fourfold, comprising the four divisions beginning with Ṛg. The statement that it was measured at one hundred thousand expresses this. From it proceeded the complete sacrifice. Tenfold means ten kinds: five sacrificial rites, Agnihotra, Darśa-Pūrṇamāsa, Cāturmāsya, Paśu, and Soma, with primary and derivative forms making ten. Alternatively, it is tenfold when the five sacrifices taught in the Gṛhya texts are included.

VP 3.4.2

ततोऽत्र मत्-सुतो व्यासो अष्टाविंशतिमेऽन्तरे।
वेदम् एकं चतुष्पादं चतुर्धा व्यभजत् प्रभुः

Then, in this twenty-eighth Dvāpara, my son Vyāsa, the Lord, divided the one fourfold Veda into four parts.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

वेदम् एकम् चतुष्पादम् (vedam ekam catuṣpādam) — the one fourfold Veda

व्यभजत् (vyabhajat) — divided

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tato'tra mat-suto vyāso aṣṭāviṃśatime'ntare|vedam ekaṃ catuṣpādaṃ caturdhā vyabhajat prabhuḥ

Śrīdhara

atra manvantare aṣṭāviṃśatitame dvāpare catuṣpādamekaṃ santaṃ vedaṃ caturddhā ṛgyajuḥsāmātharvarūpeṇa pṛthag vyabhajata । prabhuḥ īśvaraḥ avatārarūpaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत्र मन्वन्तरे अष्टाविंशतितमे द्वापरे चतुष्पादमेकं सन्तं वेदं चतुर्द्धा ऋग्यजुःसामाथर्वरूपेण पृथग् व्यभजत । प्रभुः ईश्वरः अवताररूपः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Here, in this Manvantara and in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara, the Lord—an incarnation of Īśvara—divided the single fourfold Veda separately into the forms of Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman, and Atharvan.

VP 3.4.3

Just as the wise Vedavyāsa divided the Vedas, so they were divided by all those Vyāsas, and likewise by me.

The Vedas were divided by all those earlier Vyāsas collectively, and by me in the twenty-sixth Dvāpara.

VP 3.4.4

तद् अनेनैव वेदानां शाखा-भेदान् द्विजोत्तम।
चतुर्युगेषु रचितान् समस्तेष्व् अवधारय

Therefore, O best of the twice-born, understand from this example the divisions of the Veda made in every one of the four yugas.

Śrīdhara

tat tasmāt anena kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛtena vedavibhāgena dṛṣṭāntena caturyugeṣu sarveṣvapi vedavibhāgān pūrvaiḥ kṛtān avadhāraya jānīhi

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तत् तस्मात् अनेन कृष्णद्वैपायनकृतेन वेदविभागेन दृष्टान्तेन चतुर्युगेषु सर्वेष्वपि वेदविभागान् पूर्वैः कृतान् अवधारय जानीहि

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Therefore, using the division of the Veda made by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana as the example, understand that the divisions of the Veda were made by earlier Vyāsas in all four yugas.

VP 3.4.5

कृष्ण-द्वैपायनं व्यासं विद्धि नारायणं प्रभुम्।
कोऽन्यो हि भुवि मैत्रेय महाभारत-कृद् भवेत्

Know Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa as the Lord Nārāyaṇa. Who else on earth, O Maitreya, could have composed the Mahābhārata?

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
कृष्णद्वैपायनम् व्यासम् (kṛṣṇadvaipāyanam vyāsam) — Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kṛṣṇa-dvaipāyanaṃ vyāsaṃ viddhi nārāyaṇaṃ prabhum|ko'nyo hi bhuvi maitreya mahābhārata-kṛd bhavet

Śrīdhara

tadevaṃ kṛṣṇadvaipāyanavyāsasya mukhyatvamuktvā tatra hetum āha—kṛṣṇa iti । ataḥ asmin manvantara aṣṭāviṃśe eva dvāpare bhāratāvirbhāva iti gamyate

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदेवं कृष्णद्वैपायनव्यासस्य मुख्यत्वमुक्त्वा तत्र हेतुम् आह—कृष्ण इति । अतः अस्मिन् मन्वन्तर अष्टाविंशे एव द्वापरे भारताविर्भाव इति गम्यते

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Having stated the pre-eminence of Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa, Śrīdhara gives the reason in the next verse: Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa should be known as Lord Nārāyaṇa. From this it is understood that, in this Manvantara, the Bhārata appeared in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The expression in Itihāsa and Purāṇa is an archaic construction. These are varied narratives resting on accounts of divine sages. Itihāsa is said to be connected with accounts of past and future dharma. It has five characteristics beginning with creation, and ten when distinctions among creation, incarnations, and the like are intended.

eka āsīditi yajurvedikādhvaryavakriyābāhulyāt pradhānakarmaṇaśca yājanasya tadvihitatvāt yajuḥprādhānyādevamuktam— yacchiṣṭañca yajurveda tena yajñamayuñjata । yājanāddhi yajurveda iti śāstrasya niścayaḥ ॥ iti vāyukteḥ । hotropalakṣitāścatvāra ṛtvijaścatvāro hotāraḥ । taiḥ anuṣṭheyaṃ karma cāturhotram, tat yasminnabhūt, jātaṃ, tena caturddhā vibhaktena vedena yajñam akarot pravartitavān

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एक आसीदिति यजुर्वेदिकाध्वर्यवक्रियाबाहुल्यात् प्रधानकर्मणश्च याजनस्य तद्विहितत्वात् यजुःप्राधान्यादेवमुक्तम्— यच्छिष्टञ्च यजुर्वेद तेन यज्ञमयुञ्जत । याजनाद्धि यजुर्वेद इति शास्त्रस्य निश्चयः ॥ इति वायुक्तेः । होत्रोपलक्षिताश्चत्वार ऋत्विजश्चत्वारो होतारः । तैः अनुष्ठेयं कर्म चातुर्होत्रम्, तत् यस्मिन्नभूत्, जातं, तेन चतुर्द्धा विभक्तेन वेदेन यज्ञम् अकरोत् प्रवर्तितवान्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains that the statement there was one refers to the Yajurveda. Adhvaryu actions predominate in it, it is prescribed as a means of sacrifice, and Yajus predominates there. The Vāyu Purāṇa citation says that the remaining Yajus is used to join the sacrifice and derives the name Yajurveda from sacrificial action. The four officiating priests are the Hotṛ and the three other priests, and their rite is the Cāturhotra. Because that rite arose in the fourfold Veda, Vyāsa instituted the sacrifice with that Veda.

saṃhitā-tritayaṃ cakre śāka-pūrṇas tathetaraḥ|niruktam akarot tadvac caturthaṃ muni-sattama

Śrīdhara

śākapūrṇistu indrapramatiśiṣyastatsaṃhitāyāḥ saṃhitātritayaṃ niruktañca vedaśabdanirvacanarūpam akarot

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

शाकपूर्णिस्तु इन्द्रप्रमतिशिष्यस्तत्संहितायाः संहितात्रितयं निरुक्तञ्च वेदशब्दनिर्वचनरूपम् अकरोत्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śākapūrṇi, a disciple of Indrapramiti, made three Saṃhitās from that Saṃhitā and also made the Nirukta, a Vedāṅga concerned with deriving the meanings of Vedic words.

VP 3.4.24

क्रौञ्चो वैतालिकस् तद्वद् बलाकश् च महा-मुनिः।
निरुक्त-कृच् चतुर्थोऽभूद् वेद-वेदाङ्ग-पारगः

Krauñca, Vaitālika, and the great sage Balāka were three. Niruktakṛt was the fourth, accomplished in the Veda and its auxiliary disciplines.

Śrīdhara

tacca saṃhitātritayaṃ krauñcādibhyaḥ adāt । caturtho niruktakṛt nāmeti kecit paṭhanti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तच्च संहितात्रितयं क्रौञ्चादिभ्यः अदात् । चतुर्थो निरुक्तकृत् नामेति केचित् पठन्ति

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śākapūrṇi gave those three Saṃhitās to Krauñca and the others. Some readers take Niruktakṛt as the name of the fourth recipient.

VP 3.4.25

इत्य् एताः प्रतिशाखाभ्यो ह्य् अनुशाखा द्विजोत्तम।
बाष्कलश् चापरास् तिस्रः संहिताः कृतवान् द्विज।
शिष्यः कालायनिर् गार्ग्यश् तृतीयश् च कथाजवः

Thus, O best of the twice-born, subsidiary branches arose from the branch-divisions. Bāṣkali also composed three further Saṃhitās, O twice-born one. The disciples were Kālāyani, Gārgya, and Kathājavā, the third.

ity etāḥ pratiśākhābhyo hy anuśākhā dvijottama|bāṣkalaś cāparās tisraḥ saṃhitāḥ kṛtavān dvija|śiṣyaḥ kālāyanir gārgyaś tṛtīyaś ca kathājavaḥ

Śrīdhara

anuśākhāḥ avāntaraśākhāḥ । bāskaliḥ pailaśiṣyaḥ tisraḥ saṃhitāścake ityuktaṃ, sa evānyāstisraḥ saṃhitāḥ kṛtavān । apara eva śākalyasatīrtho bāskaliḥ tacchiṣyāḥ kālāyanipramukhāstrayaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अनुशाखाः अवान्तरशाखाः । बास्कलिः पैलशिष्यः तिस्रः संहिताश्चके इत्युक्तं, स एवान्यास्तिस्रः संहिताः कृतवान् । अपर एव शाकल्यसतीर्थो बास्कलिः तच्छिष्याः कालायनिप्रमुखास्त्रयः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains that the first Bāṣkali mentioned is Paila’s disciple, who made three Saṃhitās and then three more. The line about Kālāyani, Gārgya, and Kathājavā refers to a different Bāṣkali, Śākalya’s fellow student, and that Bāṣkali’s three disciples.
