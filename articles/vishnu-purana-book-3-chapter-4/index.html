VP 3.4.1

आद्यो वेदश् चतुष्पादः शतसाहस्रसम्मितः । ततो दशगुणः कृत्स्नो यज्ञोऽयं सर्वकामधुक् ॥१॥

The primordial Veda was fourfold and measured at one hundred thousand. From it proceeded this complete tenfold sacrifice, which fulfils every desire.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara explains that the Vedas were divided by Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana specifically in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara. The primordial Veda arose from the Lord and was fourfold, comprising the four divisions beginning with Ṛg. The statement that it was measured at one hundred thousand expresses this. From it proceeded the complete sacrifice. Tenfold means ten kinds: five sacrificial rites, Agnihotra, Darśa-Pūrṇamāsa, Cāturmāsya, Paśu, and Soma, with primary and derivative forms making ten. Alternatively, it is tenfold when the five sacrifices taught in the Gṛhya texts are included.

VP 3.4.2

ततोऽत्र मत्सुतो व्यासो[^३०] अष्टाविंशतिमेऽन्तरे । वेदम् एकं चतुष्पादं चतुर्धा व्यभजत् प्रभुः ॥२॥

Then, in this twenty-eighth Dvāpara, my son Vyāsa, the Lord, divided the one fourfold Veda into four parts.

Śrīdhara translation

Here, in this Manvantara and in the twenty-eighth Dvāpara, the Lord—an incarnation of Īśvara—divided the single fourfold Veda separately into the forms of Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman, and Atharvan.

Śrīdhara explains that the statement there was one refers to the Yajurveda. Adhvaryu actions predominate in it, it is prescribed as a means of sacrifice, and Yajus predominates there.
