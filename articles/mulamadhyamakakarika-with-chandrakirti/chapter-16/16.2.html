MMK 16.2

pudgalaḥ saṃsarati cet skandhāyatanadhātuṣu
pañcadhā mṛgyamāṇo 'sau nāsti kaḥ saṃsariṣyati || 2 ||

If a person transmigrates, when sought fivefold among the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, he does not exist. Who will transmigrate?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pudgalaḥ saṃsarati cet skandhāyatanadhātuṣu | pañcadhā mṛgyamāṇo 'sau nāsti kaḥ saṃsariṣyati || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: This sequence of reasoning by which transmigration of formations is not established cannot be the same for a being, because it has been said here that there is no transmigration of formations that are permanent or impermanent. But the self is not like that, because it is established as neither permanent nor impermanent, just as it is undescribable as identical with or different from the aggregates. Therefore the self indeed transmigrates, and the stated fault does not follow. The response:

If there were some person, he would transmigrate. But he does not exist, because when sought fivefold among aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, he is not found. How?

“Moreover, fuel is not fire; fire is not elsewhere than fuel; fire is not possessed of fuel; fuel is not in fire; it is not in them.”

“The sequence concerning self and appropriation has been explained through fire and fuel.”

In this way, the self is not the own-being of aggregates, sense-fields, and elements; nor is it apart from them; it is not one possessing aggregates, sense-fields, and elements; self is not in aggregates, sense-fields, and elements; aggregates, sense-fields, and elements are not in the self. In this way, when sought fivefold, the self does not exist by the reasoning stated before. And that which, when examined in this way fivefold among aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, does not exist, how will that, being nonexistent, transmigrate? Thus there is no saṃsāra even for the self, because it is nonexistent, like a barren woman’s son.
