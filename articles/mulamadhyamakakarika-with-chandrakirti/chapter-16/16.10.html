MMK 16.10

na nirvāṇasamāropo na saṃsārāpakarṣaṇam
yatra kas tatra saṃsāro nirvāṇaṃ kiṃ vikalpyate || 10 ||

There is no superimposition of nirvāṇa and no removal of saṃsāra. Where is saṃsāra there? What nirvāṇa is imagined?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na nirvāṇasamāropo na saṃsārāpakarṣaṇam | yatra kas tatra saṃsāro nirvāṇaṃ kiṃ vikalpyate || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Therefore, from this authoritative text, ultimate truth is stated:

“There is no superimposition of nirvāṇa, and no removal of saṃsāra. Where is saṃsāra there? What nirvāṇa is imagined?”

Here, indeed, in ultimate truth, neither a superimposition of nirvāṇa—an imputation of nirvāṇa—is possible, because it is not found; nor is a removal of saṃsāra, the exhaustion of saṃsāra, possible. What saṃsāra is there that is imagined for the sake of exhausting it? Or what nirvāṇa is there that is imagined for the sake of attaining it? Or alternatively: There, even by one making an effort, it is not possible to remove any being from saṃsāra and to superimpose him in nirvāṇa, because saṃsāra and nirvāṇa also are not found. What nirvāṇa is imagined there? For nothing at all is proper to imagine. And for one who does not imagine, surely there will be passing beyond the wilderness and desolate region of saṃsāra described above, and attainment of the city of nirvāṇa. Therefore it is said in the noble Māradamana Sūtra:

“Then the young Mañjuśrī at that time attended in such a way that Māra the Evil One, bound by a fetter fastened to a stake, fallen to the ground, cried out: ‘I am bound by a tight bondage.’ Mañjuśrī said: ‘Evil One, is there a bondage tighter than this bondage, by which you are permanently bound and are not bound again? And what is that? This bondage of the inversion of the conceit “I am,” the bondage of craving and views: this is the bondage, Evil One. There is no bondage tighter than this bondage. By it you are permanently bound and are not bound again.’ The remainder is abbreviated. He said: ‘Would you be glad, Evil One, if you were released?’ He said: ‘I would be glad; I would be exceedingly glad.’

“Then the god’s son Suyāma said this to the young Mañjuśrī: ‘Release Māra the Evil One, Mañjuśrī. Let him go to his own abode.’ Then the young Mañjuśrī said this to Māra the Evil One: ‘By what are you bound, Evil One, since you release yourself?’ He said: ‘I do not know, Mañjuśrī, by what I am bound.’ He said: ‘Just as you, Evil One, being unbound, have the perception “bound,” in just the same way all foolish ordinary people have the perception of permanence in the impermanent, the perception of ease in suffering, the perception of purity in the impure, the perception of self in what is not self, the perception of form in the formless, the perception of feeling in the absence of feeling, the perception of perception in the absence of perception, the perception of formations in what is unconditioned, the perception of consciousness in the absence of consciousness. But further, Evil One, you who will be released, from what will you be released?’ He said: ‘I do not know; I will be released from somewhere.’ He said: ‘In just the same way, Evil One, those who will be released are not released from anything at all, except that false perception. They know it; having known it, they are called released.’ Thus, in detail.”

Therefore, from this authoritative text, the severing of the vine-bondage that is merely the imagining of a false inversion is called release, nirvāṇa, like the extinguishing, by wind and water, of the flame of a fire seen in a dream.

Thus ends the sixteenth chapter, called “Investigation of Bondage and Release,” in the Madhyamaka commentary, the Prasannapadā, composed by the Teacher Candrakīrti.
