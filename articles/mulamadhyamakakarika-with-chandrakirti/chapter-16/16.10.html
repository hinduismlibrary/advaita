MMK 16.10

When nirvana is not born and samsara not eliminated, then what is samsara? And what is considered as nirvana?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na nirvāṇasamāropo na saṃsārāpakarṣaṇam | yatra kas tatra saṃsāro nirvāṇaṃ kiṃ vikalpyate || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

In ultimate truth there is no superimposition of nirvāṇa and no removal of saṃsāra. If neither can be found as an independently existing state, what saṃsāra could be eliminated, and what nirvāṇa could be attained? Nothing should be grasped through conceptual construction. Without that fixation, one crosses the wilderness of saṃsāra and reaches peace.

Candrakīrti concludes with a sūtra image: Māra is bound most tightly by the belief in “I am” and by craving and views. When Mañjuśrī asks what binds him, Māra cannot identify a real binder; likewise, beings mistake the impermanent for permanent, suffering for happiness, and what is not self for self. They are freed by recognizing these ideas as false. It is like extinguishing a fire seen in a dream with water from the same dream: liberation is the ending of mistaken grasping, not the acquisition of a separate thing called nirvāṇa.
