MMK 16.9

"I, without clinging, am beyond misery. Nirvana is mine." Those who grasp in that way have great grasping and clinging.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nirvāsyāmy anupādāno nirvāṇaṃ me bhaviṣyati | iti yeṣāṃ grahas teṣām upādānamahāgrahaḥ || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent worries that denying bondage and liberation makes the path pointless. Candrakīrti answers that the thought “I will be free of all clinging, and nirvāṇa will be mine” itself holds tightly to “I” and “mine.” When all phenomena are like reflections, mirages, dreams, or illusions, grasping a self who will possess liberation is a particularly strong form of appropriation. The seeker should relinquish this claim to a self and a personal nirvāṇa.

A sūtra explains that beings remain in saṃsāra because they grasp a self and another, then become attached, hostile, and confused. Even a practitioner may turn the path into a story about “my” virtue, “my” liberation, and “my” attainment. The Buddha’s teaching of the four truths is not another set of things to possess: formations are unborn, causes do not arise, cessation is not an object, and the path is cultivated without fixation. Freedom comes through seeing this, not through acquiring a self-owned state.
