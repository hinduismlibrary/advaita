MMK 16.8

baddho na mucyate tāvad abaddho naiva mucyate
syātāṃ baddhe mucyamāne yugapad bandhamokṣaṇe || 8 ||

The bound one is not released; the unbound one is certainly not released. If a bound one is being released, bondage and release would be simultaneous.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

baddho na mucyate tāvad abaddho naiva mucyate | syātāṃ baddhe mucyamāne yugapad bandhamokṣaṇe || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: Although bondage has been denied by you, nevertheless that liberation exists for the sake of which the Tathāgatas, great in compassion, taught the three heaps consisting of discipline, concentration, and wisdom for beings, countless, bound in the prison of the wandering in saṃsāra. And there is no liberation for an unbound person. Therefore bondage too exists. The response: Bondage would exist if liberation itself existed. Here, this liberation, when conceived, would be conceived either for one who is bound or for one who is unbound. What follows? It is not reasonable in either way, he says:

“The bound one is not released; the unbound one is certainly not released. If a bound one is being released, bondage and release would be simultaneous.”

There, liberation of one who is bound is not possible, because he is bound. But if it is said that one who is bound is released later by a means, then one should not say, “The bound one is released”; rather, what should be said? “He will be released.” If it is said “he is being released” because of proximity to the present, if liberation could ever occur, then it would be near. But when, in any state whatsoever, liberation is intended to be impossible because liberation of one who is bound is impossible, whence the proximity to the present? Thus it is established for now that the bound one is not released.

Now, the unbound one too is not released. For he is already released. What would liberation do for him again? And because the liberated Arhats would again need liberation, there would be only a state of bondage, and thus there would be bondage even for an Arhat.

Or it may be: Because liberation of one who is unbound is impossible, it is precisely one who is bound who is being released. In that case, when one who is bound is conceived as being released, because of being bound and being in the process of release, bondage and release would be simultaneous. But because they mutually contradict one another, bondage and release cannot occur at one time in one thing, like light and darkness.

Since in this way liberation of those who are bound and unbound is impossible, therefore liberation too does not exist; and because that does not exist, it is established that bondage too does not exist.
