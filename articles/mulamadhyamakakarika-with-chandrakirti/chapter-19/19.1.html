MMK 19.1

pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca yadyatītamapekṣya hi
pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca kāle 'tīte bhaviṣyataḥ || 1 ||

If the present and future depend on the past, the present and future would exist in past time.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca yadyatītamapekṣya hi | pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca kāle 'tīte bhaviṣyataḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The nineteenth chapter, called “Examination of Time.” An opponent says: “The own-nature of entities indeed exists, because it is the cause for designating the three times. Here, the Blessed One taught the times as dependent on what is past, future, and present. And they are based on entities. For an entity’s own-nature, when it has arisen and ceased, is designated ‘past’; when it has arisen and has not ceased, it is ‘present’; and when it has not obtained its own-nature, it is ‘future.’ Thus times based upon, and dependent on, the own-nature of entities were taught. They exist. Therefore the own-nature of entities on which they depend also exists.” It is replied: “An own-nature of entities would be the cause for designating the three times if the three times that you intend existed. But they do not exist. And showing how they do not exist, he says:”

“If the present and future depend on the past, the present and future would exist in past time.” || 1 ||

Here, first: if present and future times exist, do they exist in dependence on past time, or independently of it? In this matter, if they are established in dependence on past time, then they would certainly be in past time. For that which does not exist in something is not dependent on it, as a barren woman is not dependent on her own son, a sky-jasmine vine on its own flower, or sand on its own oil. Someone may say: “Though it does not exist, darkness is dependent on a lamp, and a lamp too is dependent on darkness, as its opposite.” This is not so, because this too is equal to what is being established. Here, if the times present and future are accepted as existing in past time, then, in order that their dependence be established, since they exist in past time, they too would be past, just like the nature of past time. Then even the past would not exist. For that which has passed beyond the present state is past; that which has not yet been reached is future. But when present and future do not exist at all, how could anything be past? Therefore even the past would not exist. || 1 ||
