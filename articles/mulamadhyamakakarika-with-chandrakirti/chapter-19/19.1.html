MMK 19.1

If the present and the future were contingent on the past, then the present and the future would have existed in the past.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca yadyatītamapekṣya hi | pratyutpanno 'nāgataśca kāle 'tīte bhaviṣyataḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that the past, present, and future are established through the things that occupy them, so those things must have their own nature. Candrakīrti asks whether the present and future depend on the past or exist independently of it.

If present and future were already contained in the past, they would themselves count as past, and the past would lose its distinction as what has passed. The three times cannot be established by making one time contain the others.
