MMK 1.8

An existent phenomenon is clearly said to have no object at all. If the phenomenon has no object, where can the object exist?

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Which phenomena here are said to possess an object? According to scripture, all minds and mental factors. The object-condition of these is the object, such as a visible form, in dependence upon which mind and mental factors arise in their respective ways. This would have to be imagined either for existent phenomena or for nonexistent ones.

For what already exists, there is no need of an object-condition. An object is proposed for the purpose of producing a phenomenon; yet that phenomenon is already in existence before the object. If in this way the phenomenon is established by itself without an object, what use is an imagined relation to an object? This existent phenomenon—mind or something else—is merely called 'possessing an object' by you through your own conception, although it has no relation with an object.

If instead an object is posited for what does not exist, that too is unreasonable, as is indicated by the expression 'this is without an object' and what follows. Something nonexistent has no connection with an object.

The passage says, 'this existent phenomenon is taught to be without an object'; the words 'by you as possessing an object' complete the statement. As for 'If the phenomenon is without an object, how then could there be an object?' the particle atha introduces a question and kutaḥ concerns the reason. Its meaning is: if the phenomenon is thus without an object and does not exist, whence again could there be an object? Since there is nothing that is the possessor of an object, the object too is absent.

How, then, can mind and mental factors possess objects? There is no fault, because this characterization is conventional, not ultimate. [Nāgārjuna] now speaks to refute the immediately preceding condition.
