MMK 1.10

Because the existence of essence-less things does not exist, it is incorrect to say:"When this exists, that arises."

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, to refute the nature of the dominant condition, he says:

Here the definition of the dominant condition is that [the factor] on whose presence another occurs is dominant with respect to that [other]. But, since things are dependently arisen and therefore lack intrinsic nature, where is that which is designated as 'this' in the role of a cause, and where is that which is designated as 'that' in the role of the effect? Therefore conditions are not established even through [their] defining characteristics.

Objection: 'Having observed a cloth and similar things in dependence on threads and other things, one calls the threads and others the conditions of the cloth and so on.' Reply: The occurrence of results such as cloth is not itself present by intrinsic nature; how then could the things called conditions be established as conditions? In order to explain how results such as cloth fail to arise [by intrinsic nature], [Nāgārjuna] speaks in the next verse.
