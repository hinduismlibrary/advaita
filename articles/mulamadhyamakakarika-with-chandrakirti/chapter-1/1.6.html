MMK 1.6

It is impossible for something that either exists or not to have conditions. If it were non-existent, of what would these be the conditions? If it were existent, why would it need conditions?

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

How could there be a condition for a nonexistent thing, for one which is not present? If the designation is made with reference to what will exist in the future, this is not acceptable, because of the faults stated in such passages as 'If designation by what is future is accepted, there is no future existence without a capacity.' For what exists and has already been born, the imagining of a condition is likewise fruitless.

Thus, after demonstrating that conditions taken together have no capacity to produce an effect and hence are not conditions, [Nāgārjuna] proceeds to establish that the separate kinds of conditions are not conditions either.

An opponent says: 'Even if conditions cannot be established in this way, their existence is nevertheless certain because their characteristics are taught. The definition of a causal condition is that it is a producer. It is not reasonable to assign a characteristic to something nonexistent, just as one cannot assign a characteristic to the son of a barren woman.' We reply: a causal condition might exist if its defining characteristic existed. For:
