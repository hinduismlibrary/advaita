MMK 4.1

Apart from the cause of form, form is not perceived. Apart from "form", the cause of form also does not appear.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rūpakāraṇanirmuktaṃ na rūpamupalabhyate | rūpeṇāpi na nirmuktaṃ dṛśyate rūpakāraṇam || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that if the sense faculties are not established, neither are the aggregates; since the faculties are included in the aggregates and have not been separately refuted, they must exist. Candrakīrti replies that the aggregates would exist only if the elements they depend on were established. He begins with the aggregate of form.

“Form” here means material form. Its causes are the four great elements. Apart from those elements, no separate material form—whether visible, audible, fragrant, flavorful, or tangible—is found, just as one does not find a cloth separate from its material basis. Nor is a cause of form found separately from form itself.
