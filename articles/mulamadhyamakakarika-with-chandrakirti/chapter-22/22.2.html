MMK 22.2

buddhaḥ skandhānupādāya yadi nāsti svabhāvataḥ
svabhāvataśca yo nāsti kutaḥ sa parabhāvataḥ || 2 ||

If the Buddha, depending on the aggregates, does not exist by own-nature, how can he, who does not exist by own-nature, exist by another-nature?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

buddhaḥ skandhānupādāya yadi nāsti svabhāvataḥ | svabhāvataśca yo nāsti kutaḥ sa parabhāvataḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here some say: “We do not say that the aggregates are the Tathāgata, because the faults stated above would follow; nor [do we say] that he is separate from the aggregates. We do not describe stainless aggregates as being in the Tathāgata, as a grove of trees is on Mount Himavat; nor do we describe the Tathāgata as being in the aggregates, as a lion is among a grove of trees. Nor do we describe him as one who possesses aggregates, like a universal monarch who possesses marks, precisely because we do not accept identity or difference. What then? Depending on the stainless aggregates, we establish the Tathāgata as inexpressible by identity, difference, and so forth. Therefore this method does not refute us.” To this we reply:

If, depending on the aggregates, the Buddha is designated as inexpressible as identical with them or different from them, then it plainly follows that he does not exist by own-nature, because he is designated in dependence, like a reflection. And that which now does not exist by own-nature, in its own form, how can that, absent by own-nature, exist by another-nature in dependence on the aggregates? It is not reasonable that a barren woman’s son, who does not exist, comes into being in dependence on another nature.
