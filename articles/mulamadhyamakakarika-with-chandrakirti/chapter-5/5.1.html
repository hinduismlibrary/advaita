MMK 5.1

Not the slightest bit of space exists prior to the characteristics of space. If [space] existed prior to its characteristics, it would follow that it would be without characteristics.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nākāśaṃ vidyate kiṃcitpūrvamākāśalakṣaṇāt | alakṣaṇaṃ prasajyeta syātpūrvaṃ yadi lakṣaṇāt || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that the elements exist because scripture does not deny them. The Buddha is said to have taught, “Great king, this person consists of six elements,” and the aggregates and sense bases are accepted on the authority of the teaching. Candrakīrti replies that these would be established only if the elements themselves were established; he begins by examining space.

The six elements are earth, water, fire, air, space, and consciousness. Candrakīrti focuses on space, whose defining characteristic is non-obstruction. If space existed before that characteristic, it would be an entity with no defining mark. But no such unmarked entity can be established independently of the characteristic by which it is known.
