MMK 5.6

avidyamāne bhāve ca kasyābhāvo bhaviṣyati
bhāvābhāvavidharmā ca bhāvābhāvamavaiti kaḥ || 6 ||

When an entity does not exist, whose nonexistence could there be? And who, of a nature different from existence and nonexistence, understands existence and nonexistence?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

avidyamāne bhāve ca kasyābhāvo bhaviṣyati | bhāvābhāvavidharmā ca bhāvābhāvamavaiti kaḥ || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

“If space is not an entity, then let it be a non-entity.” This too is not so, because:

“When an entity does not exist, whose nonexistence could there be?”

When space is not an entity, then, when an entity is not existent, of what could nonexistence be conceived? For he will say:

“If an entity is not established, nonexistence certainly is not established; for people call the other state of an entity ‘nonexistence.’”

Therefore, because entity and non-entity are absent, space as a non-entity is also not possible. Space is defined as the absence of form. If form existed, then the absence of form would be space. But when form itself does not exist by the reasoning already stated, of what would space be the absence?

Someone says: “Entity and non-entity do indeed exist, because the examiner of them exists. You are an examiner of entity and non-entity, for you yourself say, ‘When an entity does not exist, whose nonexistence could there be?’ Therefore, because you, the examiner of entity and non-entity, exist, the two things examined, entity and non-entity, also exist.” Reply: This too is unreasonable, because:

“And who, of a nature different from existence and nonexistence, understands existence and nonexistence?”

If entity and non-entity existed, then the examiner of them would be either an entity or a non-entity. If it is accepted as an entity, the refutation stated is: “Apart from the characterized thing and the characteristic, no entity at all is found.” If it is a non-entity, the refutation stated here is: “When an entity does not exist, whose nonexistence could there be?” And there is no third entity, whose nature differs from entity and non-entity, that could know these two. Thus there is no examiner of entity and non-entity. For this very reason the Blessed One said:

“Whoever understands ‘entity’ and ‘non-entity’ is never attached to any entities. Whoever is never attached to any entities enters the concentration without signs.”

And likewise:

“Whoever thinks of empty dharmas is also a fool who has fallen onto a wrong path. The empty dharmas are proclaimed by syllables, yet they are said by syllables to be beyond syllables.

“Whoever thinks of peaceful, utterly peaceful dharmas has never thought what has never been. All the proliferations of mental thought-construction are subtle; know the dharmas as inconceivable.”

This is the detailed statement.
