MMK 8.12

Investigation of Act and Actor

MMK 8.12

pratītya kārakaḥ karma taṃ pratītya ca kārakam
karma pravartate nānyatpaśyāmaḥ siddhikāraṇam || 12 ||

Depending on action, there is an actor; depending on that actor, action proceeds. We see no other cause of establishment.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pratītya kārakaḥ karma taṃ pratītya ca kārakam | karma pravartate nānyatpaśyāmaḥ siddhikāraṇam || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Have you determined that entities do not exist?” No. But, for you, who assert that entities have own-nature, because entities are opposed to own-nature, the denial of all entities is conceivable. We, however, because all entities have arisen dependently, do not apprehend own-nature at all. What, then, would we deny? As has been said in the Noble Ratnāvalī:

“If someone, having come here and thinking that this mirage is water, then takes that water, thinking ‘it does not exist,’ he is indeed a fool.

“For one who grasps the world, which is like a mirage, as ‘it exists,’ this delusion that it ‘does not exist’ also remains. While there is delusion, one is not liberated.

“What was previously imagined by ignorance—when later, on ascertaining the meaning of reality, one does not find an entity, where then is non-entity?”

Thus, since all entities are without own-nature, how could they be established in the way stated? Therefore, accepting worldly inversion, he says that conventional entities, like water in a mirage, are established only by accepting the mere conditionality of “this depends on that,” and not by anything else:

“Depending on action, there is an actor; depending on that actor, action proceeds. We see no other cause of establishment.”

Here, because agency does not exist for one who is not doing, an actor’s agency occurs in dependence on action, without which he has no agency. And because something not being done by an actor is not an action, only what is being done receives the designation “action.” Thus, action proceeds in dependence on that actor. Apart from the mutual dependence of action and actor, we see no other cause of their establishment.
