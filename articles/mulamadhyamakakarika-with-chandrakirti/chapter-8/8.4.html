MMK 8.4

If there were no cause, effect and cause would not be evident. If they were non-existent, activity and agent and doing would not be evident.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

hetāvasati kāryaṃ ca kāraṇaṃ ca na vidyate | tadabhāve kriyā kartā karaṇaṃ ca na vidyate || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Without a cause there is no established effect, and without an effect there is no established cause. A pot, for example, is an effect of clay and other conditions; the potter is called its maker in relation to the work being done. If cause and effect are absent, what action is being performed, who is performing it, and what instrument is being used? Clay is not an instrument in isolation from the making of a pot. The categories of action, actor and instrument all depend on one another.
