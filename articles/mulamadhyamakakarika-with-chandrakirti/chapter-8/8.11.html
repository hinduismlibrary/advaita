MMK 8.11

One who neither exists nor does not exist as an actor does not do that which exists and does not exist as an act. Here too this is to be known through the proof demonstrated above.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

karoti sadasadbhūto na sannāsacca kārakaḥ | karma tattu vijānīyātpūrvoktaireva hetubhiḥ || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An actor that is neither existent nor nonexistent cannot perform an action that is existent, nonexistent, or both. The earlier reasoning rules out each combination. Does this mean that nothing exists? Candrakīrti says no: things arise dependently, so no intrinsic nature is found to deny. The Ratnāvalī compares the world to a mirage. Someone who mistakes a mirage for water and then says “there is no water” is still confused; one who understands the world as mirage-like sees neither a self-existing thing nor sheer nothingness. When inquiry finds no independent thing, how could it establish a separate non-thing?
