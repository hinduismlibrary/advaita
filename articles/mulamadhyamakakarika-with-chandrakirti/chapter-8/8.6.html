MMK 8.6

If there were no fruit, the path of liberation and higher states would not be appropriate. Also it would follow that all activities are meaningless.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

phale 'sati na mokṣāya na svargāyopapadyate mārgaḥ | sarvakriyāṇāṃ ca nairarthakyaṃ prasajyate || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If there were no results of action, neither a worldly path leading to higher states nor the liberating path would have a result. Activities undertaken for their results—farming, trade, service and the rest—would all be pointless. Candrakīrti rejects this as a nihilistic consequence, opposed to both ordinary experience and the Buddha’s teaching of karma. Refuting an actor or action with intrinsic existence does not require denying conventional actions and their results.
