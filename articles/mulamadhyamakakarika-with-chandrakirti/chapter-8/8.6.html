MMK 8.6

phale 'sati na mokṣāya na svargāyopapadyate mārgaḥ
sarvakriyāṇāṃ ca nairarthakyaṃ prasajyate || 6 ||

When there is no fruit, the path is not appropriate for liberation or heaven; and the purposelessness of all actions would follow.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

phale 'sati na mokṣāya na svargāyopapadyate mārgaḥ | sarvakriyāṇāṃ ca nairarthakyaṃ prasajyate || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And further:

“When there is no fruit, the path is not appropriate for liberation or heaven.”

If there were a desired and undesired fruit, heaven, characterized by the attainments of meditative and formless concentrations, would be the fruit of the worldly path. Then cultivation of the worldly path for that purpose would be beneficial, and abstention from the courses of action leading to bad destinations would be fruitful. And if nirvāṇa characterized by liberation were the fruit, cultivation of the supramundane noble eightfold path for that purpose would be fruitful. But when there is no fruit:

“When there is no fruit, the path is not appropriate for liberation or heaven.”

Further, when there is no fruit in this way:

“The purposelessness of all actions would follow.”

All the actions undertaken for the sake of a result—such as farming, trade, military service, and the like—would not be reasonable if there were no result. Thus, for you, the purposelessness of all actions would follow. But these actions are not purposeless. Therefore, this position, which denies heaven and liberation, becomes a grove of poisonous trees of every fault, a cause of falling into great dangers such as hell, and a contradiction of causes and entities seen and unseen. The thesis that an existent actor performs a nonexistent action is contemptible and should be abandoned.
