MMK 8.13

Likewise, one should understand clinging, because act and actor are dispelled. Remaining things too should be understood by means of actor and act.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ vidyādupādānaṃ vyutsargāditi karmaṇaḥ | kartuśca karmakartṛbhyāṃ śeṣān bhāvān vibhāvayet || 13 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The same method applies to appropriation and the appropriator: taking up the aggregates establishes a person as appropriator, while the appropriator is designated in dependence on what is taken up. Their relationship is no more self-established than actor and action. A wise person should use this analysis for all paired phenomena: producer and produced, goer and going, seer and seeing, mark and marked, cause and effect, whole and parts, quality and possessor, measure and measurable. Seeing their dependent designation loosens the bonds of birth, aging and death.

The commentary cites the Ārya Upāli inquiry. Terrifying hells and their inhabitants may appear to those who are frightened, yet no independently existing being is found that transmigrates there; no self-established agent or cause makes the swords that seem to pierce the body. Splendid, flower-decked heavenly palaces also appear, yet no independent actor is found there either. The world is imagined through conceptual designation: what is grasped as a solid thing is like an illusion or mirage. The citation illustrates conventional appearance without an intrinsically existing actor, action or result.
