MMK 8.5

dharmādharmau na vidyete kriyādīnāmasaṃbhave
dharme cāsatyadharme ca phalaṃ tajjaṃ na vidyate || 5 ||

When action and the rest are impossible, merit and demerit do not exist. When merit and demerit do not exist, the fruit produced by them does not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dharmādharmau na vidyete kriyādīnāmasaṃbhave | dharme cāsatyadharme ca phalaṃ tajjaṃ na vidyate || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And further:

“When action and the rest are impossible, merit and demerit do not exist.”

Here, when Devadatta, as an actor acting freely, performs the act of abstaining from taking life as an instrument and an action of his own free choice, merit arises for him. This should be applied to all ten wholesome courses of action, which are accomplished through wholesome action, and to the beginnings of wholesome dharmas characterized by reverence for the Three Jewels, parents, and others worthy of honor. Likewise, with demerit, in the case of taking life and the rest, the reverse of the wholesome should be applied: when action, actor, and instrument do not exist, the consequence that actions do not exist should be made clear.

And when merit and demerit are not possible in this way, he shows that their fruit too does not exist:

“When merit and demerit do not exist, the fruit produced by them does not exist.”

When merit and demerit, which produce desired and undesired fruits in good and bad destinations, do not exist, their fruits would not arise.
