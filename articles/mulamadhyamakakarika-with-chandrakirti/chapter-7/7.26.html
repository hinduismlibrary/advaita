MMK 7.26

nirudhyate nāniruddhaṃ na niruddhaṃ nirudhyate
tathāpi nirudhyamānaṃ kimajātaṃ nirudhyate || 26 ||

What is not ceased does not cease; what is ceased does not cease. And what is ceasing—what unborn thing is ceased?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nirudhyate nāniruddhaṃ na niruddhaṃ nirudhyate | tathāpi nirudhyamānaṃ kimajātaṃ nirudhyate || 26 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Abiding and arising certainly exist, because the dharma that accompanies them exists. Impermanence of conditioned things accompanies the characteristics of arising and abiding; therefore abiding and arising too exist.” Reply: Abiding and arising would exist if impermanence existed. But it does not. How? Because:

“What is not ceased does not cease; what is ceased does not cease. And what is ceasing—what unborn thing is ceased?”

If there were some impermanence, it would belong to an entity that is ceased, not ceased, or being ceased. Of these, it is not reasonable that what is ceased should cease, because of the contradiction between past and present. Nor is what is not ceased reasonable, because it is without cessation; moreover, there is a mutual contradiction in saying that what is not ceased is being ceased. Nor does what is being ceased cease, because there is no thing being ceased, and because two cessations would follow. Since cessation is impossible in all three times, there is no cessation at all. How, then, could there be abiding and arising that accompany it? Further, because cessation is impossible when arising has already been denied, he says:

“What unborn thing is ceased?”

This is the meaning.
