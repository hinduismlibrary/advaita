MMK 7.3

utpādasthitibhaṅgānāmanyatsaṃskṛtalakṣaṇam
asti cedanavasthaivaṃ nāsti cette na saṃskṛtāḥ || 3 ||

If another characteristic of arising, abiding, and destruction exists, there is an infinite regress; if it does not exist, there are no conditioned things.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

utpādasthitibhaṅgānāmanyatsaṃskṛtalakṣaṇam | asti cedanavasthaivaṃ nāsti cette na saṃskṛtāḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Regarding what was said—“If arising is conditioned,” and so on—if a triad of characteristics of arising and the rest follows, what fault is there? Or if arising is unconditioned, there is no fault in this either. Reply:

“If another characteristic of arising, abiding, and destruction exists, there is an infinite regress; if it does not exist, there are no conditioned things.”

Surely the fault has been stated in both positions; why state it again? It is true that the fault was stated, but not by the Ācārya; rather, it was stated by the commentator. Or the Ācārya has stated it again to make clear, by another statement of the refutation, the very point previously asserted. If another arising and the rest are accepted as characteristics of arising, abiding, and destruction, then another for those, and another for those, and so on: there would be the fault of no end. And when there is no end, there is no arrangement of what would be first, from which a later thing would be subsequent; thus arising and the rest are impossible. Alternatively, previously only the refutation of arising was stated because it is primary; now it is stated generally. If another characteristic does not exist, the meaning is already established: there are no conditioned things.
