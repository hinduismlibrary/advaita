MMK 23.9

How can the pleasant and unpleasant occur in those [things] which are like phantoms and similar to reflections?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

aśubhaṃ vā śubhaṃ vāpi kutas teṣu bhaviṣyati | māyāpuruṣakalpeṣu pratibimbasameṣu ca || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If the objects are like magical people or reflections, where could an intrinsically pleasant or unpleasant nature be located in them? The judgments arise through mistaken conditions rather than from a fixed quality in the objects.

Candrakīrti invokes the related image of aggregates arising from a false sense of “I”: if the seed of the imagined self is false, its supposed products cannot serve as an ultimate foundation.
