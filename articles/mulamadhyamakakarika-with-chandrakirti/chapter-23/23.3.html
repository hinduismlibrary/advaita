MMK 23.3

The existence or non-existence of self is not established in any way. Without that, how can the existence or non-existence of afflictions be established?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmano 'stitvanāstitve na kathaṃ cic ca sidhyataḥ | taṃ vināstitvanāstitve kleśānāṃ sidhyataḥ katham || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The previous chapter has shown that a self cannot be established as intrinsically existent or nonexistent. Afflictions are said to belong to, or depend on, a self; without such a self, how could they be established as intrinsically real?

The same reasoning applies to anything said to depend on the self. The verse does not deny that distress and habits appear conventionally; it denies a self-existing owner of them.
