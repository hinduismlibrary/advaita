MMK 23.4

kasya cid dhi bhavantīme kleśāḥ sa ca na sidhyati
kaścid āho vinā kaṃcit santi kleśā na kasyacit || 4 ||

These afflictions belong to someone; but that one is not established. Or, are there afflictions without anyone? The afflictions are not anyone’s.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kasya cid dhi bhavantīme kleśāḥ sa ca na sidhyati | kaścid āho vinā kaṃcit santi kleśā na kasyacit || 4 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here, passion and the rest depend upon a support for their arising, like a picture on a wall, or like ripening and the like. Therefore they belong to someone; they do not exist without someone as a support. And that support, when imagined, would be either a self or a mind. But that support of theirs does not exist, because it was refuted earlier. And without some support, for whom could the afflictions exist? They do not exist for anyone, because that one does not exist. Or, are afflictions present without anyone, belonging to no one?
