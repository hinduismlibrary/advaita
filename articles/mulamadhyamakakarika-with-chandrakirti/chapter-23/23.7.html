MMK 23.7

rūpaśabdarasasparśā gandhā dharmāś ca ṣaḍvidham
vastu rāgasya doṣasya mohasya ca vikalpyate || 7 ||

Form, sound, taste, touch, smell, and mental objects: these six kinds of objects are conceived as the basis for passion, aversion, and delusion.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rūpaśabdarasasparśā gandhā dharmāś ca ṣaḍvidham | vastu rāgasya doṣasya mohasya ca vikalpyate || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here an opponent says: “The afflictions certainly exist, because their objects exist. For that which does not exist has no object, like a barren woman’s son. But there exists a sixfold object called form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and mental objects. Therefore, because their objects exist, the afflictions certainly exist.”

We reply: it is true that by you this sixfold object is conceived as the basis for passion, aversion, and delusion. There, “object” means support; or it is that on which passion and the rest dwell, because they arise from it. That support or object is sixfold, because the six sense faculties that distinguish [their objects] differ from one another: forms, sounds, smells, tastes, things that can be touched, and mental objects.

There, “form” is so called because one determines, “This is here; that is there,” and because it is formed. “Sound” is that by which things are sounded and made known. “Smells” are so called because, where they are encountered, they are smelled or injured and do not go elsewhere. “Taste” is what is tasted. “Touch” is what is touched. “Mental objects” are so called because they have individual characteristics not shared [with other things] and are the support for mental objects up to the highest dharma, nirvāṇa. This is the sixfold object. Of what? Of passion, aversion, and delusion.

There, “passion” is coloring, attachment, adherence; or the mind is colored by it, therefore it is passion. “Aversion” is spoiling, fault, attack, directed either toward a living being or toward a nonliving thing; or the mind is spoiled by it, therefore it is aversion. “Delusion” is bewilderment, confusion, not understanding the own-character of things; or the mind is deluded by it, therefore it is delusion. Thus the sixfold object consisting of form and the rest is the object of these afflictions. There, by superimposing a pleasing aspect, passion arises from forms and the rest; by superimposing an unpleasing aspect, aversion arises; by superimposing permanence, self, and the like, delusion occurs.
