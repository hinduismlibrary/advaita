MMK 23.10

anapekṣya śubhaṃ nāsty aśubhaṃ prajñapayemahi
yat pratītya śubhaṃ tasmāc chubhaṃ naivopapadyate || 10 ||

Without relying on the good, we cannot designate the bad. Because the good is designated in dependence on that [bad], the good itself is not established.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anapekṣya śubhaṃ nāsty aśubhaṃ prajñapayemahi | yat pratītya śubhaṃ tasmāc chubhaṃ naivopapadyate || 10 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And not only, because the support is false, are the marks, good and bad, false; he also shows by this reasoning that these two are false:

Here, if something called “good” existed, it would necessarily exist in dependence on “bad,” like the far shore and this shore, seed and sprout, or short and long, because good depends upon another related thing. And that bad, which must be depended upon, does not exist without good. “The bad is not [designated] without relying on the good” is the meaning. Whatever bad there is, depending on which we would designate good; the word “which” refers to the immediately preceding bad, and “we would designate” is connected with the subsequent good. And because, in designating the good in this way, there is no other thing called bad to be depended upon, therefore the good itself is not reasonable, just as the long is not possible when the short is not possible, or the far shore when the near shore is not possible.
