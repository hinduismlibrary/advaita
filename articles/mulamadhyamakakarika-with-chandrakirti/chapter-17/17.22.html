MMK 17.22

karma svabhāvataś cet syāc chāśvataṃ syād asaṃśayam
akṛtaṃ ca bhavet karma kriyate na hi śāśvatam || 22 ||

If action existed by its own nature, it would undoubtedly be eternal. And action would be unmade, for the eternal is not made.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

karma svabhāvataś cet syāc chāśvataṃ syād asaṃśayam | akṛtaṃ ca bhavet karma kriyate na hi śāśvatam || 22 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And this must necessarily be understood in just this way: action is without own-nature. For otherwise:

“If action existed by its own nature, it would undoubtedly be eternal. And action would be unmade, for the eternal is not made.”

For if action existed by its own nature, it would certainly be eternal, because there is no change of own-nature. And then action itself would be unmade. Action is that which is most desired by an independent agent through an act. And this is not reasonable. What is the reason? Because the eternal is not made. For that which is called eternal has existence as what is present; and because the making of what is present is not possible, it does not depend on a cause.
