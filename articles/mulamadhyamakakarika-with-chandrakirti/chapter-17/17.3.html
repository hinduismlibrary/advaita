MMK 17.3

tatra yac cetanetyuktaṃ karma tanmānasaṃ smṛtam
cetayitvā ca yat proktaṃ tat kāyikavācikam || 3 ||

There, the karma called volition is regarded as mental; but what is called karma after intending is bodily and verbal.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tatra yac cetanetyuktaṃ karma tanmānasaṃ smṛtam | cetayitvā ca yat proktaṃ tat kāyikavācikam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

That which is born in the mind is mental. Because it reaches its completion through the mind-door alone and is independent of the activity of body and speech, volition alone, associated with mind-consciousness, is called mental karma. The word “there” marks a determination.

But that second kind, called “karma after intending,” is to be understood as bodily and verbal. What is done after thinking in the mind, “I shall act in this way through body and speech,” is called karma after intending. That again is twofold: bodily and verbal, because it arises from body and speech and reaches completion through their door. Thus it is threefold: bodily, verbal, and mental.

This threefold action, when further divided, becomes sevenfold. Thus the Blessed One described the division of that action in many ways. How so?
