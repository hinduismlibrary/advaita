MMK 3.3

na paryāpto 'gnidṛṣṭānto darśanasya prasiddhaye
sadarśanaḥ sa pratyukto gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 3 ||

The fire example is not sufficient to establish seeing; it, together with seeing, is refuted by the being-traversed, the traversed, and the untraversed.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na paryāpto 'gnidṛṣṭānto darśanasya prasiddhaye | sadarśanaḥ sa pratyukto gamyamānagatāgataiḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone might say: “Although seeing does not see itself, it will nevertheless see others, like fire. For fire burns only what is other than itself, not itself; in the same way, seeing will see only others, not itself.” This is wholly unreasonable. Why? Because:

“The fire example is not sufficient to establish seeing.”

The fire example that you have brought forward to establish seeing is not sufficient, not enough, not capable, and not proper; that is its meaning. Why? Because:

“It, together with seeing, is refuted by the being-traversed, the traversed, and the untraversed.”

“Together with seeing” means that it is accompanied by seeing. The fire example that you have put forward to establish seeing is also refuted, together with seeing as the thing to be demonstrated. By what? By the being-traversed, the traversed, and the untraversed. Just as what has been traversed is not traversed, what has not been traversed is not traversed, and what is being traversed is not traversed, so too, with regard to fire, one should say: “What has been burned does not burn; what has not been burned does not burn,” and so forth. And just as what has been traversed, what has not been traversed, and what is being traversed is not traversed, so too:

“What has been seen is not seen; what has not been seen is certainly not seen. Apart from what has been seen and what has not been seen, what is being seen is not seen.”

And just as it was said, “First, the goer does not go,” and so forth, so too one should say, “First, the burner does not burn,” and so forth. Thus, because the same refutation applies to the fire example, together with the analysis of the being-seen, the seen, and the unseen, it is not reasonable that seeing is established like fire. Hence this is established: just as seeing does not see itself, it does not see others either.
