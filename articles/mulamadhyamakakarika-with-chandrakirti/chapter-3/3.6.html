MMK 3.6

tiraskṛtya draṣṭā nāstyatiraskṛtya ca darśanam
draṣṭavyaṃ darśanaṃ caiva draṣṭaryasati te kutaḥ || 6 ||

When seeing is set aside, there is no seer; when seeing is not set aside, there is no seer. If the seer is absent, where do the seen and seeing come from?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tiraskṛtya draṣṭā nāstyatiraskṛtya ca darśanam | draṣṭavyaṃ darśanaṃ caiva draṣṭaryasati te kutaḥ || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Just as a refutation was stated of seeing, beginning with “Seeing does not see its own self,” so too the seer should be understood to be refuted like seeing. For example:

“The seer does not see his own self by seeing; when he does not see himself, how will he see others?”

And so forth should be said. Therefore, just like seeing, the seer also does not exist; this is established.

Here someone says: “The seer indeed exists because the object and instrument of seeing exist. For what does not exist has no object or instrument, like the son of a barren woman. But the seer has seeing as his instrument and the visible object as his object. Therefore, like a cutter, the seer exists because he has an object and an instrument.” In reply: There is no seeing and no visible object; how, then, could there be a seer? The visible object and seeing depend on the seer. When he is analyzed:

“When seeing is set aside, there is no seer; when seeing is not set aside, there is no seer. If the seer is absent, where do the seen and seeing come from?”

When the seer is absent, the seen and seeing cannot exist without a cause. From their existence, how would the seer be established?
