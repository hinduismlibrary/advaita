MMK 18.5

Through the ceasing of action and affliction, there is freedom. Action and affliction [come] from thoughts and they from fixations. Fixations are stopped by emptiness.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

karmakleśakṣayānmokṣaḥ karmakleśā vikalpataḥ | te prapañcātprapañcastu śūnyatāyāṃ nirudhyate || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Candrakīrti traces afflictions to distorted conceptualizing: desire, aversion, and confusion arise when things are imagined as intrinsically attractive or repulsive. Conceptuality expands into mental proliferation, which treats signs and distinctions as fixed.

Emptiness brings that proliferation to rest by exposing the lack of independent nature in what is grasped. Liberation comes through ending the mistaken constructions that sustain action and affliction, not through destroying a real self or a real world.
