MMK 18.3

The one who does not grasp at me and mine likewise does not exist. Whoever sees the one who does not grasp at me and mine does not see. ([c-d are omitted from the translated verse on the grounds of their being a reiteration of a-b])

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nirmamo nirahaṃkāro yaśca so 'pi na vidyate | nirmamaṃ nirahaṃkāraṃ yaḥ paśyati na paśyati || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent might say that even if self and mine are not found, the yogin who has let them go must still exist. Candrakīrti answers that no additional person can be established apart from the aggregates. Nor does someone see reality by treating a fixed “mine-less, ego-less” state as an object.

The texts he cites direct the practitioner to see inner and outer things as empty, while also denying a separate meditator who possesses emptiness. Taking emptiness itself as something inherently real would preserve the grasping that the analysis is meant to undo.
