MMK 18.7

That to which language refers is denied, because an object experienced by the mind is denied. The unborn and unceasing nature of reality is comparable to nirvana. [Tsongkhapa (326) explains that c-d are an answer to the question implied in 5c-d, i.e. "how does emptiness stop fixations?"]

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nivṛttamabhidhātavyaṃ nivṛtte cittagocare | anutpannāniruddhā hi nirvāṇamiva dharmatā || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent asks what the Buddhas teach if neither self nor non-self is ultimately established. Candrakīrti replies that reality is beyond what can be spoken, because it is not an object for the mind to grasp. Its nature is unarisen and unceased, like nirvāṇa; when thought has no fixed object, there is no mark for language to name.

This does not deny conventional teaching. Candrakīrti compares the Buddha’s speech to sounds produced by an instrument set in motion: teaching arises responsively through compassion without requiring an independently existing speaker or object. Reality itself remains beyond conceptual capture.
