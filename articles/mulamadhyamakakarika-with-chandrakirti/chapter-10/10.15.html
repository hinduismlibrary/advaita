MMK 10.15

agnīndhanābhyāṃ vyākhyāta ātmopādānayoḥ kramaḥ
sarvo niravaśeṣeṇa sārdhaṃ ghaṭapaṭādibhiḥ || 15 ||

The sequence concerning self and appropriation has been explained through fire and fuel, entirely, without remainder, together with pot, cloth, and the like.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

agnīndhanābhyāṃ vyākhyāta ātmopādānayoḥ kramaḥ | sarvo niravaśeṣeṇa sārdhaṃ ghaṭapaṭādibhiḥ || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And indicating that just as fire does not exist when examined in five ways, so too the self does not exist, he says:

“Appropriation” is that which is appropriated: the five appropriated aggregates. That which is designated in dependence on them is called the appropriator, the grasping one, the producer, the self; because it is the object of ego-making, “the I-conceit is placed or produced in this.” The entire sequence of establishment of this self and appropriation is to be understood without remainder as explained through fire and fuel.

What, then, is the distinction between “entire” and “without remainder”? By the word “entire,” the five positions just discussed are connected. All these five positions, without exception, are to be applied to self and appropriation just as to fire and fuel. By the sequence of reasoning described earlier in setting this out, the refutation of self and appropriation without remainder is to be understood. He says “entire, without remainder” to show that the equality of refutation in every respect of self and appropriation with fire and fuel is to be understood. There, “that which is the appropriation itself is the self” is unreasonable because the consequence of identity of agent and action follows. Nor is one appropriation other than the aggregates a different appropriator, because it would follow that the self is perceived apart from the aggregates, and because of the consequence beginning “independent of another.” And because identity and otherness are denied, the self is not one possessing the aggregates. Because otherness is absent, aggregates are not in the self, nor is the self in them. Since, in these five modes, the self does not exist, it is established that, just as with agent and action, the establishment of self and appropriation depends on each other.

The sequence concerning self and appropriation is not only concerning these two. Rather:

It is to be understood as explained in every way with all things, without remainder. For pots and the like may be related as effect and cause, as part and whole, as characteristic and characterized, or as quality and possessor of quality. There, clay, stick, wheel, thread, water, potter, effort, and so on are causes of the pot; the pot is the effect. Pieces and the like, or blue and the like, are parts; the pot is the whole. Broad base, belly, projecting lip, long neck, and so on are characteristics; the pot is what is characterized. Blackness and the like are qualities; the pot is the possessor of qualities. Having established them thus, the sequence is to be applied as with fire and fuel. The explanation of these pots and the like, and of self and appropriation, is to be understood from the chapter of the Madhyamakāvatāra.
