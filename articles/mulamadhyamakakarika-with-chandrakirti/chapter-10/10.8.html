MMK 10.8

yadīndhanamapekṣyāgnirapekṣyāgniṃ yadīndhanam
kataratpūrvaniṣpannaṃ yadapekṣyāgnirindhanam || 8 ||

If fire depends on fuel, and fuel depends on fire, which of these is established first, on which does fire or fuel depend?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadīndhanamapekṣyāgnirapekṣyāgniṃ yadīndhanam | kataratpūrvaniṣpannaṃ yadapekṣyāgnirindhanam || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here [the opponent] says: Although an establishment of fire and fuel independent of each other, as with woman and man, does not exist, still their mutual dependence does exist. And thus, because of their mutual dependence, the establishment of the own-form of fire and fuel indeed exists. For mutual dependence between two nonexistent things—a barren woman's son and daughter—has not been seen. The response: Even so:

If, depending on fuel, fire; depending on fire, fuel—of these two, which is established first? What is the fuel on which fire depends, or what is the fire on which fuel depends? Here, if it is supposed that fuel is established first, that is unreasonable, because fuel does not exist without dependence on fire, when it is not being kindled; and it would follow that everything, including grass and so on, is fuel. But if fire is first established and fuel afterward, that too is unreasonable, because fire established before fuel is impossible, and it would follow that it is without a cause. And afterward, through dependence, it would be without purpose. Therefore there is nothing here established first, on which the other might be established.
