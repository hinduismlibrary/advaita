MMK 10.14

indhanaṃ punaragnirna nāgniranyatra cendhanāt
nāgnirindhanavānnāgnāvindhanāni na teṣu saḥ || 14 ||

Moreover, fuel is not fire; fire is not elsewhere than fuel; fire is not possessed of fuel; fuel is not in fire; it is not in them.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

indhanaṃ punaragnirna nāgniranyatra cendhanāt | nāgnirindhanavānnāgnāvindhanāni na teṣu saḥ || 14 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, concluding the meaning that has been set forth, he says:

There, by this: “If that which is fuel is that, fire, there is identity of agent and action,” the identity of fire and fuel is denied.

“Moreover, fuel is not fire; if fire is different from fuel, it would exist even apart from fuel,” and so on: by this, otherness is denied.

“Fire is not elsewhere than fuel.”

Because, precisely by denial of the positions of identity, otherness, and both, the positions of possessor and possessed, basis and supported, are also denied in meaning, he concludes those too:

“Fire is not possessed of fuel; fuel is not in fire; it is not in them.” There, fire is not possessed of fuel, whether explained by separation as “Devadatta possessing a cow,” or by non-separation as “Devadatta possessing intelligence,” “Devadatta possessing form,” and so on: because both positions regarding fire and fuel have been denied, the denial “fire is possessed of fuel” is made. Moreover, a bowl takes the position of a support for curds. But because fire is not other than fuel, “fuel is in fire” is not reasonable. Nor is fire in fuel, because otherness is denied. Thus the denial of support and supported is also established in meaning.
