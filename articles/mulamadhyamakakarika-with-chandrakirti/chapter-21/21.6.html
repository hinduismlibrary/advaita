MMK 21.6

sahānyonyena vā siddhirvinānyonyena vā yayoḥ
na vidyate tayoḥ siddhiḥ kathaṃ nu khalu vidyate || 6 ||

For those whose establishment neither together with one another nor apart from one another exists, how indeed does their establishment exist?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sahānyonyena vā siddhirvinānyonyena vā yayoḥ | na vidyate tayoḥ siddhiḥ kathaṃ nu khalu vidyate || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent might say: “Although birth and death are not established as either one or different, nevertheless arising and passing away certainly exist, because they can be spoken of, like consciousness.” It is replied: If their establishment is desired because they can be spoken of, then the son of a barren woman too should be accepted.

And further:

“For those whose establishment neither together with one another nor apart from one another exists, how indeed does their establishment exist?” || 6 ||

Since there is no other alternative apart from togetherness or apartness by which the establishment of arising and passing away could occur, how indeed does the establishment of those two exist? If it is said that their establishment occurs through being inexpressible, what is this inexpressibility? If it is a mixture, that is not reasonable, because when they are established separately, there is no mixture. Since their own-natures cannot be determined, arising and passing away do not exist, just as the dark or fair complexion of a barren woman’s son does not. And since arising and passing away do not exist in this way, it is established that time, their cause, does not exist either. || 6 ||
