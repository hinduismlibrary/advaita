MMK 15.9

prakṛtau kasya cid asatyām anyathātvaṃ bhaviṣyati
prakṛtau kasya ca satyām anyathātvaṃ bhaviṣyati || 9 ||

When nature is absent in anyone, whose alteration will there be? When nature exists in anyone, whose alteration will there be?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

prakṛtau kasya cid asatyām anyathātvaṃ bhaviṣyati | prakṛtau kasya ca satyām anyathātvaṃ bhaviṣyati || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: If it is said that the nature of these entities is absent because alteration is impossible for what exists by nature and because alteration is observed, then, even so:

“When nature is absent in anyone, whose alteration will there be?”

For whom, then, would there be alteration when nature, own-being, is absent? For something that does not exist by its nature, like a sky-flower, how will there be alteration? Therefore, because alteration is not perceived for one whose nature is absent, and because alteration is seen, own-being indeed exists. The response: If, according to your view, because there is no alteration for what does not exist by nature, and because alteration is seen, nature is said to exist, even so:

“When nature exists in anyone, whose alteration will there be?”

For whom, then, will there be alteration for what exists by nature, by own-being, and in its present state? Therefore alteration of what exists by nature does not exist. Thus, in every way, alteration is impossible. And therefore it is to be understood that entities have no nature.
