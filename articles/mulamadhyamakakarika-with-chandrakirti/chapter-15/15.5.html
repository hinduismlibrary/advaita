MMK 15.5

If things were not established, non-things would not be established. [When] a thing becomes something else, people say that it is a non-thing.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvasya ced aprasiddhir abhāvo naiva sidhyati | bhāvasya hy anyathābhāvam abhāvaṃ bruvate janāḥ || 5 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent shifts to non-things, arguing that even if things are not established, non-existence still is. Candrakīrti explains that people call something a “non-thing” when an existing thing changes into another state: a pot, for example, ceases to be in its former condition. But if the pot and other things were never established as things in the first place, what could change into non-existence? Non-things cannot be established without things.
