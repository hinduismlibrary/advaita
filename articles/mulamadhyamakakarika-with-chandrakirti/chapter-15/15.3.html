MMK 15.3

If an essence does not exist, how can the thingness of the other exist? [For] the essence of the thingness of the other is said to be the thingness of the other.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kutaḥ svabhāvasyābhāve parabhāvo bhaviṣyati | svabhāvaḥ parabhāvasya parabhāvo hi kathyate || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent proposes that even if things lack own-nature, they still possess “other-nature,” since one thing may be different from another. Candrakīrti answers that other-nature is called other only in relation to an own-nature. Fire’s heat might be called other relative to water’s fluid nature. But if no own-nature is established in the first place, there is nothing from which other-nature could be distinguished. Other-nature therefore fails as well.
