MMK 24.12

ataś ca pratyudāvṛttaṃ cittaṃ deśayituṃ muneḥ
dharmaṃ matvāsya dharmasya mandair duravagāhatām || 12 ||

Therefore the Sage’s mind turned back from teaching, thinking this Dharma difficult for people of dull understanding to fathom.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ataś ca pratyudāvṛttaṃ cittaṃ deśayituṃ muneḥ | dharmaṃ matvāsya dharmasya mandair duravagāhatām || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Since emptiness, when wrongly grasped in this way, destroys the one who grasps it, and since those of dull understanding cannot grasp it rightly:

Because this Dharma, whose characteristic is emptiness, destroys a being of dull intellect and little wisdom through grasping it in a mistaken way, the Sage’s mind, the Blessed Buddha, endowed with the special knowledge of great skillful means, turned back from teaching the true Dharma. He considered that this Dharma is difficult for people of dull understanding to fathom; after awakening to unsurpassed complete awakening and looking upon the whole realm of beings, he considered the profound depth of the Dharma. As it is said in a sūtra:

“Then, shortly after the Blessed One had become completely awakened, this thought occurred to him: ‘A Dharma has been attained by me that is profound, with profound appearance, beyond thought, outside the range of thought, subtle, and to be known by the wise and discerning. If I were to explain it to others, and others did not understand me, that would be an obstacle for me, a weariness for me, a lack of joy in my mind. Why should I not live alone in the forest, in seclusion, having attained the pleasant dwelling in the present life?’” And so forth.
