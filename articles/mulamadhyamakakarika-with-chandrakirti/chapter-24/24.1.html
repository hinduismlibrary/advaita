MMK 24.1

"If all were empty, nothing could come about or perish. It would follow for you that the four ennobling truths could not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi śūnyam idaṃ sarvam udayo nāsti na vyayaḥ | caturṇām āryasatyānām abhāvas te prasajyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that if everything is empty, nothing can arise or cease, so the Four Noble Truths cannot exist. Candrakīrti presents this as the consequence of confusing emptiness with sheer nonexistence.

The objection assumes that only self-existing things can arise and cease. The chapter answers that the truths concern dependently arisen suffering and its cessation; emptiness does not erase those processes.
