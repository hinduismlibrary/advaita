MMK 24.1

yadi śūnyam idaṃ sarvam udayo nāsti na vyayaḥ
caturṇām āryasatyānām abhāvas te prasajyate || 1 ||

If all this is empty, there is no arising and no passing away. It follows for you that the four noble truths do not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadi śūnyam idaṃ sarvam udayo nāsti na vyayaḥ | caturṇām āryasatyānām abhāvas te prasajyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here an opponent says:

If, because it is not reasonable by reasoning, all this multitude of external and internal existent things has been shown to be empty, then many great faults follow for you. How so? If all this were empty, then what is empty would not exist; and what does not exist, because it is absent, would not arise and would not cease, like a barren woman’s son. Thus there would be no arising or passing away of any thing. And because these do not exist, for you, the proponent of emptiness, it would follow that the four noble truths do not exist. How so?

Here, the five aggregates subject to appropriation, which are produced by a previous cause and arise in dependence, are called suffering because they have the nature of affliction: they are suffering as suffering, suffering through change, suffering as conditioned formations, and they operate adversely. And only the noble ones, when mistaken views have been abandoned, recognize this as suffering; non-noble ones do not, because they follow mistaken views. The nature of things is established according to how they are seen.

For example, for those whose faculties are mistaken and who, being afflicted by fever and the like, perceive as bitter what is naturally sweet, such as molasses and sugar, bitterness alone is true in relation to their cognition; sweetness is not perceived by them in that form. In the same way here, although the five aggregates subject to appropriation have the nature of suffering, suffering is established only for those who see them as having the nature of suffering, not for those who, following mistaken views, perceive them otherwise. Therefore the nature of suffering is true only for the noble ones, and suffering is called a noble truth.

An opponent says: “Non-noble people distinguish painful feeling as suffering. How then is suffering true only for the noble ones?” That is true. But painful feeling alone is not the noble truth of suffering; what then? All five aggregates subject to appropriation. For this reason, because it is true only for the noble ones, it is established as a noble truth. As it is said:

“Just as an eyelash lying on the palm of the hand is not perceived by people, but that same thing, when it enters the eye, produces discomfort and pain; the childish person, like the palm of the hand, does not know the eyelash that is the suffering of conditioned formations, but the learned person, like the eye, is deeply disturbed by that very thing.”

Therefore, because its nature as suffering is true only for the noble ones, it is established as the noble truth of suffering.

And when is that suffering reasonable as a noble truth? When the arising and passing away of formations are possible. But when, because of emptiness, nothing arises and nothing ceases, then there is no suffering.

And when there is no suffering, whence is there the truth of origin? The cause from which suffering comes forth and arises, characterized by craving, action, and afflictions, is called origin. But when the noble truth of suffering, which is the result, does not exist, then origin too does not exist, because it is not reasonable that a cause has no result.

The disappearance of suffering, its not arising again, is called cessation. But when suffering itself does not exist, whose cessation could there be? Therefore the cessation of suffering is not possible. For when there is no suffering, the truth of cessation too does not exist. And when there is no cessation of suffering, how could there be a practice that follows the noble eightfold path and leads to the cessation of suffering? Therefore the truth of the path too does not exist. In this way, for one who says that things are empty, it follows that the four noble truths do not exist.
