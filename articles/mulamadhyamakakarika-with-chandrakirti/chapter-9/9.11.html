MMK 9.11

If that to which seeing and hearing etc. and feeling etc. belong is not evident, they too could not be evident.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha | na vidyate cedyasya sa na vidyanta imānyapi || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent argues that even if the person is refuted, seeing and the other experiences remain; since pots and other inanimate things do not see, a person who sees must exist. Candrakīrti replies that the experiences themselves are designated as what a person takes up. If the appropriator is not independently established, neither are the appropriated senses and experiences.

This does not mean that the person is simply nonexistent. Candrakīrti denies a self established by its own nature; he does not posit sheer nonexistence as another absolute. Both clinging to existence and clinging to nonexistence must be relinquished. He quotes Āryadeva: what you call “self” is not thereby a fixed self in itself; conceptions arise around impermanent things.
