MMK 9.11

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha
na vidyate cedyasya sa na vidyanta imānyapi || 11 ||

If the one for whom seeing and the rest, and feeling and the rest, exist is not found, these too are not found.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

darśanaśravaṇādīni vedanādīni cāpyatha | na vidyate cedyasya sa na vidyanta imānyapi || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Someone says: “Although the self is denied in this way, seeing and the rest exist, because they have not been denied. And seeing and the rest do not belong to things such as pots, which have no nature of self; therefore their possessor, the self, certainly exists.” Reply: It would be so if seeing and the rest existed. But they do not. Since it has been explained that the one who appropriates seeing and the rest does not exist, where is the existence of seeing and the rest, which are the appropriations, when that self, the appropriator, does not exist? He says:

“If the one for whom seeing and the rest, and feeling and the rest, exist is not found, these too are not found.”

When it has been said that the one for whom seeing and the rest are conceived does not exist, it is clearly shown that seeing and the rest too do not exist. Therefore, because seeing and the rest are absent, self does not exist.

Someone says: “Have you determined that self certainly does not exist? Who said this?” Surely, immediately before, it was said that because seeing and the rest are absent, self too does not exist. We have said this, but you have not properly determined its meaning. The self imagined as an entity does not exist by own-nature. My statement, opposed to the false inversion, was spoken only to turn away the fixation on own-nature; its nonexistence was not conceived. For these two are to be abandoned: fixation on entities and fixation on non-entities. As Āryadeva said:

“The self that is yours is not my self; by that, I do not assert a determinate self. Surely a conception arises concerning impermanent entities.”
