MMK 9.12

prāk ca yo darśanādibhyaḥ sāṃprataṃ cordhvameva ca
na vidyate 'sti nāstīti nivṛttāstatra kalpanāḥ || 12 ||

That which is before seeing and the rest, now, and afterwards, is not found. There, the conceptions ‘it exists’ and ‘it does not exist’ have ceased.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

prāk ca yo darśanādibhyaḥ sāṃprataṃ cordhvameva ca | na vidyate 'sti nāstīti nivṛttāstatra kalpanāḥ || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Showing this very point, he says:

“That which is before seeing and the rest, now, and afterwards, is not found. There, the conceptions ‘it exists’ and ‘it does not exist’ have ceased.”

First, self does not exist before seeing and the rest, because its existence is absent there. Nor does it exist together with seeing and the rest, because co-existence is not seen between things separately established, like the horns of a hare. Since self and appropriations are not separately established, independent of one another, it does not exist now either. Nor does it exist afterwards. If seeing and the rest were first, self could exist later. But this is not so, because an action without an actor is not established. Since such a self, examined before, after, and simultaneously with seeing and the rest, does not exist, what wise person would conceive of its existence or nonexistence, whose own-nature is not now apprehended? Thus it is established that the appropriator and appropriations, just like action and actor, are established through mutual dependence and not inherently.

For this very reason, the Blessed One said in the Noble Samādhirāja:

“At that time the stainless Jina, the Ten-Powered One, spoke this supreme concentration: all things are like dreams. No one is born, and no one dies.

“No being, no living person, no human is found. These dharmas are like foam and the core of a plantain; they are like illusion, like lightning in the sky, like the moon in water, like a mirage.

“No human being dies in this world and goes or passes to another world. And karma is never destroyed: when done, it gives its fruit, black or bright, as one moves through saṃsāra.

“There is no eternalism and no annihilation; no accumulation of karma, nor any abiding. The one who has acted does not touch [the fruit] again; another, having acted, does not experience it again.

“There is no transmigration, nor returning again; neither does everything exist, nor again does it not exist. There is no purification of the stations of views or of the path here; nor is there a course of beings leading to perfect peace.

“Non-arising, peace, the signless state, the sphere of the Sugatas, the qualities of the Jinas; the powers and dhāraṇīs, the strength of the Ten-Powered Ones—this is the highest, supreme, excellent awakening of the Buddhas.”

Thus, in the Prasannapadā, the Madhyamaka commentary composed by the venerable Ācārya Candrakīrti, the ninth chapter, called “Examination of the Prior,” is complete.
