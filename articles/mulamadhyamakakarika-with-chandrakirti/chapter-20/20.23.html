MMK 20.23

na ca pratyayahetūnāmiyamātmānamātmanā
yā sāmagrī janayate sā kathaṃ janayetphalam || 23 ||

This collection of conditions and causes does not produce itself by itself; how could that collection produce a fruit?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

na ca pratyayahetūnāmiyamātmānamātmanā | yā sāmagrī janayate sā kathaṃ janayetphalam || 23 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent says: “It is not the cause alone that is the instigator of the act of producing the fruit; rather, a fruit is produced by a collection of causes and conditions.” It is replied: This is not reasonable, because it has the stated faults. Moreover, if this collection of causes and conditions is imagined to be the producer of the fruit, does it produce itself by itself, or not? If it is imagined to produce itself, this is not reasonable. For an instigating capacity is not seen in what has not obtained its own being. Therefore the collection must necessarily have obtained its own being. But it is not reasonable for that which has obtained its own being to be an instigator again of the production of itself. Therefore the collection does not produce itself. Showing that a collection which does not produce itself cannot produce a fruit, he says:

“This collection of conditions and causes does not produce itself by itself; how could that collection produce a fruit?” || 23 ||

This collection of conditions and causes does not produce itself by itself, because operation in regard to itself is contradictory, and because, if it already exists, producing it again is purposeless. And that which does not produce even itself in this way, how will it produce a fruit? For it is not reasonable that a barren woman’s daughter, being unable to produce herself, would produce a son. In this way, it is not reasonable that a collection, which does not produce itself, produces a fruit. || 23 ||
