MMK 20.18

kathamutpatsyate śūnyaṃ kathaṃ śūnyaṃ nirotsyate
śūnyamapyaniruddhaṃ tadanutpannaṃ prasajyate || 18 ||

How will an empty fruit arise? How will an empty fruit cease? It would follow that even an empty fruit is unceased and unborn.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

kathamutpatsyate śūnyaṃ kathaṃ śūnyaṃ nirotsyate | śūnyamapyaniruddhaṃ tadanutpannaṃ prasajyate || 18 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now he shows that an empty fruit too is not possible, because the consequence would be that it has neither arising nor passing away:

“How will an empty fruit arise? How will an empty fruit cease? It would follow that even an empty fruit is unceased and unborn.” || 18 ||

Here, what is empty is said to be that which does not exist by its own-nature. And whatever thing does not exist by its own-nature, how will it arise, or how will it cease? For arising and passing away are not seen in what is absent by its own-nature, such as space and the like. Therefore it follows that even that fruit, if accepted as empty, would be unceased and unborn. || 18 ||
