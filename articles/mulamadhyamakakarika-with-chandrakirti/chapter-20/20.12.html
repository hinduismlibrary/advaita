MMK 20.12

nātītasya hyatītena phalasya saha hetunā
nājātena na jātena saṃgatirjātu vidyate || 12 ||

For a past fruit with a past cause there is never connection; nor with an unborn cause, nor with a born cause.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nātītasya hyatītena phalasya saha hetunā | nājātena na jātena saṃgatirjātu vidyate || 12 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And further: if the connection between cause and fruit that you accept, characterized by their approaching one another, were to exist, then their relation as producer and produced would occur. For no relation of producer and produced is seen between light and darkness, which are not joined to one another, or between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa. Therefore an opponent who wishes the relation of cause and fruit to be one of producer and produced must necessarily accept connection. But when examined in all three times, that connection is not possible. Therefore a cause does not produce a fruit. Showing how there is no connection, he says:

“For a past fruit with a past cause there is never connection; nor with an unborn cause, nor with a born cause.” || 12 ||

A past fruit is never at any time connected with a past cause, because both are absent as past. Nor is a past fruit ever connected with an unborn cause, because both are absent as destroyed and unborn, and because they are in different times. Nor can a past fruit be connected with a born, present cause, because they are in different times, and because the fruit, being destroyed, is absent, like a barren woman’s son to Devadatta. Such is the intention. || 12 ||
