MMK 20.9

If [when] a cause stops, it is forever transferred to the fruit, then it would follow that the cause which was born before would be born again.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

niruddhe cetphalaṃ hetau hetoḥ saṃkramaṇaṃ bhavet | pūrvajātasya hetośca punarjanma prasajyate || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If the fruit arises after its cause has ceased and is taken to be the cause’s own nature, the cause has merely migrated into another form. No new fruit has been produced.

This makes the cause permanent and requires a cause that was already born to be born again. Candrakīrti rejects that account as a disguised transfer of an enduring substance.
