MMK 20.9

niruddhe cetphalaṃ hetau hetoḥ saṃkramaṇaṃ bhavet
pūrvajātasya hetośca punarjanma prasajyate || 9 ||

If, when the cause has ceased, the fruit arises, then a transference of the cause would occur; and rebirth of the cause that was born before would follow.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

niruddhe cetphalaṃ hetau hetoḥ saṃkramaṇaṃ bhavet | pūrvajātasya hetośca punarjanma prasajyate || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Others, however, describe it this way: “The cause itself produces the fruit, not the collection. And the consequence of the stated faults does not follow, because the cause is not one thing and the fruit another. For then the examination would be whether the cause ceases after giving a causal factor for the fruit, or ceases without giving it. Rather, the cause itself has ceased, established in the form of the fruit.” It is replied: Even so:

“If, when the cause has ceased, the fruit arises, then a transference of the cause would occur; and rebirth of the cause that was born before would follow.” || 9 ||

If, according to your view, the fruit arises when the cause has ceased, and that fruit is imagined to have the very nature of the cause, then a transference of the cause would occur. It would be merely the passage of the cause, like an actor’s passage into another costume after abandoning one costume; there would be no production of a new fruit. Therefore the cause itself would be permanent. But permanent things do not exist anywhere. As it is said in the Śataka:

“Existence that is not dependent does not exist at any time for anyone anywhere; therefore no permanent one exists at any time anywhere.”

Moreover, in this conception, rebirth would befall the cause that had been born before. And it is not fitting that what has been born be born again, because it would be purposeless and the consequence would be an infinite regress.

If you think: “That which exists as one nature is not born in that same nature; and that which does not exist as a nature is born in that nature,” this too is not reasonable. The fruit is called the cause’s own form, whose own-nature as cause has not been abandoned, through a mere difference of name and a difference of state; but it is not possible to establish that the substance is not different. And in the state of the fruit, when the cause’s own-nature has been abandoned, it is called “fruit.” To say that the cause remains in the form of the fruit is just some such thing. || 9 ||
