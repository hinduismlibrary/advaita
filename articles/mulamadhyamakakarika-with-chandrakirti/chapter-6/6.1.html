MMK 6.1

rāgādyadi bhavetpūrvaṃ rakto rāgatiraskṛtaḥ
taṃ pratītya bhavedrāgo rakte rāgo bhavetsati || 1 ||

If passion and the rest existed first, there would be a passionate person excluded from passion. Depending on that, passion would arise; when the passionate person exists, passion would exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rāgādyadi bhavetpūrvaṃ rakto rāgatiraskṛtaḥ | taṃ pratītya bhavedrāgo rakte rāgo bhavetsati || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: “The aggregates, sense-fields, and elements certainly exist. Why? Because affliction, which depends on them, is observed. Here, what does not exist has no affliction dependent on it, just as a barren woman’s son does not. And afflictions such as passion exist, which are the basis of affliction. As the Blessed One said: ‘Monks, an ignorant, ordinary person, fallen into designation, having seen forms with the eye, fixes upon those that are bases for gladness. Having fixed upon them, he produces passion; being passionate, he fabricates action born of passion, born of hatred, and born of delusion, with body, speech, and mind,’ and so on.” Reply: The aggregates, sense-fields, and elements would exist if afflictions such as passion existed. Here, when passion is posited by ordinary people, is it posited when a passionate person exists or when one does not exist? Neither way is reasonable, he says:

“If passion and the rest existed first, there would be a passionate person excluded from passion. Depending on that, passion would arise; when the passionate person exists, passion would exist.”

Here, passion, attachment, adherence, clinging, and fixation are synonyms. The passionate person is the support of passion. If the passionate person existed before passion, excluded from passion, without passion, then, depending on that passionate person who is excluded from passion, passion would arise. In that way, it would be reasonable that attachment exists when the passionate person exists. But it is not possible that the passionate person should be without passion, because that would entail passion even for arhats.
