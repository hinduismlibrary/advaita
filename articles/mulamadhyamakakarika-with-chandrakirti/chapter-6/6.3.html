MMK 6.3

It is not reasonable for desire and the desirous one to arise as co-existent. In this way desire and the desirous one would not be mutually contingent.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

sahaiva punarudbhūtirna yuktā rāgaraktayoḥ | bhavetāṃ rāgaraktau hi nirapekṣau parasparam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent proposes that desire and the desirous person arise together: a mental state of desire occurs at the same time as the mind that is affected by it. Candrakīrti replies that simultaneous arising does not establish their mutual dependence. If they arise together without depending on one another, they would be independent, like the left and right horns of a cow. But the opponent wants desire and the desirous person to be mutually related, not merely two unrelated things appearing at once.

Thus co-arising is no better than arising in sequence. If the desirous person is the basis of desire, the person must already be present; if desire makes the person desirous, desire must already be present. Their simultaneous appearance leaves the same problem unresolved.
