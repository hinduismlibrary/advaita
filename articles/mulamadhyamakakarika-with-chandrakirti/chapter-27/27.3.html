MMK 27.3

It is incorrect to say: "I occurred at a time in the past." Whatever occurred before, that is not this.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

abhūm atītam adhvānam ity etan nopapadyate | yo hi janmasu pūrveṣu sa eva na bhavaty ayam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The claim “I existed in the past” would be reasonable only if the person who existed then were exactly the person who exists now. That cannot be maintained: it would make the self permanent, and a permanent self cannot undergo transmigration. A being who was born in a hell realm and is now born among humans could not, on that account, rightly say, “I am that very hell-being.” The changing courses of rebirth make the identity claim untenable.

When a sūtra says, “At that time, at that moment, I was King Māndhātṛ,” it denies that the earlier person was wholly other; it does not establish that the earlier and present persons are numerically one. If they were exactly the same, the fault of permanence would follow.
