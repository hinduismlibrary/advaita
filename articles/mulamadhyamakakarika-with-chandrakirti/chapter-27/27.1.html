MMK 27.1

Those views such as "I occurred or did not occur in the past," "the world is permanent," are dependent on the extreme of Before.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

abhūm atītam adhvānaṃ nābhūvam iti dṛṣṭayaḥ | yās tāḥ śāśvatalokādyāḥ pūrvāntaṃ samupāśritāḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The sūtra says that one who truly sees dependent arising does not turn back toward either the past or the future. Candrakīrti explains that, in relation to one’s present existence, earlier lives are called the “past limit.” Not turning back means not taking up a view that fixes what was otherwise: seeing dependent arising as it is, one does not cling to a different account of reality.

Eight views take the past as their basis. The words “I existed in the past” and “I did not exist in the past” stand for four positions: “I existed,” “I did not exist,” “I both existed and did not,” and “I neither existed nor did not.” The other four are views about the world, such as “the world is eternal,” “the world is not eternal,” “it is both,” or “neither.” Those too rely on the past. The groups are close, but the first concerns one’s own past directly, while the second speaks of the past in general.
