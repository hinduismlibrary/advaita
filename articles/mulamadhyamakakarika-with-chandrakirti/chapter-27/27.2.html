MMK 27.2

Those views such as "I will occur or not occur at another time in the future," "the world has an end," are dependent on the extreme of Later.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

dṛṣṭayo na bhaviṣyāmi kim anyo 'nāgate 'dhvani | bhaviṣyāmīti cāntādyā aparāntaṃ samāśritāḥ || 2 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Future lives, considered in relation to the present, are called the “future limit.” One who sees dependent arising does not take up views about this limit either. The two views named in the verse stand for four positions about oneself: “Will I exist in the future?”, “Will I not exist?”, “Will I both exist and not exist?”, and “Will I neither exist nor not exist?” The other four views concern the world’s future limit, such as whether the world will be eternal, not eternal, both, or neither. The latter concern the future generally, while the former focus on one’s own future.
