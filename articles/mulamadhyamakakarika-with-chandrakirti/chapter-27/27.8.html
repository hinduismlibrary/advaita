MMK 27.8

evaṃ nānya upādānān na copādānam eva saḥ
ātmā nāsty anupādāno nāpi nāsty eṣa niścayaḥ || 8 ||

Thus the self is neither different from appropriation nor appropriation itself; the self is not without appropriation, and the certainty that it does not exist is not reasonable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ nānya upādānān na copādānam eva saḥ | ātmā nāsty anupādāno nāpi nāsty eṣa niścayaḥ || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now, concluding the meaning explained in this way, he says:

“Thus the self is neither different from appropriation nor appropriation itself; the self is not without appropriation.”

Someone may say: “If the self is not of the nature of appropriation, because identity of appropriation and appropriator would follow, and because arising and passing away would follow; if it is not different either, because it would follow that it is apprehended separately, without depending on appropriation; and if it is not without appropriation either, because it would follow that it is apprehended independent of appropriation—then let the conclusion be that the self does not exist.”

It is said in reply:

“Nor is the certainty that it does not exist [reasonable].”

For how could that which is designated in dependence on the aggregates be nonexistent? A nonexistent son of a barren woman is not designated in dependence on aggregates. When appropriation exists, how is it reasonable that the appropriator does not exist? Therefore, its nonexistence too is not reasonable. Thus this certainty, “the self does not exist,” also does not obtain.

The establishment of this self is to be understood in detail from the Madhyamakāvatāra. Here too its establishment has already been made, in the appropriate places; no effort is made here again to establish it.
