MMK 14.6

yady anyad anyad anyasmād anyasmād apy ṛte bhavet
tad anyad anyad anyasmād ṛte nāsti ca nāsty ataḥ || 6 ||

If one thing were other than another thing apart from another, then that other thing would exist as other apart from another; but it does not exist, therefore it is not other.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yady anyad anyad anyasmād anyasmād apy ṛte bhavet | tad anyad anyad anyasmād ṛte nāsti ca nāsty ataḥ || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: If a pot were other than a cloth, and another pot were other than it by depending on that cloth which is separate, then what is the fault? The response:

One word “other” here indicates; another refers to a different meaning; and another is common usage. Thus three uses of the word “other” are taken. If this entity called a pot were other than another entity called a cloth, then that entity called a pot would be other even apart from another entity called a cloth. And then the otherness of each individual pot would exist without dependence on a cloth. For what is other than something is established even without it; for example, that very pot does not depend on another pot for the establishment of its own form. Likewise, if the otherness of the pot existed apart from another, a cloth, then the pot’s otherness would exist independent of a cloth. But the otherness of each individual pot without dependence on a cloth is not seen. Therefore, by saying that one thing becomes other, it is clearly accepted that whatever is other in dependence on something is not other than that.

Here it is said: If otherness of anything from anything does not exist in this way, then this too cannot be said: “Because one thing becomes other depending on another, therefore it is not other than that.” The response: Precisely because otherness of entities is established in dependence on one another, it is spoken of as “other” while standing in worldly convention. But we say that, when examined as a real thing, otherness does not occur.

Then, even if, though otherness does not exist, a pot is designated as other than a cloth by worldly convention, why is otherness not also designated in this way for seed and sprout? The response: The world does not accept otherness between seed and sprout as it does between pot and cloth, because it would follow that they are not producer and produced, as in the case of pot and cloth, and that they do not occur simultaneously. Moreover, because a person in the world, having seen the tree, points to the seed alone as its cause and says, “This tree was planted by me,” it is established even in the world that there is no otherness of things that are cause and effect.
