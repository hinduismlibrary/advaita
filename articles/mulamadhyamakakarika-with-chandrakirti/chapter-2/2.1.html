MMK 2.1

gataṃ na gamyate tāvad agataṃ naiva gamyate
gatāgatavinirmuktaṃ gamyamānaṃ na gamyate || 1 ||

First, what has been traversed is not being traversed; what has not been traversed is certainly not being traversed; and what is being traversed, apart from what has and has not been traversed, is not being traversed.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gataṃ na gamyate tāvad agataṃ naiva gamyate | gatāgatavinirmuktaṃ gamyamānaṃ na gamyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here someone says: Although, through the refutation of arising, the establishment of the characteristics of dependent arising, such as non-cessation, has been accomplished, nevertheless, in order to establish dependent arising as non-coming and non-going, let some further reasoning be stated for the sake of refuting the acts of going and coming that are accepted in the world. In reply: If there were such a thing as going, it would necessarily be conceived in either a path that has been traversed, one that has not been traversed, or one that is being traversed; in no way is this reasonable. Thus he says:

Here, a stretch of path in which the act of going has ceased is called “traversed.” A stretch that is being entered and is present in the act of going is called “being traversed.” Since what has been traversed, in which the act of going has ceased, is incoherent when expressed by the word “is being traversed,” which conveys conjunction with the present act of going, it is therefore not reasonable to say, “What has been traversed is being traversed.” By the word “first,” he also shows the order of the refutation.

What has not been traversed is also not being traversed. For what has not been traversed, into which the act of going has not yet arisen, is called future, whereas “is being traversed” is present. Therefore, because the future and the present are utterly distinct, it is also not reasonable to say that what has not been traversed is being traversed: if it has not been traversed, how is it being traversed? If it is being traversed, then it is not what has not been traversed.

There is also no going in what is being traversed, because: “What is being traversed, apart from what has been traversed and what has not been traversed, is not being traversed.” For here, the place that the goer has crossed is what has been traversed by him, and what he has not crossed is what has not yet been traversed by him. Apart from what has been traversed and what has not been traversed, we do not see a third, separate stretch of path called “being traversed.” Since, in this way, what is being traversed is not traversed and is not understood as “is being traversed,” therefore what is being traversed does not exist. Hence it is not entered by the act of going; and because it is not being traversed, there is no going even in what is being traversed.

Now, someone might say: “The place stepped on by the feet of a goer who is going would be what is being traversed.” This is not so, because the feet too are aggregations of atoms. The region behind the atom situated at the tip of the toe is included in what has been traversed by it; the region in front of the last atom situated at the heel is included in what has not been traversed by it. And there is no foot apart from the atoms. Therefore, there is no stretch being traversed apart from what has been traversed and what has not been traversed. And just as the analysis has been made with regard to the foot, the same analysis must be made with regard to the atoms in relation to their fore and rear parts. If what is being traversed is half-traversed, the answer has been stated through the analysis of what is coming into being. Therefore, it is established that what is being traversed is not traversed.
