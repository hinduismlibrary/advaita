MMK 2.1

Then there is no going in what has gone; there is no going also in what has not [yet] gone. Motion is unknowable apart from what has gone and not [yet] gone.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gataṃ na gamyate tāvad agataṃ naiva gamyate | gatāgatavinirmuktaṃ gamyamānaṃ na gamyate || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Although arising has been refuted, someone might still accept the world's ordinary coming and going as real. Candrakīrti now offers a further argument to show dependent arising to be without coming or going. If going existed, it would have to be located in what has already been gone over, what has not yet been gone over, or what is being gone over; none of these possibilities works.

A stretch on which the action of going has ceased is called “gone.” Since the action there has ended, it cannot at the same time be what is being gone over now. What has not been gone over is what has not yet entered into the action of going; it is future. The future and present are entirely distinct, so what is not yet gone cannot be what is being gone over. If it is not yet gone, how could it be gone over? If it is being gone over, it is no longer not yet gone.

Nor is there going in what is being gone over. The place a goer has already crossed is past; the place not yet crossed is future. Apart from these, we do not find a third stretch of road called “being gone over.” Since no such third stretch can be established, it cannot be said to be undergoing the action of going. Thus no going is found in what is being gone over.

One might say that the ground beneath a walking person’s feet is what is being gone over. Candrakīrti rejects this: the feet too are aggregates of atoms. The ground at an atom by the toes is already included among what has been crossed; the ground at the last atom by the heel is included among what has not yet been crossed. There is no foot apart from its atoms, nor a stretch of ground apart from those already crossed and not yet crossed. The same analysis applies to the atoms, with their before-and-after directions. If someone proposes a “half-crossed” stretch, the analysis of something coming into being applies. Therefore, no independent present stretch is being gone over.
