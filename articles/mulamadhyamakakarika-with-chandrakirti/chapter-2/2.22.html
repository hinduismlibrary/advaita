MMK 2.22

That very going by which a goer is made evident does not [enable a goer to] go. Because there is no [goer] before going, who would be going where?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gatyā yayocyate gantā gatiṃ tāṃ sa na gacchati | yasmān na gatipūrvo 'sti kaścit kiṃciddhi gacchati || 22 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The action by which someone is spoken of as a goer is not itself an action that this goer performs. In ordinary speech, a person goes to a village or a city—something distinct from the movement itself. That does not establish a goer who performs the very movement by which the person is called a goer.

There is no goer established before the movement. If such a goer were already established independently, that person could perform the movement in question. But no agent apart from movement is found who could perform it.

One might say that the person does not perform the movement by which he is called a goer, but performs a different movement. That too would require two goings: the movement that makes him a goer and the separate movement he then performs. A single goer cannot have two such movements. So the ordinary comparison with a speaker who speaks or an agent who acts does not answer the argument.
