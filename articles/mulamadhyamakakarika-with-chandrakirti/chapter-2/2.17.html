MMK 2.17

There is no reversal of motion*, nor of what has gone [and] what has not [yet] gone. [Reversal of] going, engagement [to stay] and reversal [of staying] are similar to going.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gamanaṃ saṃpravṛttiśca nivṛttiśca gateḥ samā || 17 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The movement that was invoked to establish staying is open to the same analysis as movement itself. Earlier, staying was offered as a reason to establish going, and that argument failed because a goer cannot be established as staying. Now going is offered to establish staying; the reasoning must be reversed, and it fails in the same way. A person who stays does not go, just as a goer does not stay.

The beginning and ending of staying must also be examined in parallel with the beginning and ending of going. Staying does not begin in what is already stationary, not yet stationary, or presently becoming stationary; the arguments against the beginning of going apply with the terms reversed. Its cessation is likewise rejected by the earlier analysis of the cessation of going. Candrakīrti therefore concludes that staying is not established. If staying is not established, it cannot serve as the real opposite by which going is proved.
