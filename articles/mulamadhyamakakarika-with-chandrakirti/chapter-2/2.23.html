MMK 2.23

gatyā yayocyate gantā tato 'nyāṃ sa na gacchati
gatī dve nopapadyete yasmād eke pragacchati || 23 ||

The goer does not go by a movement other than the movement by which he is called a goer, because two movements are not possible in one goer.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

gatyā yayocyate gantā tato 'nyāṃ sa na gacchati | gatī dve nopapadyete yasmād eke pragacchati || 23 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Now you may think: “He does not go by the movement through which he is manifested as a goer; rather, he goes by a movement other than that.” This too is incorrect. Why? Because:

“The goer does not go by a movement other than the movement by which he is called a goer, because two movements are not possible in one goer.”

The goer, while being a goer, does not go by a movement other than the movement through which he is manifested as a goer, because the consequence of two movements would follow. Two movements would follow: the movement through which the goer is manifested as a goer, and another movement by which he, being a goer, goes. But there are not two movements in one goer. This has been stated. By this, the [analogy] “a speaker speaks speech; an agent performs an action” has been answered.
