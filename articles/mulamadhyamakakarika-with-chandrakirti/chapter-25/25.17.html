MMK 25.17

paraṃ nirodhād bhagavān bhavatīty eva nājyate
na bhavaty ubhayaṃ ceti nobhayaṃ ceti nājyate || 17 ||

The Blessed One after cessation is not said to be; nor is he said not to be; nor both; nor neither.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paraṃ nirodhād bhagavān bhavatīty eva nājyate | na bhavaty ubhayaṃ ceti nobhayaṃ ceti nājyate || 17 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

In every way, just as these four conceptions do not obtain in nirvāṇa, so too these conceptions do not obtain in the Tathāgata, the one who attains nirvāṇa. To state this, he says:

“The Blessed One after cessation is not said to be; nor is he said not to be; nor both; nor neither.”

For it was said earlier:

“‘But whoever, seized by dense grasping, conceives the Tathāgata as “exists” or conceives him as “does not exist,” would conceptualize the one who has passed into nirvāṇa.’”

Thus, after final cessation, the Tathāgata is not asserted to exist or not exist. Because these two are absent, he is not asserted as both; precisely because both are absent, he is not apprehended as neither.
