MMK 25.21

paraṃ nirodhād antādyāḥ śāśvatādyāś ca dṛṣṭayaḥ
nirvāṇam aparāntaṃ ca pūrvāntaṃ ca samāśritāḥ || 21 ||

Views beginning with “after cessation” and views beginning with “eternal” rely on the far end, nirvāṇa, and the former end.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

paraṃ nirodhād antādyāḥ śāśvatādyāś ca dṛṣṭayaḥ | nirvāṇam aparāntaṃ ca pūrvāntaṃ ca samāśritāḥ || 21 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And not only, because saṃsāra is not different from nirvāṇa, are conceptions of an earlier and a later limit not possible; also these views—

“Views beginning with ‘after cessation’ and views beginning with ‘eternal’ rely on the far end, nirvāṇa, and the former end”—

do not obtain for this very reason, because both saṃsāra and nirvāṇa have the same taste, being peaceful by their very nature.

Here, by the expression “after cessation,” four views are included: “The Tathāgata exists after death”; “the Tathāgata does not exist after death”; “the Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after death”; and “the Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death.” These four views proceed by taking nirvāṇa as their basis.

The views beginning with “finite,” and so on, are also four: “The world is finite,” “the world is infinite,” “the world is both finite and infinite,” and “the world is neither finite nor infinite.” These four views proceed by taking the future limit as their basis.

There, one who does not see the future arising of self or of the world, conceiving “the world is finite,” proceeds by taking the future limit as a basis. In the same way, one who sees future arising proceeds with the view “the world is infinite.” Seeing and not seeing, one adopts both views. By denying both, one adopts the view “the world is neither finite nor infinite.”

“The world is eternal,” “the world is non-eternal,” “the world is both eternal and non-eternal,” and “the world is neither eternal nor non-eternal”: these four views proceed by taking the former limit as their basis. There, one who sees the past arising of self or of the world adopts the view “the world is eternal”; one who does not see it adopts the view “the world is non-eternal”; one who both sees and does not see it adopts the view “the world is both eternal and non-eternal”; and one who neither sees nor does not see adopts the view “the world is neither eternal nor non-eternal,” taking the former limit as a basis.
