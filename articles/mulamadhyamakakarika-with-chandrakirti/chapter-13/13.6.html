MMK 13.6

tasya ced anyathābhāvaḥ kṣīram eva bhaved dadhi
kṣīrād anyasya kasyātha dadhibhāvo bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

If alteration of that very thing were so, milk itself would become curd. Then from what other than milk would curd come to be?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tasya ced anyathābhāvaḥ kṣīram eva bhaved dadhi | kṣīrād anyasya kasyātha dadhibhāvo bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Moreover:

“If alteration of that very thing were so, milk itself would become curd.”

Then it may be said: The state of curd arises by abandoning the state of milk; therefore milk itself does not become curd. The response: If it is not accepted that milk becomes curd, because they mutually contradict each other:

“Then from what other than milk would curd come to be?”

Would curd come to be from water? Therefore it is completely unrelated that curd would come to be from something else. Thus, because alteration is impossible, from seeing what would the possession of own-being by entities be established? This is not reasonable. As is said in the noble Mahāyāna Ratnākarasūtra:

“The dharma that is not born, does not arise, does not pass away, and does not age—the Victor, the lion among men, shows that; there the great seer instructs beings.

“For it has no own-being at all, nor is any other-being obtained by anyone. It is not found from within or from without; the Lord establishes beings there.

“The Sugata has declared the destinies peaceful, and no destiny at all obtains. Yet there you speak, free from destiny, and free you make many beings.

“You teach all dharmas as without self; you free the world from grasping at beings. Released yourself from destiny, you are free from destiny; thus you have gone beyond, and no one has crossed.

“You have gone to the far shore; you have crossed the ocean of existence. No one who has gone to the far shore is found. There is no far shore and no near shore, yet you say the words, ‘I have gone to the far shore.’

“The speech that you speak does not exist; that which you speak does not exist either. The one to whom you speak does not exist; whoever understands does not exist either.

“This whole world has perished through the force of settling into false conceptualization. The person who peacefully knows dharmas has seen the self-arisen Tathāgata.

“He understands peaceful, excellent dharmas, finds delight, and gladdens beings. He becomes a Victor by conquering the afflictions, self ............; by the awakening of the Buddhas, known through wisdom, he awakens the world too.”

And so forth.
