MMK 13.6

If this itself changes into something else, milk itself would be curds. Something other than milk would be the being of curds.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tasya ced anyathābhāvaḥ kṣīram eva bhaved dadhi | kṣīrād anyasya kasyātha dadhibhāvo bhaviṣyati || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If the same thing changed into another state, milk itself would become curd. If milk does not itself become curd, then what other thing could become curd in its place—water, for example? That would be unrelated. The alternatives of an identical thing and a wholly different thing both fail, so observed transformation does not prove that things possess own-nature.

Candrakīrti cites a sūtra praising the Buddha for showing that phenomena are not born, do not reappear, do not cease, and do not age. No intrinsic nature is found within them or outside them. The Buddha teaches a peaceful path beyond fixed destinations: conventionally he speaks of a path and guides beings, while no independently existing traveller or destination can be found.
