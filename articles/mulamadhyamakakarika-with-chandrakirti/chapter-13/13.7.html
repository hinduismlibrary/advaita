MMK 13.7

yadyaśūnyaṃ bhavet kiṃcit syāc chūnyam iti kiṃcana
na kiṃcid asty aśūnyaṃ ca kutaḥ śūnyaṃ bhaviṣyati || 7 ||

If anything were nonempty, something could be empty. Since nothing is nonempty, how could anything be empty?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yadyaśūnyaṃ bhavet kiṃcit syāc chūnyam iti kiṃcana | na kiṃcid asty aśūnyaṃ ca kutaḥ śūnyaṃ bhaviṣyati || 7 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

And this too, which was said—“An entity without own-being does not exist, and emptiness of entities is accepted; therefore the own-being of entities, which is the basis of emptiness, exists”—is not reasonable, he says:

If there were any nonempty thing, something empty would exist. But there is no nonempty thing. How, then, will there be something empty?

If something called emptiness existed, then there would be an own-being of entities that is its basis. But this is not so. For it is accepted here that emptiness is the common characteristic of all dharmas. Since there is no nonempty dharma, there is no nonemptiness. And when entities are not nonempty and nonemptiness does not exist, then, because it does not depend on an opposite, emptiness too is absent, like a garland of sky-flowers. And when emptiness does not exist, it is established without remainder that entities which would be its basis do not exist either.
