MMK 13.1

The Bhagavan said that whatever dharma is deceptive, that is false. All conditions [are] deceptive dharmas, thus they are false.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

tanmṛṣā moṣadharma yad bhagavān ity abhāṣata | sarve ca moṣadharmāṇaḥ saṃskārās tena te mṛṣāḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The previous analysis has shown that things are not established as produced by themselves, by another, by both, or without a cause. No independent producer is found either. Nevertheless, ordinary people whose understanding is clouded by ignorance see things as though they had arisen in their own right, like the horses conjured by a magician. The Buddha, out of compassion, teaches the truth of their nature to free beings from mistaken views. He says that conditioned formations are deceptive and therefore false.

A sūtra explains that whatever is conditioned is deceptive; nirvāṇa alone is the highest truth because it is not deceptive. Conditioned things are false in the way a painted figure, a puppet, or a mechanical elephant can mislead an inexperienced observer: what appears to be a solid, self-existing thing is not so. Like a mirage or a circle made by a whirling firebrand, formations deceive through mistaken appearance. This reasoning and the scripture together show that all things lack inherent nature.
