MMK 13.3

bhāvānāṃ niḥsvabhāvatvam anyathābhāvadarśanāt
asvabhāvo bhāvo nāsti bhāvānāṃ śūnyatā yataḥ || 3 ||

The absence of own-being in entities is from seeing their alteration. An entity without own-being does not exist, because emptiness belongs to entities.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvānāṃ niḥsvabhāvatvam anyathābhāvadarśanāt | asvabhāvo bhāvo nāsti bhāvānāṃ śūnyatā yataḥ || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Here it is said: This scriptural authority does not reveal the non-arising of the own-being of entities. Rather, it reveals absence of own-being, the non-persistence of own-being, its destruction. If you ask, “How is this so?”

“The absence of own-being in entities is from seeing their alteration.”

The meaning is: alteration of entities being examined is from seeing transformation. This is what is meant: If there were no own-being of entities, then no alteration of them would be perceived. But transformation is perceived. Therefore it is to be understood that the meaning of the sūtra is only the non-persistence of own-being.

For this reason too it is so, because:

“An entity without own-being does not exist, because emptiness belongs to entities.”

For an entity that is without own-being does not exist. And emptiness is held to be a quality of entities. But when the bearer of a quality does not exist, a quality that depends on it is not established. For when a barren woman’s son does not exist, the blueness belonging to him is not established. Therefore the own-being of entities indeed exists.
