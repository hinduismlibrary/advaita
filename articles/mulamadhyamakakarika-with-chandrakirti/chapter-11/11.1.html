MMK 11.1

When asked, "is a before-extreme evident?" the great Muni said, "it is not." Samsara has no beginning, no end; it has no before, no after.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

pūrvā prajñāyate koṭirnetyuvāca mahāmuniḥ | saṃsāro 'navarāgro hi nāsyādirnāpi paścimam || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent argues that a self must exist because saṃsāra exists: without a self, who would move through repeated births and deaths among the five realms? Candrakīrti answers that if saṃsāra existed intrinsically, it would have a first and last limit, as a pot does. The Buddha instead taught that no beginning of saṃsāra is found. In ultimate analysis, a cycle with neither beginning nor end is not an independently existing thing.

The teaching that saṃsāra is beginningless applies conventionally to beings whose ignorance and craving remain. For those who uproot ignorance through the supramundane path, an end is taught. Just as a seed can have an end when burned even though no first seed is identified, the teaching of an end encourages beings bound in the prison of saṃsāra. When reality is examined, however, saṃsāra itself is not found; where no independently existing cycle is found, there is no separate thing whose destruction must be sought.
