VP 1.21.1

संह्लादपुत्र आयुष्मान् शिबिर् बाष्कल एव च।
विरोचनस् तु प्राह्लादिर् बलिर् जज्ञे विरोचनात्

The sons of Saṃhlāda were Āyuṣmān, Śibi, and Bāṣkala; Virocana was Prahlāda’s son, and Bali was born from Virocana.

VP 1.21.7

स्वर्भानोस् तु प्रभा कन्या शर्मिष्ठा वार्षपर्वणी।
उपदानवी हयशिराः प्रख्याता वरकन्यकाः

Svarbhānu’s daughter was Prabhā; Śarmiṣṭhā, Upadānavī, and Hayaśirā were the celebrated daughters of Vṛṣaparvan.

The famous sons are counted among Danu’s hundred sons in the Harivaṃśa, Matsya and other texts; Vaishvānara and the rest are also indicated. Śarmiṣṭhā was the daughter of Vṛṣaparvan.

Śrīdhara translation

Brahmā appointed the gods, the seven sages, Gandharvas and others born in the former manvantara as progenitors for the work of creating beings; he did not create them anew. VP 1.21.40

एकैकं सप्तधा चक्रे वज्रेणाद्रिविदारिणा ।
मरुतो नाम देवास्ते बभूवुरतिवेगिनः ॥४०॥

Indra split each one into seven with the thunderbolt that cleaves mountains. They became the very swift gods called the Maruts.

Śrīdhara translation

“Being afflicted” and “being cut” explain the wordplay: because of the command “do not cry,” they became the Maruts. “Vajra” indicates the thunderbolt; the hand holding it is Vajrapāṇi, and one who possesses that hand is Vajrapāṇī.
