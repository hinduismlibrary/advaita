VP 1.3.1

निर्गुणस्याप्रमेयस्य शुद्धस्याप्य् अमलात्मनः।
कथं सर्गादि-कर्तादि-कर्तृत्वं ब्रह्मणोऽभ्युपगम्यत

How can creative agency be attributed to that Brahma, who is without qualities, illimitable, pure, and free from imperfection?

Śrīdhara translation

Thus Brahmā’s authorship of creation and the rest has been stated; the objection is now raised. “Of the qualityless one” means one free from the qualities beginning with sattva. “Immeasurable” means not delimited by place, time, and the like. “Pure” may mean bodiless and without auxiliary causes; “stainless in self” may likewise mean free from the impressions of merit and demerit and from attachment and the rest. How can Brahmā, being such, be accepted as the creator of creation and so forth, when in the world agency over pots and similar things is seen only in one different from this? That is the meaning.

VP 1.3.2

शक्तयः सर्व-भावानाम् अचिन्त्य-ज्ञान-गोचराः।
यतोऽतो ब्रह्मणस् तास् तु सर्गाद्या भाव-शक्तयः।
भवन्ति तपसां श्रेष्ठ पावकस्य यथोष्णता

The essential properties of existent things are objects of observation, of which no foreknowledge is attainable; and creation, and hundreds of properties, belong to Brahma, as inseparable parts of his essence, as heat, oh chief of sages, is inherent in fire.

Śrīdhara translation

The objection is answered in a verse and a half beginning “śaktayaḥ.” In the world, the powers of all entities—such as jewels and mantras—are beyond ordinary thought, yet they are accessible to knowledge inferred from the impossibility of explaining their effects otherwise. Alternatively, they are inconceivable because their being different, non-different, and so on cannot be established by reasoning; they are known only through such necessary inference. Since this is so, Brahman—Śrī Viṣṇu—also possesses essential powers that are the causes of creation and the other cosmic functions, just as fire possesses heat and the power to burn. Therefore Brahmā’s creative agency is possible even though he is free from the guṇas, because he possesses inconceivable powers. The śruti says: “He has neither work nor instrument; his equal or superior is nowhere seen. His supreme power is heard of as manifold, and its knowledge, strength, and action are intrinsic.” It also teaches, “Know Māyā to be Prakṛti and the Lord of Māyā to be the great Lord,” and similar passages. Another arrangement of the argument is this: every entity has powers, like fire’s power of heat, that are inaccessible to discursive thought but known through their proper evidence. In Brahman these powers are intrinsic and non-different from his nature, as the śruti says that his supreme power is manifold. They cannot be obstructed by anything, just as the heat of fire cannot be prevented by jewels or mantras; therefore his sovereignty is unrestricted. The śruti also says, “This Self is the ruler of all, the lord of all, and the sovereign of all.” By addressing Parāśara as “best of those who practise austerity,” the text indicates that the power of austerity is directly known by the ascetic himself. Since this is so, creation and the other functions arise from Brahman, and there is no inconsistency.

VP 1.3.4

उत्पन्नः प्रोच्यते विद्वन् नित्यम् एवोपचारतः

Brahmā is said to be born: a familiar phrase, to signify his manifestation;

Śrīdhara translation

If Brahmā is called Nārāyaṇa, how can the śruti say, “Hiraṇyagarbha arose in the beginning,” and speak of his birth? The answer is: “He is said to be born” only figuratively, because manifestation by his own will resembles birth.

VP 1.3.7

अन्येषां चैव जन्तूनां चराणाम् अचराश् च ये।
भू-भूभृत्-सागरादीनाम् अशेषाणां च सत्तम

and of all other sentient beings, as well as of those which are unconscious, as the mountains, oceans, and the like.

Śrīdhara translation

VP 1.3.26

एवं तु ब्रह्मणो वर्षम् एवं वर्ष-शतं च यत्।
शतं हि तस्य वर्षाणां परमायुर् महात्मनः

Of such days and nights is a year of Brahmā composed; and a hundred such years constitute his whole life.

Śrīdhara translation

With such day-and-nights, and with the calculation of fortnights, months, and the rest, a year is formed.

VP 1.3.27

एकम् अस्य व्यतीतं तु परार्धं ब्रह्मणोऽनघ।
तस्यान्तेऽभून् महा-कल्पः पाद्म इत्य् अभिविश्रुतः

One Parārdha, or half his existence, has expired, terminating with the Mahā Kalpa called Pādma.

Śrīdhara translation

“Mahā-Kalpa” means an intermediate Kalpa that is called great because of qualities such as the manifestation of Puṣkara and the rest.
