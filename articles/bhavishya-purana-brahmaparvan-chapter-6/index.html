Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 6: Women’s Conduct and Marriage Alliances

BP 1.6.1

शतानीकउवाच
सद्वृत्तं श्रोतुमिच्छामि देवस्त्रीणां सुविस्तरात् ।
उत्तमाधममध्यं च सम्बन्धे स्त्रीकृते यथा ॥ १

Śatānīka said: I wish to hear in detail the good conduct of women and how marriage alliances may be superior, inferior or intermediate.

BP 1.6.2

सुमन्तुरुवाच
शतानीक महाबाहो ब्रह्मलोके पितामहः ।
उक्त्वा स लक्षणं स्त्रीणां सद्वृत्तं चोक्तवान्पुनः ॥ २

Sumantu said: O mighty-armed Śatānīka, after Brahmā in Brahmaloka described the marks of women, he spoke again about good conduct.

BP 1.6.3

यथोक्तं ब्रह्मणां तेषामृषीणां कुरुनन्दन ।
स प्रेयो वचनं श्रुत्वा ब्रह्मा वचनमब्रवीत् ॥ ३

O delight of the Kurus, after those brāhmaṇa sages had spoken as told, Brahmā heard their pleasing words and replied.

BP 1.6.4

शृणुध्वं द्विजशार्दूलाः स्त्रीणां सद्वृत्तमादितः ।
वक्ष्ये युष्मानशेषं वै लोकानुग्रहकाम्यया ॥ ४

Brahmā said: ‘Listen, O tigers among the twice-born. Desiring to benefit the world, I shall tell you without omission the good conduct of women from its beginning.’

BP 1.6.5

त्रिवर्गप्राप्तये वक्ष्ये स्त्रीवृत्तं गृहमेधिनाम् ।
प्राग्विद्यादीनुपादाय तैरर्थांश्च यथाक्रमम् ॥
विन्देत सदृशीं भार्यां शास्त्रदृष्टेन कर्मणा ॥ ५

‘For householders’ attainment of the three aims of life, I shall explain conduct concerning a wife. Having first acquired learning and the other prerequisites, and then wealth in due order, a man should obtain a suitable wife by the procedure recognized in scripture.’

BP 1.6.6

गृहाश्रमो हि निःस्वानां महत्येषा विडम्बना ।
तस्मात्पूर्वमुपादेयं वित्तमेव गृहैषिणा ॥ ६

‘For those without means, the householder’s state is a great ordeal. Therefore one who seeks a household should first acquire wealth.’

BP 1.6.7

वरं सोढा मनुष्येण तीव्रा नरकवेदना ।
न त्वेव च गृहे दृष्टं पुत्रदारक्षुधार्दितम् ॥ ७

‘It would be better for a person to endure the fierce torments of hell than to see a wife and children at home afflicted by hunger.’

BP 1.6.8

असम्भवे शिशुं दृष्ट्वा रुदन्तं प्रार्थनापरम् ।
वज्रसारमयं मन्ये हदयं यन्न दीर्यते ॥ ८

‘Seeing a helpless child crying and continually asking for food, I consider the heart that does not break to be made from the substance of a thunderbolt.’

BP 1.6.9

साध्वीं भार्यां प्रियां दृष्ट्वा कुचैलां क्षुत्कृशीकृताम् ।
अस्य दुःखस्य तन्नास्ति सुखं यत्समतां व्रजेत् ॥ ९

‘Seeing his virtuous and beloved wife in poor clothing and emaciated by hunger, there is no happiness that can equal the suffering a man feels.’

BP 1.6.10

रूक्षान्विवर्णान्क्षुधितान्भूमिप्रस्तरशायिनः ।
पुत्रदारान्निजान्दृष्ट्वा किमकार्यं भवेन्नृणाम् ॥.6.१०

‘When people see their own wife and children rough, pale, hungry, and sleeping upon the bare ground, what deed might they not commit?’

BP 1.6.11

बाहूत्तरीयं क्षुत्क्षामं दृष्ट्वा दीनमुखं सुतम् ।
मृत्युरेवोत्सवः पुंसां व्यसनं जीवितं द्विजाः ॥१

‘Seeing his son using his arms as an upper garment, wasted by hunger, and downcast in face, death itself becomes a celebration for a man and life a calamity, O twice-born sages.’

BP 1.6.12

परिसीदत्स्वपत्येषु दृष्ट्वा दीनमुखो प्रियाम् ।
वज्रकार्यशरीरास्ते ये न यान्ति सहस्रधा ॥२

‘Those who see their children sinking in distress and their beloved wife downcast possess bodies as hard as a thunderbolt if they do not shatter into a thousand pieces.’

BP 1.6.13

तस्मादर्थविहीनस्य पुंसो दारपरिग्रहात् ।
कुतस्त्रिवर्ग संसिद्धिर्यातनैव हि तस्य सा ॥३

‘How can a man without means attain the three aims by taking a wife? For him that very marriage becomes torment.’

BP 1.6.14

अभार्यस्याधिकारोऽस्ति न द्वितीयाश्रमे यथा ।
तद्वदर्थविहीनानां सर्वत्र नाधिकरिता ॥४

‘Just as a man without a wife has no eligibility for the second stage of life, so those without means possess no effective capacity anywhere.’

BP 1.6.15

केचित्त्वपत्यमेवाहुस्त्रिवर्गावाप्तिसाधनम् ।
पुंसामर्थः कलत्रं च येऽन्ये नीतिविदो विदुः ॥५

‘Some say that offspring alone is the means by which people attain the three aims; other authorities on statecraft understand wealth and a wife to be the means.’

BP 1.6.16

धर्मोऽपि द्विविधो यस्मादिष्टापूर्तक्रियात्मकः ।
स च दारात्मकः सर्वं ज्ञेयमर्थैकसाधनम् ॥६

‘Dharma itself is twofold, consisting of sacrificial and charitable works, and all of it depends upon a wife; it should be understood that wealth alone makes these possible.’

BP 1.6.17

निजेनापि दरिद्रेण लोको लज्जति बन्धुना ।
परोऽपि हि मनुष्याणामैश्वर्यात्स्वजनायते ॥७

‘The world is embarrassed even by a poor relative of its own, while through prosperity even an outsider becomes like one’s own kin.’

BP 1.6.18

न दरिद्रं समीपेऽपि स्थितवन्तं प्रपश्यति ।
दूरस्थमपि वित्ताढ्यमादराद्भजते जनः ॥८

‘People do not notice a poor person even when he stands nearby, but respectfully attend upon a wealthy person though he is far away.’

BP 1.6.19

तस्मात्प्रयत्नतः पूर्वमर्थमेव प्रसाधयेत ।
स हि मूलं त्रिवर्गस्य गुणानां गौरवस्य च ॥९

‘Therefore one should first establish wealth through effort, for it is the root of the three aims, of virtues, and of social esteem.’

BP 1.6.20

सर्वेऽपि हि गुणा विद्याकुलशीलादयो नृणाम् ।
सन्ति तस्मिन्नसन्तोऽपि सन्ति सन्तोऽपि नासति ॥.6.२०

‘All human virtues—learning, lineage, character, and the rest—are perceived to exist in one who possesses wealth, even when absent; in one without wealth, they are perceived as absent even when present.’

BP 1.6.21

शास्त्रं शिल्पं कलाः कर्म यच्चान्यदपि चेष्टितम् ।
साधनं सर्वमर्थानामर्था धर्मादिसाधनाः ॥१

‘Scriptural learning, craft, arts, occupation, and every other undertaking are all means to wealth; wealth in turn is the means to dharma and the other aims.’

BP 1.6.22

साधनानां त्रिवर्गोऽस्ति तं विना केवलं नृणाम् ।
अजागलस्तनस्येव निधनायैव संभवः ॥२

‘The three aims constitute the purpose of those means; without them, a person’s mere existence leads only to death, like the useless teats on a goat’s neck.’

BP 1.6.23

प्राक्पुण्यैर्विपुला सम्पद्धर्मकामादिहेतुजा ।
भूयो धर्मेण सामुत्र तया३ ताविति च क्रमः. ॥३

‘Through former merit comes abundant prosperity, which becomes the cause of dharma, desire, and the other aims; through dharma again comes benefit in the next world. Such is the sequence between them.’

BP 1.6.24

एकचक्रकमेतद्धि प्रोक्तमन्योन्यहेतुकम् ।
पूर्वपश्चिमबाहुभ्यामुत्तराधरमध्यमाः ॥४

‘This has been described as a single wheel whose parts mutually cause one another, with upper, lower, and middle positions related like its front and rear arms.’

BP 1.6.25

विज्ञाय मतिमानेवं यस्त्रिवर्गं निषेवते ।
संख्याशतसमायुक्तैरवाप्नोत्युत्तरोत्तरम् ॥५

‘The intelligent person who understands this and pursues the three aims obtains each successive one multiplied a hundredfold.’

BP 1.6.26

नाभार्यस्याधिकारोऽस्ति त्रिवर्गे निर्धनस्य वा ।
ना भार्यायामतः पूर्वमर्थमैव प्रसाधयेत् ॥६

‘One without a wife, or without wealth, has no eligibility for the three aims. Therefore, before taking a wife, one should first establish wealth.’

BP 1.6.27

तस्मात्क्रमागतैरर्थेः स्वयं वाधिगतैर्युतः ।
क्रियायोग्यैः समर्थश्च कुर्याद्दारपरिग्रहम् ॥७

‘Therefore, furnished with inherited wealth or wealth acquired by himself, capable and possessing resources suitable for household duties, he should take a wife.’

BP 1.6.28

अनुरूपे कुले जातां श्रुतवित्तक्रियादिभिः ।
लभेतानिन्दितां कन्यां मनोज्ञां धर्मसाधनाम् ॥८

‘He should obtain a blameless and pleasing maiden, born in a fitting family comparable in learning, wealth, and ritual practice, who will assist in the fulfilment of dharma.’

BP 1.6.29

पुमानर्धपुमांस्तावद्यावद्भार्यां न विदन्ति ।
तस्माद्यथाक्रमं काले कुर्याद्दारपरिग्रहम् ॥९

‘A man is only half a man until he obtains a wife. Therefore, at the proper time and in due order, he should enter marriage.’

BP 1.6.30

एकचक्रो रथो यद्वदेकपक्षो यथा खगः ।
अभार्योऽपिनरस्तद्वदयोग्यः सर्वकर्मसु ॥.6.३०

‘Just as a chariot with one wheel or a bird with one wing is unfit for movement, so a man without a wife is unfit for all household rites.’

BP 1.6.31

पत्नीपरिग्रहाद्धर्मस्तथार्थो बहुलाभतः ।
सत्प्रीतियोगात्कामोऽपि त्रयमस्यां विदुर्बुधाः ॥१

‘Through taking a wife comes dharma; through manifold gain comes wealth; and through the union of mutual affection comes desire. The wise recognize all three in her.’

BP 1.6.32

त्रिधा विवाहसम्बन्धो हीनतुल्याधिकैः सह ।
तुल्यैः सह समस्तेषामितरौ नीचमध्यमौ ॥२

‘Marriage alliance is threefold: with those who are inferior, equal, or superior. Alliance with equals is balanced for everyone; the other two are respectively low and intermediate.’

BP 1.6.33

असमैर्निन्द्यते सद्भिरुत्तमैः परिभूयते ।
तुल्यैः प्रशस्यते यस्मात्तस्मात्साधुतमो मतः ॥३

‘An unequal alliance is censured by the virtuous and slighted by superiors, while an alliance with equals is praised; therefore the latter is held to be best.’

BP 1.6.34

कृत्वैवाधिकसम्बन्धमपमानं समश्नुते ।
न चैषामानतिं गच्छेन्नैव नीचैः सहेष्यते२ ॥४

‘By forming an alliance with a superior family, one experiences humiliation; one cannot obtain their respectful submission, while an alliance with those below is likewise considered undesirable.’

BP 1.6.35

उत्तमोऽपि च सम्बन्धो नीचैस्तत्समतां व्रजेत् ।
अतस्तं वर्जयेद्धीमान्निन्दितं सदृशोत्तमैः ॥५

‘Even a superior alliance made with those below descends to their level. Therefore an intelligent person should avoid an alliance censured by equals and superiors.’

BP 1.6.36

विजातीयैश्च सम्बन्धं सहेच्छन्ति न सूरयः ।
उभयोर्भ्रश्यते तेन यथा कोकिलया शुकः ॥६

‘The wise do not approve an alliance with those of a different community, for through it both sides fall from their position, just as a parrot paired with a cuckoo.’

BP 1.6.37

तद्भाति कुलबाह्यत्वादवश्यं चावमानतः ।
प्रतिपत्तेरशक्यत्वाच्चोत्तमोऽपि न शस्यते ॥७

‘Such an alliance appears alien to the family and inevitably brings disrespect; because mutual obligations cannot be fulfilled, even an alliance that seems superior is not praised.’

BP 1.6.38

एकेऽपि परिहर्तव्या अन्ये परिहरन्त्युत ।
तस्माद्द्वावपि नैवेष्टौ सम्बन्धावधमोत्तमौ ॥८

‘Some avoid one kind, while others avoid the other. Therefore neither the inferior nor the superior alliance is considered desirable.’

BP 1.6.39

एकपात्रादिभिर्येषामुपचारैः परस्परम् ।
प्रत्यहं वर्धते स्नेहः सम्बन्धः सोऽभिधीयते ॥९

‘That is called a true alliance in which mutual affection grows every day through sharing one vessel and other forms of familiar interaction.’

BP 1.6.40

यत्रावाहविवाहादावन्योऽन्याः प्रतिपत्तयः ।
स्पर्धयैव प्रवर्धन्ते तं सम्बन्धं विदुर्बुधाः ॥.6.४०

‘Where, in receiving and giving a bride and similar occasions, each side’s respectful attentions toward the other increase as if in friendly rivalry, the wise recognize that as a true alliance.’

BP 1.6.41

य्यसनेऽभ्युदये वापि येषां प्राणैर्धनैरपि ।
सहैकप्रतिपत्तित्वं सम्बन्धानां स उत्तमः ॥१

‘The best alliance is that in which, during adversity or prosperity, both sides adopt one common purpose even at the cost of life and wealth.’

BP 1.6.42

स्नेहव्यक्तौ मनुष्याणां द्वावेव निकषोपलौ ।
तथा कृतज्ञतायां च व्यसनाभ्युदयागमौ ॥४२

‘For revealing people’s affection and likewise their gratitude, there are precisely two touchstones: the arrival of adversity and the arrival of prosperity.’

BP 1.6.43

स च स्नेहो नृणां प्रायः समेष्वेव१ हि दृश्यते ।
साम्यं चाप्युपगन्तव्यं वित्तशीलकुलादिभिः ॥४ ३

‘Such affection among people is generally seen only between equals, and equality should be judged by wealth, character, family, and similar qualities.’

BP 1.6.44

तस्माद्विवाहसम्बन्धं संख्यमेकान्तकारिणाम् ।
सदृशैरेव कुर्वीत नोत्तमेनाप्यनुत्तमैः२ ॥४

Therefore one should form a marriage alliance and friendship only with families of similar standing, not with those either superior or inferior.
