Preface

Introduction

Hindu traditions identify different deities with Brahman, the supreme being. Smārtas generally describe Brahman as without form, qualities, or name at the absolute level, while worshipping five deities at the empirical level. Śaivas identify Śiva as supreme; Vaiṣṇavas identify Viṣṇu as supreme and other deities as subordinate. Śāktas identify Devī as supreme. This paper presents the Vaiṣṇava case for Viṣṇu’s supremacy through scriptural interpretation and answers Śaiva and Smārta objections.

This paper presents the Vaiṣṇava view. It is not an attack on Śaivas, Smārtas, or other Hindu traditions. It argues from scripture in the spirit of respectful debate.

Methodology

Scripture is the criterion used here to assess doctrines about Brahman. Hindu epistemology recognizes three pramāṇas, or means of knowledge: pratyakṣa, anumāna, and śabda. They refer to direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony. Because Brahman cannot be known through the senses, this paper treats scripture as the relevant source of knowledge. Brahma Sūtra 1.1.3 states this principle.

Brahma Sūtra 1.1.3

शास्त्रयोनित्वात् ॥ १.१.३ ॥

Due to scripture being the yoni (pramāṇa)

Commentary by Śaṅkarācārya

Ādi Śaṅkarācārya, Brahma Sūtra Bhāṣya 1.1.3

यथोक्तमृग्वेदादिशास्त्रं योनिः कारणं प्रमाणमस्य ब्रह्मणो यथावत्स्वरूपाधिगमे । शास्त्रादेव प्रमाणात् जगतो जन्मादिकारणं ब्रह्माधिगम्यत इत्यभिप्रायः ॥

As stated, the scriptures, beginning with Rigveda, are the Yoni, i.e., cause (of knowledge) or pramāṇa of Brahman. By the scriptural pramāṇa alone Brahman, the cause of the creation, preservation and destruction of the world, is known. This is the meaning.

This is accepted by all aforementioned schools, who all allege that their doctrine is backed by all the scriptures. Thus we will argue only based on what is acceptable by all of them as Pramāṇa.

Having established scripture as the basis, the paper next defines what counts as scripture. The highest scriptures are the Vedas, or Śruti. "Veda" here means not merely the four Saṃhitās, as is common in modern discussion, but also their associated Brāhmaṇas, Aranyakas, and Upaniṣads.

The Saṃhitās and Brāhmaṇas together form the karma-kāṇḍa, the ritual division. Their praises of particular deities occur in ritual contexts and are therefore not decisive for doctrinal purposes. The Aranyakas and Upaniṣads together form the jnana-kāṇḍa, the knowledge division concerned with Brahman, the self, and the universe.

Smṛti

Aside from the Vedas, there are other scriptures within Hinduism, usually called Smṛtis. These derive their authority directly from the Vedas and are mentioned in them. They are:

1. Dharmaśāstras

Primarily featuring the Manusmṛti, these are texts dealing largely with social laws.

2. Itihāsas and Purāṇas

Made up of 2 Itihāsas, viz Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata, and 18 Purāṇas, they explain the Vedic teachings through the historic narratives and conversations of the realised people.

3. Vedāṅgas

Made of 6 auxiliary sciences, viz., pronunciation, prosody, etymology, grammar, astronomy/astrology and ritual performance, they are required for the practice of the Vedas.

These scriptures are acceptable to all sects, except perhaps the Purāṇas, which have clear sectarian divisions within them.

It should be understood that these texts are not merely secondary literature which can be ignored in favour of using the Vedas alone, but are essential for properly understanding the Vedas themselves. The Mahābhārata states in its first chapter:

Mahābhārata 1.1.204

इतिहासपुराणाभ्यां वेदं समुपबृंहयेत् । बिभेत्यल्पश्रुताद् वेदो मामयं प्रतरिष्यति ॥

One should understand the Vedas using the Itihāsas and Purāṇas (for) the Vedas fear the one who has studied little, saying "this one shall misinterpret me

There is a maxim in case of contradiction between the Veda, smṛtis (not including Purāṇas) and Purāṇas, as noted in Vyāsa Smṛti (1.4):

Vyāsa Smṛti 1.4

श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानां विरोधो यत्र दृश्यते । तत्र श्रौतं प्रमाणं तु तयोर्द्वैधे स्मृतिर्वरा ॥

Where there is contradiction between Śruti, Smṛti and Purāṇa, there, Śruti is the pramāṇa. And on conflict between the two (Smṛti and Purāṇa), Smṛti is supreme.

Though this is a valid maxim, it should be noted that it has a very clear condition: "where there is contradiction". i.e., this maxim can only be applied in case of absolutely irreconcilable contradiction between passages of the Śruti and smṛti, which is extremely rare. We should be cautious and always look for ways of reconciliation before blindly applying this maxim. It is not a licence to ignore Smṛti and Purāṇa. A note on reconciliation

This topic is of course not anything new, and this debate has been going on for thousands of years. However, in most modern takes on this topic I have seen, I have noticed that, if they even invoke scriptures in the first place, they are largely dismissive or exclusionary rather than reconciliatory. They will list the pramāṇas that seemingly support their position, and either omit pramāṇas that do not fit their framework, declare them to be interpolations or forgeries or invoke the aforementioned maxim to claim it is contradicting some śruti with thin backing. This is not such a paper. One of the axioms of Mīmāṃsā is samanvaya or reconciliation. Thus this paper will neither omit nor dismiss any pramāṇas used by the opponent, and expects the opponent to do the same. It attempts to reconcile the entire scriptural corpus.

Vedas

Karma-kāṇḍa

As explained earlier, the Vedas have two divisions, the Karma-kāṇḍa being ritualistic and eulogistic and hence doctrinally indecisive, and the Jnana Kāṇḍa being doctrinal. Hence our focus will be on Jnana-kāṇḍa. There is however a common misconception that Indra or Agni or any other such deities are "supreme" in the Vedas or that Viṣṇu is a "minor deity", merely because he has less hymns in the karma-kāṇḍa, is rejected on its very premise, as they are praised in the Vedas for their importance in ritual, and not for their actual superiority. However, even aside from that, Viṣṇu is no "Minor deity" in the karma-kāṇḍa merely because he has less hymns. It is the quality of the Hymns that matter, what they say, and not their quantity.

Saṃhitā

The Rigveda (10.113.2) clarifies that Viṣṇu is worshipped due to his own intrinsic nature, whereas Indra is worshipped circumstantially:

Rigveda 10.113.2

तं अस्य विष्णुर्महिमानमोजसा अंशुं दधन्वान् मधुनो वि रप्शते । देवेभिरिन्द्रो मघवा सयावभिर्वृत्रं जघन्वाँ अभवद्वरेण्यः ॥

That Viṣṇu is great by his own vigour and greatness, whereas Indra, lord of wealth, became deserving of honour only after slaying Vrtra with the deities.

The Rigveda also states that Viṣṇu is the most ancient deity, the creator and the self-born. Rigveda 1.156.2 says:

Rigveda 1.156.2

यः पूर्व्याय वेधसे नवीयसे सुमज्जानये विष्णवे ददाशति । यो जातमस्य महतो महि ब्रवत् सेदु श्रवोभिर्युज्यं चिदभ्यसत् ॥

He who presents (offerings) to Viṣṇu, the ancient (and yet) the recent, the creator, the self-born; he who celebrates the great birth of that mighty one, he verily becomes possessed of abundance

The passage states that Viṣṇu is the most ancient and Self-born, and yet also the most recent and having a birth. This is a subtle allusion to Avatara-vada, Viṣṇu, though eternal and birthless, taking birth in the form of Vāmāna, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, etc.

The Rigveda also states that Viṣṇu's position (padam) is supreme and always contemplated by the wise (1.22.20):

Rigveda 1.22.20

तद्विष्णोः परमं पदं सदा पश्यन्ति सूरयः ॥

That supreme position of Viṣṇu is always contemplated by the wise

Brāhmaṇas

Aside from the Saṃhitās, there are the Brāhmaṇas, which comment on the Saṃhitās and explain their use in yajña.

The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 5.2.3.6 and Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.1.1 give the same hierarchy, placing Viṣṇu above the other deities:

Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 5.2.3.6; Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.1.1

अग्निर्वै देवानामवमो विष्णुः परमस्तदन्तरेण सर्वा अन्याः देवताः ॥

Agni is the lowest and Viṣṇu the highest amongst the deities, in between them are all other deities.

Also in Taittirīya Saṃhitā (5.5.1) with slightly different wording

अग्निरवमो देवतानां विष्णुः परमः ॥
विष्णोरुत्तमत्वं च युक्तम् ॥
तस्मादाहुर्विष्णुर्देवानां श्रेष्ठः ॥

Agni is the lowest and Viṣṇu is the highest amongst the deities

Objections

Agni is closest to the sacrifice being performed, whereas Viṣṇu is the highest in heaven or farthest away.

Answer: Not so, for Sāyaṇa states in his Bhāṣya on the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (1.1.1):

Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 1.1.1 — Sāyaṇa Bhāṣya

Here superiority (uttamatva) is stated.

Regardless, there is an even more explicit statement:

Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 14.1.1.5

Therefore it is said: Viṣṇu is the supreme (śreṣṭha) among the deities.

Hence, even in the karma-kāṇḍa, though the number of hymns for Viṣṇu is less, those hymns and their Brāhmaṇas still clearly state his status as supreme, most ancient, self-born, creator, and possessing the highest position. They also clarify that his worship is intrinsic, whereas that of Indra is circumstantial.

Jnana-kāṇḍa

We now come to the jnana-kāṇḍa, the doctrinally decisive section of the Vedas. Firstly, the Nārāyaṇa Sukta, part of the Taittirīya Aranyaka, clearly identifies the supreme Brahman with Nārāyaṇa, i.e., Viṣṇu:

Nārāyaṇa Sūkta, Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.13.4

नारायणः परो ज्योतिरात्मा नारायणः परः । नारायणः परं ब्रह्म तत्त्वं नारायणः परः ।
नारायणः परो ध्याता ध्यानं नारायणः परः ॥

Nārāyaṇa is the supreme effulgence, Nārāyaṇa is the supreme self. Nārāyaṇa is the supreme Brahman, Nārāyaṇa is the supreme tattva. Nārāyaṇa is the supreme meditator and Nārāyaṇa is the supreme meditation.

Objections

Answer: The etymology of the word “Nārāyaṇa,” as given in all the scriptures, shall be seen shortly, which will make clear that it can only refer to Viṣṇu. Here, however, the text itself confirms that it refers to Viṣṇu in the final verse:

The etymology of the word "Nārāyaṇa" throughout scripture will be considered shortly. Here, however, such a discussion is unnecessary, because the text itself identifies Nārāyaṇa with Viṣṇu in its final verse:

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.13 (Nārāyaṇa Sūkta, concluding mantra)

ॐ नारायणाय विद्महे वासुदेवाय धीमहि । तन्नो विष्णुः प्रचोदयात् ॥

OM. Nārāyaṇa we acknowledge, Vasudeva we meditate upon, may that Viṣṇu propel us forward.

Thus, Vasudeva, Viṣṇu and Nārāyaṇa are synonyms for the same deity as per the Nārāyaṇa sūktam itself.

Though this passage is decisive against the Śaiva paksha, it is not so against the Smārtas, for they affirm Viṣṇu as supreme and Brahman alongside Śiva and others. The following Vedic passages establish not merely Viṣṇu's superiority, but His exclusive superiority over Śiva, Brahma, and the other deities. The Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, Khanda 1, states:

Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, Khanda 1

नारायणाद् ब्रह्मा जायते । नारायणाद् रुद्रो जायते । नारायणादिन्द्रो जायते । नारायणात् प्रजापतयः प्रजायन्ते । नारायणाद् द्वादशादित्या रुद्रा वसवः सर्वाणि च छन्दांसि । नारायणादेव समुत्पद्यन्ते । नारायणे प्रवर्तन्ते । नारायणे प्रलीयन्ते ॥

Brahma is born from Nārāyaṇa. Rudra is born from Nārāyaṇa. Indra is born from Nārāyaṇa. From Nārāyaṇa the Prajapatis are born. From Nārāyaṇa, the twelve Adityas, Rudras and all Vedas are born. They proceed also in Nārāyaṇa. They eventually dissolve in Nārāyaṇa.

Thus Nārāyaṇa is characterised as the origin of Brahma, Rudra, Indra, all deities, and the scriptures; He is also their basis and the one in whom they finally dissolve. The Smārta claim that Nārāyaṇa is supreme only in addition to Rudra and others therefore cannot be maintained, since He is explicitly their source. Similarly, the first verse of the Mahopanishad states:

Mahopaniṣad 1.1

एको ह वै नारायण आसीन्न ब्रह्मा नेशानो नापो नाग्नीषोमौ नेमे द्यावापृथिवी न नक्षत्राणि सूर्यो न चन्द्रमाः ॥

Indeed, Nārāyaṇa alone was (in the beginning), Not Brahma, not Śiva, not water, not fire not soma, neither sky not earth, not the constellations, Sun nor moon.

An almost identical verse is found in the Paiṅgi-Rahasya Brāhmaṇa; the only difference is that it uses "Śaṅkara" instead of "Īśāna" for Śiva:

Paiṅgi-Rahasya Brāhmaṇa, cited in Madhva’s Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya 1.2.23

एको ह वै नारायण आसीन्न ब्रह्मा न च शङ्करः ॥

Nārāyaṇa alone was (in the beginning), neither Brahma nor Śiva.

By all these verses, Viṣṇu is much prior to Śiva and other deities and is their creator, and therefore cannot be the same as them (Smārta view), neither inferior (Śaiva view). Also, the definition of Brahman as stated in Brahma Sūtra 1.1.2 is as the cause of the world, thus this also reinforces Viṣṇu's exclusive status as Brahman.

Objections

Answer: That cannot be so, for these verses are quoted by prominent ācāryas of both the Smārtas and the Śaivas.

Among the Smārtas, Madhusudana Sarasvati, one of the most influential and revered Smārta ācāryas, cites the Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad verse on page 8 of the cited edition, the Mahopanishad verse on pages 8 and 767, and the Paingi-rahasya Brāhmaṇa verse on page 763.

Among the Śaivas, Shripati Panditācārya, a prominent Viraśaiva ācārya and defender of Śaiva theology, quotes the Mahopanishad and Nārāyaṇa Upaniṣad verses on page 321 of his commentary on the Brahma Sūtras, the Shrikara Bhashya. Although he presents them in the purvapaksha and offers a Śaiva explanation, he does not deny their authenticity.

The traditional ācāryas therefore preferred samanvaya to dismissing difficult passages as interpolations. For that reason, subsequent objections based only on alleging forgery or interpolation are not repeated.

Once Viṣṇu's identity as Brahman has been established by these pramāṇas, references to Brahman in the Upaniṣads may be read as referring to Viṣṇu. This reading is not limited to Vaiṣṇava commentators: Śaṅkarācārya, commenting on Chandogya Upaniṣad 8.12.3 and its "uttama puruṣa," writes:

Chandogya Upaniṣad 8.12.3

उत्तमपुरुषः – कृतनिर्वचनो ह्ययं गीतासु ।

Uttama-puruṣa-He is the one spoken of in the Gītā.

Objections

Answer: Not so. Firstly, Kṛṣṇa’s Gītā is the only one commented on by Śaṅkara, and secondly Ānandagirī, a Smārta ācārya who wrote a sub-commentary on Śaṅkara’s, has quoted the exact Gītā passage Śaṅkara was referencing:

Bhagavad Gītā 15.18

यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तमः । अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुषोत्तमः ॥

Because I transcend the perishable and am higher even than the imperishable, I am celebrated in the world and in the Veda as Purushottama.

Anandagiri quotes this exact Gītā passage in his sub-commentary. While commenting on Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.7.3, Śaṅkara writes:

Śaṅkarabhāṣya on Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.7.3

य ईदृगीश्वरो नारायणाख्यः

This īśvara, who is known as Nārāyaṇa.

Notably, he makes no such statement linking the Upaniṣadic supreme to Śiva or his epithets.

Puruṣa Sūktam

The most famous Vedic Sukta is the Puruṣa Sukta, for it describes not elemental deities but the supreme being from whom everything came to be. Its best-known recension has sixteen verses in the Rigveda Saṃhitā; the Taittirīya Aranyaka preserves a longer twenty-four-verse recension in the jnana-kāṇḍa.

Puruṣa Sukta, Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 3.12.1

सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः सहस्राक्षः सहस्रपात् । स भूमिं विश्वतो वृत्वा अत्यतिष्ठद्दशाङ्गुलम् ॥

The Puruṣa has a thousand faces, a thousand eyes and a thousand feet. He pervades the entire world, extending in the ten directions.

Further:

Puruṣa Sukta, Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 3.12.2

एतावानस्य महिमा अतो ज्यायाँश्च पूरुषः । पादोऽस्य विश्वा भूतानि त्रिपादस्यामृतं दिवि ॥

This much is his greatness, and even greater is he, that puruṣa. In one pada of his come all the world and its beings, and of three padas the immortal in heaven.

The sukta then goes on to describe how everything came to be from the body of this primordial being, the Puruṣa. Even from these verses, available in the Rigveda version also, an argument for the Puruṣa being Viṣṇu may be made, due to the emphasis on him pervading the world (Viṣṇu literally means one who pervades), the reference to one pada of his containing the earth and three containing the heavens, alluding to Viṣṇu's Trivikrama epithet referenced also in the Rigveda, etc. But if still there is any doubt it is cleared by the final verse of the Yajurveda version:

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 3.12.10

ह्रीश्च ते लक्ष्मीश्च पत्न्यौ । अहोरात्रे पार्श्वे ॥

Hri and Lakshmi are your wives, the day and night your two sides

Thus this verse clears any debate entirely. The primordial Puruṣa, the source of everything, is none other than the husband of Lakshmi, lord Viṣṇu. Hence, in the Jnana-kāṇḍa, which is the doctrinal foundation of Hinduism, Viṣṇu is stated to be the supreme Brahman, prior to and the creator of other deities including Śiva and Brahma, identified with the supreme Puruṣa even as per the Smārta commentators.

Smṛtis

Dharmaśāstras and Vedāṅgas

We come now to the Smṛtis, which not only further the conclusion derived from Śruti but also validate it, since Śruti is interpreted in light of Smṛti. Here "Smṛti" means the Dharmaśāstras, Vedāṅgas, and Itihāsas. The Purāṇas, though technically also Smṛti, are considered separately.

In the first chapter of the Manusmṛti (1.10-11), the creation of the world is described, and the identity of its cause is stated to be Nārāyaṇa:

Manusmṛti 1.10–11

आपो नारा इति प्रोक्ता आपो वै नरसूनवः । ता यदस्यायनं पूर्वं तेन नारायणः स्मृतः ॥ १.१० ॥
यत्तत्कारणमव्यक्तं नित्यं सदसदात्मकम् । तद्विसृष्टः स पुरुषो लोके ब्रह्मेति कीर्त्यते ॥ १.११ ॥

Water is called nara, water being the offspring of Nara. Since water was the original residence (ayana) of that being, he is therefore called Nārāyaṇa. That unmanifest and eternal cause, partaking of the nature of both the existent and non-existent, produced the being called Brahma among people.

Nārāyaṇa here is identified with Lord Viṣṇu, the eternal cause and creator of Brahma.

Even in the Vedāṅgas, Viṣṇu is described as the original and supreme deity. For example, the Brhat Parashara Hora Śāstra, attributed to Parashara, father of Vyāsa, opens by answering how the universe is created and dissolved (1.9-11):

Bṛhat-Parāśara-Horā-Śāstra 1.9–11

कथं सृष्टिरियं जाता जगतश्च लयः कथम् ।
एकोऽव्यक्तात्मको विष्णुरनादिः प्रभुरीश्वरः । शुद्धसत्त्वो जगत्स्वामी निर्गुणस्त्रिगुणान्वितः ॥
संसारकारकः श्रीमान्निमित्तात्मा प्रतापवान् । एकांशेन जगत्सर्वं सृजत्यवति लीलया ॥
त्रिपादं तस्य देवस्य ह्यमृतं तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥

Viṣṇu is the unmanifest one, the lord and deity, master of the world, of the nature of pure sattva, ultimately devoid of guṇas yet endowed with them (as per his will), the creator of the universe, the husband of Lakshmi, the cause. By a single fraction of his he creates and preserves the world as a lila, his three padas are the nectar of those who truly see.

Śiva and Brahma are described separately in the subsequent verses (1.20), without such absolute language.

Bṛhat-Parāśara-Horā-Śāstra 1.20

भूशक्त्या सृजते ब्रह्मा नीलशक्त्या शिवोऽत्ति हि ॥

Endowed with Bhushakti, Brahma creates, endowed with Nilashakti, Śiva destroys.

Thus, even in the Dharmaśāstras and Vedāṅgas, which are focused on very different topics from doctrinal theology, Viṣṇu is still consistently upheld as the supreme, while Brahma and Śiva are mentioned as subservient.

Rāmāyaṇa

Near the very beginning of the Rāmāyaṇa, the supremacy of Viṣṇu is shown when all the deities, including Śiva, approach Him in prayer (1.15.32):

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bālakāṇḍa 1.15.32

तदा देवर्षिगन्धर्वाः सरुद्राः साप्सरोगणाः । स्तुतिभिर्दिव्यरूपाभिस्तुष्टुवुर्मधुसूदनम् ॥

Then all the Devas Rishis and gandharvas, including Śiva, along with all the apsaras and respective followers, began to worship Viṣṇu with divine eulogies.

Another instance occurs near the end of the first kāṇḍa, where Parashurama-himself a great devotee of Śiva-narrates how the deities once disputed whether Śiva or Viṣṇu was supreme (1.74.14-16):

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bālakāṇḍa 1.74.14–16

तदा तु देवताः सर्वाः पृच्छन्ति स्म पितामहम् । शितिकण्ठस्य विष्णोश्च बलाबलनिरीक्षया ॥
अभिप्रायं तु विज्ञाय देवतानां पितामहः । विरोधं जनयामास तयोः सत्यवतां वरः ॥
विरोधे तु महद्युद्धमभवद्रोमहर्षणम् । शितिकण्ठस्य विष्णोश्च परस्परजयैषिणोः ॥

Then all the deities began to ask Brahma to test the relative superiority of the blue throated Śiva and Viṣṇu. Having known this purpose of the deities, he created a dispute between them. From the dispute a great battle commenced between the blue throated Śiva and Viṣṇu, each desirous of victory over the other.

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bālakāṇḍa 1.74.17–19

तदा तु जृम्भितं शैवं धनुरभीमपराक्रमम् । हुंकारेण महादेवः स्तम्भितोऽथ त्रिलोचनः ॥
देवैस्तदा समागम्य सर्षिसङ्घः सचारणैः । याचितौ प्रशमं तत्र जग्मतुस्तौ सुरोत्तमौ ॥
जृम्भितं तद्धनुर्दृष्ट्वा शैवं विष्णुपराक्रमैः । अधिकं मेनिरे विष्णुं देवाः सर्षिगणास्तदा ॥

Then, Śiva's bow, of terrifying exploits, was broken by the mere roaring of Viṣṇu, and the three-eyed Mahadeva himself was stunned. Then all the deities along with the Rishis and caranas came and beseeched both of them for peace, whereby they both, best of the deities, left. Having seen the bow of Śiva broken by the valour of Viṣṇu, the deities along with all the Rishis accepted Viṣṇu as superior.

Thus, the deities and Rishis themselves concluded regarding their doubt on the superiority of the two deities, that it is Viṣṇu who is supreme. The commentaries on this passage should also be seen:

Commentary by Maheśvara Tirtha Tattvadīpikā

Maheśvara Tirtha Tattvadīpikā on the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa Balakanda 1.74

जृम्भितमिति । जृम्भितं विष्णुपराक्रमैर्जडीकृतम्, धनुः शितिकण्ठं च दृष्ट्वा सकलजगद्व्यापकं विष्णुं शितिकण्ठपराक्रमैः ज्ञानशक्त्यादिभिश्च अधिकं सर्वोत्तमं मेनिरे

Seeing the bow and Śiva himself made inert by the valour of Viṣṇu, they accepted the all-pervading Viṣṇu as superior to Śiva in both power and knowledge, i.e., the supreme amongst all (sarvottama).

A more detailed commentary, addressing an objection of the Śaivas and Smārtas, is by Govindarāja:

Commentary by Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa)

Govindarāja, Bhūṣaṇa commentary on Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bālakāṇḍa 1.74

आधिक्यावबोधेन न पक्षपातमूलमित्याह सर्षिगणा इति । अतीन्द्रियार्थद्रष्टार ऋषयः । न तत् ज्ञानं न पक्षपातमूलं न वा भ्रान्तिमूलमिति भावः ।

If it be said that the acceptance of Viṣṇu as superior arose from bias or ignorance, that is not so, because the text says 'along with the rshis.' The rshis perceive what lies beyond the senses; their conclusion can therefore be neither biased nor erroneous.

It is not actually the narrative itself which is the most important point of this passage, but the fact that the Rishis, who can never err nor be biased, concluded based on this that Viṣṇu is superior.

Govindarāja, Bhūṣaṇa commentary on Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bālakāṇḍa 1.74

एतेन “एक एव रुद्रो न द्वितीयाय तस्थे” इत्यादिश्रुतिः “नकिरिन्द्र त्वदुत्तरः” इत्यादिवदितरापेक्षया श्रेष्ठत्वाभिप्राया । “एको ह वै नारायण आसीत्” इत्यादिश्रुत्युक्तो नारायणापरपर्यायो विष्णुरेव परं तत्त्वमित्यभिहितम् ॥

By this it should be understood that śruti passages such as 'Rudra is one; there is no second to him' are to be read like 'O Indra, there is none superior to you': they state superiority over others within the deity's own domain. By contrast, the śruti 'Nārāyaṇa alone existed' establishes the supreme reality as Viṣṇu, another name of Nārāyaṇa.

The Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad passage is examined more fully in the objections section. Here Govindarāja explains that praises of Śiva and Indra express relative superiority within their respective domains, whereas the Mahopanishad establishes Viṣṇu's absolute superiority by excluding Śiva and the other deities. The Rāmāyaṇa then confirms Viṣṇu's superiority over Śiva.

The Rāmāyaṇa further states Rāma's (i.e., Viṣṇu's) superiority in terms of power:

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Sundarakāṇḍa 5.51.45

ब्रह्मा स्वयंभूश्चतुराननो वा रुद्रस्त्रिणेत्रस्त्रिपुरान्तको वा । इन्द्रो महेन्द्रः सुरनायको वा त्रातुं न शक्तो युधि रामवध्यम् ॥

Neither the four-faced self-born Brahma, nor the three-eyed Rudra, the killer of the demon tripura, nor Indra, the great king of the deities, can save the one who Rāma wishes to slay in battle

Near the end of the Rāmāyaṇa, when Rāma slays Ravana, all the deities approach him in prayer again and eulogise him as Viṣṇu, where he is specifically stated to be Brahman (6.105.12-14):

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddhakāṇḍa 6.105.12–14

भवान्नारायणो देवः श्रीमांश्चक्रायुधो विभुः । एकशृङ्गो वराहस्त्वं भूतभव्यसपत्नजित् ॥
अक्षरं ब्रह्म सत्यं च मध्ये चान्ते च राघव । लोकानां त्वं परो धर्मो विष्वक्सेनश्चतुर्भुजः ॥
शार्ङ्गधन्वा हृषीकेशः पुरुषः पुरुषोत्तमः ॥

You are the glorious deity Nārāyaṇa, the husband of Lakshmi, having the Cakra as your weapon. You are the one-tusked Varaha, victor over your past, present and future foes. You are the imperishable Brahman, the truly real. The greatest dharma in all the realms, you are the four-armed Vishvaksena, wielder of the bow, master of your senses, you are Puruṣa and Purushottama.

Thus, the Rāmāyaṇa consistently describes Viṣṇu as being the supreme deity worshipped by the other deities, being recognised as superior to Śiva by the Rishis and deities, more powerful than Śiva, Brahma and Indra, and directly identified as the supreme Brahman.

Mahābhārata

One does not need to look very far in the Mahābhārata to find the praise of Viṣṇu, in fact the very first word of the entire hundred thousand verse epic is "Nārāyaṇa", the first one saluted in the opening benedictions. In that same opening chapter (1.1.22-24) he is praised further, and identified as Brahman:

Mahābhārata 1.1.22-24

आद्यं पुरुषमीशानं पुरुहूतं पुरुष्टुतम् । ऋतमेकाक्षरं ब्रह्म व्यक्ताव्यक्तं सनातनम् ॥
असच्च सच्चैव च यद्विश्वं सदसतः परम् । परावराणां स्रष्टारं पुराणं परमव्ययम् ॥
मङ्गल्यं मङ्गलं विष्णुं वरेण्यमनघं शुचिम् । नमस्कृत्य हृषीकेशं चराचरगुरुं हरिम् ॥

Having saluted Sri Hari Viṣṇu, the primal puruṣa, the deity, the one worshipped and offered to by men, the one true imperishable eternal Brahman, who is both manifest and unmanifest, asat and sat, yet also beyond sat and asat, who is the whole world, creator of all, the ancient, the supreme unending one, the auspicious and the cause of auspiciousness, the worshippable, the flawless, the pure, the master of his senses, the master of the mobile and immobile beings, I shall narrate the entire view of the great Rshi, the venerable high-souled Vyāsa, of unmatched brilliance.

Conversation between Brahma and Rudra

It is generally understood that of the 3 deities of the Trimurti, it is either Śiva or Viṣṇu or both who are supreme, and Brahma is lower. However, in the Mahābhārata, Brahma is actually superior to Śiva and is stated to be his father, Mahābhārata 12.338.11-12

Mahābhārata 12.338.11-12

अथ तत्रासतस्तस्य चतुर्वक्त्रस्य धीमतः । ललाटप्रभवः पुत्रः शिव आगाद्यदृच्छया ॥

Earlier, while the intelligent four-headed Brahma was seated on the mountain, his forehead-born son-the three-eyed Śiva, lord of yogins-came from heaven before him and lovingly saluted his feet.

What follows is a conversation between the two, where Śiva enquires Brahma regarding who the supreme being is (12.338.22-25)

Śiva enquired

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.338.22–23

रुद्र उवाच । बहवः पुरुषा ब्रह्मंस्त्वया सृष्टाः स्वयम्भुवा । सृज्यन्ते चापरे ब्रह्मन् स चैकः पुरुषो विराट् ॥
को ह्यसौ चिन्त्यते ब्रह्मंस्त्वया वै पुरुषोत्तमः । एतन्मे संशयं ब्रूहि महत्कौतूहलं हि मे ॥

There are many beings, O Brahma, which are created by you, who are self born. But there is also the supreme Purusa. Who is that one, that even you meditate upon, that purusottama? This is my doubt, answer me this, for it is my great curiosity.

Brahma answered

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.338.24–25

ब्रह्मोवाच ॥
बहवः पुरुषाः पुत्र ये त्वया समुदाहृताः । एवमेतदतिक्रान्तं दृश्यं नैवमित्यपि ॥
आधारं तु प्रवक्ष्यामि एकस्य पुरुषस्य ते । बहूनां पुरुषाणां स यथैका योनिरुच्यते ॥

There are many beings, son, as you have said. But there is something beyond this and it is not limited to this, thus should be understood. I shall narrate unto you the basis of that singular Purusa, who is said to be the origin of the many beings.

Thus it leads into the next chapter:

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.339

शृणु पुत्र यथा ह्येष पुरुषः शाश्वतोऽव्ययः । अक्षयश्चाप्रमेयश्च सर्वगश्च निरुच्यते ॥
न स शक्यस्त्वया द्रष्टुं मयान्यैर्वापि सत्तम । सगुणो निर्गुणो विश्वो ज्ञानदृश्यो ह्यसौ स्मृतः ॥
अशरीरः शरीरेषु सर्वेषु निवसत्यसौ । वसन्नपि शरीरेषु न स लिप्यति कर्मभिः ॥
ममात्मा तव च ये चान्ये देहसंस्थिताः । सर्वेषां साक्षिभूतश्च न ग्राह्यः केनचित्क्वचित् ॥

Listen, son, of this eternal and undying Purusa. He is said to be imperishable, unknowable (by senses), pervading everything. You cannot see him, neither me nor others, O best of the righteous. He is saguna yet also nirguna, he is complete, visible by knowledge is he said to be. He is present in the bodies of all though bodiless, and though residing so in bodies, he is not tainted by karma. He is my own antaratman, as also yours, as also of others. He is the witness in all, not graspable by anyone in any way.

The chapter continues thus, describing the greatness of this Purusa. Finally at the end, Brahma reveals his identity (Verse 17):

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.339.17

स हि नारायणो ज्ञेयः सर्वात्मा पुरुषो हि सः । न लिप्यते फलैश्चापि पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा ॥ १७ ॥

He that Purusa is to be Known as Nārāyaṇa alone, who does not get tainted by fruits as a lotus petal is not tainted by the muddy water.

Thus, upon being enquired regarding the supreme being by Śiva, Brahma says it is none other than Nārāyaṇa alone. Notably, the supreme Puruṣa described here cannot be only a nirguna being, as argue the Smārtas, for he is explicitly stated to be both Saguna and nirguna. His presence in the material world is compared to the lotus, which, though blooming in muddy water, is not tainted by it.

Viṣṇu's supremacy stated in Shanti Parva

Another key passage occurs in the Shanti Parva, Mahābhārata 12.350.11-18:

Mahābhārata 12.350.11-18

नमोऽतियशसे तस्मै देहिनां परमात्मने । नारायणाय विश्वाय निर्गुणाय गुणात्मने ॥
यस्य प्रसादजो ब्रह्मा रुद्रश्च क्रोधसम्भवः । योऽसौ योनिर्हि सर्वस्य स्थावरस्य चरस्य च ॥
तदादेशितपन्थानौ सृष्टिसंहारकारकौ । निमित्तमात्रं तावत्र सर्वप्राणिवरप्रदौ ॥

Salutations to that greatly glorious one, the supreme self of all embodied beings: Nārāyaṇa, the universe, nirguna yet the ground of the guṇas. By His grace Brahma was born, and from His wrath Rudra; He is the source of every moving and unmoving being." The passage continues: "From Him came sacrifice and the sacrificer, the supreme Puruṣa called Aniruddha, creator and destroyer of the worlds. At the close of Brahma's night a lotus arises from His grace, and Brahma is born from it. From His anger a son named Rudra arises from the forehead for the work of destruction. Brahma and Rudra, born respectively from grace and wrath, carry out creation and dissolution under Nārāyaṇa's command and are merely His instruments.

Objections

The objection is that Brahma's son might be a different, inferior Rudra rather than the deity worshipped as supreme by Śaivas.

Answer: Not so, for this same section describes this said Rudra in the subsequent verses:

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.350.18

कपर्दी जटिलो मुण्डः श्मशानगृहसेवकः । उग्रव्रतधरो रुद्रो योगी त्रिपुरदारणः । दक्षक्रतुहरश्चैव भगनेत्रहरस्तथा ॥

Wearing matted hair, residing in crematoriums, having fierce vows, the Yogi Rudra is the slayer of the demon Tripura, the destroyer of the sacrifice of Dakṣa, and the one who tore an eye of Bhaga.

Unless the Śaivas wish to argue that the slayer of Tripura and the destroyer of Dakṣa’s sacrifice is not the one they worship as supreme, it is beyond reasonable doubt that it is the same one.

Kṛṣṇa's statement outside the Gītā

Aside from the testimony of others to Viṣṇu's supremacy, Kṛṣṇa Himself repeatedly asserts it throughout the Mahābhārata. The most famous example is the Bhagavad Gītā, and another statement appears outside it:

Mahābhārata 14.118.37-38

रुद्रं समाश्रिता देवा रुद्रो ब्रह्माणमाश्रितः । ब्रह्मा मामाश्रितो राजन्नाहं कञ्चिदुपाश्रितः ॥
ममाश्रयो न कश्चित्तु सर्वेषामाश्रयो ह्यहम् ॥

All the other deities seek refuge in Rudra; Rudra seeks refuge in Brahma; Brahma seeks refuge in Me. I, O king, seek refuge in no one. No one is My refuge, and I am the refuge of all.

The hierarchy that was implicit in the conversation between Śiva and Brahma, viz other deities < Śiva < Brahma < Viṣṇu, is here stated explicitly by Kṛṣṇa. This is another example of not just relative superiority, but absolute superiority, which the Smārtas cannot reinterpret by their framework of all deities being the same, because not only is Viṣṇu (as Kṛṣṇa) stated to be supreme, but Śiva and Brahma are explicitly stated to be inferior and dependent on him.

The Bhagavad Gītā

We come now to the crest-jewel of the Mahābhārata, the Bhagavad Gītā, where Kṛṣṇa elaborately describes his own supremacy. Before analysing Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu's supremacy in the Gītā, we must address a claim circulating on the internet, that it is in fact Śiva who is speaking through Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā. Though it is sometimes claimed that Abhinavagupta or some other Śaiva Ācārya has claimed this, they have not. This claim is entirely a modern imagination. The claim, lacking any support from scripture or even from the Śaiva Ācāryas, may be discarded there and then. Even still I shall highlight some points on how it is impossible.

1. As seen above, the Gītā is not the only place where Kṛṣṇa states His supremacy; He does so in the Shanti and Ashvamedhika Parvans as well. The Bhagavad Gītā is therefore not an exceptional case requiring the claim that another deity speaks through Him.

Bhagavad Gītā 18.74

इत्यहं वासुदेवस्य पार्थस्य च महात्मनः । संवादमिममश्रौषमद्भुतं रोमहर्षणम् ॥
व्यासप्रसादाच्छ्रुतवानेतद्गुह्यमहं परम् । योगं योगेश्वरात्कृष्णात्साक्षात्कथयतः स्वयम् ॥

Thus have I heard this wondrous and thrilling dialogue between Vasudeva and the great-souled Partha. Through Vyāsa's grace I heard this supreme and secret yoga directly from Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of Yoga, as He Himself declared it.

Sanjaya therefore identifies the speaker unmistakably as Kṛṣṇa, not Śiva. Sanjaya's vision was granted by Vyāsa and enabled him to see even the universal form; Kṛṣṇa also identifies Himself as Vasudeva elsewhere in the Gītā.

Kṛṣṇa's statements in the Gītā

The Gītā's statements of Kṛṣṇa's supremacy include these passages: Kṛṣṇa identifies Himself as the origin of all, as unsurpassed, and presents worship of Him as superior to worship directed elsewhere.

Bhagavad Gītā 10.8

अहं सर्वस्य प्रभवो मत्तः सर्वं प्रवर्तते । इति मत्वा भजन्ते मां बुधा भावसमन्विताः ॥

I am the origin of all; everything proceeds from Me. Knowing this, the wise worship Me with devotion.

Bhagavad Gītā 10.2

न मे विदुः सुरगणाः प्रभवं न महर्षयः । अहमादिर्हि देवानां महर्षीणां च सर्वशः ॥

Neither the hosts of gods nor the great rshis know My origin, for I am in every way the source of the gods and the great rshis.

Śaṅkara glosses "the gods" as "Brahma and the others."

The suffix "-adi," meaning "and others," ordinārīly indicates a familiar list beginning with the named member. Examples include bhvadi and adadi lists of grammatical roots, bhrgvadi for a list of rshis, and simhadi for animals. The familiar list "Brahmadi" must therefore be identified.

Nārada Purāṇa 2.55.96

ब्रह्मरुद्रादयः सुराः ॥

The gods beginning with Brahma and Rudra.

The Narada Purāṇa (2.55.96) gives the expression "the gods beginning with Brahma and Rudra." Kṛṣṇa's statement thus includes Brahma and Rudra among the deities who do not know His origin. Kṛṣṇa further declares without exception:

Nārada Purāṇa 2.55.96

मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय । मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव ॥

There is nothing whatsoever superior to Me, O Arjuna; all this is strung on Me like pearls on a thread." - Bhagavad Gītā 7.7

Aside from his own supremacy, Kṛṣṇa also states in detail the supremacy of his worship over the worship of other deities (9.23):

Bhagavad Gītā 9.23

येऽप्यन्यदेवता भक्ता यजन्ते श्रद्धयान्विताः । तेऽपि मामेव कौन्तेय यजन्त्यविधिपूर्वकम् ॥

Even those devotees who worship other deities with faith also worship Me, O son of Kunti, but they do so avidhipurvakam-without the proper method." - Bhagavad Gītā 9.23

Some Śaiva ācāryas, notably Abhinavagupta, interpret avidhipurvakam as anya-vidhi-purvakam, "by another method." Śaṅkarācārya instead glosses the phrase: the worship is "without the proper procedure because of ignorance."

Bhagavad Gītā 9.25

यान्ति देवव्रता देवान् पितॄन् यान्ति पितृव्रताः । भूतानि यान्ति भूतेज्या यान्ति मद्याजिनोऽपि माम् ॥

Worshippers of the gods go to the gods; worshippers of the ancestors go to the ancestors; worshippers of other beings go to those beings; My worshippers come to Me." - Bhagavad Gītā 9.25

Śaṅkara explains "other beings" as Vinayaka (Ganesha), the Mothers or goddesses, the "four sisters," and others. Those who worship them attain those beings; Kṛṣṇa's own worshippers-the Vaiṣṇavas-attain Him. This means that worship directed elsewhere through ignorance has a lesser and finite fruit, while devotion to Kṛṣṇa has unending fruit.

This is not the only time Śaṅkara has named a deity and stated its worship to be inferior. See 6.47:

Bhagavad Gītā 6.47

योगिनामपि सर्वेषां मद्गतेनान्तरात्मना । श्रद्धावान्भजते यो मां स मे युक्ततमो मतः ॥

Amongst all the Yogis also, the one who is full of devotion, focused on me with his very soul, and worships me thus, he is set to be best of the engrossed (Or best of the Yogis)

Śaṅkara, Bhagavad Gītā Bhāṣya 6.47

योगिनामपि सर्वेषां रुद्रादित्यादिध्यानपराणां मध्ये मद्गतेन मयि वासुदेवे समाहितेन अन्तरात्मना अन्तःकरणेन श्रद्धावान् श्रद्धाधानः सन् भजते सेवते यो मां स मे मम युक्ततमः

Amongst all the Yogīs, i.e., amongst the worshippers of Rudra, the sun deity (Āditya), etc, it is the one focused on me, i.E Vasudeva (the son of Vasudeva), fully focused with his very soul or consciousness, full of devotion, who then worships me thus, who is said to be best of the engrossed.

Thus, though the Smārtas today worship 5 deities as supreme, in his own Gītā bhasya, Sankara has called the worship of the four except Viṣṇu, viz, Ganesa and the goddesses in 9.25 and Rudra and the Sun deity in 6.47 to be inferior, and explicitly stated that the Vaiṣṇavas, the ones who worship the son of Vasudeva, are supreme.

Purāṇas

Meta-analysis of the Purāṇas

ॐ नमो भगवते वासुदेवाय ॥

The final category of scripture is the Purāṇas. Unlike the Vedas, Dharmaśāstras, Vedāṅgas, Rāmāyaṇa, and Mahābhārata, they have clear sectarian divisions. There are Śaiva Purāṇas such as the Liṅga, Skanda, and Śiva Purāṇas; Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas such as the Bhagavata, Garuda, and Viṣṇu Purāṇas; and texts devoted to other deities, each praising its own subject as supreme. Several Vaiṣṇava arguments seek to reconcile this diversity.

Difference of scope

Each Purāṇa begins with sages approaching a wise narrator-such as Shuka, Parashara, or Romaharshana-and asking questions. The Purāṇa is narrated as an answer. Their opening questions differ:

Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas

(I) Garuda Purāṇa 1.1. The sages ask: "Who among the deities is the supreme Lord? Who alone is worshippable? Who is to be meditated upon? Who creates the world, who preserves it, and who destroys it?"

सूत. जानासि सर्वं त्वं पृच्छामस्त्वामतो वयम् । देवतानां हि को देव ईश्वरः पूज्य एव कः । को ध्येयः को जगत्स्रष्टा जगत्पाति च हन्ति कः ।

(Ii) Padma Purāṇa 1.1. They ask how the primordial lotus arose, how Brahma was born from it, and how he performed creation.

कथं पद्मं समुत्पन्नं ब्रह्म तत्र कथं न्यभूत् । प्रादुर्भूतेन कथं सृष्टिः कृता तां तु तथा वद । एवं पृष्टस्ततस्तांस्तु प्रत्युवाच शुभां गिरम् ।

(Iii) Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.1. The question concerns how the world arose and will arise again, in whom the moving and unmoving universe depends, from whom it came, and in whom and how it dissolves.

सोऽहमिच्छामि धर्मज्ञ श्रोतुं त्वत्तो यथा जगत् । बभूव भूयश्च यथा महाभाग भविष्यति ॥ ४ ॥ यन्मयं च जगद्ब्रह्मन्यतश्चैव चराचरम् । लीनमासीद्यथा यत्र लयमेष्यति यत्र च ॥ ५ ॥

These questions are general: they concern the nature, creation, preservation, and dissolution of the world, and the identity of the supreme deity or being.

Śaiva Purāṇas

(I) Liṅga Purāṇa 1.1. The sages specifically request the divine Purāṇa "endowed with praise of the Śiva-liṅga."

(Ii) Skanda Purāṇa 1.1. The ṛṣis ask for the greatness of the trident-bearing Devadeva, including his meditation and worship.

ऋषय ऊचुः । कथयस्व महाप्राज्ञ देवदेवस्य शूलिनः । महिमानं महाभाग ध्यानार्चनसमन्वितम् ।

(Iii) Śiva Purāṇa 2.1. They request the auspicious forms and divine deeds of Śiva and Śivā (Pārvatī).

इदानीं कथय प्राज्ञ शिवरूपमनुत्तमम् । दिव्यानि वै चरित्राणि शिवयोश्च विशेषतः ॥ २१ ॥

The opening questions of the Śaiva Purāṇas are specific: Śiva or the liṅga is expressly named. This suggests that statements identifying Śiva with Brahman function as praise of the stated subject, while Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas answer general questions about Brahman and the origin of the world by identifying the supreme with Viṣṇu.

Thus identification of Brahman with Viṣṇu is primary and taken as a matter of fact, whereas for Śiva and others it is only for praise as subject matter. This is in fact stated in the Agni Purāṇa, which is neither Śaiva nor Vaiṣṇava (Chapter 379.9-12):

Agni Purāṇa 379.9

वेदादिविद्या ह्यपरमक्षरं ब्रह्म तत्परम् । तदेतद्भगवद्वाच्यमुपचारेऽर्चनेऽन्यतः ॥ ३७९.९ ॥

Even the knowledge of Vedas is said to be ‘lower’ and the imperishable Brahman is ‘higher’ than it. This word ‘Brahman’ is principally used only for Bhagavān (the supreme lord), and for other (deities) as a courtesy or in worship.

Then it describes what "Bhagavān" (one who has Bhaga) means:

Agni Purāṇa 379.10–11

संहर्तेति तथा भर्ता भकारोऽर्थद्वयान्वितः । नेता गमयिता स्रष्टा गकारोऽयं महामुने ॥ ३७९.१० ॥
ऐश्वर्यस्य समग्रस्य वीर्यस्य यशसः श्रियः । ज्ञानवैराग्ययोश्चैव षण्णां भग इतीङ्गना ॥ ३७९.११ ॥

‘Bha’ means one who is the destroyer or the sustainer, it has two meanings. Similarly ‘Ga’ means one who is the leader or creator, O sage. (Or) ‘Bhaga’ are the six glories, Majesty, Valour, Success, Wealth, Knowledge, renunciation and fame.

The passage then asks who this "Bhagavān" is and identifies the name principally with Viṣṇu:

Agni Purāṇa 379.12

वसन्ति विष्णौ भूतानि स च धातुस्त्रिधात्मकः । एवं हरौ हि भगवान् शब्दोऽन्यत्रोपचारतः ॥ ३७९.१२ ॥

In Viṣṇu all beings are situated, he is the support, being three-natured. Thus for Hari Viṣṇu alone is this word ‘Bhagavān’ (and thereby ‘Brahman’) principally to be used, and for others as a courtesy.

Thus, "Brahman" or "Bhagavān" are used primarily and actually for Viṣṇu, and for others as a courtesy. Accordingly, when a general question is asked about Brahman, the answer glorifies Viṣṇu. These terms may be used as courtesy or for worship for other deities, as when such questions about specific deities are asked in non-Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas. Thus the statements of Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas are treated as plain doctrinal facts, while those of others glorify the subject matter.

Guṇa-classification of Purāṇas

Another approach to understanding the contradictory nature of the Purāṇas with regard to the supremacy of a particular deity is the guṇa-classification of Purāṇas, argued by Rāmānuja and others. The complete classification is given in Padma Purāṇa 6.236.18-20:

Padma Purāṇa 6.236.18-20

मात्स्यं कौर्मं तथा लैङ्गं शैवं स्कान्दं तथैव च । आग्नेयं च षडेतानि तामसानि निबोध मे ॥
वैष्णवं नारदीयं च तथा भागवतं शुभम् । गारुडं च तथा पाद्मं वाराहं शुभदर्शने ॥
सात्त्विकानि पुराणानि विज्ञेयानि शुभानि वै ॥

Matsya, Kurma, Liṅga, Śiva, Skanda, and Agni-these are known as tāmasa Purāṇas. Viṣṇu, Narada, Bhagavata, Garuda, Padma, and Varaha are known to be sāttvika and auspicious, O beautiful one. The Brahma Purāṇas are known as rajasa. The sāttvika grant moksha, whereas the rajasa are generally auspicious.

Objections

The objection is that a Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa cannot by itself decide the status of all other Purāṇas.

Answer: Surely, but this classification is corroborated in the Śaiva and neutral Purāṇas as well.

The same classification is nevertheless stated in Śaiva and comparatively neutral Purāṇas. The Skanda Purāṇa 7.1.2.87 says:

An almost identical statement occurs in Matsya Purāṇa 56.68.

सात्त्विकेषु च कल्पेषु माहात्म्यमधिकं हरेः । राजसेषु च माहात्म्यमधिकं ब्रह्मणो विदुः ।
तद्वदग्नेश्च माहात्म्यं तामसेषु शिवस्य च ॥

In the sāttvika Purāṇas the greatness of Hari predominates. In the rajasa Purāṇas, Brahma's greatness is prominent; in the tāmasa Purāṇas, the greatness of Agni and Śiva. In mixed Purāṇas the greatness of Sarasvati and the Pitrs is narrated.

Objections

The objection asks whether the labels sāttvika, rajasa, and tāmasa necessarily establish a hierarchy of doctrinal authority.

Answer: It does, for such a meaning is clear not only from the lexical meanings of the words (sattva itself means goodness and tamas means darkness or ignorance), but is in fact clarified by Śiva himself in the Varāha Purāṇa (70:20–24).

This is suggested by the ordinary meanings of the words-sattva denotes goodness, while tamas denotes darkness or ignorance-and is stated explicitly by Śiva in the Varaha Purāṇa 70.20-24:

Varāha Purāṇa 70.20–24

सत्त्वं रजस्तम इति गुणास्त्रय उदाहृताः । सत्त्वेन मुच्यते जन्तुः सत्त्वं नारायणात्मकम् ॥

Sattva, rajas, and tamas are the three guṇas. Through pure sattva a being is liberated, and sattva is of the nature of Nārāyaṇa. Sattva mixed with rajas is associated with Brahma. Practices outside the Vedic fold and undertaken while disregarding scripture are associated with Rudra and are called inferior; they are pure tamas, lacking even rajas.

Whichever method is used-analysis of the Purāṇas' scope or their guṇa-classification-both lines of reasoning reach the same conclusion: Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas possess superior authority.

Statements of the Purāṇas

I. Tāmasa Purāṇas

The Śaiva Purāṇas praise Śiva because He is their subject, yet in many passages they also acknowledge Viṣṇu as ultimately supreme, even over Śiva.

The Liṅga Purāṇa acknowledges Viṣṇu as the supreme lord and creator of Brahma and Śiva, stating:

Liṅga Purāṇa 2.1.74

यः स परः पुरुषः परमात्मा स जनार्दनः । तस्माद् ब्रह्मा ततः रुद्रस्तथा सर्वं जगत् ॥

The one who is the supreme Puruṣa, the supreme soul, is Janardana. From him came Brahma, from him Rudra, and in that manner the entire world.

Similarly, the Skanda Purāṇa describes the primordial Vedic Puruṣa, Nārāyaṇa, as manifested by the impulse of Vasudeva:

Skanda Purāṇa 2.9.24.11-13

प्रेरिता वासुदेवेन स्वस्वांशैरैश्वरं वपुः ।
अजीजनन्विराट्संज्ञं ते चराचरसंश्रयम् ॥
स च वैराजपुरुषः स्वसृष्टास्वप्स्वशेत यत् ।
तेन नारायण इति प्रोच्यते निगमादिभिः ॥
तन्नाभिपद्माद्ब्रह्मासीद्राजसोऽथ हृदम्बुजात् ।
जज्ञे विष्णुः सत्त्वगुणो ललाटात्तामसो हरः ॥

By the impulse of Vasudeva there came to be the lordly Virat Puruṣa, the refuge of all things mobile and immobile. He lay upon the waters He Himself created and is therefore called Nārāyaṇa by the Vedas. From His navel came a lotus, from the lotus the rajasic Brahma, and from His forehead the tamasic Śiva.

Many modern recensions read "from whom Brahma and all others," while the reading explicitly mentioning Rudra is cited by Yamunācārya in the Āgama-pramanya.

Sāttvika Purāṇas

Garuda Purāṇa

The Garuda Purāṇa corroborates the Agni Purāṇa's claim that "Brahman" is used primarily for Viṣṇu and secondarily or honorifically for Brahma, Rudra, and others:

Lakshmi says: "You are supreme, superior to all other deities; there is none equal to or greater than You. You alone are Brahman-not four-faced Brahma, nor I, nor Rudra, nor Brhaspati. The word 'Brahman' applies primarily to Viṣṇu and secondarily to Brahma, Rudra, and the others."

Garuḍa Purāṇa 3.2.32

न ते समोऽस्त्यभ्यधिकः कुतोऽन्यो लोकत्रयेऽप्यप्रतिमप्रभाव ।
त्वं ब्रह्म एको न चतुर्मुखश्च नाहं रुद्रो न बृहस्पतिश्च । विष्णावेव ब्रह्मशब्दो हि मुख्यो ह्यन्येष्वमुख्यो ब्रह्मरुद्रादिकेषु ॥

In the beginning there was nothing besides Viṣṇu and Lakshmi.

किञ्चिदासीद्विना विष्णुमथ श्रियं च ॥

The same teaching appears in a hymn by Shesha:

Garuḍa Purāṇa 3.6.44

शेष उवाच । नाहं च जाने तव पादमूलं रुद्रो न वेत्ति गरुडो न वेद ॥

I do not know even the base of Your feet; neither Rudra nor Garuda knows it.

The Garuda Purāṇa also gives a particularly clear account of the status of Brahma and Śiva in Vaiṣṇava theology:

Garuḍa Purāṇa 3.4.4-9

अन्तरं ये विजानन्ति ते यान्त्यन्धन्तमः परम् । पुरुषस्तु त्रिरूपोऽभूद्विष्णुर्ब्रह्मा भवेतिसः ॥
सत्त्वेन लोकान् वर्धयितुं विष्णुः साक्षाद्धरिः स्वयम् । सृष्टिं कर्तुं च रजसा ब्रह्मणि प्राविशद्धरिः ॥
तमसापि समाहन्तुं रुद्रे च प्राविशद्धरिः । रुद्रे स्थितो रुद्रसंज्ञो न रुद्रस्तु हरिः स्वयम् ॥
विष्णुरेव हरिः साक्षात्तावुभौ न हरी स्मृतौ । आविष्टरूपौ विज्ञेयौ ब्रह्मरुद्राभिधायकौ ॥

The primordial Puruṣa took on three forms: Viṣṇu, Brahma and Śiva. For preservation through sattva He is directly Viṣṇu, Hari Himself. For creation He enters Brahma through rajas, and for destruction He enters Rudra through tamas. Situated in Rudra, Hari is called Rudra, but Rudra is not Hari Himself. Viṣṇu alone is directly Hari; Brahma and Rudra are His indwelt forms.

The passage presents a middle position: Brahma and Rudra are neither wholly identical with Viṣṇu nor wholly separate, because Viṣṇu indwells them as their antaryamin.

Brahma Saṃhitā 5.45

क्षीरं यथा दधिविकारविशेषयोगात् सञ्जायते न हि ततः पृथगस्ति हेतोः । यः शम्भुतामपि तथा समुपैति कार्याद् गोविन्दमादिपुरुषं तमहं भजामि ॥

Just as milk is transformed into curd through a particular process and is neither entirely separate nor simply identical with it, so Govinda, the primeval Puruṣa, assumes the state of Shambhu for a particular function.

Garuḍa Purāṇa 2.50.72

यथा सुराणां प्रवरो जनार्दनो यथायुधानां प्रवरः सुदर्शनम् । यथाश्वमेधः प्रवरः क्रतूनां भक्तेषु तथैव रुद्रः ॥

Just as Viṣṇu is supreme among all deities, just as Sudarshana is foremost among weapons, and the Ashvamedha among sacrifices, so Rudra is foremost among the devotees.

Bhagavata Purāṇa

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.13.16

निम्नगानां यथा गङ्गा देवानामच्युतो यथा । वैष्णवानां यथा शम्भुः पुराणानामिदं तथा ॥

As the Ganga is foremost among rivers, as Viṣṇu is foremost among deities, and as Śiva is foremost among the devotees of Viṣṇu, so this Purāṇa is foremost among Purāṇas." - Bhagavata Purāṇa 12.13.16

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 2.5.15

नारायणपरा वेदा देवा नारायणाङ्गजाः । नारायणपरा लोका नारायणपरा मखाः ॥

The Vedas are centred upon Nārāyaṇa; the deities arise from Nārāyaṇa; the worlds are centred upon Nārāyaṇa; sacrifices are centred upon Nārāyaṇa.

The Bhagavata recounts Kṛṣṇa's battle with Banasura, during which Śiva fights on Bana's side and the Maheśvara fever is overcome by the Vaiṣṇava fever:

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.63.24, 34–35

माहेश्वरः समाक्रन्दन् वैष्णवेन बलार्दितः । अलब्ध्वाभयमन्यत्र भीतो माहेश्वरो ज्वरः । शरणार्थी हृषीकेशं तुष्टाव प्रयताञ्जलिः ॥
बाहुषु छिद्यमानेषु बाणस्य भगवान् भवः । भक्तानुकम्प्युपव्रज्य चक्रायुधमभाषत ॥
त्वं हि ब्रह्म परं ज्योतिर्गूढं ब्रह्मणि वाङ्मये । यं पश्यन्त्यमलात्मान आकाशमिव केवलम् ॥

Lord Śiva's fever was subdued by the might of Viṣṇu's own fever. Afraid and finding no refuge elsewhere, it approached Hrshikesha with folded hands. Seeing Bana's arms being cut off, Lord Bhava approached the wielder of the discus and said: 'You are the supreme Brahman, the hidden effulgence within the Vedic word.'

Objections

Answer: This is explained by the commentators of the Bhāgavata in the opening verses of this chapter:

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.63.6

बाणार्थे भगवान् रुद्रः ससुतः प्रमथैर्वृतः । आरुह्य नन्दिवृषभं युयुधे रामकृष्णयोः ॥ ६ ॥

Bhagavān Rudra, for Bāṇāsura, came with his son (Kārtikeya), accompanied by his attendants, riding on his bull, Nandi, to battle with Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa.

Here Śrīdhara, acceptable to both Vaiṣṇavas and Smārtas, comments:

Śrīdhara Svāmī, Bhāvārtha-dīpikā on Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.63.6

भगवानिति स्वयं सर्वज्ञोऽपि स्वपराभवेन बाणमन्यांश्च प्रति कृष्णमहिमानमबोधयदिति भावः ।

Bhagavān — the sense here is that Rudra, though himself having complete knowledge (one of the Bhagas), has come to show the greatness of Kṛṣṇa to Bāṇa and others by his own defeat.

Bhagavān means one who has Bhaga(s), the six glories. It is used primarily for the supreme Lord Viṣṇu, as he alone possesses all glories, but it may be used secondarily for others in the meaning of specific Bhagas. Here Śrīdhara says Bhagavān is used in the meaning of knowledge: Lord Śiva, though perfectly aware of the supremacy of Kṛṣṇa, still came to battle him merely to show to everyone Kṛṣṇa’s supremacy. This explanation works also for the story of the Rāmāyaṇa we saw earlier.

The Bhāgavatam also corroborates the creation sequence (Viṣṇu > Brahmā > Śiva) seen many times earlier (3.8.15):

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.8.15

तल्लोकपद्मं स उ एव विष्णुः प्रावीविशत्सर्वगुणावभासम् । तस्मिन् स्वयं वेदमयो विधाता स्वयंभुवं यं स्म वदन्ति सोऽभूत् ॥ १५ ॥

Viṣṇu himself entered that universal lotus (as an Antaryāmin), and born in it was the creator himself, whom they call the Self-born.

And in the subsequent chapters (3.12.7–12):

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.12.7–12

सद्योऽजायत तन्मन्युः कुमारो नीललोहितः ॥ ७ ॥
स वै रुरोद देवानां पूर्वजो भगवान्भवः ॥ ८ ॥
यदरोदीः सुरश्रेष्ठ सोद्वेग इव बालकः । ततस्त्वामभिधास्यन्ति नाम्ना रुद्र इति प्रजाः ॥ १० ॥
मन्युर्मनुर्महिनसो महान् शिव ऋतध्वजः । उग्ररेता भवः कालो वामदेवो धृतव्रतः ॥ १२ ॥

From the wrath of he (Brahmā) was born Lord Śiva. He, born earlier to the other deities, gave a great cry. (Brahmā said) ‘O great deity, you have cried (ruroda) like a child, therefore I shall give you the name “Rudra”. Your other names are: Manyu, Manu, Mahinasa, Mahān, Śiva, Ṛtadhvaja, Ugraretas, Bhava, Kāla, Vāmadeva and Dhṛtavrata.’

Varaha Purāṇa

Varāha Purāṇa 70.16 to 18

नारायणः परो देवः सत्त्वरूपो जनार्दनः । त्रिधात्मानं स भगवान् ससर्ज परमेश्वरः ॥
रजस्तमोभ्यां युक्तोऽभूद्रजः सत्त्वाधिकं विभुः । ससर्ज नाभिकमले ब्रह्माणं कमलासनम् ॥
रजसा तमसा युक्तः सोऽपि मां त्वसृजत् प्रभुः । यत्सत्त्वं स हरिर्देवो यो हरिस्तत्परं पदम् ॥

Śiva says: "Janardana Nārāyaṇa, whose form is sattva, is the supreme Lord. He manifested in three ways. Endowed with rajas mixed with sattva, He created Brahma from His navel-lotus; endowed with rajas and tamas, He created me. Pure sattva is Hari Himself, the supreme station.

Padma Purāṇa 5.97.27

येऽन्यदेवं परत्वेन वदन्त्यज्ञानमोहिताः । नारायणाज्जगन्नाथात्ते वै पाषण्डिनः स्मृताः ॥
यस्तु नारायणं देवं ब्रह्मरुद्रादिदैवतैः । सममन्यैर्निरीक्षेत स पाषण्डी भवेत्सदा ॥

Let the respective Purāṇas and Āgamas proclaim their own deity supreme for the delusion of the world. But in the proven conclusion of all scripture, and in the judgement of the discerning, Bhagavān Viṣṇu alone is established as supreme.

स्युर्मोहाय चराचरस्य जगतस्ते ते पुराणागमास्तां तामेव हि देवतां परमिकां जल्पन्तु कल्पे विधौ । सिद्धान्ते पुनरेक एव भगवान् विष्णुः समस्तागमव्यापारेषु विवेकिनां व्यतिकरं नीतेषु निश्चीयते ॥

While special attention has been given to valid objections in the main body, several further objections deserve separate treatment.

Objections

Objection I: If Viṣṇu alone is supreme, why do contrary scriptures and other deities exist?

Answer: Part of the answer to this is present in Padma Purāṇa 6.236 (the chapter which gives the Purāṇa classification), where Viṣṇu says to Śiva:

Padma Purāṇa 6.236

मोहनानि च शास्त्राणि कुरुष्व च महामते ॥

O Intelligent one, make the delusive scriptures.

Delusive scriptures including both Tāmasa Purāṇas and Āgamas. This conversation briefly mentioned there is described in much more detail in the Kūrma Purāṇa (1.16):

Kūrma Purāṇa 1.16

ततः स भगवान् विष्णुः शरण्यो भक्तवत्सलः । गोपतिं प्राह विप्रेन्द्रानालोक्य प्रणतान् हरिः ॥
न वेदबाह्यपुरुषे पुण्यलेशोऽपि शंकर । संगच्छते महादेव धर्मो वेदाद्विनिर्बभौ ॥
तथापि भक्तवात्सल्याद्रक्षितव्या महेश्वर । अस्माभिः सर्व एवेमे गन्तारो नरकानपि ॥
तस्माद्वै वेदबाह्यानां रक्षणाय पापिनाम् । विमोहनाय शास्त्राणि करिष्यामो वृषध्वज ॥

Then he, Lord Viṣṇu, the refuge and guardian of his devotees, seeing all the Brāhmaṇas saluting him, stated thus to Śiva: ‘In the person outside the Vedic fold, there is not even the slightest merit, for dharma has come from the Vedas alone. Even still, being like parents to our devotees, they are to be protected by us, even if they are bound for hell. Therefore, for the protection of those sinners outside the Vedic fold, let us create the delusive scriptures, O bull-bannered one.’ Thus spoken by Viṣṇu, slayer of Mura, Rudra created delusive scriptures, and Viṣṇu, requested by Śiva, did similarly.

Kṛṣṇa states in the Gītā:

Bhagavad Gītā 6.25

शनैः शनैरुपरमेद्

Slowly slowly one should renounce.

A nāstika, steeped in sin, cannot immediately become cognizant of pure Vaiṣṇava dharma, just as one situated on the ground cannot reach the top floors except through stairs or such. Therefore these delusive scriptures have been made as said “stairs” by which a complete nāstika may slowly make his way to Vaiṣṇava dharma over many births. The Kūrma Purāṇa continues:

Kūrma Purāṇa 1.16

सृष्ट्वा तावूचतुर्देवौ कुर्वाणाः शास्त्रचोदितम् । पतन्तो निरये घोरे बहून् कल्पान् पुनः पुनः ॥
जायन्तो मानुषे लोके क्षीणपापचयास्ततः । ईश्वराराधनबलाद्गच्छध्वं सुकृतां गतिम् ॥

Having created those (scriptures), the two (Viṣṇu and Śiva) said to the deities: ‘Doing as is stated in those delusive scriptures, they shall still fall into hell for some kalpas again and again, but be born in the humān race with lesser sin each time. In this way, purified by the worship of the supreme Lord, they attain the supreme destination (i.e. Mokṣa).’

Objection II: Many scriptural sages and venerable saints worshipped deities other than Viṣṇu. Were they all wrong?

Answer: No, in fact, there is no issue at all with worship of these deities. Kṛṣṇa himself advises it in Gītā 3.11 and elsewhere, stating that devotees of such deities may attain material benefits and may even go to their realms. Their worship is also his worship, just done ignorantly (of his status as their supreme antaryāmin). Even Vaiṣṇavas may worship other deities, though recognizing Viṣṇu as their antaryāmin.

Kṛṣṇa Himself recommends honouring other deities. Their devotees may receive material benefits or attain their respective realms, and their worship ultimately reaches Viṣṇu as their antaryamin, though without full knowledge of His position. Temporary fruits are distinguished from moksha.

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 5.5.6; 10.63.28

प्रीतिर्न यावन्मयि वासुदेवे न मुच्यते देहयोगेन तावत् ॥
तावत्तापो देहिनां तेऽङ्घ्रिमूलं नो सेवेरन् यावदाशानुबद्धाः ॥

One is not liberated from bodily bondage until one has devotion to Me, Vasudeva; embodied beings suffer while bound by desire until they serve Your lotus feet.

Objection III: Why does Viṣṇu, in His avataras, worship Śiva?

Answer: This has been clarified to Arjuna by Kṛṣṇa himself (Mahābhārata 12.350):

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.350

यद्यहं नार्चयेयं वै ईशानं वरदं शिवम् । आत्मानं नार्चयेत्कश्चिदिति मे भावितं मनः ॥
मया प्रमाणं हि कृतं लोकः समनुवर्तते । प्रमाणानि हि पूज्यानि ततस्तं पूजयाम्यहम् ॥

If I did not worship boon-giving Lord Śiva, who is My own self as I indwell him, no one else would do so. Whatever example I establish, the world follows; therefore I worship him.

Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 12.350

न हि मे केनचिद्देयो वरः पाण्डवनन्दन । इति सञ्चिन्त्य मनसा पुराणं विश्वमीश्वरम् ॥
पुत्रार्थमाराधितवानात्मानमहमात्मना । न हि विष्णुः प्रणमति कस्मैचिद्विबुधाय तु ॥
ऋते आत्मानमेवेति ततो रुद्रं भजाम्यहम् । सब्रह्मकाः सरुद्राश्च सेन्द्रा देवाः सहर्षिभिः । अर्चयन्ति सुरश्रेष्ठं देवं नारायणं हरिम् ॥

No one can grant Me a boon. Knowing this, I nevertheless worshipped the ancient Lord for a son. Viṣṇu does not bow to any deity; I worship Rudra as My own indwelt self. Brahma, Rudra, Indra, the gods and sages worship the supreme deity Hari Nārāyaṇa.

Objection IV: What about Vedic texts such as the Shvetashvatara Upaniṣad and Shri Rudram, which praise Rudra?

Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.2

एको हि रुद्रो न द्वितीयाय तस्थुः

Rudra is one, there is no second like him.

Śrī Rudram, Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5

नमस्ते अस्तु भगवन् विश्वेश्वराय.... सर्वेश्वराय सदाशिवाय श्रीमन्महादेवाय नमः

Salutations unto lord Sadāśiva, who is lord of the world, lord of all, the great deity

Answer: Such references may be dealt with by the Vaiṣṇava siddhānta in many ways. One such way was seen earlier, stated by Govindarāja, that such references, being of relative superiority and not absolute superiority (they do not explicitly state that he is superior to Viṣṇu, etc.), may be taken as superiority within their domains, whereas the verses for Viṣṇu, explicitly making Rudra and others subordinate, are of absolute supremacy.

One approach treats such verses as statements of relative superiority within a deity's domain. Another takes "Rudra" as a name of Viṣṇu, since Rudra appears in the Viṣṇu-sahasranama and Viṣṇu is the antaryamin of Rudra.

Śaṅkara explains the name Kathita: "He alone is proclaimed supreme by the Vedas; or, He is proclaimed by all the Vedas, and therefore is called Kathita."

Bhagavad Gītā 15.15

परत्वेन वेदादिभिरयमेव कथितः, सर्ववेदैः कथित इति वा कथितः ॥
सर्वे वेदा यत्पदमामनन्ति ॥
वेदे रामायणे चैव पुराणे भारते तथा । आदौ मध्ये तथा चान्ते हरिः सर्वत्र गीयते ॥

I alone am to be known through all the Vedas. I am the author of Vedānta and the knower of the Veda." - Bhagavad Gītā 15.15

वेदैश्च सर्वैरहमेव वेद्यो वेदान्तकृद्वेदविदेव चाहम् ॥
वेदैश्च सर्वैरिन्द्रादिदेवताप्रकाशकैरप्यहमेव वेद्यः सर्वात्मत्वात् ॥
अतः अग्निवायुसूर्यसोमेन्द्रादीनां मदन्तर्यामिकत्वेन मदात्मकत्वात् तत्प्रतिपादनपरैरपि सर्वैर्वेदैरहमेव वेद्यः ॥

Madhusudana Sarasvati and Rāmānuja similarly explain that even Vedic passages addressed to Agni, Vayu, Surya, Soma, Indra and others ultimately reveal Viṣṇu as their indwelling self.

Objection V: The Śiva Sankalpa Upaniṣad 2.18 places Isha above Hari.

Śiva Saṅkalpa Upaniṣad 2.18

परात्परतरो ब्रह्मा तत्परात्परतो हरिः । तत्परात्परतोऽधीशः तन्मे मनः शिवसङ्कल्पमस्तु ॥ १८ ॥

Greater than the greatest is Brahma, greater than that great one is Hari (Viṣṇu), and greater than that great is Isa (Śiva). May my mind be resolved towards Śiva.

Answer: The paper attributes this interpretation to Vijayendra Tīrtha’s Paratattvaprakāśikā. It gives no section locator for that attribution. The following citation is the exact Mīmāṃsā rule it invokes.

Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 5.4.1

क्रमकोपोऽर्थशब्दाभ्यां श्रुतिविशेषादर्थपरत्वाच्च ॥ १ ॥

In ritual interpretation, textual order may be set aside when a specific scriptural statement or the purpose of the rite requires another sequence.

The paper applies this rule to the order of names in Śiva Saṅkalpa Upaniṣad 2.18. The reordered Sanskrit below is the paper’s interpretation of that verse.

Śiva Saṅkalpa Upaniṣad 2.18 as interpreted in the paper

परात्परतरो ब्रह्मा तत्परात्परतोऽधीशः तत्परात्परतो हरिः । तन्मे मनः शिवसङ्कल्पमस्तु ॥

This interpretation places Isha before Hari.

Objection: Does this interpretation violate context?

A Vedic hymn may include praise of a deity other than its immediate subject. The Vishvakarman Sukta, for example, contains a verse applied to Viṣṇu in the Mahābhārata:

Mahābhārata 12.47.40

अजस्य नाभावध्येकं यस्मिन्विश्वं प्रतिष्ठितम् । पुष्करं पुष्कराक्षस्य तस्मै पद्मात्मने नमः ॥

In the navel of the unborn was fixed the lotus of the lotus-eyed one, in which the whole world was established. Salutations to that lotus-like Lord.

Objection VI: What about verses declaring equality or non-difference between Śiva and Viṣṇu?

Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan 12.330.64

उवाच देवमीशानमीशः स जगतो हरिः ॥ यस्त्वां वेत्ति स मां वेत्ति यस्त्वामनु स मामनु । नावयोरन्तरं किञ्चिन्मा ते भूद्बुद्धिरन्यथा ॥

Thus said Lord Hari to Lord Śiva: ‘Who knows you, knows me, and who follows you, follows me. There is no difference whatsoever between us, let not your thinking be otherwise.’

Answer: This is actually an application of a principle stated by Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā itself (7:16and18).

Bhagavad Gītā 7.16–18

चतुर्विधा भजन्ते मां जनाः सुकृतिनोऽर्जुन । आर्तो जिज्ञासुरर्थार्थी ज्ञानी च भरतर्षभ ॥
तेषां ज्ञानी नित्ययुक्त एकभक्तिर्विशिष्यते । प्रियो हि ज्ञानिनोऽत्यर्थमहं स च मम प्रियः ॥
उदाराः सर्व एवैते ज्ञानी त्वात्मैव मे मतम् । आस्थितः स हि युक्तात्मा मामेवानुत्तमां गतिम् ॥

Four kinds of virtuous people worship Me: the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of gain, and the wise. Among them, the wise one, always steadfast and devoted to Me alone, excels. I am exceedingly dear to him, and he is dear to Me. All are noble, but the wise one I regard as My very self." - Bhagavad Gītā 7.16-18

Kṛṣṇa says to Arjuna: "You are Mine and I am yours; what is Mine is yours. Whoever hates you hates Me, and whoever follows you follows Me. No difference between us can be known."

Mahābhārata 3.13.38, 40

ममैव त्वं तवैवाहं ये मदीयास्तवैव ते । यस्त्वां द्वेष्टि स मां द्वेष्टि यस्त्वामनु स मामनु ॥

Objection VII: From an Advaitic perspective, all deities are the same being, so hierarchy does not matter.

Answer: It is not within the purview of the work to dispute the Advaitins’ idea regarding the absolute (paramārthika) oneness of all deities (really, all beings in general), as it is neither within the scope of the document nor required for its thesis. What shall be disputed is the Advaitin idea of the shared supremacy and oneness of the five deities within the pragmatic (vyāvahārika) reality.

The Advaitins do not disregard the social hierarchies (varṇāśrāma), neither the political hierarchies, nor the filial hierarchies, based on their idea of paramārthika oneness. All of these are taken as real under vyāvahārika reality. They do not say that the father and son are equal in age, nor that the king and subject are equal in authority, merely because of their paramārthika oneness. If they do not disregard these other hierarchies, they can similarly not deny the deific hierarchies, at least not prima facie on the basis of paramārtha. If the scriptures and their ācāryas affirm a deific hierarchy within vyavahāra, they must accept it.

The entire paper was written with the Smārtas in mind. There was a special effort to present not just pramāṇas demonstrating that Viṣṇu is supreme, but that Śiva and others are not, and he is superior to them. These passages do not fit in the Smārta idea.

Similarly, the paper extensively cited not Vaiṣṇava ācāryas, but Śaṅkara. He takes three separate instances of an Upaniṣadic statement mentioning a supreme being, not even explicitly alluding to Viṣṇu, as referring to Viṣṇu, and never as Śiva, Sūrya, or Gaṇeśa. He wrote a commentary not on any Śaiva Gītā but on the Bhagavad Gītā, and within it stated the worship of the other deities besides Kṛṣṇa, including Śiva, Gaṇeśa, goddess and Sūrya, to be inferior, explicitly stating “Vaiṣṇavas” to be superior. He wrote a commentary not on Śiva-sahasranāma or Lalitā-sahasranāma but only on Viṣṇu-sahasranāma, and within it stated and proved that Vedas praise Viṣṇu alone as supreme, and even stated Rudra to be a vibhūti (partial glory) of Viṣṇu on name 233. Thus, the core commentative work, which is most definitely from Śaṅkara, is unequivocally Vaiṣṇava in characteristic, at least at the vyāvahārika level.

One may object based on the various stotras and such works attributed to “Śaṅkarācārya.” However, there we must note that that vast corpus of poetry is attributed to a Śaṅkarācārya, not necessarily Ādi Śaṅkarācārya. The adjective “Ādi” (first) is used for him because his four successor lineages also use “Śaṅkarācārya” as the title of their leader. Thus, there being no certainty of this stotra literature being composed by Ādi Śaṅkarācārya or some other Śaṅkarācārya, it cannot be used to dispute what is found in his core commentative work.

Even his immediate successors, such as Sureśvarācārya, were Vaiṣṇavas in their devotion. Sureśvarācārya begins and ends his Vārttika on the Yājñavalkya–Maitreya conversation with salutations to Viṣṇu, not any other deity. Prominent Smārta ācāryas like Madhusūdana Sarasvatī and Śrīdhara Svāmī were Vaiṣṇavas in their devotion. It is not until Appayya Dīkṣita, seven hundred years after Śaṅkarācārya, that we see a prominent Smārta ācārya being Śaiva in their devotion. In fact, the Smārtas earlier used to primarily apply the tripuṇḍra tilaka of Vaiṣṇavas, and only later switched to primarily applying the Śaiva tripuṇḍra to distinguish themselves from the rival Vaiṣṇava sects.

From all this it is clear that both the scriptures accepted by the Smārtas, and their own founding ācārya, support the idea that Viṣṇu and his worship is superior to Śiva and other deities and their worship, within the vyavahāra hierarchy.

Summary and Conclusion

Objections

Table not required. Summary:

Why tāmasic texts exist: for gradual upliftment (Kūrma 1.16).

What about worship of other deities: also valid, though inferior, unless done with right knowledge of Viṣṇu as antaryāmin; only worship of Viṣṇu grants mokṣa (Bhāgavata 10.63.28 and 5.5.6).

Why Viṣṇu worships Śiva: he does not worship Śiva but himself as his antaryāmin; he does so to inspire others (Mahābhārata 12.350).

What about Śiva supremacy in some Vedic passages: Rudra is itself a name (115th) in the Viṣṇu-sahasranāma. Śaṅkara says on name 848 that only Viṣṇu is stated to be supreme in the Vedas, as per Gītā 15.15, Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.15, and the Mahābhārata.

What about the Śiva-saṅkalpa Sūkta verse: it is reordered using Mīmāṃsā 5.4.1 to say Viṣṇu is supreme.

What about verses of equality between Śiva and Viṣṇu: explained by Gītā 17.18 as hyperbole by a god to his devotee, also seen for Arjuna (Mahābhārata 3.13.38, 40) and Nandi (Varāha Purāṇa 213.75).

What about oneness as per Advaita: it does not affect vyāvahārika reality; Śaṅkara affirms Viṣṇu’s exclusive supremacy in vyavahāra.

Conclusion

Thus, using close to fifty scriptural citations across Śruti, Smṛti, and Purāṇas, the supremacy of Viṣṇu, his primary status as Brahman, as creator of Śiva, Brahmā and everything else and his superiority over them, has been established. The supremacy of his worship, which is the only way to mokṣa, over that of other deities has also been established. The status of Śiva as having Viṣṇu as his antaryāmin, being his devotee, and being neither fully the same as nor fully different from him, as curd to milk, has been explained. In line with the stated methodology of samanvaya, no pramāṇa has been ignored or disregarded, and all objections have been addressed. Aside from scriptural pramāṇas, non-Vaiṣṇava Ācāryas such as Sāyaṇa, Śaṅkara, Madhusūdana Sarasvatī, and Paṇḍitācārya have been cited as supporting evidence where required.

Though I have intentionally avoided citing Vaiṣṇava Ācāryas in the document, they deserve mention here. The final Padma Purāṇa verse invokes not just the scriptures but also the consensus of the Ācāryas as evidence for Viṣṇu’s supremacy. Indeed, most Ācāryas throughout history have been Vaiṣṇava, belonging to sects such as Śrī, Mādhva, Gauḍīya, Puṣṭimārgī, Rāmānand, and Vārakarī.

I would also restate the disclaimer at the beginning: this work is not an attack on the Smārtas, Śaivas, or any other sect, but presents the Vaiṣṇava stance from a scriptural perspective.
