Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 26 — Bodily Signs and Their Results

BP 1.26.1

ब्रह्मोवाच
समकुक्षिर्भवेद्भोगी निम्नकुक्षिर्धनापहः ।
मायावी विषमा कुक्षिस्तथा कुहककृत्सदा ॥

Brahmā said: An even abdomen makes an enjoyer; a sunken abdomen takes away wealth. An uneven abdomen marks a deceptive man who always practices fraud.

BP 1.26.2

राजा स्यान्निम्नकुक्षिस्तु सार्वभौमो महाबलः ।
सर्पोदरा दरिद्राः स्युर्बहुभक्षाश्च सुव्रत ॥

A sunken-bellied man may become a king, a mighty universal ruler. Snake-bellied men are poor and gluttonous, O keeper of good vows.

BP 1.26.3

विस्तीर्णाभिर्मण्डलाभिरुन्नताभिश्च नाभिभिः ।
भवन्ति सुखिनो वीरा धनधान्यसमन्विताः ॥

People with broad, round, raised navels become happy and heroic, endowed with wealth and grain.

BP 1.26.4

निम्नाभिरथ स्वल्पाभिः क्लेशभाजो भवन्ति हि ।
वलिर्मध्यङ्गता वीरा विषमा च विशेषतः ॥
धनहानिं तथा शूलं नित्यं जनयते विभो ॥

Those with sunken or small navels become sufferers. A fold running through the middle, especially an uneven one, always causes loss of wealth and pain, O mighty one.

BP 1.26.5

वामावार्ता सदा शान्तिं करोतीति विदुर्बुधाः ।
करोति मेधां दक्षिणेन संप्रवृत्ता दिवस्पते ॥

The wise know that a fold turning to the left always brings peace. One extending to the right brings intelligence, O lord of heaven.

BP 1.26.6

पार्श्वायता दीर्घमायुरैश्वर्यमूर्ध्वतः स्मृतम् ।
गवाढ्यतामधस्तात्तु करोतीति विदुर्बुधाः ॥

Extending to the side, it signifies long life; upward, sovereignty; downward, abundance of cattle—so the wise know.

BP 1.26.7

शतपत्रकर्णिकाभा नाभिर्यस्य महामते ।
भूपत्वं कुरुते सा तु पुरुषस्य न संशयः ॥

A navel resembling the seed-vessel of a hundred-petalled lotus undoubtedly makes the man who has it a ruler of the earth, O great-minded one.

BP 1.26.8

समोदरो भवेद्भोगी निस्वः स्याद्विषमोदरः ।
सूक्ष्मोदरो भवेद्वाग्मी बहुसम्पत्समन्वितः ॥

Even-bellied, a man becomes an enjoyer; uneven-bellied, destitute; slender-bellied, eloquent and endowed with much wealth.

BP 1.26.9

शस्त्रेणान्तं व्रजेद्वीर स्त्रीभोगं चाप्नुयात्तथा ।
आचार्यो बहुपुत्रश्च यथासङ्ख्यं विनिर्दिशेत् ॥

In order, one should declare [the signs] as death by a weapon, enjoyment of women, a teacher, and many sons, O hero.

BP 1.26.10

वलिभिर्देवशार्दूल इत्याह स पयोनिधिः ।
अगम्यागामिनो ज्ञेया विषमाभिर्न संशयः ॥

The Ocean spoke thus of the folds, O tiger among the gods. Uneven folds undoubtedly mark one who approaches forbidden women.

BP 1.26.11

ऋजुभिर्वसुभोगी स्यात्परदारविनिन्दकः ।
मांसलैर्मृदुभिः पार्श्वै राजा स्यान्नात्र संशयः ॥

With straight sides, a man enjoys wealth and censures another man's wife; with fleshy, soft sides, he becomes a king, no doubt.

BP 1.26.12

अनूर्ध्वचिबुका ये तु सुभगास्ते भवन्ति वै ।
निर्धना विषमैर्दीर्घैर्भवन्तीह सुवीरज ॥

Those whose chins do not rise are fortunate; those with long, uneven chins become poor in this world, O valiant one.

BP 1.26.13

पीनैश्चोपचितैर्निम्नैः स्कन्धैर्भीमाङ्गसम्भव ।
राजानः सुखिनश्चापि भवन्तीह न संशयः ॥

With full, well-developed, low-set shoulders, men become kings and happy, no doubt, O son of Bhīma.

BP 1.26.14

समोन्नतं तु हृदयं समं च पृथु चैव हि ।
अवेपनं मांसलं च पार्थिवानां न संशयः ॥

The chest that is evenly raised, level, broad, steady, and fleshy belongs to kings, no doubt.

BP 1.26.15

खररोमचितं वीरशिरालं च विशेषतः ।
अधनानां भवेदेव हृदयं ऋभवोत्तम ॥
समवक्षसोऽर्थयुताः पीनैः शूराः स्मृता बुधैः ॥

The chest of the poor is coarse-haired and especially veined, O best among the Ribhus. Those with even chests are wealthy; those with full chests are considered brave by the wise.

BP 1.26.16

तनुभिर्द्रव्यहीनाः स्युरसमैश्चाप्यकिञ्चनाः ।
वध्यन्ते चापि शस्त्रेण नात्र कार्या विचारणा ॥

Thin chests mean lack of property; uneven chests, poverty. They are also killed by a weapon—there is no doubt.

BP 1.26.17

हनुभिर्विषमैर्वीर जन्महीनो भवेन्नरः ।
यस्योन्नतो भवेद्धनुः स भोगी स्यान्न संशयः ॥

A man with uneven jaws is low-born, O hero. He whose jaw is raised becomes an enjoyer, no doubt.

BP 1.26.18

निर्मांसैर्विषमैर्वीर निःस्वो निम्नैः प्रचक्ष्यते ।
धनवांश्च भवेत्पीनैः सुखभोगसमन्वितः ॥
विषमैरर्थहीनः स्याद्दुःखभागी सदा नरः ॥

Fleshless, uneven jaws mark a destitute man, O hero; sunken jaws mark a poor man. Full jaws make him wealthy and given to pleasure; uneven jaws mark a man who always suffers.

BP 1.26.19

चिपिटग्रीवको दुष्टो मतो लोके स वै गुह ।
शूरः स्यान्महिषग्रीवो मृगग्रीवो भयातुरः ॥

A flat-necked man is considered wicked in the world, O Guha. One with a buffalo's neck is brave; one with a deer's neck is fearful.

BP 1.26.20

कम्बुग्रीवो भवेद्राजा लम्बकण्ठोऽग्रलक्षणः ।
ह्रस्वग्रीवस्तु धनवान्सुसुखी भोगवांस्तथा ॥

A conch-necked man becomes a king; a long-throated man has excellent marks. A short-necked man is wealthy, very happy, and an enjoyer.

BP 1.26.21

निमांसौ रोमशौ भग्नावल्पौ वापि विशेषतः ।
निर्धनस्येदृशावंसौ प्रख्यातौ व्योमकेशज ॥

Shoulders that are fleshless, hairy, broken, or especially small are known to belong to a poor man, O son of Vyomakeśa.

BP 1.26.22

भवेदरोमशं पृष्ठं धनिनां भीमसम्भव ।
सलोमशं तथा वक्रं निर्धनानां बलाधिप ॥

A hairless back belongs to the wealthy, O son of Bhīma; a hairy and crooked back belongs to the poor, O lord of might.

BP 1.26.23

अस्वेदनावुन्नतौ च तथा पीनौ षडानन ।
समरोमसुगन्धौ च कक्षौ ज्ञेयौ धनान्वितौ ॥

Armpits that do not sweat, are raised and full, and have even hair and a pleasant smell are known as marks of wealth, O six-faced one.

BP 1.26.24

अव्युच्छिन्नौ तथा श्लिष्टौ विपुलौ च सुराधिप ।
शूराणामीदृशावंसौ नगजानन्दवर्धन ॥

Unbroken, joined, broad shoulders such as these belong to heroes, O increaser of the mountain-born goddess's joy.

BP 1.26.25

उद्वृद्धबाहुको यस्तु वधबन्धनमाप्नुयात् ।
दीर्घबाहुर्भवेद्राजा समुद्रवचनं यथा ॥

One whose arms are raised high obtains execution and imprisonment. A long-armed man becomes a king, according to the Ocean's statement.

BP 1.26.26

प्रलम्बबाहुर्विज्ञेयो नरः सर्वगुणान्वितः ।
ह्रस्वबाहुर्भवेद्दासः परप्रेष्यकरोऽपि वा ॥

A man with long, hanging arms is known to possess every virtue. A short-armed man becomes a servant or does another's errands.

BP 1.26.27

वामावर्तभुजा ये तु दीर्घायतभुजाश्च ये ।
सम्पूर्णबाहू राजा स्यादित्याह स पयोनिधिः ॥

A man whose arms turn to the left and are long, extended, and full becomes a king, so the Ocean said.

BP 1.26.28

ग्रीवा च वर्तुलाकारा कम्बुरेखासमावृता ।
स भवेत्पार्थिवो भूमौ सर्वदुष्टनिबर्हणः ॥

A rounded neck covered with conch-lines makes a man a ruler on earth, the remover of all evildoers.

BP 1.26.29

दीर्घग्रीवा बकग्रीवा शुकग्रीवाश्च ये नराः ।
उष्ट्रग्रीवाः करिग्रीवाः सर्वे ते निर्धनाः स्मृताः ॥

Men with long necks, heron necks, parrot necks, camel necks, and elephant necks are all considered poor.

BP 1.26.30

इभाङ्गसदृशौ वृत्तौ समौ पीनौ च सुव्रत ।
आजानुलम्बिनौ बाहू पार्थिवानां न संशयः ॥

Arms round and even, full and resembling an elephant's trunk, hanging down to the knees: these belong to rulers, no doubt, O keeper of good vows.

BP 1.26.31

दरिद्राणां लोमशौ ह्रस्वौ बाहू ज्ञेयौ सुरोत्तम ।
तस्कराणां च विषमौ स्थूलौ सूक्ष्मौ च सुव्रत ॥

The arms of the poor should be known as hairy and short, O best of the gods. Those of thieves are uneven, one thick and one thin, O keeper of good vows.

BP 1.26.32

निम्नं करतलं यस्य पितृवित्तं न तस्य वै ।
भवेदार्भवशार्दूल तथा भीरुश्च मानवः ॥

A man whose palm is hollow has no paternal wealth and is timid, O tiger among children.

BP 1.26.33

सुवृत्ततनुनिम्नेन धनवान्करतलेन तु ।
उत्तानकरतलो दाता भवतीति न संशयः ॥

With a well-rounded, slender, hollow palm, a man is wealthy; one with an upward-facing palm is a giver, no doubt.

BP 1.26.34

विषमा भवन्ति विषमैर्निम्नाश्चापि विशेषतः ।
करतलैर्देवशार्दूल लाक्षाभैरीश्वराः स्मृताः ॥

Uneven palms make men uneven, especially when hollow. Those with lac-colored palms are considered lords, O tiger among the gods.

BP 1.26.35

अगम्यागमनं पीतै रूक्षैर्निर्धनता स्मृता ।
अपेयपानं कुर्वन्ति नीलकृष्णैः सदैव हि ॥

Yellow palms mark approach to forbidden women; rough palms signify poverty. Those with blue-black palms always drink what should not be drunk.

BP 1.26.36

निम्नाः स्निग्धा भवेन्नृणां रेखा करतले गुह ।
धनिनां न दरिद्राणामित्याह स पयोनिधिः ॥

The lines in men's palms should be deep and smooth in the wealthy, not in the poor, so the Ocean said, O Guha.

BP 1.26.37

विरलाङ्गुलयो ये तु ते दरिद्राः प्रचक्षते ।
धनिनस्तु महाबाहो ये घनाङ्गुलयो नराः ॥

Those whose fingers are spaced apart are declared poor; men whose fingers are close-set are wealthy, O great-armed one.

BP 1.26.38

वदनं मण्डलं यस्य धर्मशीलं तमादिशेत् ।
शुण्डवक्त्रा नरा ये तु दुर्भगास्ते न संशयः ॥

One whose face is round should be called righteous in character. Men with trunk-like faces are unfortunate, no doubt.

BP 1.26.39

हरिवक्त्रा जिह्मवक्त्रा विकृतास्यास्तथा नराः ।
भग्नवत्राः करालास्या सर्वे ते तस्कराः स्मृताः ॥

Men with lion-like, crooked, deformed, broken, or fearsome faces are all considered thieves.

BP 1.26.40

सम्पूर्णवक्त्रा राजानो गजसिंहाननास्तथा ।
छागवानरवक्त्राश्च धनिनः परिकीर्तिताः ॥

Those with full faces are kings, as are those with elephant or lion faces; those with goat or monkey faces are proclaimed wealthy.

BP 1.26.41

यस्य गण्डौ सुसम्पूर्णौ पद्मपत्रसमप्रभौ ।
कृषिभागी भवेन्नित्यं बहुवित्तश्च मानवः ॥

A man whose two cheeks are very full and shine like lotus leaves always has a share in agriculture and much wealth.

BP 1.26.42

सिंहव्याघ्रगजेन्द्राणां कपोलः सदृशो यदि ।
महाभोगी स विज्ञेयः सेनायाश्चैव नायकः ॥

If a cheek resembles that of a lion, tiger, or lordly elephant, its owner should be known as a great enjoyer and a leader of an army.

BP 1.26.43

वदनं तु समं श्लक्ष्णं सौम्यं संवृतमेव हि ।
पार्थिवानां महाबाहो विपरीतन्तु दुःखदम् ॥

A face that is even, smooth, gentle, and well-contained belongs to rulers, O mighty-armed one; the opposite brings suffering.

BP 1.26.44

महामुखं तु देवेश दुर्भगत्वं प्रयच्छति ।
स्त्रीमुखं पुत्रनाशाय मण्डलं सुखितां व्रजेत् ॥

A very large face gives misfortune, O lord of the gods. A woman's face brings loss of sons; a round one attains happiness.

BP 1.26.45

द्रव्यनाशाय वै दीर्घं पापदं भयदं तथा ।
धूर्तानां चतुरस्रं स्यात्पुत्रहानिकरं शृणु ॥

A long face causes loss of wealth, sin, and fear. A square face belongs to rogues and causes loss of sons; hear this.

BP 1.26.46

निम्नवक्त्रं च देवेन्द्र पुत्रहानिकरं भवेत् ।
ह्रस्वं भतति कीनाशे पूर्णकान्तं च भोगिनाम् ॥

A sunken face causes loss of sons, O Indra of the gods. A short face belongs to a poor laborer; a full, beautiful face to enjoyers.

BP 1.26.47

रक्ताधरो नरपतिर्धनवान्कमलाधरः ।
स्थूलोष्ठा हनुमूलाश्च शुष्कैस्तीक्ष्णैश्च दुःखिताः ॥

A man with a red lower lip is a king; a lotus-like lower lip marks a wealthy man. Those with thick lips, lips reaching the jaw-root, or dry, sharp lips suffer.

BP 1.26.48

अस्फोटिताग्रं स्निग्धं च नतं मृदु तथा गुह ।
सम्पूर्णं च सदा शस्तं श्मश्रु भूमिपतेर्गुह ॥

Facial hair unsplit at the tips, smooth, bent, soft, and full is always auspicious for a ruler, O Guha.

BP 1.26.49

रक्तैश्चाल्पैस्तथा रूक्षैः श्मश्रुभिर्भीमनन्दन ।
नराश्चौरा भवन्त्येव परदाररतास्तथा ॥

O son of Bhīma, men with reddish, scant, rough facial hair are thieves and are also devoted to other men's wives.

BP 1.26.50

निर्मांसौ यस्य वै कर्णौ संग्रामान्नाशमृच्छति ।
चिपिटाभ्यां भवेद्रोगी ह्रस्वौ च कृपणस्य च ॥

One whose ears are fleshless meets destruction through battle. With flattened ears, a man is sickly; short ears belong to a miser.

BP 1.26.51

शङ्कुकर्णश्च भूनाथः सर्वशत्रुभयङ्करः ।
दीर्घायू रोमशाभ्यां तु विपुलाभ्यां नराधिपः ॥
भोगी च स भवेन्नित्यं देवब्राह्मणपूजकः ॥

A pointed-eared man is a ruler, terrible to all enemies. With hairy, broad ears, a man is long-lived, a ruler and always an enjoyer, worshipping the gods and Brahmins.

BP 1.26.52

शिरावबद्धौ क्रूरस्य व्यालम्बौ च विशेषतः ।
मांसलौ सुखदौ ज्ञेयौ श्रवणौ व्योमकेशज ॥

Ears bound with veins and especially drooping are known to belong to the cruel; fleshy ears bring happiness, O son of Vyomakeśa.

BP 1.26.53

भोगी स्यान्निगण्डो वै मन्त्री सम्पूर्णगण्डकः ।
शुभभाक्छुकनासस्तु चिरजीवी शुष्कनासिकः ॥

A man without full cheeks is an enjoyer; one with completely full cheeks is a minister. A parrot-nosed man bears good fortune; a dry-nosed man is long-lived.

BP 1.26.54

कुन्दकुण्डलसङ्काशैः प्रकाशैर्दशनैर्नृपः ।
ऋक्षवानरदन्ताश्च नित्यं क्षुत्परिपीडिताः ॥

A king has bright teeth resembling jasmine buds. Those with bear-like or monkey-like teeth are always tormented by hunger.

BP 1.26.55

हस्तिदन्ताः खरदन्ताः स्निग्धदन्ता गुणान्विताः ।
करालैर्विरलै रूक्षैर्दशनैर्दुःखजीविनः ॥

Those with elephant teeth, donkey teeth, or smooth teeth are endowed with good qualities. Those with fearsome, sparse, rough teeth live in suffering.

BP 1.26.56

द्वात्रिंशद्दन्ता राजान एकत्रिंशच्च भोगवान् ।
त्रिंशद्दन्ता नरा नित्यं सुखदुःखित्वभागिन ॥
ऊनत्रिंशच्य दशनैः पुरुषाः दुःखभागिनः ॥

Men with thirty-two teeth are kings; with thirty-one, an enjoyer; with thirty, they share in both happiness and sorrow; with fewer than thirty, they share in sorrow.

BP 1.26.57

कृष्णजिह्वो भवेत्प्रेष्यः सवलया तु जिह्वया ।
भवेत्कोपस्य कर्ता वै स्थूलरूक्षश्च जिह्वया ॥

A black-tongued man becomes a servant. One with a folded tongue becomes a cause of anger; so too one with a thick, rough tongue.

BP 1.26.58

श्वेतजिह्वा नरा ज्ञेयाः शौचाचारसमन्विताः ।
पद्मपत्रसमा जिह्वा सूक्ष्मा दीर्घा सुशोभना ॥
स्थूला च न च विस्तीर्णा येषां ते मनुजाधिपाः ॥

White-tongued men should be known as endowed with purity and good conduct. Those whose tongue is like a lotus leaf, slender, long, beautiful, thick but not broad, are rulers of men.

BP 1.26.59

निम्ना स्निग्धा च ह्रस्वा च रक्ताग्रा रसना यदि ।
सर्वविद्याप्रवक्तारस्ते भवन्ति न संशयः ॥

A man with a black palate becomes the destroyer of his lineage. A man with a yellow palate shares in happiness and sorrow.

BP 1.26.60

कृष्णतालुर्नरो यस्तु स भवेत्कुलनाशनः ।
सुखभागी दुःखभागी पीततालुर्नराधिपः ॥

A man with a black palate becomes the destroyer of his lineage. A man with a yellow palate shares in happiness and sorrow.

BP 1.26.61

विकृतं स्फुटितं रूक्षं तालुकं न प्रशस्यते ।
सिंहतालुर्नरपतिर्गजतालुस्तथैव च ॥
पद्मतालुर्भवेद्राजा श्वेततालुर्धनेश्वरः ॥

A deformed, cracked, rough palate is not praised. A lion-like or elephant-like palate belongs to a king; a lotus-like palate makes a king; a white palate, a lord of wealth.

BP 1.26.62

हंसस्वरा नरा धन्या मेघगम्भीरनिःस्वनाः ।
क्रौंचस्वनाश्च राजानो भोगवन्तो महाधनाः ॥

Men with swan voices are fortunate, as are those with a sound deep as a cloud. Those with crane voices are kings, enjoyers, and very wealthy.

BP 1.26.63

चक्रवाकस्वना धन्या राजानो धर्मवत्सलाः ।
कुम्भस्वनो नरपतिर्दुन्दुभिस्वन एव च ॥
रूक्षदीर्घस्वराः क्रूराः पशूनां सदृशा न तु ॥

Those with ruddy-goose voices are fortunate kings, devoted to dharma. A pot-like or kettledrum-like voice belongs to a ruler. Those with rough, long voices are cruel and resemble animals.

BP 1.26.64

गुर्गुरस्वरसंयुक्ताः पुरुषाः क्लेशभागिनः ।
चाषस्वना भाग्ययुता भिन्नकांस्यस्वराश्च ये ॥
क्षीणभिन्नस्वरा ये स्युरधमास्ते प्रकीर्तिताः ॥

Men with gurgling voices share in distress. Those with blue-jay voices are fortunate. Those with broken-bronze voices, and those whose voices are weak and broken, are called low.

BP 1.26.65

पार्थिवास्तनुनासाश्च दीर्घनासाश्च भोगिनः ।
ह्रस्वनासा नरा ये तु धर्मशीलास्तु ते मताः ॥

Rulers have slender noses, and long-nosed men are enjoyers. Men with short noses are considered righteous in character.

BP 1.26.66

हस्त्यश्वसिंहनासाश्च सूचीनासाश्च ये नराः ।
तेषां सिध्यति वाणिज्यं हयानां चैव विक्रयः ॥

Men with elephant, horse, lion, or needle-like noses succeed in trade and in the sale of horses.

BP 1.26.67

विकृता नासिका यस्य स्थूलाग्रा रूपवर्जिता ।
पापकर्मा स विज्ञेयः सामुद्रवचनं यथा ॥

One whose nose is deformed, thick at the tip, and without beauty should be known as sinful, according to Ocean's pronouncement.

BP 1.26.68

दाडिमीपुष्पसंकाशे भवेतां यस्य लोचने ।
भूपतिः स तु विज्ञेयः सप्तद्वीपाधिपो गुह ॥

One whose eyes resemble pomegranate flowers should be known as a ruler, lord of the seven continents, O Guha.

BP 1.26.69

व्याघ्राक्षाः कोपना ज्ञेयाः कर्कटाक्षाः कलिप्रियाः ।
बिडालहंसनेत्राश्च भवन्ति पुरुषाधमाः ॥

Tiger-eyed men are wrathful; crab-eyed men delight in quarrels. Men with cat-like or goose-like eyes are the lowest of men.

BP 1.26.70

मपूरनकुलाक्षाश्च नरास्ते मध्यमाः स्मृताः ।
न श्रीस्त्यजति सर्वज्ञ पुरुषं मधुपिङ्गलम् ॥

Men with peacock or mongoose eyes are considered middling. Fortune never abandons a man whose eyes are honey-brown, O all-knowing one.

BP 1.26.71

आपिङ्गलाक्षा राजानः सर्वभोगसमन्विताः ।
रोचना हरितालाक्षा गुञ्जापिङ्गा धनेश्वराः ॥
बलसत्त्वगुणोपेताः पृथिवीचक्रवर्तिनः ॥

Tawny-eyed men are kings, endowed with every enjoyment. Those with eyes like yellow pigment, orpiment, or red-and-black seeds are lords of wealth, endowed with strength, courage, and virtue, universal rulers of the earth.

BP 1.26.72

द्विमात्रावीक्षणा नित्यं जीवन्ति परमाश्रिताः ।
त्रिमात्रास्यन्दिनो ज्ञेयाः पुरुषाः सुखभागिनः ॥

Those whose glance lasts two measures live continually dependent [on others]. Men whose glance lasts three measures should be known as sharing in happiness.

BP 1.26.73

चतुर्मात्रानिमेषैश्च नयनैरीश्वराः स्मृताः ।
दीर्घायुषो धर्मरताः पञ्चमात्रानिमेषिणः ॥

Those whose eyes remain unblinking for four measures are remembered as lords; those whose eyes remain unblinking for five measures are long-lived and devoted to dharma.

BP 1.26.74

ह्रस्वकर्णा महाभागा महाकर्णाश्च ये नराः ।
आवर्तकर्णा धनिनः स्निग्धकर्णास्तथैव च ॥

Men with short ears are very fortunate, as are men with large ears. Those with whorled or smooth ears are wealthy.

BP 1.26.75

दीर्घायुषः शुक्तिकर्णाः शङ्खकर्णा महाधनाः ।
दीर्घायुषो दीर्घकर्णा लम्बकर्णास्तपस्विनः ॥

Those with shell-like ears are long-lived; those with conch-like ears are very wealthy. Long ears signify long life; hanging ears belong to ascetics.

BP 1.26.76

ललाटेनार्धचन्द्रेण भवन्ति पृथिवीश्वराः ।
विपुलेन ललाटेन महाधनपतिः स्मृतः ॥
स्वल्पेन तु ललाटेन नरो धर्मरतः स्मृतः ॥

Those with a half-moon-shaped forehead become rulers of the earth. A broad forehead marks a lord of great wealth; a small forehead marks a man devoted to dharma.

BP 1.26.77

रेखा पञ्च ललाटे तु स्त्रिया वा पुरुषस्य वा ।
शतं जीवति वर्षाणामैश्वर्यं चाधिगच्छति ॥

A woman or man with five lines on the forehead lives a hundred years and attains sovereignty.

BP 1.26.78

चतूरेखामशीतिं तु त्रिभिः सप्ततिमेव च ।
द्वाभ्यां षष्टिं तु रेखाभ्यां चत्वारिंशत्तथैकया ॥
अरेखेन ललाटेन विज्ञेया पञ्चविंशतिः ॥

Four forehead lines indicate eighty years, three indicate seventy, two indicate sixty, and one indicates forty. A forehead without lines indicates twenty-five.

BP 1.26.79

रेखाच्छेदैस्तु विज्ञेया हीनमध्योत्तमा नराः ।
अल्पायुषस्तथाल्पाभिर्व्याधिभिः परिपीडिताः ॥

From breaks in the lines, men are known as inferior, middling, or superior. They are short-lived and afflicted by a few illnesses.

BP 1.26.80

त्रिशूलं पट्टिशं वापि ललाटे यस्य दृश्यते ।
ईश्वरं तं विजानीयाद्भोगिनं कीर्तिमाश्रितम् ॥

One whose forehead bears a trident or spear should be known as a lord, an enjoyer established in fame.

BP 1.26.81

उत्क्रान्तनिम्नं तु शिरः स्वल्पोपहतमेव च ।
चन्द्राकारं नरेन्द्राणां गवाढ्यं मङ्गलं स्मृतम् ॥

A head with raised and hollow parts, slightly impaired and moon-shaped, is considered auspicious for kings and rich in cattle.

BP 1.26.82

विषमं तु दरिद्राणां शिरो दीर्घं तु दुःखिनाम् ।
नागकुम्भनिभं राज्ञः समं सर्वत्र भोगिनः ॥

An uneven head belongs to the poor, a long head to sufferers. A head like an elephant's frontal globe belongs to a king; a head even everywhere belongs to an enjoyer.

BP 1.26.83

कपिलः स्फुटितै रूक्षैः स्थूलैश्व शिखरेशयैः ।
दुःखिता पुरुषा ज्ञेया रोमश्मश्रुभिरेव च ॥

Men whose body hair and beard are tawny, split, rough, thick, and standing erect should be known as suffering.

BP 1.26.84

रूक्षा विवर्णा निस्तेजाः खराः स्थूलाश्च मूर्धजाः ।
नातिस्तोका न बहुशो मूर्धजा दुःखभागिनः ॥

Men whose head-hair is rough, discolored, lustreless, coarse, and thick, neither very sparse nor abundant, share in suffering.

BP 1.26.85

विरलाश्च मृदुस्निग्धा भ्रमराञ्जनसप्रभाः ।
कचा यस्य तु दृश्यन्ते स भवेत्पृथिवीपतिः ॥

One whose head-hair is sparse, soft, smooth, and lustrous like bees and black collyrium becomes a ruler of the earth.
