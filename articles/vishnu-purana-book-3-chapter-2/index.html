VP 3.2.1

प्रोक्तान्येतानि भवता सप्तमन्वन्तराणि वै।
भविष्याण्यपि विप्रर्षे ममाख्यातुं त्वमर्हसि

You have told me these seven Manvantaras. O Brahmin seer, tell me also about the future ones.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
प्रोक्तान्येतानि भवता (proktānyetāni bhavatā) — these have been told by you
सप्तमन्वन्तराणि वै (saptamanvantarāṇi vai) — the seven Manvantaras indeed
भविष्याण्यपि विप्रर्षे (bhaviṣyāṇyapi viprarṣe) — future ones too, O Brahmin seer
ममाख्यातुं त्वमर्हसि (mamākhyātuṃ tvamarhasi) — you should tell me
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

proktānyetāni bhavatā saptamanvantarāṇi vai|bhaviṣyāṇyapi viprarṣe mamākhyātuṃ tvamarhasi

Śrīdhara

dvitīye sapta manvādīn vakṣyannādau raveḥ sutaḥ । yathā sāvarṇirabhavaditihāsaṃ tamabravīt ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

द्वितीये सप्त मन्वादीन् वक्ष्यन्नादौ रवेः सुतः । यथा सावर्णिरभवदितिहासं तमब्रवीत् ॥१॥

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Śrīdhara frames this chapter as Parāśara's account of the future Manus. Before listing them, Parāśara recounts how Sāvarṇi, the Sun's son, came to be.

VP 3.2.2

सूर्यस्य पत्नी संज्ञाभूत्तनया विश्वकर्मणः।
मनुर्यमो यमी चैव तदपत्यानि वै मुने

Saṃjñā, daughter of Viśvakarman, was the Sun's wife. Manu, Yama, and Yamī were her children, O sage.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
सूर्यस्य पत्नी (sūryasya patnī) — the Sun's wife
संज्ञाभूत्तनया विश्वकर्मणः (saṃjñābhūttanayā viśvakarmaṇaḥ) — Saṃjñā was, daughter of Viśvakarman
मनुर्यमो यमी चैव (manuryamo yamī caiva) — Manu, Yama, and Yamī
तदपत्यानि वै मुने (tadapatyāni vai mune) — are indeed her children, O sage
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sūryasya patnī saṃjñābhūttanayā viśvakarmaṇaḥ|manuryamo yamī caiva tadapatyāni vai mune

Śrīdhara

manuḥ śrāddhadevaḥ ||2||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

मनुः श्राद्धदेवः ॥२॥

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Śrīdhara identifies Manu here as Śrāddhadeva.

VP 3.2.3

असहन्ती तु सा भर्तुस्तेजश्छायां युयोज वै।
भर्तुः शुश्रूषणेऽरण्यं स्वयं च तपसे ययौ

Unable to bear the Sun's radiance, Saṃjñā appointed a shadow to serve the Sun. Saṃjñā herself went to the forest for austerity.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
असहन्ती तु सा (asahantī tu sā) — she, unable to bear
भर्तुस्तेजश्छायां युयोजवै (bhartustejaśchāyāṃ yuyojavai) — the Sun's radiance, she appointed a shadow
भर्तुःशुश्रूषणेऽरण्यं (bhartuḥśuśrūṣaṇe'raṇyaṃ) — for the husband's service, to the forest
स्वयं च तपसे ययौ (svayaṃ ca tapase yayau) — she herself went for austerity
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

asahantī tu sā bhartustejaśchāyāṃ yuyoja vai|bhartuḥ śuśrūṣaṇe'raṇyaṃ svayaṃ ca tapase yayau

Śrīdhara

chāyāṃ bimbatulyāṃ svasadṛśīmanyāṃ striyaṃ sūryatejaḥsahiṣṇuṃ nirmāya bhartuḥ śuśrūṣaṇe yuyoja, svayañca tapase yayāvityarthaḥ ||3||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

छायां बिम्बतुल्यां स्वसदृशीमन्यां स्त्रियं सूर्यतेजःसहिष्णुं निर्माय भर्तुः शुश्रूषणे युयोज, स्वयञ्च तपसे ययावित्यर्थः ॥३॥

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Śrīdhara explains that Saṃjñā made an image resembling herself in the form of another woman able to endure the Sun's heat. Saṃjñā placed Chāyā in her husband's service and went to the forest for austerity.

VP 3.2.4

संज्ञेयमित्यथार्कश्च छायायामात्मजत्रयम्।
शनैश्चरं मनुं चान्यं तपतीं चाप्यजीजनत्

Thinking the shadow was Saṃjñā, the Sun fathered three children by her. They were Śanaiścara, another Manu, and Tapatī.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
संज्ञेयमित्यथार्कश्च (saṃjñeyamityathārkaśca) — thinking this is Saṃjñā, the Sun
छायायामात्मजत्रयम् (chāyāyāmātmajatrayam) — three children by Chāyā
शनैश्चरं मनुं चान्यं (śanaiścaraṃ manuṃ cānyaṃ) — Śanaiścara and another Manu
तपतीं चाप्यजीजनत् (tapatīṃ cāpyajījanat) — and Tapatī too, the Sun fathered
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

saṃjñeyamityathārkaśca chāyāyāmātmajatrayam|śanaiścaraṃ manuṃ cānyaṃ tapatīṃ cāpyajījanat

Śrīdhara

atra manuṃ sāvarṇim । tapatīṃ saṃvaraṇasya rājño bhāryām ||4||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत्र मनुं सावर्णिम् । तपतीं संवरणस्य राज्ञो भार्याम् ॥४॥

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Here Manu means Sāvarṇi. Tapatī is the wife of King Saṃvaraṇa.

VP 3.2.5

छायासंज्ञा ददौ शापं यमाय कुपिता यदा।
तदान्येयमसौ बुद्धिरित्यासीद्यमसूर्ययोः

When Shadow-Saṃjñā became angry, she cursed Yama. Then Yama and the Sun realized that she was another woman.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
छायासंज्ञा ददौ शापं (chāyāsaṃjñā dadau śāpaṃ) — Shadow-Saṃjñā gave a curse
यमाय कुपिता यदा (yamāya kupitā yadā) — to Yama when angry
तदान्येयमसौ बुद्धिरित्यासीद् (tadānyeyamasau buddhirityāsīd) — then the thought arose that she was another woman
यमसूर्ययोः (yamasūryayoḥ) — in Yama and the Sun
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

chāyāsaṃjñā dadau śāpaṃ yamāya kupitā yadā|tadānyeyamasau buddhirityāsīdyamasūryayoḥ

Śrīdhara

chāyaiva sañjñārūpeṇa sthitā chāyāsañjñā । sā kadācit svāpatyatrayasnehavatī ātmani pādaprahārodyatāya yamāya pādaste patatviti śāpaṃ dadau । tadātinirdayatvamālakṣya neyamasau sañjñā, kintu anyeyaṃ saṃhoti yamasūryayorbuddhirabhūdityarthaḥ ||5||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

छायैव सञ्ज्ञारूपेण स्थिता छायासञ्ज्ञा । सा कदाचित् स्वापत्यत्रयस्नेहवती आत्मनि पादप्रहारोद्यताय यमाय पादस्ते पतत्विति शापं ददौ । तदातिनिर्दयत्वमालक्ष्य नेयमसौ सञ्ज्ञा, किन्तु अन्येयं संहोति यमसूर्ययोर्बुद्धिरभूदित्यर्थः ॥५॥

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Chāyā remained in Saṃjñā's form. Favoring her own three children, Chāyā cursed Yama when Yama raised his foot to strike her. The Sun and Yama saw her extreme harshness and concluded that she was another woman, not Saṃjñā.

VP 3.2.6

तामश्वां तपसि स्थिताम् (tāmaśvāṃ tapasi sthitām) — her as a mare, engaged in austerity
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tato vivasvānākhyāte tayaivāraṇyasaṃsthitām|samādhidṛṣṭyā dadṛśe tāmaśvāṃ tapasi sthitām

Śrīdhara

tataścātinirbandhaṃ pṛṣṭayā tayā nāhaṃ sañjñā kintu tasyāśchāyeti ākhyāte vivasvān sūryaḥ samādhinottarakuruṣvaraṇye aśvāṃ vaḍavārūpāṃ tapasi sthitāṃ sañjñāṃ dadṛśe dadarśa । tataḥ so'pi vivasvānaśvarūpadharastasyāmaśvinau devau revantañceti trayamajījanat । retaso'nta iti revantanāmaniruktiḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततश्चातिनिर्बन्धं पृष्टया तया नाहं सञ्ज्ञा किन्तु तस्याश्छायेति आख्याते विवस्वान् सूर्यः समाधिनोत्तरकुरुष्वरण्ये अश्वां वडवारूपां तपसि स्थितां सञ्ज्ञां ददृशे ददर्श । ततः सोऽपि विवस्वानश्वरूपधरस्तस्यामश्विनौ देवौ रेवन्तञ्चेति त्रयमजीजनत् । रेतसोऽन्त इति रेवन्तनामनिरुक्तिः

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

After persistent questioning, Chāyā told Vivasvān that she was not Saṃjñā but her shadow. Vivasvān saw Saṃjñā in Uttarakuru through meditation, in a mare's form and engaged in austerity. Vivasvān took a horse's form and fathered the two Aśvins and Revanta. The phrase at the end of the semen explains the name Revanta.

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Śrīdhara identifies Bhrami as a wheel-shaped carpenter's lathe. Śrīdhara reads the eighth part as imperishable and not cut away.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवमेव सर्वकल्पमन्वन्तरादिस्थितिं सप्रपञ्चमाह—ततः प्रबुद्ध इत्यादिना यावदध्यायसमाप्तिः

उपसंहरति—एवमिति द्वाभ्याम् । अत्र वा अन्यत्र वा कल्पादौ तस्माद् व्यतिरिक्तं यद्भूतादि तन्नास्ति, किन्तु भेदकल्पनाशून्यो भगवानेवास्तीत्येष एव सद्भावः परमार्थः कथित इति

Commentary in the Śrīdhara recension.

Śrīdhara explains that these verses elaborate the states of each Kalpa, Manvantara, and related cycle. The explanation begins with the Lord's awakening and continues through the end of the chapter.

Śrīdhara treats verses 58 and 59 as the chapter's conclusion. The passage says that nothing exists apart from Bhagavān, who is free from imagined distinctions. This alone is the ultimate truth.
