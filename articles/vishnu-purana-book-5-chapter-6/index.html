VP 5.6.1

कदाचिच्छकटस्याधः शयानो मधुसूदनः ।
चिक्षेप चरणावूर्ध्वं स्तन्यार्थी प्ररुरोद ह

Sri Paraasharar - Below the cart, they had made a small bed for Krishna, and He was sleeping there. He felt hungry, and He wanted to be fed with milk. He lifted His small feet and kicked the cart. He cried loudly, asking for milk.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara commentary.

VP 5.6.2

तस्य पादप्रहारेण शकटं परिवर्तितम् ।
विध्वस्तकुंभभाण्डं तद्विपरीतं पपात वै

As soon as child Krishna kicked the cart, with His feet, immediately, that whole cart turned upside down, and all the vessels kept on that, were completely broken, destroyed. The pots which were kept, and instruments kept on the cart were destroyed. A raakshasa by name Mura had come in the form of that cart, Shakata, and nobody knew that. That Shakata was completely shattered into pieces, having been turned upside down. That his name is Mura is told in Harivamsha, he is known as Shakataasura.

Transliteration (IAST)
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tasya pādaprahāreṇa śakaṭaṃ parivartitam | vidhvastakuṃbhabhāṇḍaṃ tadviparītaṃ papāta vai

Śrīdhara

tasyeti | vidhvasta-kumbha-bhāṇḍaṃ viśīrṇa-kumbhādy-upakaraṇam | kupya-bhāṇḍām iti pāṭhe kupyaṃ svarṇa-rajata-vyatiriktaṃ dravyam || 2 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तस्येति । विध्वस्त-कुम्भ-भाण्डं विशीर्ण-कुम्भाद्य्-उपकरणम् । कुप्य-भाण्डाम् इति पाठे कुप्यं स्वर्ण-रजत-व्यतिरिक्तं द्रव्यम् ॥२॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

No separate English commentary is supplied.

VP 5.6.3

ततो हाहाकृतः सर्वो गोपरोपीजनो द्विज ।
आजगामाथ ददृशे बालमुत्तानशायिनम्

All the people around there, the gopaas and gopis, were suddenly disturbed, having heard this great sound, of this cart getting totally destroyed, and all the pots thrown away and broken, and they made a lot of noise. They did not know what had happened. Immediately, all of them came and saw the boy lying on His back there.

VP 5.6.4

When they came there, they asked - "Who did this, who did this?", "Who was responsible for turning this cart upside down like this?". The kids who were playing around there, they said that this baby who is lying here, did like this - that baby only kicked and did all of this.

VP 5.6.8

गर्गश्च गोकुले तत्र वसुदेवप्रचोदितः ।
प्रच्छन्न एव गोपानां संस्कारानकरोत्तयोः

Vasudeva sent Garga Muni to Gokula, secretly. Secretly, without revealing his identity, without anyone knowing, Garga Rishi, did naamakarana samskaara to the boys.

Transliteration (IAST)
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

gargaśca gokule tatra vasudevapracoditaḥ | pracchanna eva gopānāṃ saṃskārānakarottayoḥ

Śrīdhara

gargaś ceti | gopānāṃ pracchanna ity atra śukoktir anusandheyā | gargo h gokulam āgato nandena bālayor nāma-karaṇārthaṃ yadā prārthitas tadā tenoktaṃ—ahaṃ tāvad yadukulācāryaḥ prakhyātaḥ | tvaṃ ca vasudevasya preṣṭhaḥ sakhā aśarīra-dārikā-vākyābhyāṃ ca devakyāḥ putraḥ svasya śatruḥ kvacij jāto’stīti kaṃso manyate | kiṃ ca, sa tu mayā yadu-purodhasā nāma karmādi-kṛte jñātvā devakī-putra eva kathañcid gokulaṃ prāpto nandasya abhimata iti jāta-śaṅkaḥ kaṃso yadi vā syāt tarhi mahān anarthaḥ syād iti | tadā nanenoktaṃ madīyair gopair avijñātaḥ san rahasi dvijocitaṃ saṃskāra-mātraṃ kuru iti | tad etad uktaṃ—pracchanna eva gopānāṃ saṃskārān akarot tayoḥ iti | uttaratra mahā-matir iti || 8 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

गर्गश् चेति । गोपानां प्रच्छन्न इत्य् अत्र शुकोक्तिर् अनुसन्धेया । गर्गो ह् गोकुलम् आगतो नन्देन बालयोर् नाम-करणार्थं यदा प्रार्थितस् तदा तेनोक्तं—अहं तावद् यदुकुलाचार्यः प्रख्यातः । त्वं च वसुदेवस्य प्रेष्ठः सखा अशरीर-दारिका-वाक्याभ्यां च देवक्याः पुत्रः स्वस्य शत्रुः क्वचिज् जातोऽस्तीति कंसो मन्यते । किं च, स तु मया यदु-पुरोधसा नाम कर्मादि-कृते ज्ञात्वा देवकी-पुत्र एव कथञ्चिद् गोकुलं प्राप्तो नन्दस्य अभिमत इति जात-शङ्कः कंसो यदि वा स्यात् तर्हि महान् अनर्थः स्याद् इति । तदा ननेनोक्तं मदीयैर् गोपैर् अविज्ञातः सन् रहसि द्विजोचितं संस्कार-मात्रं कुरु इति । तद् एतद् उक्तं—प्रच्छन्न एव गोपानां संस्कारान् अकरोत् तयोः इति । उत्तरत्र महा-मतिर् इति ॥८॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

No separate English commentary is supplied.

VP 5.6.43

मार्गा बभूवुरस्पष्टास्तृणशष्पचयावृताः ।
अर्थान्तरमनुप्राप्ताः प्रजडानामिवोक्तयः

The roads became covered with all the plants. One could not even see where there was a road, or not. Because plants were grown all over. It was as though the ignorant ones speak with one intention, and it gets conveyed in a different intention; where they want to say something and they say something else. Whatever they want to say, it conveys a different meaning. In the same way, the paths were all covered by plants and one could not make out which was a path, and which was not. This was not clearly noticeable.
