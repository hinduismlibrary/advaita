Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 7: Marriage, Marriage Rites and Sacred Regions

BP 1.7.1

ब्रह्मोवाच३
असपिण्डा च या मातुरसगोत्रा च या पितुः ।
सा प्रशस्ता द्विजातीनां दारकर्मणि मैथुने ॥ १

Brahmā spoke: A woman who is not a close blood-relative on her mother's side and is not of her father's paternal lineage is approved for marriage among the twice-born.

BP 1.7.2

सहजो न भवेद्यस्या न च विज्ञायते पिता ।
नोपयच्छेत तां प्राज्ञः पुत्रिकाधर्मशङ्कया ॥ २

A discerning man should not marry a woman who has no brother or whose father is unknown, for fear of the appointed-daughter custom.

BP 1.7.3

ब्राह्मणानां प्रशस्ता स्यात्सवर्णा दारकर्मणि ।
कामशस्तु प्रवृत्तानामिमाः स्युः क्रमशोऽवराः ॥ ३

For a Brahmin, a woman of the same varṇa is approved in marriage; for those moved by desire, the others are lower in order.

BP 1.7.4

क्षत्रस्यापि सवर्णा स्यात्प्रथमा द्विजसत्तमाः ।
द्वे चावरे तथा प्रोक्ते कामतस्तु न धर्मतः ॥४

For a Kshatriya, a woman of the same varṇa is first; two lower ones are also declared, by desire but not by duty.

BP 1.7.5

वैश्यस्यैका वरा प्रोक्ता सवर्णा चैव धर्मतः ।
तथावरा कामतस्तु द्वितीया न तु धर्मतः ॥ ५

For a Vaishya, one of the same varṇa is declared best by duty; a second, lower one is for desire, not duty.

BP 1.7.6

शूद्रैव भार्या शूद्रस्य धर्मतो मनुरब्रवीत् ।
चतुर्णामपि वर्णानां परिणेता द्विजोत्तमः ॥ ६

Manu declared that a Śūdra woman alone is the dutiful wife of a Śūdra. A Brahmin, highest of the twice-born, may marry from all four varṇas.

BP 1.7.7

न ब्राह्मणक्षत्रिययोरापद्यपि हि तिष्ठतोः ।
कस्मिंश्चिदपि वृतान्ते शूद्रा भार्योपदिश्यते ॥ ७

For a Brahmin or Kshatriya who maintains his station, a Śūdra wife is not prescribed, even in adversity or any circumstance.

BP 1.7.8

हीनजातिस्त्रियं मोहादुद्वहन्तो द्विजातयः ।
कुलान्येव नयन्त्याशु ससन्तानानि शूद्रताम् ॥ ८

Twice-born men who through delusion marry women of lower birth quickly bring their families and descendants to Śūdra status.

BP 1.7.9

शूद्रमारोप्य वेद्यां तु पतितोत्रिर्बभूव ह ।
उतथ्यः पुत्रजननात्पतितत्वमवाप्तवान् ॥ ९

Atri fell from his standing after placing a Śūdra woman on the marriage altar, and Utathya incurred fallen status by fathering a son through one.

BP 1.7.10

शूद्रस्य पुत्रमासाद्य शौनकः शूद्रतां गतः ।
भृग्वादयोप्येवमेव पतितत्वमवाप्नुयुः ॥.7.१०

Śaunaka, having accepted a son born to a Śūdra woman, became a Śūdra; Bhṛgu and the others would likewise incur fallen status.

BP 1.7.11

शूद्रां शयनमारोप्य ब्राह्मणो यात्यधोगतिम् ।
जनयित्वा सुतं तस्यां ब्राह्मण्यादेव हीयते ॥१

A Brahmin who takes a Śūdra woman to his bed goes to a lower state; by fathering a son through her, he loses his Brahmin status.

BP 1.7.12

देवपित्र्यातिथेयानि तत्प्रधानानि यस्य तु ।
नादन्ति पितरो देवाः स च स्वर्गं न गच्छति ॥२

For one whose rites depend on her, ancestors and gods do not accept offerings made to gods, ancestors, and guests, and he does not reach heaven.

BP 1.7.13

वृषलीफेनपीतस्य निःश्वासोपहतस्य च ।
तस्यां चैव प्रसूतस्य निष्कृतिर्न विधीयते ॥३

No expiation is prescribed for one who has drunk foam from a Śūdra woman's lips, been affected by her breath, or fathered a child through her.

BP 1.7.14

चतुर्णामपि विप्रेन्द्राः प्रेत्येह च हिताहितम् ।
समासतो ब्रवीम्येष विवाहाष्टकमुत्तमम् ॥४

O foremost Brahmins, I shall briefly explain what is beneficial and harmful here and after death for all four classes: the excellent teaching on the eight forms of marriage.

BP 1.7.15

कालो दैवस्तथा चार्षः प्राजापत्यस्तथासुरः ।
गान्धर्वो राक्षसश्चैव पैशाचश्चाष्टमोऽधमः ॥५

The learned-gift, divine-gift, seer's-gift, progenitor's, payment, mutual-desire, forceful-abduction, and the eighth and lowest, secret-violation forms.

BP 1.7.16

ये यस्य धर्मा वर्णस्य गुणदोषौ च यस्य यौ ।
शृणुध्वं तद्द्विजश्रेष्ठाः प्रसवे च गुणागुणम् ॥६

Listen, O best of the twice-born, to which forms accord with the duty of each varṇa, to the merits and faults of each, and to the qualities or faults they produce in offspring.

BP 1.7.17

विप्रस्य चतुरः पूर्वान्क्षत्रस्य चतुरोऽवरान् ।
विट्शूद्रयोस्तु त्रीनेव विद्याद्धर्मानराक्षसान् ॥७

Know the first four forms for a Brahmin and the latter four for a Kshatriya; for Vaishyas and Śūdras, know three forms as lawful, not the abduction form.

BP 1.7.18

चतुरो ब्राह्मणस्याद्यान्प्रशस्तान्कवयो विदुः ।
राक्षसं क्षत्रियस्यैकमासुरं वैश्यशूद्रयोः ॥८

The learned recognize the first four forms as approved for a Brahmin, the abduction form alone for a Kshatriya, and the payment form for Vaishyas and Śūdras.

BP 1.7.19

क्षत्रियाणां त्रयो धर्म्या द्वावधर्म्यौ२ स्मृताविह ।
पैशाचश्चासुरश्चैव न कर्त्तव्यौ कथञ्चन ॥९

Three forms are held lawful for Kshatriyas and two contrary to duty; the violation and payment forms should never be performed.

BP 1.7.20

पृथक्पृथग्वा मिश्रौ वा विवाहौ पूर्वचोदितौ ।
गान्धर्वो राक्षसश्चैव धर्म्यौ क्षत्रस्य तौ स्मृतौ ॥.7.२०

The mutual-desire and abduction marriages, performed separately or combined as stated before, are held lawful for a Kshatriya.

BP 1.7.21

आच्छाद्य चार्चयित्वा तु श्रुतशीलवते स्वयम् ।
आहूय दानं कन्याया बाह्यो धर्मः प्रकीर्तितः ॥१

After clothing and honoring a man of Vedic learning and good character, the father himself summons him and gives the maiden; this is called the learned-gift marriage.

BP 1.7.22

वितते चापि यज्ञे तु कर्म कुर्वति चार्त्विजि ।
अलङ्कृत्य सुतादानं दैवो धर्म उदाहृतः ॥२

When a sacrifice is underway, giving an adorned daughter to the officiating priest who is performing the rite is called the divine-gift marriage.

BP 1.7.23

एकं गोमिथुनं द्वे वा वरादादाय धर्मतः ।
कन्याप्रदानं विधिवदार्षीयो धर्म उच्यते ॥३

When one or two pairs of cattle are received lawfully from the bridegroom and the maiden is then given according to rule, it is called the seer's-gift marriage.

BP 1.7.24

सहोभौ चरतं धर्ममिति वाचानुभाष्य तु ।
कन्याप्रदानमभ्यर्च प्राजापत्यविधिः स्मृतः ॥४

After honoring the couple and saying, May you both practise duty together, the giving of the maiden is remembered as the progenitor's marriage.

BP 1.7.25

ज्ञातिभ्यो द्रविणं दत्त्वा कन्यायाश्चैव शक्तितः ।
कन्याप्रदानं स्वच्छन्दादासुरो धर्म उच्यते ॥५

When wealth is given according to one's means to the maiden and her relatives, and she is given by mutual consent, it is called the payment marriage.

BP 1.7.26

इच्छयान्योऽन्यसंयोगः कन्यायाश्च वरस्य च ।
गान्धर्वः स विधिर्ज्ञेयो मैथुन्यः कामसम्भवः ॥६

The voluntary union of a maiden and bridegroom through mutual desire is the mutual-desire marriage, a sexual union arising from love.

BP 1.7.27

हत्वा छित्त्वा च भित्त्वा च क्रोशन्तीं रुदतीं गृहात् ।
प्रसह्य कन्याहरणं राक्षसो विधिरुच्यते ॥७

The forcible taking of a crying and weeping maiden from her home after killing, wounding, and breaking resistance is called the forceful-abduction marriage.

BP 1.7.28

सुप्तां मत्तां प्रमत्तां च रहो यत्रोपगच्छति ।
स पापिष्ठो विवाहानां पैशाचः कथितोऽष्टमः ॥८

When a man secretly violates a woman who is asleep, intoxicated, or unconscious, that most sinful union is described as the eighth, the secret-violation marriage.

BP 1.7.29

जलपूर्वं द्विजाग्र्याणां कन्यादानं प्रशस्यते ।
इतरेषां तु वर्णानामितरेतरकाम्यया ॥९

For the foremost twice-born, giving a maiden preceded by an offering of water is praised; for the other varṇas, marriage may take place through mutual desire.

BP 1.7.30

यो यस्यैषां विवाहानां विभूनां कीर्तितो गुणः ।
तं निबोधत वै विप्राः सम्यक्तकीर्तयतो मम ॥.7.३०

O Brahmins, now hear the particular merit attributed to each of these forms of marriage as I explain it correctly.

BP 1.7.31

कुलानि दश पूर्वाणि तथान्यानि दशैव तु ।
स हि तान्यात्मना चैवं मोचयत्येनसो ध्रुवम् ॥१

He surely releases ten family lines before him and ten after him, and himself, from wrongdoing.

BP 1.7.32

ब्राह्मीपुत्रः सुकृतकृद्दैवोढाजं सुतं शृणु ।
दैवोढाजः सुतो विप्राः सप्त सप्त परावरान् ॥
आर्षोढाजः सुतः स्त्रीणां पुरुषांस्तारयेद्द्विजाः ॥२

The son of a learned-gift marriage does good; now hear of a son born from a divine-gift marriage. O Brahmins, he delivers seven generations before and after. A son of a seer's-gift marriage delivers three generations of men.

BP 1.7.33

ब्रह्मादिषु विवाहेषु चतुर्ष्वेवानुपूर्वशः ।
ब्रह्मवर्चस्विनः पुत्रा जायन्ते शिष्टसम्मताः ॥३

In the first four marriages beginning with the learned-gift form, sons endowed with sacred radiance are born in due succession and approved by the learned.

BP 1.7.34

रूपसत्त्वगुणोपेता धनवन्तो यशस्विनः ।
पुत्रवन्तोऽथ धर्मिष्ठा जीवन्ति च शतं समाः ॥४

They are endowed with beauty, strength, and virtue; wealthy, renowned, blessed with sons, righteous, and they live for a hundred years.

BP 1.7.35

इतरेषु निबोधध्वं नृशंसानृतवादिनः ।
जायन्ते दुर्विवाहेषु ब्रह्मधर्मद्विषः सुताः ॥५

Understand that from the other, improper marriages are born sons who are cruel, speak falsehood, and oppose sacred duty.

BP 1.7.36

अनिन्दितैः स्त्रीविवाहैरनिन्द्या भवति प्रजा ।
निन्दितैर्निंदिता नृणां तस्मान्निद्यान्विवर्जयेत् ॥६

From blameless forms of marriage blameless offspring arise; from censured forms, censured offspring arise among people. Therefore one should avoid the censured forms.

BP 1.7.37

करग्रहणसंस्काराः सवर्णासु भवन्ति वै ।
असवर्णास्वयं ज्ञेया विधिरुद्वाहकर्मणि ॥७

The hand-taking rite applies to women of the same varṇa; for women of a different varṇa, the following procedure is to be understood in the marriage rite.

BP 1.7.38

बाणः क्षत्रियया ग्राह्यः प्रतोदो वैश्यकन्यया ।
वसनस्य दशा ग्राह्या शूद्रयोत्कृष्टवेदने ॥८

A Kshatriya woman should take an arrow, a Vaishya woman a goad, and a Śūdra woman the hem of the garment when joined to a man of higher varṇa.

BP 1.7.39

न कन्यायाः पिता विद्वान्गृह्णीयाच्छुल्कमण्वपि ।
गृह्णन्हि शुल्कं लोभेन स्यान्नरोऽपत्यविक्रयी ॥९

A learned father of a maiden should not accept even the smallest bride-price; a man who accepts payment from greed becomes a seller of his offspring.

BP 1.7.40

स्त्रीधनानि तु ये मोहादुपजीवन्ति बान्धवाः ।
नारीयानानि वस्त्रं वा ते पापा यान्त्यधोगतिम् ॥.7.४०

Relatives who through delusion live from a woman's property, her conveyances, or her clothing are sinful and go to a lower state.

BP 1.7.41

आर्षे गोमिथुनं शुल्कं केचिदाहुर्मृषैव तत् ।
अल्पो वापि महान्वापि विक्रयस्तावदेव सः ॥१

Some call the pair of cattle in the seer's-gift marriage a bride-price, but this is false. Whether small or great, a payment is indeed a sale.

BP 1.7.42

यासां नाददते शुल्कं ज्ञातयो न स विक्रयः ।
अर्हणं तत्कुमारीणामानृशंस्यं च केवलम् ॥२

Where the relatives accept no bride-price, it is not a sale; that gift is simply an honoring of the maidens and an act of kindness.

BP 1.7.43

इत्थं दारान्समासाद्य देशमग्र्यं समावसेत् ।
ब्राह्मणो द्विजशार्दूल य इच्छेद्विपुलं यशः ॥३

Having thus obtained a wife, a Brahmin who desires abundant renown should live in an excellent region, O tiger among the twice-born.

BP 1.7.44

ऋषय ऊचुः
को देशः परमो ब्रह्मन्कश्च पुण्यो मतस्तव ।
प्रवसन्यत्र विप्रेन्द्र यशः प्राप्नोति कञ्जज ॥४

The seers said: Which region is supreme, O Brahmā, and which do you consider sacred? O foremost of Brahmins, by dwelling where does one obtain renown?

BP 1.7.45

ब्रह्मोवाच
न हीयते यत्र धर्मश्चतुष्पात्स कलो द्विजाः ।
स देशः परमो विप्राः स च पुण्यो मतो मम ॥५

Brahmā spoke: O twice-born sages, the region where duty does not lose any of its four feet is supreme; O Brahmins, that region I consider sacred.

BP 1.7.46

विद्वद्भिः सेवितो धर्मो यस्मिन्देशे प्रवर्तते ।
शास्त्रोक्तश्चापि विप्रेन्द्राः स देशः परमो मतः ॥६

The region where duty practised by learned people and duty declared in śāstra prevail is considered supreme, O foremost Brahmins.

BP 1.7.47

ऋषय ऊचू
विद्वद्भिः सेवितं धर्मं शास्त्रोक्तं च सुरोत्तम ।
वदास्मासु सुरश्रेष्ठ कौतुकं परमं हि नः ॥७

The seers said: O best of the gods, explain to us the duty practised by the learned and declared in śāstra, for our curiosity is great.

BP 1.7.48

ब्रह्मोवाच
विद्वद्भिः सेवितः सद्भिर्नित्यमद्वेषरागिभिः ।
हृदयेनाभ्यनुज्ञातो यो धर्मस्तं निबोधत ॥८

Brahmā spoke: Understand that duty which is continually practised by learned and virtuous people free from hatred and attachment, and which is approved by the heart.

BP 1.7.49

कामात्मता न प्रशस्ता न वेहास्याप्यकामता ।
काम्यो हि वेदाधिगमः कर्मयोगश्च वैदिकः ॥९

A nature ruled by desire is not praised, but neither is absence of desire here; the study of the Veda and Vedic rites are indeed undertaken for a desired end.

BP 1.7.50

सङ्कल्पाज्जायते कामो यज्ञाद्यानि च सर्वशः ।
व्रताः नियमधर्माश्च सर्वे सङ्कल्पजाः स्मृताः ॥.7.५०

From intention arises desire, and from intention arise sacrifices and all similar acts. Vows, observances, and duties are all said to arise from intention.

BP 1.7.51

कामादृते क्रियाकारी दृश्यते नेह कर्हिचित् ।
यद्यद्धि कुरुते कश्चित्तत्तत्कामस्य चेष्टितम् ॥१

No one acting without desire is ever seen here; whatever anyone does is the action of desire.

BP 1.7.52

निगमो धर्ममूलं स्यात्स्मृतिशीले तथैव च ।
तथाचारश्च साधूनामात्मनस्तुष्टिरेव च ॥२

The Veda is the root of duty, as are remembered tradition and conduct; likewise the practice of virtuous people and one's own inward satisfaction.

BP 1.7.53

सर्वं तु समवेक्षेत निश्चयं ज्ञानचक्षुषा ।
श्रुतिप्राधान्यतो विद्वान्स्वधर्मे निवसेत वै ॥३

A learned person should examine everything decisively with the eye of knowledge and, giving primacy to revelation, remain established in his own duty.

BP 1.7.54

श्रुतिस्मृत्युदितं धर्ममनुतिष्ठन्सदा नरः ।
प्राप्य चेह परां कीर्तिं याति शक्रसलोकताम् ॥४

A person who always follows the duty declared in revelation and remembered tradition obtains great renown here and reaches the world of Indra.

BP 1.7.55

श्रुतिस्तु वेदो विज्ञेयो धर्मशास्त्रं तु वै स्मृतिः ।
ते सर्वार्थेषु मीमांस्ये ताभ्यां धर्मो हि निर्बभौ ॥५

Revelation is to be understood as the Veda, and the treatise on duty as remembered tradition. Both are to be examined in every matter, for duty has emerged from them.

BP 1.7.56

योऽवमन्येत ते चोभे हेतुशास्त्राश्रयाद्द्विजः ।
स साधुभिर्बहिष्कार्यो नास्तिको वेदनिन्दकः ॥६

A twice-born person who despises both, relying on reasoning alone, should be excluded by the virtuous as an unbeliever and reviler of the Veda.

BP 1.7.57

वेदः स्मृतिः सदाचारः स्वस्य च प्रियमात्मनः ।
एतच्चतुर्विधं विप्राः साक्षाद्धर्मस्य लक्षणम् ॥७

The Veda, remembered tradition, good conduct, and what is pleasing to oneself—these four, O Brahmins, are the direct marks of duty.

BP 1.7.58

धर्मज्ञानं भवेद्विप्रा अर्थकामेष्वसज्जताम् ।
धर्मं जिज्ञासमानानां प्रमाणान्नैगमं परम् ॥८

Knowledge of duty is for those unattached to wealth and desire, O Brahmins; for those seeking to know duty, the Vedic text is the highest authority.

BP 1.7.59

निषेकादिश्मशानान्तो मन्त्रैर्यस्योदितो विधिः ।
अधिकारो भवेत्तस्य वेदेषु च जपेषु च ॥९

One whose prescribed rites with mantras extend from conception through cremation becomes entitled to the Vedas and sacred recitation.

BP 1.7.60

सरस्वतीदृषद्वत्योर्देवनद्योर्यदन्तरम् ।
तदेव निर्मितं देशं ब्रह्मावर्तं प्रचक्षते ॥.7.६०

The land between the divine rivers Sarasvatī and Dṛṣadvatī is called Brahmāvarta, the fashioned region.

BP 1.7.61

यस्मिन् देशे य आचारः पारम्पर्यक्रमागतः ।
वर्णानां सान्तरालानां स सदाचार उच्यते ॥१

The practice in a region, handed down in succession among the varṇas and intermediate groups, is called good conduct.

BP 1.7.62

कुरुक्षेत्रं च मत्स्याश्च पञ्चालाः शूरसेनयः ।
एष ब्रह्मर्षिदेशो वै ब्रह्मावर्तादनन्तरम् ॥२

Kurukṣetra, the Matsyas, the Pañcālas, and the Śūrasenas form the land of the Brahmin sages, immediately adjoining Brahmāvarta.

BP 1.7.63

एतद्देशप्रसूतस्य सकाशादग्रजन्मनः ।
स्वं स्वं चरित्रं शिक्षन्ति पृथिव्यां सर्वमानवाः ॥३

All people on earth learn their respective conduct from a Brahmin born in that region.

BP 1.7.64

हिमवद्विन्ध्ययोर्मध्ये यत्प्राग्विनशनादपि ।
प्रत्यगेव प्रयागाच्च मध्यदेशः प्रकीर्तितः ॥४

The region between the Himālaya and Vindhya, east of Vinaśana and west of Prayāga, is called the Middle Region.

BP 1.7.65

आ समुद्रात्तु वै पूर्वादासमुद्रात्तु पश्चिमात् ।
तयोरेवान्तरं गिर्योरार्यावर्त्तं विदुर्बुधाः ॥५

The wise know as the Aryan Realm the land extending from the eastern sea to the western sea and lying between those two mountain ranges.

BP 1.7.66

अटते यत्र कृष्णा गौर्मृगो नित्यं स्वभावतः ।
स ज्ञेयो याज्ञिको देशो म्लेच्छदेशस्त्वतः परः ॥६

The region where the black antelope naturally roams is known as land fit for sacrifice; beyond it is the land of outsiders.

BP 1.7.67

एतान्नित्यं शुभान्देशान्संश्रयेत द्विजोत्तमः ।
यस्मिन्कस्मिंश्च निवसेत्पादजो२ वृत्तिकर्शितः ॥७

A foremost twice-born person should always resort to these auspicious regions; one born from the feet may live wherever, when distressed by the need for a livelihood.

BP 1.7.68

प्रकीर्तितेयं धर्मस्य बुधैर्योनिर्द्विजोत्तमाः ।
सम्भवश्चास्य सर्वस्य समासान्न तु विस्तरात् ॥८

O best of the twice-born, the wise have proclaimed this source of duty and the origin of all this briefly, not at length.
