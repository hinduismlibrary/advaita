Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 8: Householder’s Conduct and the Protection of Wives

BP 1.8.1

ब्रह्मोवाच
कर्तव्यं यद्गृहस्थेन तदिदानीं निबोधत ।
गदतो द्विजशार्दूल विस्तराच्छास्त्रतस्तथा ॥ १

Brahmā said: Now learn what a householder should do, O tiger among the twice-born, as I tell it at length according to the śāstras.

BP 1.8.2

वैवाहिकेऽग्नौ कुर्वीत गृह्यं कर्म यथाविधि ।
शुभदेशाश्रयश्चैव पत्नी वैवाहिकी गृहे ॥ २

‘He should perform the domestic rites according to rule in the fire kindled for marriage, reside in an auspicious region, and keep his lawfully wedded wife in the household.’

BP 1.8.3

स्वाश्रयेण विना शक्यं न यस्माद्रक्षणादिकम् ।
वित्तानामिव दाराणामतस्तद्विधिरुच्यते ॥ ३

‘Because protection and related duties are not possible without a secure refuge, whether for wealth or for a wife, the procedure concerning that refuge is now explained.’

BP 1.8.4

हेतवो हि त्रिवर्गस्य विपरीतास्तु१ मानद ।
अरक्षणाद्भवन्त्यस्मादमीषां रक्षणं मतम् ॥ ४

The causes of the three aims, O giver of honour, become their opposites when unprotected; therefore their protection is considered necessary.

BP 1.8.5

निसर्गात्पुंस्यसन्तोषाद्गुणदोषविमर्षतः ।
दुष्टानां चापि संसर्गाद्रक्ष्या एव च योषितः ॥ ५

Because of their nature, men’s dissatisfaction, discernment of qualities and faults, and association with the wicked, women are indeed to be protected.

BP 1.8.6

पुरुषस्थानवेश्मानि त्रिविधं प्राहुराश्रयम् ।
वित्तानां रक्षणाद्यर्थमपूर्वाधिगमाय च ॥ ६

‘A man, a suitable locality, and a dwelling are said to constitute the threefold refuge, both for protecting wealth and for acquiring what has not yet been obtained.’

BP 1.8.7

कुलीनो नीतिमान्प्राज्ञः सत्यसन्धो दृढव्रतः ।
विनीतो धार्मिकस्त्यागी२ विज्ञेयः पुरुषाश्रयः ॥ ७

‘A man who provides protection should be known as well-born, versed in policy, discerning, true to his word, firm in vows, disciplined, devoted to dharma, and generous.’

BP 1.8.8

नगरे खर्वटे खेटे ग्रामे चापि क्रमागते ।
यात्रावशाद्वा निवसेद्धार्मिकाद्यजनान्विते ॥ ८

‘He may reside in a city, market-town, small town, or ancestral village, or settle while travelling in a place inhabited by people devoted to dharma and similar virtues.’

BP 1.8.9

गुरुणानुमतस्तत्र ग्रामण्यादिजनेन वा ।
प्रतिवेश्माद्यबाधेन शुद्धं कुर्यान्निवेशनम् ॥ ९

‘With the permission of a teacher or the village head and other local people, he should establish a clean dwelling that does not obstruct neighbouring houses.’

BP 1.8.10

द्वारचत्वरशालानां सर्वकारुकवेश्मनाम् ।
द्यूतसूनासुरावेशनटराजानुजीविनाम् ॥.8.१०

‘The dwelling and livelihood should be situated away from entrances, public squares and halls, the houses of every kind of artisan, gambling places, slaughterhouses, liquor establishments, performers, and dependants of the king—’

BP 1.8.11

पाखण्डदेववीथीनां राजमार्गकुलस्य च ।
दूरात्सुगुप्तं कर्तव्या जीविका विभवोचिता ॥१

‘—and also away from heterodox establishments, temple thoroughfares, royal roads, and crowded family compounds. It should be well protected, and the livelihood should be suited to one’s means.’

BP 1.8.12

सापिधानैकनिष्काशं शुद्धपृष्टं समन्ततः ।
सद्वृत्ताप्तजनाकीर्णमदुष्टप्रातिवेशिकम् ॥२

‘The house should have a covered entrance and a single controlled exit, be thoroughly clean on every side, filled with trustworthy people of good conduct, and have neighbours of uncorrupted character.’

BP 1.8.13

प्रागुदक्प्रवणे देशे वास्तुविद्याविधानतः ।
प्रविभक्तक्रियाकाञ्क्षं सर्वर्तुकमनोहरम् ॥३

‘On ground sloping eastward or northward, it should be laid out according to architectural science, with spaces divided according to their functions and made pleasant in every season.’

BP 1.8.14

अर्चास्नानोदकागारगोष्ठागारमहानसैः ।
युक्तं गोवाजिशालाभिः सदासीभृत्यकाश्रयैः ॥४

‘It should contain rooms for worship, bathing and water, cattle enclosures, storerooms and a kitchen, together with stalls for cattle and horses and quarters for maidservants and other attendants.’

BP 1.8.15

बहिरन्तः पुरस्त्रीकं सर्वोपकरणैर्युतम् ।
विभक्तशयनोद्देशमाप्तवृद्धैरधिष्ठितम् ॥५

‘It should include protected outer and inner women’s quarters, possess every required implement, have separately assigned sleeping places, and be supervised by trustworthy elders.’

BP 1.8.16

अरक्षणाद्धि दाराणां वर्णसङ्करजादयः ।
दृष्टा हि बहवो दोषास्तस्माद्रक्ष्याः सदा स्त्रियः ॥६

From the lack of protection of wives, many faults such as varṇasaṃkara arise; therefore women should always be protected.

BP 1.8.17

न ह्यासां प्रमदं दद्यान्न स्वातन्त्र्यं न विश्वसेत् ।
विश्वस्तवच्च चेष्टेत न्याय्यं भर्त्सनमाचरेत् ॥७

He should allow neither negligence nor freedom, nor trust them; he should act as if trusting and use fitting reproof.

BP 1.8.18

नाधिकारं क्वचिद्दद्यादृते पाकादिकर्मणः ।
स्त्रीणां ग्रामीणवत्ता हि भोगायालं सुशासिता ॥८

He should give them no authority in any matter except cooking and related work; well governed, their domestic condition suffices for enjoyment.

BP 1.8.19

नित्यं तत्कर्मयोगेन ताः कर्तव्या निरन्तराः ।
इत्येवं सर्वदा व्याप्तेः स्यादविद्यनिराश्रया ॥९

They should constantly be kept engaged in those tasks; when thus occupied at all times, ignorance has no support.

BP 1.8.20

दौर्गत्यमतिरूपं१ चाप्यसत्सङ्गः स्वतन्त्रता ।
पानाशनकथागोष्ठीप्रियत्वाकर्मशीलता२ ॥.8.२०

Hardship, excessive beauty, bad company, freedom, fondness for drinking, eating and conversation, and idleness.

BP 1.8.21

कुहकेक्षणिकामुण्डाभिक्षुकीसूतिकादिभिः ।
गोप्रसङ्गैस्तथा३ सद्भिर्लिङ्गियाचकशिल्पिभिः ॥१

‘It further lists association with deceivers, fortune-tellers, shaven-headed ascetics, mendicant women, midwives and similar visitors, as well as frequent contact involving cattle, religious mendicants, petitioners, and artisans—’

BP 1.8.22

संवाहोद्यानयात्रासूद्यानेष्वामन्त्रणादिषु ।
प्रसङ्गस्तीर्थयात्रार्थं धर्मेषु प्रकटेषु च ॥२

‘—and opportunities arising during massage, excursions and garden visits, invitations and similar occasions, journeys to sacred places, and public religious observances.’

BP 1.8.23

विप्रयोगः सदा भर्त्रा तज्ज्ञातिकुलनिःस्वता ।
अमाधुर्यकदर्यत्वे भृशं पुंसां च वाच्यता ॥३

Continual separation from the husband, poverty among his family and kin, lack of tenderness, miserliness, and severe censure by men.

BP 1.8.24

अतिक्रौर्यमतिक्षान्तिरत्यन्ताभीतिपातनम् ।
स्त्रीभिर्जितत्वमत्यर्थं सत्यं तास्ताः सदोषताः ॥४

Excessive cruelty, excessive forbearance, causing extreme fear, and being greatly overcome by women—these are faults.

BP 1.8.25

स्त्रीणां४ पत्युरधीनत्वात्पुमानेव हि निन्द्यते ।
भर्तुरेव हि तज्जाड्यं यद्भृत्यानामयोग्यता ॥५

Because women are dependent on the husband, the man himself is blamed. The unfitness of servants is the master’s own incompetence.

BP 1.8.26

तस्माद्यथोदितास्वेता रक्ष्याः शासनताडनैः ।
ताडनैश्च यथाकालं यथावत्समुपाचरेत् ॥६

Therefore such women as have been described are to be protected by rules and chastisement; one should chastise duly and at the proper time.

BP 1.8.27

परिगृह्य बहून्दारानुपचारैः समो भवेत् ।
यथाक्रमोचितैः कर्म दानसत्कारवासनैः ॥७

‘A man who has taken several wives should be impartial in his treatment of them, giving each in proper order the duties, gifts, honours, and accommodations suited to her.’

BP 1.8.28

प्रथमोऽभिजनो धर्मो योग्यत्वं च सुपुत्रता ।
पक्षे वित्तं विशेत्स्त्रीणां मानस्तत्कारणं तथा ॥८

‘Precedence among wives may derive first from family standing, then dharma, fitness, and the bearing of a worthy son; wealth may also enter the assessment, and honour should rest upon such grounds.’

BP 1.8.29

तस्मान्मानो न कर्तव्यो हेयश्चापि न तत्कृतः ।
गुरुत्वे लाघवे वापि सतां कार्यं निबन्धनम् ॥९

‘Therefore honour should not be bestowed without cause, nor should honour deserved on those grounds be withheld. Among virtuous people, superiority or inferiority should always rest upon an intelligible basis.’

BP 1.8.30

आकस्मिके प्रयुञ्जानः प्रेक्षावान्मानलाघवे ।
स यत्किञ्चनकारित्वाच्चायमेवैति लाघवम् ॥.8.३०

‘A discerning person who applies honour or slight merely by accident himself becomes slighted, because he acts arbitrarily.’

BP 1.8.31

यथा मानापमानौ हि प्रयुज्येतानिमित्ततः ।
तन्निमित्ता जनत्यागे प्रयतन्ते तदाश्रिताः ॥१

‘When honour and dishonour are assigned without a valid reason, dependants strive to abandon those persons or qualities that appear to have caused such treatment.’

BP 1.8.32

एतदेव ह्यपत्यानां१ ज्ञेयं माननकारणम् ।
यत्स्वापत्यनिमित्तेषु२ प्रधाने कुलयोग्यते ॥२

‘The same principle should be understood as the basis for honouring children: in matters concerning one’s offspring, the chief consideration is suitability to the family.’

BP 1.8.33

तत्संयोगात्सुखं पुंसां महद्दुःखं वियोगतः ।
तत्प्राप्तिः प्रति हातव्या स्वार्थायैव प्रियाण्यपि ॥३

‘Union with them brings a man great happiness and separation great sorrow. Even things dear to oneself may have to be resisted or relinquished for the sake of one’s true interest.’

BP 1.8.34

अतः स्वार्थैकनिष्ठोऽयं लोकः सर्वोऽवसीयते ।
तत्प्रसिद्धिर्भवेदस्तमानाद् भ्रान्तिविधायकः ॥४

‘From this it is concluded that the entire world is fixed upon its own interest. Recognition of that fact, arising when honour disappears, dispels mistaken perception.’

BP 1.8.35

ततो दारादिका भृत्या नियन्तव्यास्तथा द्विजाः ।
यथेहामुत्र वा श्रेयः प्राप्नुयादुत्तरोत्तरम् ॥५

‘Therefore wives, servants, and the rest should be guided in such a way, O twice-born sages, that progressively greater welfare may be obtained both here and hereafter.’

BP 1.8.36

स्त्रीणां धर्मार्थकामेषु नातिसन्धानमाचरेत् ।
तासां तेष्वभिसन्धानाद्भवेदात्माभिसंहितः ॥६

He should not deceive women regarding dharma, artha and kāma; by their intentions in these matters, he would be pursuing his own intention.

BP 1.8.37

जाया त्वर्धं शरीरस्य नृणां धर्मादिसाधने ।
नातस्तासु व्यथां काञ्चित्प्रतिकूलं समाचरेत् ॥७

‘Yet a wife is half of a man’s own body in accomplishing dharma and the other aims; therefore he should not cause her pain or act toward her in a hostile way.’

BP 1.8.38

यज्ञोत्सवादौ नाकस्मात्काञ्चिदासां विशेषयेत् ।
वस्त्रताम्बूलदानादौ प्रतिपत्तौ समो भवेत् ॥८

‘At sacrifices, festivals, and similar occasions he should not arbitrarily single out any one of them; in giving garments, betel, and other gifts, and in attentive treatment, he should be impartial.’

BP 1.8.39

प्रियाप्रियत्वं भेदो हि कामतस्तु रहोगतः ।
उपचारैः पुनर्वाक्यैस्तुल्यवृत्तिः प्रशस्यते ॥९

‘Differences of affection or dislike arising from desire belong in privacy; in outward courtesies and speech, equal conduct is praised.’

BP 1.8.40

आर्तवे तु पुनः सर्वा उपगम्याः प्रिया इव ।
पूर्वाभिजातधर्मार्था पुत्रिणी चोत्तरोत्तरम् ॥.8.४०

‘During their fertile seasons, however, all wives should be approached as beloved. Priority is assigned successively according to earlier marriage, family standing and dharma, and to one who has borne a son.’

BP 1.8.41

उदग्गच्छेदनेनैव विधिना नित्यमार्तवे ।
तुल्यवृत्तिर्यथाकालं स्वं स्वं वासमखण्डयन् ॥१

‘By this procedure he should regularly approach each wife during her fertile period, maintaining equal conduct at the proper time and not interrupting each one’s allotted residence.’

BP 1.8.42

नित्यपर्यायवासानामपादानमसून्विदुः ।
ऋतुदुःखं प्रमोदश्च तथा पूर्वं समागतः ॥२

‘For wives whose residence follows a regular rotation, denial of their turn is understood as a deprivation of life itself; the pain of the fertile period and the joy of reunion have already been described.’

BP 1.8.43

अन्यया सह यद्दुःखं सदसद्वा रहोगतम् ।
उत्कण्ठितं वा यत्किञ्चित्सपत्नीषु न तद्वसेत् ॥३

‘Whatever private sorrow, whether justified or not, occurs with another wife, or whatever longing arises, should not be communicated among co-wives.’

BP 1.8.44

यत्किञ्चिदन्यसम्बद्धमन्यथा कथितं मिथः ।
तस्य कुर्यादनिर्वेदमात्मनैव विचिन्तयेत् ॥४

‘If something concerning one wife has been related differently in private to another, he should not react with dejection; he should reflect upon it for himself.’

BP 1.8.45

अन्योऽन्यमत्सराख्यानैर्न ता वाचापि भर्त्सयेत् ।
गुणदोषौ च विज्ञाय स्वयं कुर्यान्न निष्कलौ ॥५

‘He should not rebuke them even verbally by repeating accounts born from mutual jealousy. Having himself discerned merits and faults, he should not treat either as absolute.’

BP 1.8.46

वस्त्रालङ्कारभोज्यादौ तदपत्येष्वनुक्रमात् ।
मातृदोषाननादृत्य तुल्यदृष्टिः पिता भवेत् ॥६

‘In clothing, ornaments, food, and similar provisions for their children, a father should observe due order yet maintain an equal regard, disregarding faults attributed to the mothers.’

BP 1.8.47

अन्यस्यान्यगतैर्दोषैर्दूषणं न हि नीतिमत् ।
यत्तु तेषामपत्यं तु तत्तुल्यमुभयोरपि ॥७

‘It is not just policy to condemn one person for faults belonging to another. Their offspring, moreover, belong equally to both parents.’

BP 1.8.48

प्रीतिं द्वेषमभिप्रायं शौचाशौचगतागमान् ।
बहिरन्तश्च जानीयाद्दास गूढचरैः सदा ॥८

‘Through servants and discreet observers, he should always know the affection, hostility, intentions, and reports concerning purity or misconduct both inside and outside the household.’

BP 1.8.49

आत्मानमपि विज्ञाय चित्तवृत्तेरनीश्वरम् ।
विश्वसेत कथं स्त्रीषु सर्वाविनयधामसु ॥९

Knowing even himself to be powerless over the movements of his mind, how could he trust women, all repositories of indiscipline?

BP 1.8.50

वृद्धदास्यः क्रमायाता धात्र्यश्च परिचारिकाः ।
तन्मातृपितृकाद्याश्च षण्डवृद्धाश्चरा मताः ॥.8.५०

‘Long-serving elderly maidservants, nurses, attendants, the wife’s mother, father, and other relatives, and elderly eunuchs are considered suitable observers.’

BP 1.8.51

विविधैस्तत्कथाख्यानैस्तुल्यशीलदयान्वितैः ।
प्रविश्यान्तरभिप्रायं विद्यात्काले प्रयोजितैः ॥१

‘By introducing at suitable times varied stories whose characters display comparable dispositions and compassion, he should enter into and learn the inner intention.’

BP 1.8.52

तेषु तेषु कथार्थेषु कथ्यमानेषु लक्षयेत् ।
मुखाकारादिभिर्लिङ्गैरभिप्रायं मनोगतम् ॥२

‘As those various narrative themes are being told, he should infer the thought held in the mind from signs such as the shape and expression of the face.’

BP 1.8.53

सीतारुन्धतिसम्बन्धैस्तथा शाकुन्तलादिभिः ।
सदसच्चरिताख्यानैर्भावं विद्यात्प्रवृत्तितः ॥३

‘Through narratives concerning Sītā and Arundhatī, and likewise Śakuntalā and others, portraying both good and bad conduct, he should discern a person’s disposition from the response.’

BP 1.8.54

तद्दुष्टानामदुष्टेषु साधूनामितरेषु च ।
प्रीतिः कथाप्रबन्धेषु स्यात्सख्यं पुरुषेष्वितः ॥४

The wicked delight in accounts of the virtuous, and the virtuous in accounts of others; from their narratives, friendship with men may be known.

BP 1.8.55

एवमागमदुष्टाभ्यामनुमित्या च तत्त्वतः ।
स्त्रीणां विदित्वाभिप्रायं वर्तेताशु यथोचितम् ॥५

‘Having thus learned women’s intentions in their true nature from received testimony and from inference, he should promptly act as appropriate.’

BP 1.8.56

स्त्रीभ्यो विप्रतिपन्नाभ्यः प्राणैरपि वियोजनम् ।
दृष्टं हि च यथा राज्ञामतो रक्षेत्प्रयत्नतः ॥६

Men have indeed been deprived of life by women who turned against them, as has been seen among kings; therefore one should protect them diligently.

BP 1.8.57

वेण्या गूढेन शस्त्रेण हतो राजा शुभध्वजः ।
मेखलामणिना देव्या सौवीरश्च नराधिपः ॥७

‘King Śubhadhvaja was killed by a weapon concealed in a braid, and the ruler of Sauvīra by a jewel fastened in his queen’s girdle.’

BP 1.8.58

भ्रात्रा देवीप्रयुक्तेन भद्रसेनो निपातितः ।
तथा पुत्रेण कारूषो घातितो दर्पणासिना ॥८

‘Bhadrasena was struck down by his brother at the queen’s instigation; likewise the ruler of Karūṣa was killed by his son with a sword concealed in a mirror.’

BP 1.8.59

द्वौ काशिराजौ वै वन्द्यौ चानन्दापुरयोषिता ।
विषं प्रयुज्य पञ्चत्वमानीतौ पूजितात्मकौ ॥९

‘Two honoured kings of Kāśī were brought to death by a woman of Ānandapura who employed poison against them.’

BP 1.8.60

एवमादि महाभागा राजानो ब्राह्मणाश्च ह ।
स्त्रीभिर्यत्र निपात्यन्ते तत्रान्येष्विह का कथा ॥.8.६०

‘When highly fortunate kings and brāhmaṇas have been brought down by women in examples such as these, what need is there to speak of other people here?’

BP 1.8.61

तस्मान्नित्याप्रमत्तेन जाया रक्ष्याश्च नित्यशः ।
यथावदुपचर्याश्च गुणदोषानुरूपतः ॥१

Therefore a wife should always be protected by one who is ever vigilant, and properly treated according to her merits and faults.

BP 1.8.62

वैषम्यादुपचाराणां विकारैश्चानिमित्तजैः ।
विशेषेण सपत्नीकैरकस्माच्चापि वेदनैः ॥२

‘It identifies inequality of treatment, unexplained changes of behaviour, special distinctions involving co-wives, and sudden infliction of distress as sources of conflict.’

BP 1.8.63

असम्भागे च वाग्दण्डपारुष्यादप्रसङ्गतः ।
प्रद्वेषो भर्तरि स्त्रीणां प्रकोपश्चापि जायते ॥३

‘When sharing is unequal and verbal or punitive harshness is used without cause, hatred toward the husband and anger arise among wives.’

BP 1.8.64

ततश्चायाति वार्धक्यमुद्वोढुश्चापि शत्रुताम् ।
तस्मान्न तान्प्रयुञ्जीत दोषान्दारविनाशकान् ॥४

‘Then old age arrives and hostility toward the husband also develops. Therefore he should not employ those faults that destroy marriage.’

BP 1.8.65

न चैताः स्वकुलाचारमधर्मं वापि चाञ्जसा ।
न गुणांश्चाप्युपेक्षन्ते प्रकृत्या किमु पीडिताः ॥५

They do not readily disregard family custom, dharma or virtues; how much less when they are distressed?

BP 1.8.66

सतीत्वे प्रायशः स्त्रीणां प्रदृष्टं कारणत्रयम् ।
परपुंसामसम्प्रीतिः प्रिये प्रीतिः स्वरक्षणे ॥६

‘Three causes of fidelity are generally observed among women: lack of attraction toward other men, affection for the beloved husband, and concern for their own security.’

BP 1.8.67

तस्मात्सुरक्षिता नित्यमुपचारैर्यथोचितैः ।
सुभृता१ नित्यकर्माणः कर्तव्या योषितः सदा ॥७

‘Therefore women should always be securely protected through appropriate attentions, well supported, and continuously engaged in their regular duties.’

BP 1.8.68

उत्तमां सामदानाभ्यां मध्यमाभ्यां तु मध्यमाम् ।
पश्चिमाभ्यामुभाम्यां च अधमां सम्प्रसाधयेत् ॥८

‘Using the classical four strategies, one should guide a woman of superior character through conciliation and gifts, one of middling character through the two middle means, and one of inferior character through the final two means.’

BP 1.8.69

भेददण्डौ प्रयुज्यापि प्रागपत्याद्यपेक्षया ।
तच्छिष्टानां तदा पश्चात्सामदानप्रसाधने ॥९

‘Even when division and punishment are employed, regard should first be given to children and other ties; afterward, those who remain amenable should be won through conciliation and gifts.’

BP 1.8.70

यास्तु विध्वस्तचारित्रा भर्तुश्चाहितकारिकाः ।
त्याज्या एवं स्त्रियः सद्भिः२ कालकूटविषोपमाः ॥ ७०

Women whose character is destroyed and who act against their husbands’ welfare should be abandoned by good men, like deadly Kālakūṭa poison.

BP 1.8.71

इष्टाः कुलोद्गताः साध्व्यो विनीता भर्तृवत्सलाः ।
सर्वदा साधनीयास्ताः सम्प्रदायोत्तरोत्तरैः॥ ७१

‘Those who are cherished, born of good families, virtuous, disciplined, and affectionate toward their husbands should always be supported through an ever-increasing succession of gifts and courtesies.’

BP 1.8.72

एवमेव यथोद्दिष्टं स्त्रीवृत्तं योऽनुतिष्ठति ।
प्राप्नोत्येव स सम्पूर्णं त्रिवर्गं लोकसम्भवम् ॥ ७२

‘A person who follows the conduct concerning women exactly as set forth here obtains in full the three worldly aims of dharma, wealth, and desire.’
