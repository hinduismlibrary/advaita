VP 3.5.1

यजुर्वेदतरोः शाखाः स्पत्विंशन् महामुनिः । वैशम्पायननामासौ व्यासशिष्यश् चकार वै ॥१॥

Parāśara said. Vaiśampāyana, the great sage and disciple of Vyāsa, formed twenty-seven branches of the Yajurveda tree.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara says that this chapter briefly describes the Yajurveda branches, including the Taittirīya and Vāji branches. The text gives twenty-seven principal branches of the Yajus. The Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa is cited for Āpastamba’s statement of one hundred and one Adhvaryu branches.

VP 3.5.4

VP 3.5.7

शिष्यान् आह स भो शिष्या ब्रह्महत्यापहं व्रतम् । चरध्वं मत्कृते सर्वे न विचार्यम् इदं तथा ॥७॥

Vaiśampāyana told Vaiśampāyana’s disciples to perform the vow that removes the sin of killing a Brahmin for Vaiśampāyana’s sake. Vaiśampāyana told the disciples to undertake the vow without deliberation.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara says that Vaiśampāyana set aside the question of whether one person can perform expiation for another person’s great sin. Because Vaiśampāyana had directly committed the killing, Śrīdhara understands the instruction as asking the disciples to perform the vow for Vaiśampāyana in the principal way prescribed by scripture.

VP 3.5.10

निस्तेजसो वदस्य् एतान् यत् त्वं ब्राह्मणपुङ्गवान् । तेन शिष्येण नार्थोऽस्ति ममाज्ञाभङ्गकारिणा ॥१०॥

Vaiśampāyana told Yājñavalkya that Yājñavalkya had called the excellent Brahmins lacking in vigor. Vaiśampāyana had no use for a disciple who broke Vaiśampāyana’s command.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara explains the word command-breaker by recalling that Vaiśampāyana instructed all disciples to perform the vow, while Yājñavalkya replied that Yājñavalkya alone would perform it. Yājñavalkya broke Vaiśampāyana’s command by rejecting that instruction.

VP 3.5.30

शाखाभेदास् तु तेषां वै दश पञ्च च वाजिनाम् । काण्वाद्याः सुमहाभाग याज्ञवल्क्याः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥३०॥

The Vājins have fifteen branch divisions. O greatly blessed one, the branches beginning with Kaṇva are known as Yājñavalkya branches.
