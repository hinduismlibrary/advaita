VP 3.5.1

श्रीपराशर उवाच
यजुर्वेदतरोः शाखाः सप्तविंशन् महामुनिः।
वैशंपायननामाऽसौ व्यासशिष्यश्चकार वै

Parāśara said. Vaiśampāyana, the great sage and disciple of Vyāsa, formed twenty-seven branches of the Yajurveda tree.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
यजुर्वेदतरोः शाखाः (yajurvedataroḥ śākhāḥ) — branches of the tree of the Yajurveda
सप्तविंशन् (saptaviṃśan) — twenty-seven
महामुनिः (mahāmuniḥ) — the great sage
वैशंपायननामा असौ (vaiśaṃpāyananāmā asau) — that one named Vaiśampāyana
व्यासशिष्यः (vyāsaśiṣyaḥ) — Vyāsa’s disciple
चकार (cakāra) — made
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yajurvedataroḥ śākhāḥ saptaviṃśan mahāmuniḥ|vaiśaṃpāyananāmā'sau vyāsaśiṣyaścakāra vai

Śrīdhara

pañcame’tha yajuḥśākhāḥ kathyante’tra samāsataḥ । setihāsaṃ taittirīyaṃ vājiśākhāpravarttanam ॥ saptaviṃśat saptaviṃśatiḥ yajuṣaḥ pradhānaśākhāḥ । brahmāṇḍe tu ekādhikaśatamadhvaryuśākhā āpastamboktā uktāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पञ्चमेऽथ यजुःशाखाः कथ्यन्तेऽत्र समासतः । सेतिहासं तैत्तिरीयं वाजिशाखाप्रवर्त्तनम् ॥ सप्तविंशत् सप्तविंशतिः यजुषः प्रधानशाखाः । ब्रह्माण्डे तु एकाधिकशतमध्वर्युशाखा आपस्तम्बोक्ता उक्ताः

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara says that this chapter briefly describes the Yajurveda branches, including the Taittirīya and Vāji branches. The text gives twenty-seven principal branches of the Yajus. The Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa is cited for Āpastamba’s statement of one hundred and one Adhvaryu branches.

VP 3.5.3

याज्ञवल्क्यस्तु तत्राभूद्ब्रह्मरातसुतो द्विज।
शिष्यः परमधर्मज्ञो गुरुवृत्तिपरस्सदा

Among them was Yājñavalkya, son of Brahmarāta, O twice-born one. Yājñavalkya was highly learned in dharma and always devoted to serving Yājñavalkya’s teacher.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
याज्ञवल्क्यः तु (yājñavalkyaḥ tu) — but Yājñavalkya

परमधर्मज्ञः (paramadharmajñaḥ) — highly learned in dharma
गुरुवृत्तिपरः सदा (guruvṛttiparaḥ sadā) — always devoted to serving the teacher
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yājñavalkyastu tatrābhūdbrahmarātasuto dvija|śiṣyaḥ paramadharmajño guruvṛttiparassadā

Śrīdhara

yajuḥśākhāntarāṇāmutpattiṃ vaktuṃ śiṣyāntaram āha—yājñavalkyastviti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यजुःशाखान्तराणामुत्पत्तिं वक्तुं शिष्यान्तरम् आह—याज्ञवल्क्यस्त्विति

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara introduces Yājñavalkya as another pupil in order to explain the origin of the other Yajurveda branches.

VP 3.5.4

ऋषिर्योऽद्य महामेरोः समाजे नागमिष्यति।
तस्य वै सप्तरात्रात्तु ब्रह्महत्या भविष्यति

The rule was that any sage who failed to attend that day’s assembly at Mahāmeru would incur the sin of killing a Brahmin within seven nights.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
ऋषिः (ṛṣiḥ) — a sage
यः (yaḥ) — who

महामेरोः समाजे (mahāmeroḥ samāje) — at the assembly of Mahāmeru
न आगमिष्यति (na āgamiṣyati) — will not come
तस्य (tasya) — for that sage
सप्तरात्रात् (saptarātrāt) — after seven nights
ब्रह्महत्या (brahmahatyā) — brahma-slaughter
भविष्यति (bhaviṣyati) — will occur
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ṛṣiryo'dya mahāmeroḥ samāje nāgamiṣyati|tasya vai saptarātrāttu brahmahatyā bhaviṣyati

Śrīdhara

pūrvamevaṃ munigaṇaissamayo yaḥ kṛto dvija । vaiśaṃpāyana ekastu taṃ vyatikrāntavāṃstadā ॥५॥

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तस्मादेव शाखाद्वयप्रवृत्तिं वक्तुम् इतिहासम् आह—ऋषिर् इति । अद्य अस्मत्समाजे य ऋषिर्नागमिष्यति, तस्येतः सप्तरात्रात् ब्रह्महत्या भविष्यतीति यः समयोऽभूत् तत् वैशम्पायन एवातिक्रान्तवानिति द्वयोरन्वयः

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara says that this account explains how the two Yajurveda branches arose. The assembly rule was that a sage who failed to attend that day would incur the sin of killing a Brahmin within seven nights. Vaiśampāyana alone broke the agreement.

VP 3.5.6

स्वस्त्रीयं बालकं सोऽथ पदा स्पृष्टमताडयत्

Vaiśampāyana then struck the child of Vaiśampāyana’s sister with Vaiśampāyana’s foot.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
स्वस्त्रीयम् बालकम् (svastrīyam bālakam) — the child of Vaiśampāyana’s sister
सः अथ (saḥ atha) — Vaiśampāyana then
पदा स्पृष्टम् (padā spṛṣṭam) — touched with the foot
अताडयत् (atāḍayat) — struck
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

svastrīyaṃ bālakaṃ so'tha padā spṛṣṭamatāḍayat

Śrīdhara

svatīyaṃ svasuḥ sutaṃ padā spṛṣṭaṃ santam aghātayat । ṛṣiśāpavaśāt padā spṛṣṭamātro’sau mamāretyarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

स्वतीयं स्वसुः सुतं पदा स्पृष्टं सन्तम् अघातयत् । ऋषिशापवशात् पदा स्पृष्टमात्रोऽसौ ममारेत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara identifies the child as the son of Vaiśampāyana’s sister. Śrīdhara explains that a sage’s curse caused the boy to die as soon as Vaiśampāyana’s foot touched the boy.

VP 3.5.7

शिष्यानाह च भोः शिष्या ब्रह्महत्यापहं व्रतम्।
चरध्वं मत्कृते सर्वे न विचार्यमिदं तथा

Vaiśampāyana told Vaiśampāyana’s disciples to perform the vow that removes the sin of killing a Brahmin for Vaiśampāyana’s sake. Vaiśampāyana told the disciples to undertake the vow without deliberation.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
शिष्यान् आह च (śiṣyān āha ca) — and told the disciples
भोः शिष्याः (bhoḥ śiṣyāḥ) — O disciples
ब्रह्महत्यापहं व्रतम् (brahmahatyāpahaṃ vratam) — the vow that removes brahma-slaughter
चरध्वम् (caradhvam) — undertake
मत्कृते (matkṛte) — for my sake, that is Vaiśampāyana’s
सर्वे (sarve) — all
न विचार्यम् (na vicāryam) — not to be deliberated
इदम् तथा (idam tathā) — this, so
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

śiṣyānāha ca bhoḥ śiṣyā brahmahatyāpahaṃ vratam|caradhvaṃ matkṛte sarve na vicāryamidaṃ tathā

Śrīdhara

samarthenānyena mahāpātake kṛte’nyena kathaṃ tatprāyaścitaṃ karttavyamitīdaṃ na vicārya, kintu tathā sākṣād vadhe śāstroktamukhyaprakāreṇa madarthaṃ vrataṃ caratetyarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

समर्थेनान्येन महापातके कृतेऽन्येन कथं तत्प्रायश्चितं कर्त्तव्यमितीदं न विचार्य, किन्तु तथा साक्षाद् वधे शास्त्रोक्तमुख्यप्रकारेण मदर्थं व्रतं चरतेत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara says that Vaiśampāyana set aside the question of whether one person can perform expiation for another person’s great sin. Because Vaiśampāyana had directly committed the killing, Śrīdhara understands the instruction as asking the disciples to perform the vow for Vaiśampāyana in the principal way prescribed by scripture.

VP 3.5.9

VP 3.5.10

निस्तेजसो वदसि एनान् यत् त्वं ब्राह्मणपुङ्गवान्।
तेन शिष्येण नार्थोऽस्ति ममाज्ञाभङ्गकारिणा

Vaiśampāyana told Yājñavalkya that Yājñavalkya had called the excellent Brahmins lacking in vigor. Vaiśampāyana had no use for a disciple who broke Vaiśampāyana’s command.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains the word command-breaker by recalling that Vaiśampāyana instructed all disciples to perform the vow, while Yājñavalkya replied that Yājñavalkya alone would perform it. Yājñavalkya broke Vaiśampāyana’s command by rejecting that instruction.

VP 3.5.11

Yājñavalkya replied. I said this to you out of devotion. I too have had enough of what I learned from you, O twice-born one. Here it is.

Śrīdhara Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains Yājñavalkya’s words as a statement that Yājñavalkya had spoken from devotion and had had enough of the lessons received from Vaiśampāyana. Yājñavalkya then imitated the act of vomiting.

VP 3.5.12
