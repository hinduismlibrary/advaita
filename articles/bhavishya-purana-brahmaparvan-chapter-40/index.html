Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 40 — Birth, Conduct and the Basis of Varṇa

BP 1.40.1

शतानीक उवाच
अहो व्रतं महत्कष्टं संशयो हृदि वर्तते ।
कार्तिकेयस्य माहात्म्यं श्रुत्वा जन्म तथा द्विज ॥

Śatānīka said. Ah, this vow is very difficult. A doubt remains in my heart after hearing of the greatness and birth of Kārttikeya, O Brahmin.

BP 1.40.2

अनेकजनितस्येह कार्त्तिकेयस्य सुव्रत ।
माहात्म्यं सुमहद्विप्र कथमेतद्विभाव्यते ॥

O keeper of good vows, O Brahmin, how is the great majesty of Kārttikeya, born in many ways here, understood.

BP 1.40.3

जातिः श्रेष्ठा भवेद्वीर उत कर्म भवेद्वरम् ।
संशयस्तु महानत्र दृष्ट्वा मे कृत्तिकासुतम् ॥

Is birth superior, O hero, or is action better. This great doubt is in me after seeing the son of the Kṛttikās.

BP 1.40.4

एतद्वद विनिश्चित्य न यथा संशयो भवेत् ।
जन्मतः कर्मणश्चैव यज्ज्वायस्तद् ब्रवीहि मे ॥

Determine and tell me this, so that there may be no doubt. Tell me which is greater, birth or action.

BP 1.40.5

सुमन्तुरुवाच
इममर्थं पुरा पृष्टो ब्रह्मा शिष्यैर्मनीषिभिः ।
यदुक्तं तेन तेषां च तत्ते वच्मि निबोध मे ॥

Sumantu said. Brahmā was formerly asked this matter by wise disciples. What he said to them, that I tell you. Hear me.

BP 1.40.6

सुरज्येष्ठं सुखासीनमभिगम्य महर्षयः ।
प्रणम्य च महाबाहो विश्वामित्रस्य विप्रताम् ॥

The great sages approached the eldest of the gods, seated at ease, and bowed regarding the Brahminhood of Viśvāmitra, O mighty armed one.

BP 1.40.7

दृष्ट्वा विस्मयमागत्य कौतूहलसमन्विताः ।
भक्तिं श्रद्धां पुरोधाय प्रणम्यानतकन्धराः ॥

Having seen it, they became amazed and filled with curiosity, placing devotion and faith foremost, they bowed with lowered necks.

BP 1.40.8

ऋषय ऊचुः
भो ब्रह्मन्नादकल्पे हि ब्राह्मण्यं ब्रूहि किं भवेत् ।
जात्यध्ययनदेहात्मसंस्काराचारकर्मणाम् ॥

The sages said. O Brahmā, at the beginning of the age, tell us what Brahminhood is among birth, study, body, self, rites, conduct, and action.

BP 1.40.9

बाह्याभ्यन्तरसामान्यविशेषा यदि कृत्रिमाः ।
मनोवाक्कर्मशारीरजातिद्रव्यगुणात्मकाः ॥

Are distinctions, external or internal, general or particular, artificial, consisting of mind, speech, action, body, birth, substance, and quality.

BP 1.40.10

सन्त्यक्तव्याः प्रसिद्धा ये जातिभेदविधायिनः ।
वस्तुभूताः परोक्षैर्वा प्रमाणैर्न विनिश्चिताः ॥

The known grounds that establish distinctions by birth should be abandoned, whether they are real things or are not established by imperceptible means of knowledge.

BP 1.40.11

अव्यक्तागमसिद्धश्चेज्जातिभेदविधिर्नृणाम् ।
विकल्पोऽयं न पुष्णाति भवतः शेमुषीबलम् ॥

If the rule establishing distinctions of birth among people rests on unseen tradition, this supposition does not strengthen your understanding.

BP 1.40.12

ब्रह्मोवाच
एवमेतन्न सन्देहो यथा यूयं वदन्ति ह ।
शृणुध्वं योगिनो वाक्यं सतर्कं शिष्यश्रेयसे ॥

Brahmā said. It is so, without doubt, as you say. Hear the reasoned words of the yogi, for the welfare of disciples.

BP 1.40.13

योगेश्वर उवाच
प्रमाणे हि प्रसिद्धे तु भिन्नार्थविषये यतः ।
स्पष्टयोग्यार्थविषयं प्रत्यक्षं तावदीक्षते ॥

The Lord of Yoga said. When a means of knowledge is established for a distinct object, direct perception sees the object that is clear and fit for perception.

BP 1.40.14

समान्यातीन्द्रियग्राही सिद्धान्तोऽभ्युपगम्यते ।
स एव भगवानेकं प्रमाणमिति चेन्न तत् ॥

A conclusion that grasps universals and what is beyond the senses is accepted. If you say that Bhagavān alone is the one means of knowledge, that is not so.

BP 1.40.15

यस्माद्विविधमे तत्ते सङ्कटं भद्र वर्तते ।
वेदस्य पौरुषेयत्वं नित्यजातिसमर्थकम् ॥

For this difficulty stands before you in two ways, good one. The Veda is of human authorship and supports an eternal birth class.

BP 1.40.16

कार्या विशेषा वेदोक्ता न युक्तमकृतं वचः ।
ताल्वादिकरणानां च व्यापारानन्तरं श्रुतेः ॥

The particulars stated by the Veda are produced, an unproduced utterance is not reasonable, since hearing follows the activity of the palate and other organs.

BP 1.40.17

व्यापारात्परतस्तस्य प्रागभावविशेषतः ।
तद्भावानुविधायित्वमन्वयव्यतिरकेतः ॥

It appears after that activity, having been absent before. Its existence follows that activity, as shown by agreement and difference.

BP 1.40.18

तस्माद्धूमाग्निवद्वार्थफलभावोऽवतिष्ठते ।
न च व्यापारवचसोरन्यथानुपपत्तितः ॥

Therefore, as with smoke and fire, the relation of cause and effect remains, for the activity of speech cannot be accounted for otherwise.

BP 1.40.19

पुरुषानुगता जातिर्ब्राह्मणत्वादिकास्ति चेत् ।
द्विवर्णजातिभेदेन प्रत्याक्षार्थोपलक्षणात् ॥

If a birth class such as Brahminhood inheres in a person, its distinction would be marked by the visible difference between two varnas.

BP 1.40.20

गोवर्गमध्यं च गतो यथाश्वो निर्धार्यते ज्ञैः सुविचक्षणत्वात् ।
मनुष्यभावादविशिष्यमाणस्तद्वद्द्विजः शूद्रगणान्न भिन्नः ॥

Just as the wise, by sharp discernment, distinguish a horse that has entered a herd of cows, so a twice born person, not distinguished by human nature, is not different from a group of Shudras.

BP 1.40.21

मनुष्यजातेर्न परो विशेषो यः कल्प्यते सर्वनरानुयायी ।
संस्कारयुक्ता हि क्रियाविशिष्टा द्विजन्मनां शूद्रविवेकहेतुः ॥

There is no distinction beyond the human species that follows all people. Rites and particular actions are indeed the cause of distinguishing the twice born from Shudras.

BP 1.40.22

जीवोऽपि ब्राह्मणः प्रोक्तो यैरतत्त्वज्ञमानवैः ।
प्रभ्रष्टब्राह्मणत्वास्ते जायन्ते विप्रसङ्गतः ॥

Those who do not know reality have even called the soul a Brahmin. They are born fallen from Brahminhood through connection with Brahmins.

BP 1.40.23

जराजन्मान्तरक्लेशदुष्टग्राहकुलाकुलम् ।
नर तिर्यगसच्छूद्र योनिदुःखोर्मिसंकटम् ॥

Troubled by old age, the afflictions of repeated births, and evil family bonds, a man is caught in the peril of waves of suffering in human, animal, ignoble, and Shudra wombs.

BP 1.40.24

दौस्थित्यरोगशोकार्तिजनावर्तसमन्वितम् ।
श्वानुशूकरचाण्डालकृमिकूर्मादिकायकम् ॥

Beset by hardship, disease, grief, distress, and the whirlpool of human life, it takes bodies such as dogs, pigs, outcastes, worms, tortoises, and others.

BP 1.40.25

संसारसागरं घोरं मग्नः खलु परिप्लवन् ।
भूरिपापभराक्रान्तः स जीवो ब्राह्मणः कथम् ॥

Submerged and tossed about in the terrible ocean of the cycle of existence, overwhelmed by the burden of abundant sin, how can that soul be a Brahmin.

BP 1.40.26

ब्रह्मोवाच
सप्तव्याधकथा विप्रा मनुना परिकीर्तिताः ।
तं निशम्य यदुश्रेष्ठ नित्यं जातिपदं त्यजेत् ॥

Brahmā said. O Brahmins, Manu related the story of the seven hunters. Hearing it, O best of the Yadus, one should abandon the fixed status of birth.

BP 1.40.27

सप्तव्याधा दशार्णेषु मृगाः कालञ्जरे गिरौ ।
चक्रवाकाः सरिद्द्वीपे हंसाः सरसि मानसे ॥

The seven hunters were in Daśārṇa, deer on Mount Kālañjara, ruddy geese on a river island, and swans in Lake Mānasa.

BP 1.40.28

तेऽपि जाताः कुरुक्षेत्रे ब्राह्मणा वेदपारगाः।
प्रस्थिता दीर्घमध्वानं मृयं किमवसीदथ ॥

They too were born in Kurukṣetra as Brahmins, versed in the Veda. Having set out on a long journey, why were you grieved at death.

BP 1.40.29

तस्मान्न जीवे ब्राह्मण्यं पश्यामो हि कथञ्चन ॥

Therefore we do not see Brahminhood in the soul in any way.

BP 1.40.30

शस्त्रादिमद्भार्गवजातियुक्तो गजाश्वगोजोष्ट्रखरादिकानाम् ।
शक्त्या कृतो ह्यङ्गजवर्णधर्मैर्भेदः स्फुटं लक्षणतोऽत्र यद्वत् ॥

Among elephants, horses, cows, camels, donkeys, and the like, distinctions are clearly marked by strength, bodily features, color, and characteristics, as among the weapon bearing Bhārgava classes.

BP 1.40.31

तदुत्तरान्नैव विकर्तनीया ब्राह्मण्यजातिनृषु नास्ति काचित् ।
नित्याकृतिर्नानुपभेदरूपा यथा हि भेदः परमोऽत्र सिध्येत् ॥
सिताद्यसाधारणतुल्यरूपाः सनातनोऽङ्गेषु न वर्णभेदः ॥

Beyond these, no Brahmin birth class is distinguishable among humans. There is no eternal form or distinct subtype by which a final difference could be established here, nor is there an enduring difference of color in the body, with forms alike except for white and the like.

BP 1.40.32

ब्राह्मण्यमध्रुवमिदं किल कृत्रिमत्वादकृत्रिमं भवति सामयिकत्वयोगात् ।
साङ्केतिकं सुकृतलेशविशेषलब्धं वाणिज्यभेषजकृतामिव जातिभेदाः ॥

This Brahminhood is unstable because it is artificial and conventional. It is a designation gained through a particular measure of merit, like the class distinctions created by trade and medicine.

BP 1.40.33

किं ब्राह्मणा ये सुकृतं त्यजन्ति किं क्षत्रिया लोकमपालयन्तः .।
स्वधर्महीना हि तथैव वैश्याः शूद्राः स्वमुख्यक्रियया विहीनाः ॥

Are those who abandon good deeds Brahmins. Are those who do not protect the people Kshatriyas. Likewise, are Vaishyas without their own duty, and Shudras without their principal activity.

BP 1.40.34

तस्मान्न गोश्ववत्कश्चिज्जातिभेदोऽस्ति देहिनाम् ।
कार्यशक्तिनिमित्तस्तु सङ्केतः कृत्रिमो भवेत् ॥

Therefore no birth distinction exists among embodied beings as between cows and horses. The designation is artificial, based on capacity for work.

BP 1.40.35

एवं प्रमाणैः प्रतिषिध्यमानां साङ्केतिकीं याति नरो व्यवस्थाम् ।
स्वकीयसिद्धां स्वमतैर्निषिद्धां न बुध्यते मूढमना वराकः ॥

Thus, though refuted by proofs, a man resorts to a conventional arrangement. The poor dull minded fool does not understand that what he establishes is refuted by his own opinions.

BP 1.40.36

गोमहिष्यजवाज्युष्ट्रवानेयाविगजाधिपाः ।
प्रेष्यवार्धुषिकाकार्यकरणोद्यतमानसाः ॥

Those intent on keeping cows, buffaloes, goats, horses, camels, donkeys, sheep, and elephants, or on service, usury, and hired work.

BP 1.40.37

वणिक्कारुक्रियाविष्टा दिव्यास्तेऽपि च ये द्विजाः ।
विनष्टास्ते तु विज्ञेयाः क्रव्यादाश्च कुशीलवाः ॥

Those Brahmins devoted to trade, crafts, and gambling are to be known as fallen, as are flesh eaters and performers.

BP 1.40.38

पलाण्डुलशुनादाश्च मृग्युष्ट्रीक्षीरपायिनः ।
मांससर्वरसक्षीरक्रयविक्रयकारिणः ॥

Eaters of onions and garlic, drinkers of deer milk and she camel milk, and those who buy and sell meat, every kind of juice, and milk.

BP 1.40.39

पुनर्भूवृषलीवेश्याचाण्डालस्त्रीनिषेविणः ।
शूद्रान्नरसपुष्टाङ्गाः प्रेतवस्त्रान्नभोजनाः ॥

Those who consort with remarried women, low born women, prostitutes, and outcast women, whose bodies are nourished by the essence of Shudra food, and who eat the food and wear the clothes of the dead.

BP 1.40.40

मृतसूतकलब्धान्नपानाद्यभ्यवहारिणः ।
ब्रह्मदेवपितृभूतमनुष्येषु बहिष्कृताः ॥

Those who consume food, drink, and other things received during the impurity of birth or death are excluded from Brahmā, gods, ancestors, beings, and humans.

BP 1.40.41

मात्सर्यमदविद्वेषतृष्णाकामतमोमयाः ।
हीनाचारा हि ये केचिदपरे पिशुना द्विजाः ॥
प्रकारैर्बहुभिः सर्वे ते प्रणश्यन्ति नान्यथा ॥

Those Brahmins who are filled with envy, pride, hostility, craving, desire, and darkness, who follow base conduct or slander others, all perish in many ways, and not otherwise.

BP 1.40.42

एवं शास्त्रोदितन्यायमार्गभ्रष्टास्तु ये नराः ।
विशिष्टगोत्रसंस्कारकलापसकलात्मकाः ॥

Those men who stray from the path of reasoning set forth by shastra, though possessing the whole collection of distinguished lineage and rites.

BP 1.40.43

वेदानध्यापयन्तोऽपि तेऽधीयानाः श्रुतिक्रमात् ।
ब्राह्मणत्वाद्विहीयन्ते दुराचारविघायिनः ॥

Though they teach the Vedas and study them in the received sequence, they lose Brahminhood, being practitioners of bad conduct.

BP 1.40.44

तस्मान्न जातिरेकत्र भूतात्मास्त्यनपायिनी ।
नाशित्वादत्र च श्लोकान्मानवाः समधीयते ॥ .

Therefore no birth class is imperishable in any embodied being. Because it perishes, people study verses here.

BP 1.40.45

सद्यः पतति मांसेन लाक्षया लवणेन च ।
त्र्यहेण शूद्रो भवति ब्राह्मणः क्षीरविकयी ॥

A Brahmin falls at once through dealing in meat, lac, or salt. A Brahmin selling milk becomes a Shudra in three days.

BP 1.40.46

गोरक्षकान्वाणिजिकांस्तथा कारुकुशीलवान् ।
प्रेष्यान्वार्धुषिकांश्चैव शूद्रास्तान्मनुरब्रवीत् ॥

Manu called cattle keepers, merchants, artisans, performers, servants, and usurers Shudras.

BP 1.40.47

शूद्रो ब्राह्मणतामेति ब्राह्मणश्चैति शूद्रताम् ।
क्षत्रियो याति विप्रत्वं विद्याद्वैश्यं तथैव च ॥

A Shudra attains Brahminhood, and a Brahmin attains Shudra status. A Kshatriya attains Brahminhood. Understand the same of a Vaishya.
