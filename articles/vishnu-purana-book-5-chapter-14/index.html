VP 5.14.1

प्रदोषाग्रेकदाचित् तु रासासक्ते जनार्दने ।
त्रासयन् समदो गोष्ठान् अरिष्टः समुपागमत् ॥१॥

At the beginning of evening, once, while Janārdana was absorbed in the rāsa, Ariṣṭa came to the cowpen in rut, frightening it.

Śrīdhara translation

“At the beginning of evening,” thus. “At the beginning of evening” means at the close of twilight. A Daitya named Ariṣṭa, in the form of a bull, came to the cowpen in rut, frightening it.

VP 5.14.2

सतोय-तोयद-च्छायस्तीक्ष्ण-शृङ्गोऽर्क-लोचनः ।

His hue was like a rain cloud filled with water; his horns were sharp, and his eyes were like the sun. With the tips of his hooves he greatly tore the surface of the earth.

“With water,” thus. He whose eyes are like the suns.

VP 5.14.3

लेलिहानः स-निष्पेषञ्जिह्वयौष्ठौ पुनः पुनः ।

Licking his lips again and again with his tongue and with a grinding sound, his tail raised in fury, he had hard shoulder-joints.

Śrīdhara translation

“Licking,” thus. “With a grinding sound” means with friction; he licks his lips with his tongue. He whose tail is bent and raised by fury; he whose shoulder-joints are hard.

VP 5.14.4

VP 5.14.10

आयान्तं दैत्य-वृषभन्दृष्ट्वा कृष्णो महा-बलः।

Seeing the approaching bull-demon, mighty Kṛṣṇa did not move from his place then, through playful disregard and a smile.

“Coming,” thus. The smile of disregard itself is the play; so.
