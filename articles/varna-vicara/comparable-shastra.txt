Comparable śāstric witnesses

This section collects primary-text passages that bear directly on the same question as the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa discussion: whether brāhmaṇatva/varṇa is established only by birth, or whether conduct, qualities, knowledge and action are decisive. The witnesses do not all speak in one voice, and some are narrative or polemical. They are therefore quoted with their context instead of being silently harmonised.

Sanskrit is reproduced from the linked witnesses. Verse numbering can differ between recensions and editions; the GRETIL Mahābhārata links identify the machine-readable critical-text files.

Mahābhārata — the Sarpa dialogue: vṛtta rather than jāti

Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan 3.177; GRETIL critical-text insertion immediately preceding the numbered Sarpa dialogue (03,177.014d@019_0043–0066)

जात्या कुलेन वृत्तेन स्वाध्यायेन श्रुतेन च ।

ब्राह्मण्यं केन भवति प्रब्रूहि एतद् विनिश्चयम् ॥

न जातिर्न कुलं तात न स्वाध्यायः श्रुतं न च ।

कारणानि द्विजत्वस्य वृत्तमेव तु कारणम् ॥

अनेके मुनयस्तात तिर्यग्योनिसमाश्रिताः ।

स्वधर्माचारनिरता ब्रह्मलोकमितो गताः ॥

बहुना किमधीतेन नटस्येव दुरात्मनः ।

तेनाधीतं श्रुतं तेन यो वृत्तमनुतिष्ठति ॥

कपालस्थं यथा तोयं श्वदृतौ च यथा पयः ।

दुष्टं स्यात् स्थानदोषेण वृत्तहीने तथा श्रुतम् ॥

वृत्तं यत्नेन रक्ष्यं स्याद् वित्तमेति च याति च ।

अक्षीणो वित्ततः क्षीणो वृत्ततस्तु हतो हतः ॥

किं कुलेनोपदिष्टेन विपुलेन दुरात्मनः ।

कृमयः किं न जायन्ते कुसुमेषु सुगन्धिषु ॥

तस्माद् विद्धि महाराज वृत्तं ब्राह्मणलक्षणम् ।

चतुर्वेदोऽपि दुर्वृत्तः शूद्रात् पापतरः स्मृतः ॥

योऽग्निहोत्रपरो दान्तः सन्तोषनियतः शुचिः ।

तपःस्वाध्यायशीलश्च तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः ॥

सर्वद्वन्द्वसहो धीरः सर्वसङ्गविवर्जितः ।

सर्वभूतहितो मैत्रस्तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः ॥

येन केन चिदाच्छन्नो येन केन चिदासितः ।

यत्रक्वचनशायी च तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः ॥

योऽहेरिव गणाद् भीतः सन्मानान्मरणादिव ।

कुणपादिव च स्त्रीभ्यस्तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः ॥

The dialogue asks what produces brāhmaṇya—birth, lineage, conduct, study or learning—and answers: “Not jāti, not lineage, not study or learning; conduct alone is the cause of dvijatva.” Sages who lived in non-human births are said to have reached Brahmaloka through right conduct. Learning without conduct is compared to a dancer’s display and to water polluted by its container. Wealth comes and goes, but damaged conduct destroys a person. Even a four-Veda scholar of bad conduct is worse than a śūdra, while the disciplined, pure, self-controlled and benevolent person is recognised by the gods as a brāhmaṇa.

Mahābhārata — qualities, mixed birth and the primacy of śīla

Mahābhārata, Vana-parvan 3.177.16–32

सत्यं दानं क्षमा शीलमानृशंस्यं दमो घृणा ।

दृश्यन्ते यत्र नागेन्द्र स ब्राह्मण इति स्मृतः ॥ १६ ॥

वेद्यं सर्प परं ब्रह्म निर्दुःखमसुखं च यत् ।

यत्र गत्वा न शोचन्ति भवतः किं विवक्षितम् ॥ १७ ॥

चातुर्वर्ण्यं प्रमाणं च सत्यं च ब्रह्म चैव ह ।

शूद्रेष्वपि च सत्यं च दानमक्रोध एव च ।

आनृशंस्यमहिंसा च घृणा चैव युधिष्ठिर ॥ १८ ॥

वेद्यं यच्चात्थ निर्दुःखमसुखं च नराधिप ।

ताभ्यां हीनं पदं चान्यन्न तदस्तीति लक्ष्ये ॥ १९ ॥

शूद्रे चैतद्भवेल्लक्ष्यं द्विजे तच्च न विद्यते ।

न वै शूद्रो भवेत् शूद्रो ब्राह्मणो न च ब्राह्मणः ॥ २० ॥

यत्रैतल्लक्ष्यते सर्प वृत्तं स ब्राह्मणः स्मृतः ।

यत्रैतन्न भवेत् सर्प तं शूद्रमिति निर्दिशेत् ॥ २१ ॥

यत्पुनर्भवता प्रोक्तं न वेद्यं विद्यतेति ह ।

ताभ्यां हीनमतीत्यात्र पदं नास्तीति चेदपि ॥ २२ ॥

एवमेतन्मतं सर्प ताभ्यां हीनं न विद्यते ।

यथा शीतोष्णयोर्मध्ये भवेन्नोष्णं न शीतता ॥ २३ ॥

एवं वै सुखदुःखाभ्यां हीनमस्ति पदं क्वचित् ।

एषा मम मतिः सर्प यथा वा मन्यते भवान् ॥ २४ ॥

यदि ते वृत्ततो राजन् ब्राह्मणः प्रसमीक्षितः ।

व्यर्था जातिस्तदायुष्मन् कृतिर्यावन्न दृश्यते ॥ २५ ॥

जातिरत्र महासर्प मनुष्यत्वे महामते ।

सङ्करात् सर्ववर्णानां दुष्परीक्षेति मे मतिः ॥ २६ ॥

सर्वे सर्वास्वपत्यानि जनयन्ति यदा नराः ।

वाङ्मैथुनमथो जन्म मरणं च समं नृणाम् ॥ २७ ॥

इदम् आर्षं प्रमाणं च ये यजामह इत्यपि ।

तस्माच्छीलं प्रधानेष्टं विदुर्ये तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ २८ ॥

प्राङ्नाभिवर्धनात् पुंसो जातकर्म विधीयते ।

तत्रास्य माता सावित्री पिता त्वाचार्य उच्यते ॥ २९ ॥

वृत्त्या शूद्रसमो ह्येष यावद्वेदे न जायते ।

अस्मिन्नेवं मतिद्वैधे मनुः स्वायम्भुवोऽब्रवीत् ॥ ३० ॥

कृतकृत्याः पुनर्वर्णा यदि वृत्तं न विद्यते ।

सङ्करस्तत्र नागेन्द्र बलवान् प्रसमीक्षितः ॥ ३१ ॥

यत्रेदानीं संस्कृतं वृत्तमिष्यते ।

तं ब्राह्मणमहं पूर्वमुक्तवान् भुजगोत्तम ॥ ३२ ॥

Truth, generosity, patience, good conduct, compassion, self-control and moral feeling are the marks by which a brāhmaṇa is remembered. The same truth, generosity, non-anger, compassion and non-violence are found among śūdras. “Where this conduct is found, he is a brāhmaṇa; where it is absent, designate him a śūdra.” Jāti is difficult to determine because the varṇas are mixed; human birth, speech, sexual generation and death are common. Therefore the truth-seers hold śīla to be primary. The passage also says that a dvija is “equal to a śūdra by conduct” until he is reborn through Vedic initiation, and that completed rites do not help when vṛtta is absent.

Sanskrit witness: GRETIL Mahābhārata, Araṇyakaparvan [https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/mbh_03_u.htm]. The surrounding dialogue includes a question-and-answer about Brahman and liberation; the verses above are the varṇa/character portion.

Mahābhārata — Bharadvāja’s colour objection and karma-based varṇa

Mahābhārata, Śānti-parvan 12.181.6–20

चातुर्वर्ण्यस्य वर्णेन यदि वर्णो विभज्यते ।

सर्वेषां खलु वर्णानां दृश्यते वर्णसङ्करः ॥ ६ ॥

कामः क्रोधो भयं लोभः शोकश्चिन्ता क्षुधा श्रमः ।

सर्वेषां नः प्रभवति कस्माद् वर्णो विभज्यते ॥ ७ ॥

स्वेदमूत्रपुरीषाणि श्लेष्मा पित्तं सशोणितम् ।

तनुः क्षरति सर्वेषां कस्माद् वर्णो विभज्यते ॥ ८ ॥

जङ्गमानामसङ्ख्येयाः स्थावराणां च जातयः ।

तेषां विविधवर्णानां कुतो वर्णविनिश्चयः ॥ ९ ॥

न विशेषोऽस्ति वर्णानां सर्वं ब्राह्ममिदं जगत् ।

ब्रह्मणा पूर्वसृष्टं हि कर्मभिर्वर्णतां गतम् ॥ १० ॥

कामभोगप्रियास्तीक्ष्णाः क्रोधनाः प्रियसाहसाः ।

त्यक्तस्वधर्मा रक्ताङ्गास्ते द्विजाः क्षत्रतां गताः ॥ ११ ॥

गोṣu वृत्तिं समाधाय पीताः कृष्युपजीविनः ।

स्वधर्मं नानुतिष्ठन्ति ते द्विजा वैश्यतां गताः ॥ १२ ॥

हिंसानृतप्रिया लुब्धाः सर्वकर्मोपजीविनः ।

कृष्णाः शौचपरिभ्रष्टास्ते द्विजाः शूद्रतां गताः ॥ १३ ॥

इत्येतैः कर्मभिर्व्यस्ता द्विजा वर्णान्तरं गताः ।

धर्मो यज्ञक्रिया चैषां नित्यं न प्रतिषिध्यते ॥ १४ ॥

वर्णाश्चत्वार एते हि येषां ब्राह्मी सरस्वती ।

विहिता ब्रह्मणा पूर्वं लोभात्त्वज्ञानतां गताः ॥ १५ ॥

ब्राह्मणा धर्मतन्त्रस्थास्तपस्तेषां न नश्यति ।

ब्रह्म धारयतां नित्यं व्रतानि नियमांस्तथा ॥ १६ ॥

ब्रह्म चैतत्पुरा सृष्टं ये न जानन्त्यतद्विदः ।

तेषां बहुविधास्त्वन्यास्तत्र तत्र हि जातयः ॥ १७ ॥

पिशाचा राक्षसाः प्रेता बहुधा म्लेच्छजातयः ।

प्रनष्टज्ञानविज्ञानाः स्वच्छन्दाचारचेष्टिताः ॥ १८ ॥

प्रजा ब्राह्मणसंस्काराः स्वधर्मकृतनिश्चयाः ।

ऋषिभिः स्वेन तपसा सृज्यन्ते चापरे परैः ॥ १९ ॥

आदिदेवसमुद्भूता ब्रह्ममूलाक्षयाव्यया ।

सा सृष्टिर्मानसी नाम धर्मतन्त्रपरायणा ॥ २० ॥

Bharadvāja objects that if varṇa is decided by colour, all four groups show colour-mixture; the same desire, anger, fear, hunger, sweat, urine, faeces, phlegm, bile and blood occur in everyone. Bhṛgu replies, “There is no special distinction of varṇas; this whole world is brahman,” and says that varṇa was reached through karma. Dvijas who take up kṣatriya, vaiśya or śūdra conduct are described as reaching those varṇas. The final verses retain a cosmological and normative register, so this is a direct karma/varṇa witness within a larger dialogue, not an isolated modern slogan.

Sanskrit witness: GRETIL Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan [https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/mbh_12_u.htm].

Mahābhārata — definitions by conduct and the “not every śūdra” conclusion

Mahābhārata, Śānti-parvan 12.182.1–8

ब्राह्मणः केन भवति क्षत्रियो वा द्विजोत्तम ।

वैश्यः शूद्रश्च विप्रर्षे तद् ब्रूहि वदतां वर ॥ १ ॥

जातकर्मादिभिर्यस्तु संस्कारैः संस्कृतः शुचिः ।

वेदाध्ययनसम्पन्नः षट्सु कर्मस्ववस्थितः ॥ २ ॥

शौचाचारस्थितः सम्यग् विघसाशी गुरुप्रियः ।

नित्यव्रती सत्यपरः स वै ब्राह्मण उच्यते ॥ ३ ॥

सत्यं दानं दमोऽद्रोह आनृशंस्यं क्षमा घृणा ।

तपश्च दृश्यते यत्र स ब्राह्मण इति स्मृतः ॥ ४ ॥

क्षत्रजं सेवते कर्म वेदाध्ययनसम्मतम् ।

दानादानरतिर्यश्च स वै क्षत्रिय उच्यते ॥ ५ ॥

कृषिगोरक्ष्यवाणिज्यं यो विशत्यनिशं शुचिः ।

वेदाध्ययनसम्पन्नः स वैश्य इति संज्ञितः ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वभक्षरतिर्नित्यं सर्वकर्मकरोऽशुचिः ।

त्यक्तवेदस्त्वनाचारः स वै शूद्र इति स्मृतः ॥ ७ ॥

शूद्रे चैतद् भवेल्लक्ष्यं द्विजे चैतन्न विद्यते ।

न वै शूद्रो भवेत् शूद्रो ब्राह्मणो न च ब्राह्मणः ॥ ८ ॥

The text asks how each varṇa is known. It describes a brāhmaṇa through purity, truth, generosity, self-control, compassion, austerity and conduct; a kṣatriya and vaiśya through their characteristic work; and a śūdra through the contrary conduct listed in the verse. Then it immediately qualifies the labels: the brāhmaṇa-marks may occur in a śūdra and may be absent in a dvija—“not every śūdra is a śūdra, and not every brāhmaṇa is a brāhmaṇa.”

Sanskrit witness: GRETIL Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan [https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/mbh_12_u.htm]. This is a continuation of the same Bharadvāja–Bhṛgu discussion and should be read together with 12.181.

Mahābhārata — the Śiva–Umā dialogue on falling and rising between varṇas

Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana-parvan 13.131.2–14, 26–27, 44–51

चातुर्वर्ण्यं भगवता पूर्वं सृष्टं स्वयंभुवा ।

केन कर्मविपाकेन वैश्यो गच्छति शूद्रताम् ॥ २ ॥

वैश्यो वा क्षत्रियः केन द्विजो वा क्षत्रियो भवेत् ।

प्रतिलोमः कथं देव शक्यो धर्मो निषेवितुम् ॥ ३ ॥

केन वा कर्मणा विप्रः शूद्रयोनौ प्रजायते ।

क्षत्रियः शूद्रतामेति केन वा कर्मणा विभो ॥ ४ ॥

एतं मे संशयं देव वद भूतपतेऽनघ ।

त्रयो वर्णाः प्रकृत्येह कथं ब्राह्मण्यमाप्नुयुः ॥ ५ ॥

कर्मणा दुष्कृतेनेह स्थानाद् भ्रश्यति वै द्विजः ।

ज्येष्ठं वर्णमनुप्राप्य तस्माद् रक्षेत वै द्विजः ॥ ७ ॥

स्थितो ब्राह्मणधर्मेण ब्राह्मण्यमुपजीवति ।

क्षत्रियो वाथ वैश्यो वा ब्रह्मभूयाय गच्छति ॥ ८ ॥

यस्तु विप्रत्वमुत्सृज्य क्षात्रं धर्मं निषेवते ।

ब्राह्मण्यात् स परिभ्रष्टः क्षत्रयोनौ प्रजायते ॥ ९ ॥

वैश्यकर्म च यो विप्रो लोभमोहव्यपाश्रयः ।

ब्राह्मण्यं दुर्लभं प्राप्य करोत्यल्पमतिः सदा ॥ १० ॥

स द्विजो वैश्यतामेति वैश्यो वा शूद्रतामियात् ।

स्वधर्मात् प्रच्युतो विप्रस्ततः शूद्रत्वमाप्नुते ॥ ११ ॥

तत्रासौ निरयं प्राप्तो वर्णभ्रष्टो बहिष्कृतः ।

ब्रह्मलोकपरिभ्रष्टः शूद्रः समुपजायते ॥ १२ ॥

क्षत्रियो वा महाभागे वैश्यो वा धर्मचारिणि ।

स्वानि कर्माण्यपाहाय शूद्रकर्माणि सेवते ॥ १३ ॥

स्वस्थानात् स परिभ्रष्टो वर्णसङ्करतां गतः ।

ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यः शूद्रत्वं याति तादृशः ॥ १४ ॥

एभिस्तु कर्मभिर्देवि शुभैराचरितैस्तथा ।

शूद्रो ब्राह्मणतां गच्छेद् वैश्यः क्षत्रियतां व्रजेत् ॥ २६ ॥

शूद्रकर्माणि सर्वाणि यथान्यायं यथाविधि ।

शुश्रूषां परिचर्यां च ज्येष्ठे वर्णे प्रयत्नतः ।

कुर्यादविमनाः शूद्रः सततं सत्पथे स्थितः ॥ २७ ॥

ज्ञानविज्ञानसम्पन्नः संस्कृतो वेदपारगः ।

विप्रो भवति धर्मात्मा क्षत्रियः स्वेन कर्मणा ॥ ४४ ॥

एतैः कर्मफलैर्देवि न्यूनजातिकुलोद्भवः ।

शूद्रोऽप्यागमसम्पन्नो द्विजो भवति संस्कृतः ॥ ४५ ॥

ब्राह्मणो वाप्यसद्वृत्तः सर्वसङ्करभोजनः ।

ब्राह्मण्यं पुण्यमुत्सृज्य शूद्रो भवति तादृशः ॥ ४६ ॥

कर्मभिः शुचिभिर्देवि शुद्धात्मा विजितेन्द्रियः ।

शूद्रोऽपि द्विजवत् सेव्यो इति ब्रह्माब्रवीत् स्वयम् ॥ ४७ ॥

स्वभावकर्म च शुभं यत्र शूद्रेऽपि तिष्ठति ।

विशुद्धः स द्विजातिर्वै विज्ञेय इति मे मतिः ॥ ४८ ॥

न योनिर्नापि संस्कारो न श्रुतं न च सन्नतिः ।

कारणानि द्विजत्वस्य वृत्तमेव तु कारणम् ॥ ४९ ॥

सर्वोऽयं ब्राह्मणो लोके वृत्तेन तु विधीयते ।

वृत्ते स्थितश्च सुश्रोणि ब्राह्मणत्वं निगच्छति ॥ ५० ॥

ब्राह्मः स्वभावः कल्याणि समः सर्वत्र मे मतिः ।

निर्गुणं निर्मलं ब्रह्म यत्र तिष्ठति स द्विजः ॥ ५१ ॥

Umā asks how a vaiśya, kṣatriya or dvija can become another varṇa and how the three lower varṇas can attain brāhmaṇya. Maheśvara answers that a dvija falls from his position through bad action; a kṣatriya or vaiśya who lives by brāhmaṇa-dharma can go toward Brahman; and a brāhmaṇa who takes up kṣatra, vaiśya or śūdra work falls accordingly. The later answers are explicit: “A śūdra goes to brāhmaṇatā”; a learned, disciplined person of a lower family becomes a dvija; a bad-conduct brāhmaṇa becomes a śūdra; and “neither birth, nor saṃskāra, nor learning, nor submission is the cause of dvijatva—conduct alone is the cause.”

Sanskrit witness: GRETIL Mahābhārata, Anuśāsanaparvan [https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/mbh_13_u.htm]. The same chapter also contains restrictive food and ritual instructions; the verses above are quoted because they directly address varṇa movement and its marks.

Bhagavad-gītā — guṇa, karma and svabhāva

Bhagavad-gītā 4.13; 18.41–44

चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं गुणकर्मविभागशः ।

तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम् ॥ ४.१३ ॥

ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियविशां शूद्राणां च परन्तप ।

कर्माणि प्रविभक्तानि स्वभावप्रभवैर्गुणैः ॥ १८.४१ ॥

शमो दमस्तपः शौचं क्षान्तिरार्जवमेव च ।

ज्ञानं विज्ञानमास्तिक्यं ब्रह्मकर्म स्वभावजम् ॥ १८.४२ ॥

शौर्यं तेजो धृतिर्दाक्ष्यं युद्धे चाप्यपलायनम् ।

दानमीश्वरभावश्च क्षात्रं कर्म स्वभावजम् ॥ १८.४३ ॥

कृषिगौरक्ष्यवाणिज्यं वैश्यकर्म स्वभावजम् ।

परिचर्यात्मकं कर्म शूद्रस्यापि स्वभावजम् ॥ १८.४४ ॥

Kṛṣṇa states that the fourfold order is divided according to guṇa and karma. Chapter 18 then says that the activities of brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras are divided by qualities born of svabhāva and lists their characteristic qualities and work.

Text 4.13 [https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/13/] and texts 18.41–44 [https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/41/]. Scope note: these verses clearly foreground guṇa/karma/svabhāva, but they do not by themselves state a complete legal theory of jāti or explicitly settle every birth-based question.

Bhāgavata Purāṇa — designation by observed lakṣaṇa

Śrīmad Bhāgavata Purāṇa 7.11.35

यस्य यल्लक्षणं प्रोक्तं पुंसो वर्णाभिव्यञ्जकम् ।

Manusmṛti 10.64–65

शूद्रायां ब्राह्मणाज्जातः श्रेयसा चेत् प्रजायते ।

अश्रेयान् श्रेयसीं जातिं गच्छत्यासप्तमाद् युगात् ॥ १०.६४ ॥

Chāndogya Upaniṣad — Satyakāma’s truth is recognised before initiation

Chāndogya Upaniṣad 4.4.4–5

तं होवाच किं गोत्रो नु सोम्यासीति स होवाच नाहमेतद् वेद भो यद्गोत्रोऽहमस्म्यपृच्छं मातरं सा मा प्रत्यब्रवीद् बह्वहं चरन्ती परिचरिणी यौवने त्वामलभे साहमेतन्न वेद यद्गोत्रस्त्वमसि जबाला तु नामाहमस्मि सत्यकामो नाम त्वमसीति सोऽहं सत्यकामो जाबालोऽस्मि भो इति ॥ ४.४.४ ॥

तं होवाच नैतदब्राह्मणो विवक्तुमर्हति समिधं सोम्याहर उप त्वा नेष्ये न सत्यादगा इति तमुपनीय ... ॥ ४.४.५ ॥

Satyakāma tells Gautama that he does not know his gotra: his mother Jābālā had served in many places and could not identify his father. Gautama replies, “A non-brāhmaṇa could not speak this way; bring fuel, Somya, I shall initiate you—you have not departed from truth.” This is a narrative recognition of character and truthfulness, not a standalone legal definition, but it is a clear śruti witness in which lineage uncertainty does not disqualify the seeker.

Vajrasūcika Upaniṣad — “Who is a brāhmaṇa?”

Vajrasūcika Upaniṣad (minor Upaniṣad; recension and attribution are disputed)

तत्र चोद्यमस्ति को वा ब्राह्मणो नाम किं जीवः किं देहः किं जातिः किं ज्ञानं किं कर्म किं धार्मिक इति ॥

तत्र प्रथमो जीवो ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न ... तस्मान्न जीवो ब्राह्मण इति ॥

तर्हि देहो ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न ... तस्मान्न देहो ब्राह्मण इति ॥

तर्हि जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न ... तस्मान्न जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति ॥

तर्हि ज्ञानं ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न ... तस्मान्न ज्ञानं ब्राह्मण इति ॥

तर्हि कर्म ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न ... तस्मान्न कर्म ब्राह्मण इति ॥

यः कश्चिदात्मानमद्वितीयं जातिगुणक्रियाहीनं ... कामरागादिदोषरहितः शमदमादिसम्पन्नो ... स एव ब्राह्मण इति ... अन्यथा हि ब्राह्मणत्वसिद्धिर्नास्त्येव ॥

The Upaniṣad asks whether a brāhmaṇa is the jīva, body, birth, knowledge, action or religious performance, rejects each as sufficient, and concludes that the brāhmaṇa is the person who directly knows the non-dual Self, is free from lust, anger, greed, pride and delusion, and possesses śama, dama and related virtues. It explicitly says, “Birth is not brāhmaṇa.”

Text witness: Vajrasūcika Upaniṣad of the Sāmaveda [https://gosai.com/writings/vajrasucika-upanisad]. Status note: this is a later/minor Upaniṣad with variant recensions and uncertain dating; the ellipses above mark omitted intervening prose, not a claim that the omitted wording is identical in every edition.

Nārada Bhakti Sūtra — no caste distinction among devotees

Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 72

नास्ति तेषु जातिविद्यारूपकुलधनक्रियादिभेदः ॥ ७२ ॥

“Among them there is no distinction of caste, learning, beauty, lineage, wealth, observance and the like.” Here “them” refers to devotees. It is a direct spiritual-community statement rather than a complete account of every social duty.

The Bhāgavata repeatedly makes devotional attainment override inherited status: named outsider groups are purified by taking shelter of Viṣṇu’s devotees; the dog-eater who chants the holy name is described as worshipable and as having completed the purifying Vedic disciplines; and a devoted śvapaca is praised above a learned but non-devoted brāhmaṇa, because he purifies his lineage. These are bhakti/soteriological comparisons, not a standalone social-law definition.

The epic supplies several closely related tests. Nobility is by vṛtta, not wealth or learning. Bhagavad-gītā — learn through questions, then reflect and choose

Bhagavad-gītā 4.34 and 18.63

तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया ।

उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ ४.३४ ॥

इति ते ज्ञानमाख्यातं गुह्याद्गुह्यतरं मया ।

विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥ १८.६३ ॥

The Gītā joins respect to sustained questioning: knowledge is learned from those who see the truth through praṇipāta, paripraśna and service. brāhmaṇo vā cyuto dharmād yathā śūdratvam āpnuyāt // BrP_223.65 //

This Mahāpurāṇa chapter asks explicitly which karma makes a varṇa fall or rise, how a śūdra becomes a brāhmaṇa, and how a brāhmaṇa becomes a śūdra. Śiva’s answer repeatedly makes action and conduct the mechanism of movement. The most direct lines say that a śūdra who is learned, pure and devoted to dharma receives dharma’s fruit; a śūdra with pure conduct is to be served like a dvija; “neither birth, nor saṃskāra, nor śruti, nor lineage is the cause of dvijatva—conduct alone is the cause”; and a śūdra established in conduct reaches brāhmaṇatva. The same chapter also contains restrictive food and ritual rules, so this is a strong direct witness for one strand of the text’s varṇa theory, not a claim that every Brahma Purāṇa passage has the same emphasis.

kṣudrāśā bhūrikarmāṇo bāliśā vṛddhamāninaḥ // SB 10.23.9 //

Bali refuses to lie even after his guru curses him: there is no greater adharma than falsehood. Nimi’s narrative condemns a guru who acts against dharma through greed. The Rāsa narrative warns that extraordinary divine conduct must not be imitated by those without mastery, and says to follow only conduct consistent with the speaker’s own teaching. Alongside these warnings, the Bhāgavata gives positive tests: one should not assume the role of guru, parent or protector unless one can free the dependent from death; choose a guru who is learned in śabda and the Supreme and peaceful; take the essence from small and great śāstras like a bee; and do not assume that complete knowledge must come from one guru alone. The Prahlāda and ritualist narratives likewise portray leaders proud of external status, age or ritual volume as blind or foolish.

Manusmṛti — dharma is examined through several pramāṇas and tarka

Manusmṛti 12.105–106

प्रत्यक्षं चानुमानं च शास्त्रं च विविधाऽऽगमम् ।

त्रयं सुविदितं कार्यं धर्मशुद्धिमभीप्सता ॥ १२.१०५ ॥

आर्षं धर्मोपदेशं च वेदशास्त्राविरोधिना ।

यस्तर्केणानुसन्धत्ते स धर्मं वेद नैतरः ॥ १२.१०६ ॥

One seeking purified dharma should thoroughly understand direct perception, inference and scripture/varied āgama. svayam ācarate yasmād ācāraṃ sthāpayaty api /

ācinoti ca śāstrārthān ācāryas tena cocyate // LiP 1.10.15 //

vijñeyaṃ śravaṇāc chrautaṃ smaraṇāt smārtam ucyate /

ijyā vedātmakaṃ śrautaṃ smārtaṃ varṇāśramātmakam // LiP 1.10.16 //

dṛṣṭvānurūpam arthaṃ yaḥ pṛṣṭo naivāpi gūhati /

The Liṅga Purāṇa describes a genuine ācārya as mature, non-greedy, self-controlled, sincere, disciplined and straight; he practises the conduct he establishes and understands the meaning of śāstra. Truthful speech reports what has actually been seen. Its definition of sound dharma joins śruti and smṛti with compatibility with the conduct of the śiṣṭas. These are qualification and cross-checking criteria, not a claim that every customary rule is automatically correct.

Śiva Purāṇa — choose and test a guru by the result of his teaching

Śiva Purāṇa 7.2.15.44–47

tasmād yasyaiva saṃparkāt prabodhānandasaṃbhavaḥ /

guruṃ tam eva vṛṇuyān nāparaṃ matimān naraḥ // ŚivaP 7.2.15.44 //

A discerning person should choose the guru whose association produces awakening and joy. The prospective teacher is to be examined by capable disciples; if, after a year, no awakening or joy at all is found, the seeker may approach another guru. The preceding chapter praises obedience in other contexts, so this passage should be read as a qualification and testing rule, not as a rejection of every guru–śiṣya relationship.

yaṃ tv āryāḥ kriyamāṇaṃ praśaṃsanti sa dharmo yaṃ vigarhante so 'dharmaḥ //

The sūtra says that gods, gandharvas and ancestors do not simply point from the sky and declare, “This is dharma; this is adharma.” What disciplined āryas praise as being done is dharma, and what they censure is adharma; one should follow the conduct of disciplined, self-controlled, non-greedy and non-hypocritical elders across the regions. The passage is not a modern rejection of tradition: it gives a qualified, ethical and communal test for custom.

Taittirīya Upaniṣad — imitate only blameless conduct and consult the competent

Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2–3

यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि नो इतराणि ।

यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि नो इतराणि ॥ १.११.२ ॥

अथ यदि ते कर्मविचिकित्सा वा वृत्तविचिकित्सा वा स्यात् ।

ये तत्र ब्राह्मणाः सम्मर्शिनो युक्ता आयुक्ता अलूक्षा धर्मकामाः स्युः ।

यथा ते तत्र वर्तेरन् तथा तत्र वर्तेथाः ॥ १.११.३ ॥

The teacher says to practise only the blameless actions and good conduct actually found in us—not everything indiscriminately. If doubt arises about an action or conduct, follow the thoughtful, competent, non-harsh people who are devoted to dharma. This is one of the clearest śruti procedures for separating a teacher’s authority from an unexamined imitation of everything the teacher does.

Taittirīya Upaniṣad, Śikṣāvallī 1.11 [https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_upanishhat/shikshaavalii.html] and the 1.11.3 witness [https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-taittiriya-upanishad/d/doc79789.html]. Sandhi and accent markings differ between editions.

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā, Cāṇakya and Yoga Vāsiṣṭha — reason outranks status

Yājñavalkyaśikṣā 232 (recension-dependent); Cāṇakya-rāja-nīti 120; Yoga Vāsiṣṭha/Mokṣopāya 2.18.2–3

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात् ।

सर्वशास्त्ररहस्यं तद् याज्ञवल्क्येन भाषितम् ॥ २३२ ॥

शत्रोरपि गुणा वाच्या दोषा वाच्या गुरोरपि ।

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न वचो गुरुगौरवात् ॥ १२० ॥

अपि पौरुषमादेयं शास्त्रं चेद्युक्तिबोधकम् ।

अन्यत्त्वार्षमपि त्याज्यं भाव्यं न्यायैकसेविना ॥ २.१८.२ ॥

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि ।

अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना ॥ २.१८.३ ॥

न देवचरितं चरेत् ॥ १.६५ ॥

प्रत्यक्षपरोक्षानुमानैः कार्याणि परीक्षेत् ॥ २.४० ॥

बालादपि युक्तमर्थं शृणुयात् ॥ ३.१ ॥

A reasoned statement should be accepted, not accepted merely because of reverence for a guru. Cāṇakya adds that an enemy’s merits and a guru’s faults should both be stated. The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha/Mokṣopāya formulation is deliberately extreme: even a human-authored text may be accepted if it teaches through reasoning, while an unsound statement may be rejected even if attributed to a ṛṣi or spoken by Brahmā; a reasoned sentence may be accepted even from a child. Cāṇakya’s related sūtras add “do not imitate the conduct of the gods,” “examine undertakings by direct, indirect and inferential evidence,” and “hear a reasoned point even from a child.” These are direct status-independent tests of speech.

Nyāya, Yoga, Sāṃkhya and Māṇḍūkya Kārikā — scripture is interpreted through pramāṇa and yukti

Nyāya-sūtra 1.1.3, 1.1.7; Pātañjala Yoga-sūtra 1.7; Sāṃkhya-kārikā 4; Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 3.23

pratyakṣānumānopamānaśabdāḥ pramāṇāni // Nyāya 1.1.3 //

āptopadeśaḥ śabdaḥ // Nyāya 1.1.7 //

pratyakṣānumānāgamāḥ pramāṇāni // Yoga 1.7 //

dṛṣṭam anumānam āptavacanaṃ ca sarvapramāṇasiddhatvāt /

trividhaṃ pramāṇam iṣṭaṃ prameyasiddhiḥ pramāṇād dhi // Sāṃkhya Kārikā 4 //

bhūtato 'bhūtato vāpi sṛjyamāne samā śrutiḥ /

niścitaṃ yuktiyuktaṃ ca yat tad bhavati netarat // Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 3.23 //

These philosophical śāstras do not erase āgama; they place testimony within a field of valid means. Nyāya defines testimony as the instruction of a trustworthy person, so “authority” depends on āptatva rather than a title alone. Yoga lists perception, inference and āgama. Sāṃkhya accepts perception, inference and trustworthy testimony. The Māṇḍūkya Kārikā says that where scriptural creation statements admit more than one reading, the settled conclusion is the one supported by yukti.

Pāñcarātra witness — realised yogins and the irrelevance of kula

Bharadvāja Saṃhitā 1.44, quoted in later Vaiṣṇava commentary and Pāñcarātra discussions

kim apy atrābhijāyante yoginaḥ sarva-yoniṣu /

pratyakṣitātmanāthānāṃ naiṣāṃ cintyaṃ kulādikam // Bhāradvāja Saṃhitā 1.44 //

“Yogins may be born in every kind of womb; for those who have directly realised their Lord, lineage and the like are not to be considered.” In the surrounding Pāñcarātra quotation, the issue is whether a realised person can instruct despite ordinary birth-based disqualifications. This is an Āgama witness for the exceptional force of direct realisation, not a claim that every social eligibility rule disappears in every ritual setting.

Śaiva and Śākta Āgama — a disciple may change gurus when capacity is absent

Abhinavagupta, Tantrāloka 13.335–336; Kubjikāmata Tantra 3.48; Kulārṇava Tantra 13.108 (variant readings)

āmodārthī yathā bhṛṅgaḥ puṣpāt puṣpāntaraṃ vrajet /

vijñānārthī tathā śiṣyo guror gurvantaraṃ vrajet // Tantrāloka 13.335 //

śaktihīnaṃ guruṃ prāpya mokṣajñāne kathaṃ śrayet /

naṣṭamūle drume devi kutaḥ puṣpaphalādikam // Tantrāloka 13.336 //

śaktihīnaṃ guruṃ prāpya śiṣye muktiḥ kutaḥ priye /

mūlacchinne yathā vṛkṣe kutaḥ puṣpaphalādikam // Kubjikāmata 3.48 //

guravo bahavaḥ santi śiṣyavittāpahārakāḥ /

durlabho 'yaṃ gurur devi śiṣyaduḥkhāpahārakaḥ // Kulārṇava 13.108 (received reading) //

The bee seeking fragrance moves from flower to flower; likewise a disciple seeking knowledge may move from guru to guru. A powerless guru cannot give liberation-knowledge, just as a tree with a destroyed root cannot bear flowers and fruit. The Kulārṇava line contrasts many exploitative gurus with the rare teacher who removes the disciple’s suffering. These Āgama passages concern spiritual capacity and exploitation, not varṇa classification, and the Kulārṇava pāda is especially edition-sensitive.

Viṣṇu and Padma Purāṇas — a polemical argument still appeals to yukti

Viṣṇu Purāṇa 3.18.26, 31; Padma Purāṇa, Sṛṣṭikhaṇḍa 1.13.365–370 (Māyāmoha dialogue)

नैतद्युक्तिसहं वाक्यं हिंसा धर्माय नेष्यते ॥ ३.१८.२६ ॥

न ह्याप्तवादा नभसो निपतन्ति महासुराः ।

युक्तिमद्वचनं ग्राह्यं मयान्यैश्च भवद्विधैः ॥ ३.१८.३१ ॥

नैतद्युक्तिसहं वाक्यं हिंसा धर्माय जायते ।

हवींष्यनलदग्धानि फलान्यर्हन्ति कोविदाः ॥

निहतस्य पशोः स्वर्गप्राप्तिर्यदीष्यते ।

स्वपिता यजमानेन किं वा तत्र न हन्यते ॥

न ह्याप्तवादा नभसो निपतन्ति महासुराः ।

युक्तिमद्वचनं ग्राह्यं मयान्यैश्च भवद्विधैः ॥

In the Māyāmoha debate, the claim that violence is dharma is challenged as unable to withstand reasoning; “reliable statements do not fall from the sky,” and reasoned speech should be accepted. The Padma Purāṇa preserves a parallel version and adds a reductio about sacrificing the sacrificer’s own father. Because the speaker is Māyāmoha in a polemical narrative, these verses are evidence of an internal rational argument, not an isolated statement of the Purāṇa’s final theology.

Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa — a parallel ācārya test

vṛddhāś cālolupāś caiva ātmavanto hy adāmbhikāḥ /

samyagvinītā ṛjavas tān ācāryān pracakṣate // BrāṇḍaP 1.32.31 //

The text defines teachers by maturity, freedom from greed and hypocrisy, self-control, straightforwardness, lived conduct and understanding of śāstra. It is a close Purāṇic parallel to the Liṅga and Vāyu witnesses and shows that “ācārya” is a tested description, not merely an inherited title.

Caraka and Suśruta — test the text, test the teacher, understand the meaning

Caraka Saṃhitā, Vimānasthāna 8.3–4, 8.7, 8.17, 8.37, 8.67; Suśruta Saṃhitā, Prabhāṣaṇīya 4.3–4, 4.7

buddhimān ātmanaḥ kāryagurulāghavaṃ karmaphalam anubandhaṃ deśakālau ca viditvā /

yuktidarśanād bhiṣag bubhūṣuḥ śāstram evāditaḥ parīkṣeta // Caraka Vimāna 8.3 //

tato 'nantaram ācāryaṃ parīkṣeta ... paryavadātaśrutaṃ paridṛṣṭakarmāṇaṃ dakṣam ...

anahaṅkṛtam anasūyakam akopanaṃ kleśakṣamaṃ śiṣyavatsalam adhyāpakaṃ jñāpanasamarthaṃ ca // Caraka Vimāna 8.4 //

manāḥpurasarābhir vāgbhiḥ sūtram anukraman punaḥ punar āvartayet /

buddhvā samyag anupraviśyārthatattvaṃ svadoṣaparihārārthaṃ paradoṣapramāṇārthaṃ ca // Caraka Vimāna 8.7 //

na ca nigrahabhayād udvijet nigṛhya cainaṃ na hṛṣyet /

na ca mohād ekāntagrāhī syān na cāviditam artham anuvarṇayet // Caraka Vimāna 8.17 //

siddhānto nāma sa parīkṣakair bahuvidhaṃ parīkṣya hetubhiś ca sādhayitvā sthāpyate nirṇayaḥ // Caraka Vimāna 8.37 //

tāḥ sarvāḥ samavekṣyāvekṣya sarvaṃ vākyaṃ brūyāt /

nāprakṛtakam aśāstram aparīkṣitam asādhakam ākulam avyāpakaṃ vā /

sarvaṃ ca hetumad brūyāt // Caraka Vimāna 8.67 //

adhigatam apy adhyayanam aprabhāṣitam arthataḥ /

kharasya candanabhāra iva kevalaṃ pariśramakaraṃ bhavati // Suśruta 4.3 //

yathā kharaś candanabhāravāhī bhārasya vettā na tu candanasya /

evaṃ hi śāstrāṇi bahūny adhītya cārtheṣu mūḍhāḥ kharavad vahanti // Suśruta 4.4 //

ekaṃ śāstram adhīyāno na vidyāc chāstraniścayam /

tasmād bahuśrutaḥ śāstraṃ vijānīyāc cikitsakaḥ // Suśruta 4.7 //

These medical śāstras turn the same principle into a procedure. Examine the text through reasoning before choosing a teacher; examine the teacher’s learning, experience, character and ability to explain; repeat a teaching only after entering its meaning; do not fear refutation, become one-sided, or speak about what you do not know; and call a conclusion “established” only after examiners have tested it in many ways and supported it with reasons. Suśruta adds that rote study without understanding is like a donkey carrying sandalwood and that certainty requires broad learning.

These are medical texts, so their examples are clinical; their method of testing texts, teachers and claims is nevertheless directly comparable.

Bhāgavata Purāṇa — perception, tradition, inference and reason in inquiry

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.10.4; 11.19.17; 11.22.9; 11.28.9 and 11.28.18

nivṛttaṃ karma seveta pravṛttaṃ matparas tyajet /

jijñāsāyāṃ sampravṛtto nādriyeta karmacodanām // SB 11.10.4 //

śrutiḥ pratyakṣam aitihyam anumānaṃ catuṣṭayam /

pramāṇeṣv anavasthānād vikalpāt sa virajyate // SB 11.19.17 //

paurvāparyam ato 'mīṣāṃ prasaṅkhyānam abhīpsatām /

yathā viviktaṃ yad vaktraṃ gṛhṇīmo yuktisambhavāt // SB 11.22.9 //

pratyakṣeṇānumānena nigamenātmasaṃvidā /

ādyantavad asaj jñātvā niḥsaṅgo vicared iha // SB 11.28.9 //

jñānaṃ viveko nigamas tapaś ca pratyakṣam aitihyam athānumānam /

ādyantayor asya yad eva kevalaṃ kālaś ca hetuś ca tad eva madhye // SB 11.28.18 //

The Bhāgavata says that an advanced seeker engaged in inquiry is not bound by injunctions aimed at ordinary fruit-seeking action. It names śruti, perception, tradition and inference as a fourfold field of knowledge; among differing analyses, it accepts what can be clearly established through reasoning; and it joins perception, inference, scripture, inner realisation, discrimination, austerity, time and causation in discerning the world. The wording is philosophically dense and should not be flattened into the claim that every source has identical authority.

Bṛhaspati Smṛti — a legal decision cannot rely on scripture alone

Bṛhaspati Smṛti, Vyavahārakāṇḍa 1.1.111, 114, 116–117

dharmaśāstrārthaśāstrābhyām avirodhena pārthivaḥ /

samīkṣamāṇo nipuṇaṃ vyavahāragatiṃ nayet // BṛhS 1.1.111 //

kevalaṃ śāstram āśritya na kartavyo vinirṇayaḥ /

yuktihīne vicāre tu dharmahāniḥ prajāyate // BṛhS 1.1.114 //

cauro 'cauraḥ sādhv asādhu jāyate vyavahārataḥ /

yuktiṃ vinā vicāreṇa māṇḍavyaś coratāṃ gataḥ // BṛhS 1.1.116 //

asatyāḥ satyasadṛśāḥ satyāś cāsatyasaṃnibhāḥ /

dṛśyante bhrāntijanakās tasmād yuktyā vicārayet // BṛhS 1.1.117 //

In legal procedure the ruler is to examine the case carefully in a way consistent with Dharmaśāstra and Arthaśāstra. A decision must not be made by relying on scripture alone: when inquiry lacks reasoning, dharma is harmed. Without reasoned investigation an innocent person can be made a thief, and false claims can look true while true claims look false. These are judicial verses, but they state the same anti-circular principle used in the varṇa debate.

Kauṭilya Arthaśāstra and Cāṇakya Sūtra — inquire by reasons and hear every voice

sāṃkhyaṃ yogo lokāyataṃ cety ānvīkṣikī // KAZ01.2.10 //

dharmādharmau trayyām arthānarthau vārttāyāṃ nayānayau daṇḍanītyāṃ balābale ca etāsāṃ hetubhir anvīkṣamāṇā lokasya upakaroti

vyasane 'bhyudaye ca buddhim avasthāpayati prajñāvākyakriyāvaiśāradyaṃ ca karoti // KAZ01.2.11 //

pradīpaḥ sarvavidyānām upāyaḥ sarvakarmaṇām /

āśrayaḥ sarvadharmāṇāṃ śaśvad ānvīkṣikī matā // KAZ01.2.12 //

na kaṃcid avamanyeta sarvasya śṛṇuyān matam /

bālasyāpy arthavad vākyaṃ upayuñjīta paṇḍitaḥ // Kauṭilya Arthaśāstra 1.15.22–23 //

na devacaritaṃ caret // Cāṇakya Sūtra 1.65 //

na daivamātrapramāṇānāṃ kāryasiddhiḥ // Cāṇakya Sūtra 2.37 //

Kauṭilya calls Ānvīkṣikī—Sāṃkhya, Yoga and Lokāyata—a lamp for all sciences: it examines dharma and adharma, gain and loss, policy, strength and weakness by reasons. A wise person should despise no one, hear everyone’s view, and use a meaningful statement even from a child. Cāṇakya’s compact rules say not to imitate divine conduct, not to rely on fate alone, to examine undertakings by direct, indirect and inferential evidence, and to hear a reasonable point even from a child. The first long pāda follows the GRETIL/Wisdomlib wording; sandhi and spacing vary by edition.

Garuḍa Purāṇa — examine the next footing before abandoning the first

Garuḍa Purāṇa 1.109.4–5

calaty ekena pādena tiṣṭhaty ekena buddhimān /

na parīkṣya paraṃ sthānaṃ pūrvam āyatanaṃ tyajet //

tyajed deśam asad vṛttaṃ vāsaṃ sopadravaṃ tyajet /

tyajet kṛpaṇarājaṃ mitraṃ māyāmayaṃ tyajet //

A wise person does not abandon one footing for another without examining the alternative. One should leave a corrupt country, a dangerous residence, a miserly ruler and a deceptive friend. In the present dossier this is a practical prudence parallel: changing a guru, lineage or interpretive framework should follow examination rather than impulse.

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa — hear opinions, test qualities, and decide by guṇa/doṣa

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddha-kāṇḍa 6.17.31–42 (the council’s examination of Vibhīṣaṇa)

yad uktaṃ kapirājena rāvaṇāvarajaṃ prati /

vākyaṃ hetumad atyarthaṃ bhavadbhir api tat śrutam // 6.17.32 //

suhṛdā hy arthakṛcchreṣu yuktam buddhimatā satā /

samarthenāpi saṃdeṣṭuṃ śāśvatīṃ bhūtim icchatā // 6.17.33 //

tvaṃ hi satyavrataḥ śūro dhārmiko dṛḍhavikramaḥ /

parīkṣyakārī smṛtimān nisṛṣṭātmā suhṛtsu ca // 6.17.36 //

tasmād ekaikaśas tāvad bruvantu sacivās tava /

hetuto matisampannāḥ samarthāḥ ca punaḥ punaḥ // 6.17.37 //

arthānarthau viniścitya vyavasāyaṃ bhajeta ha /

guṇataḥ saṃgrahaṃ kuryād doṣatas tu visarjayet // 6.17.41 //

yadi doṣo mahāṃs tasmiṃs tyajyatām aviśaṅkitam /

guṇān vāpi bahūn jñātvā saṃgrahaḥ kriyatāṃ nṛpa // 6.17.42 //

Rāma’s council is asked to give reasoned opinions. A good adviser speaks with intelligence in difficult matters; a good ruler acts after examination; and a person is accepted or rejected after the advantages and faults have been determined—accepted for many good qualities, rejected for serious faults. This is practical evidence-based judgment, not a varṇa definition, but it is a direct Itihāsa parallel to evaluating a person by qualities rather than status or first impression.

Nārada Purāṇa — many teachers, a tested hierarchy, and the place of tarka

adhyāpakāś ca vedānāṃ mantravyākhyākṛtas tathā // NarP_1,9.93 //

pitā ca dharmavaktā ca viśeṣaguravaḥ smṛtāḥ /

eteṣām api bhūpāla śṛṇuṣva pravaraṃ gurum // NarP_1,9.94 //

sarvaśāstrārthatattvajñair bhāṣitaṃ pravadāmi te /

The Purāṇa lists several kinds of guru, then asks the reader to hear which is pre-eminent: the learned person who explains Purāṇas joined to dharma and helps cut the bonds of saṃsāra. This is therefore a qualified companion to comparative inquiry, not an unrestricted claim that independent reasoning outranks every scriptural source.

Pāśupatasūtra with Kauṇḍinya’s Pañcārthabhāṣya — pramāṇas and a qualified teacher

evam etāni trīṇi pramāṇāni // PSBh_1.1:43–44 //

Kauṇḍinya’s commentary explicitly asks whether the promised result is for a tested disciple and answers in terms of the disciple’s qualifications. It then defines three means of knowledge—perception, inference and trustworthy testimony—and describes āgama as scripture transmitted through the guru-paramparā from Maheśvara. This is not a rejection of āgama; it is a Śaiva example of authority operating through qualification and multiple pramāṇas.

Aṣṭāvakra Gītā — multiplicity of learned opinions and dispassion

Aṣṭāvakra Gītā 9.5 (late Advaita dialogue; related rather than direct varṇa evidence)

nānā mataṃ maharṣīṇāṃ sādhūnāṃ yogināṃ tathā /

dṛṣṭvā nirvedam āpannaḥ ko na śāmyati mānavaḥ // 9.5 //

“Having seen the many opinions of great seers, saints and yogins, what person would not become quiet through dispassion?” The verse is a concise parallel for recognising plurality of learned views; it recommends detachment from opinion rather than laying out a complete theory of proof.

Sanskrit witness: Aṣṭāvakra Gītā [https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_giitaa/ashtgita.html]. This is a later Advaita dialogue, not a Vedic or Purāṇic passage.

Reading the witnesses together

The closest direct parallels to the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa’s explicit argument are the Sarpa dialogue in the Mahābhārata (vṛtta rather than jāti), Śānti-parvan 12.181–182 (colour and conduct tests), Anuśāsana-parvan 13.131 and Brahma Purāṇa 223 (movement between varṇas and vṛtta as the cause of dvijatva), Bhāgavata 7.11.35 (designation by lakṣaṇa), and the Vajrasūcika’s rejection of birth as the essence of a brāhmaṇa. The Gītā supplies guṇa–karma–svabhāva vocabulary; Manu 10.64–65 and Skanda 239.31 supply context-bound birth/saṃskāra transitions; Chāndogya 4.4.4–5, Nārada Bhakti Sūtra 72 and the Bhāgavata’s śvapaca passages give narrative and devotional parallels. The Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, Āpastamba, Taittirīya, Nārada, Bṛhaspati, Kauṭilya, Nyāya, Yoga, Sāṃkhya, Yājñavalkyaśikṣā, Yoga Vāsiṣṭha, Vivekacūḍāmaṇi, Pāśupata, Liṅga, Śiva, Hari-bhakti-vilāsa, Garuḍa, Pāñcarātra, Prakāśasaṃhitā, Śaiva/Śākta Āgama and Āyurveda passages add a second, methodological current: authority is qualified by conduct, competence, pramāṇa, yukti, lived results and ethical examination. These texts are evidence for recurring śāstric currents, not proof that every Hindu text or every historical community held one uniform position.
