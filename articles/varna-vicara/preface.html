Before we embark onto the topic of Varna in Sanatana-Dharma we must begin with an investigation into how something may be proven at all within Hinduism as a whole. The scope of this project does not exclude evidence pertaining to data external to Hinduism nor to dogmatically assert internal claims. The preceding sentence will be explained in further detail as the document goes on but was iterated as such to provide a basic pre-print of our method. Let us now investigate some pramanas which we shall reflect upon to conclude how investigation on Varna or Varṇa-vicāra.

Show Remark 1

Thus , with the support of pramanas as well as sound reasoning one may conclude that the consensus amongst acharyas is no proof of a scriptural stance. The statements , conduct and conclusions of all acharyas are to be subjected to

  1. logic and reasoning
  2. shashtra

Objection. But acharyas dedicate many years to studying the Shashtras , and follow a strict parampara. Hence the consensus of the paramparas is to be taken with confidence as one should interpret the scripture with the stance of an acharya based on the fact that an acharya is entirely based on the scripture.

Reply. This is not so for many issues. If consensus / agreement of the currently existing paramparas is to be taken as a valid stance in its own regard we reject this for many gurus have gone against the Paramparic consensus to establish their sampradaya. Shankarcharya , Ramanuja , Madhva etc. This objection will also be in conflict with the previously established pramanas that one should verify an acharyas claim using the scriptures , THERE IS NO STATEMENT anywhere saying the consensus of acharyas on a topic pertains to an established fact on that topic.

Objection. This is in regards to theological issues which we take the acharyas to be infallible on , and not philosophical

Reply. This is not so , we reject this for the very obvious reason that Varna has direct philosophical implications meaning it is to be addressed by the scope of philosophy as well. This is because the only way to judge if a theological claim is consistent with other theological claims is through some metaphysical or logical laws. Also , theological claims that directly entail certain behaviours and actions in society crosses directly over into ethics , meta-ethics and epistemology. It is impossible to imagine a theological claim entirely devoid of interaction with philosophy or empirical data and is relevant to day to day life. Another obvious reason to reject the current statement is the basic fact that acharyas conflict on matters of theology ( and philosophy ) all the time , including varna.

Show Remark 2

The notion that one needs a parampara to interpret scripture also may fall into a circular chain of reasoning

  1. Our sect is authoritative because the shashtras support it
  2. The guru parampara tells us how the shashtra is to be interpreted, including the passages establishing its own authority

Shashtra validates a parampara → Parampara determines how to interpret shashtra → The prior interpretation validates the parampara

The statements stated above are no actual issue in and of themselves as no system can prove its own fundamental foundations without some pre-existing assumptions within the particular system.

Hence it is proven that any notion of an infallible parmapara is simply a faith based claim from which their system is developed but there is no reason for any one else to accept this. Hence we should validate any claim using the maxim:

“One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.”
