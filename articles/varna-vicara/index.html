Invocation

सरस्वति नमस्तुभ्यं वरदे कामरूपिणि । विद्यारम्भं करिष्यामि सिद्धिर्भवतु मे सदा ॥

O Goddess Sarasvatī, I bow to you. You are the giver of boons and the fulfiller of wishes. As I begin my studies, please bless me so that I may attain success always.

Preface

Introduction

Before turning to varṇa in Sanātana Dharma, this inquiry begins with the means by which claims may be established in Hindu thought. It considers scriptural and relevant external evidence without assuming the conclusion.

Bhagavad Gītā 16.23–24

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः । न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥ तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ । ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥

The Supreme Lord said, He who, discarding scriptural procedure, acts according to his own wishes does not attain success, happiness, or the highest goal. Scripture is therefore your basis for determining what should and should not be done. Having understood the scriptural injunction, perform your duty in this world.

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.1.2

चोदनालक्षणोऽर्थो धर्मः ॥

That which is enjoined by the statement of scripture is dharma.

Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1.204

इतिहासपुराणाभ्यां वेदं समुपबृंहयेत् । बिभेत्यल्पश्रुतावेदो मामयं प्रतरिष्यति ।

One should explain the Vedas with the Itihāsas and Purāṇas. The Vedas fear that a person of little learning will misinterpret them.

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā 232

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात् ॥

One should accept the statement of a guru when it is reasonable or established by scripture. One should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Yoga Vāsiṣṭha 2.18.3

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि । अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना ॥

A reasonable statement should be accepted even when spoken by a child. An unreasonable statement should be discarded like a piece of straw, even if it was spoken by lotus born Brahmā himself.

Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2

यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि । नो इतराणि ॥
यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि । नो इतराणि ॥

Only those actions of ours that are unobjectionable should be followed, not the others. Only those good deeds of ours should be emulated, not the others.

Remark on Ācārya Consensus

Thus, on the basis of the pramāṇas and sound reasoning, we may conclude that consensus among ācāryas does not, in itself, establish a scriptural position. The statements, conduct and conclusions of all ācāryas must be assessed in light of:

  1. Logic and reasoning
  2. Śāstra

Objection:

Ācāryas devote many years to studying the śāstras and adhere to a strict paramparā. The consensus of these paramparās should therefore be accepted with confidence. Scripture ought to be interpreted in accordance with the position of an ācārya, since that position is grounded entirely in scripture.

Reply:

This does not hold on many issues. If the consensus of existing paramparās is regarded as sufficient grounds for accepting a position, we reject that premise. Many teachers have challenged the consensus of earlier traditions in establishing their own sampradāyas, including Śaṅkarācārya, Rāmānuja and Madhva. The objection also conflicts with the pramāṇas cited above, according to which an ācārya’s claims should be examined against scripture. There is no scriptural statement establishing that the consensus of ācāryas on a given question is, by itself, proof of the truth of their position.

Objection:

This claim of infallibility concerns theological questions, not philosophical ones.

Reply:

We reject this distinction in the present inquiry because varṇa has direct philosophical implications and must therefore also be examined philosophically. Assessing whether one theological claim is consistent with another requires appeal to logical or metaphysical principles. Moreover, theological claims that prescribe conduct in society have implications for ethics, metaethics and epistemology. It is difficult to conceive of a theological claim relevant to everyday life that has no bearing on either philosophy or empirical evidence. Finally, ācāryas frequently disagree on theological as well as philosophical matters, including questions concerning varṇa.

Remark on Paramparā and Circular Reasoning

The claim that one requires a paramparā to interpret scripture may also involve circular reasoning:

  1. Our sect is authoritative because the śāstras support it.
  2. The guru-paramparā determines how the śāstras are to be interpreted, including the passages said to establish its own authority.

Śāstra establishes the authority of a paramparā. That paramparā determines the interpretation of śāstra, and this interpretation, in turn, establishes its own authority. This is not necessarily a problem in itself, since no system can establish its own fundamental premises without relying on certain prior assumptions. The claim that a particular paramparā is infallible is therefore a faith-based premise upon which its system is developed. That premise need not be accepted by those outside the system. Claims should instead be assessed in accordance with the following maxim:

One should accept the statement of a guru when it is reasonable or established by scripture. One should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Pūrvapakṣa

Pūrvapakṣa

According to the pūrvapakṣa, varṇa is directly correlated with, and indeed predicated upon, jāti. A person born into a brāhmaṇa-yoni belongs to the brāhmaṇa jāti and the brāhmaṇa varṇa. The same principle applies to the other varṇas. However, brāhmaṇa status is not fully established by jāti alone. Vedic rites, including initiation and study, are also required. In short, birth determines initial varṇa status and eligibility, whereas saṃskāras and Vedic karmas complete the status required for certain adhikāras. On this view, one who is not born into a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya or vaiśya yoni can never attain dvija status and must follow the dharma of one's jāti. The following pramāṇas are cited in support of the pūrvapakṣa:

Manusmṛti 10.4–5

ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यस्त्रयो वर्णा द्विजातयः । चतुर्थ एकजातिस्तु शूद्रो नास्ति तु पञ्चमः ॥ सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु । आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते ॥

The brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, and vaiśya are the three twice born varṇas. The fourth, the śūdra, is once born, and there is no fifth. Among all varṇas, those born in the natural order from wives of equal status are to be regarded as belonging to the same jāti as their fathers.

Padma Purāṇa 7.47.129

जन्मना ब्राह्मणो ज्ञेयः संस्कारैर्द्विज उच्यते ॥

By birth he is known as a brāhmaṇa, and through the initiatory rites he is called a dvija, or twice born.

Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10.7

तद्य इह रमणीयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते रमणीयां योनिमापद्येरन् ब्राह्मणयोनिं वा क्षत्रिययोनिं वा वैश्ययोनिं वा ।
अथ य इह कपूयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते कपूयां योनिमापद्येरन् श्वयोनिं वा सूकरयोनिं वा चण्डालयोनिं वा ॥

Those whose conduct in this world was good quickly attain a good birth, birth as a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, or vaiśya. Those whose conduct was evil quickly attain an evil birth, birth as a dog, a pig, or a caṇḍāla.

Remark on Birth-Based Brāhmaṇa Privileges

According to this position, birth as a brāhmaṇa confers certain privileges regardless of the extent of one's wrongdoing. The following passages illustrate this:

Manusmṛti 8.380

न जातु ब्राह्मणं हन्यात् सर्वपापेष्वपि स्थितम् । राष्ट्रादेनं बहिः कुर्यात् समग्रधनमक्षतम् ॥

Verily he shall not kill the Brāhmaṇa, even though he be steeped in all crimes. He should banish him from the kingdom, with all his property and unhurt.

Manusmṛti 8.20

जातिमात्रोपजीवी वा कामं स्याद् ब्राह्मणब्रुवः । धर्मप्रवक्ता नृपतेर् न तु शूद्रः कथं चन ॥

Even a so called Brāhmaṇa, who makes a living by his caste only, may, at pleasure be the propounder of the Law for the king, but not a Śūdra under any circumstances.

Manusmṛti 8.417

विस्रब्धं ब्राह्मणः शूद्राद् द्रव्योपादानमाचरेत् । न हि तस्यास्ति किं चित् स्वं भर्तृहार्यधनो हि सः ॥

The Brāhmaṇa may confidently have recourse to seizing the goods of the Śūdra, as the latter has no property, and his property is meant to be seized by the master.

Refutation of Pūrvapakṣa

In examining the pūrvapakṣa, we must apply the following maxim:

One should accept the statement of a guru when it is reasonable or established by scripture. One should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

We begin with scriptural pramāṇas against the claim that jāti is a universal prerequisite of varṇa.

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa on Varṇa

Mlecchas and Śūdras Attaining Brāhmaṇa Status

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.16.56–58

विश्वामित्रस्तु राजेन्द्र ब्राह्मणत्वजिगीषया । तपश्चचार विपुलं सन्तापाय दिवौकसाम् ॥ ब्राह्मणत्वं न लेभेऽसौ लेभे विघ्नाननेकशः ॥ ततस्तु नियमात्तेषां तिथीनां प्रवरा तिथिः । उपोषिता बहुविधा ज्ञात्वा ब्रह्मप्रियां तिथिम् ॥ ततो ददौ ब्रह्मा विश्वामित्राय धीमते । इहैव तेन देहेन ब्राह्मणत्वं सुदुर्लभम् ॥ तिथीनां प्रवरा ह्येषा तिथीनामुत्तमा तिथिः । क्षत्रियो वैश्यशूद्रौ वा ब्राह्मणत्वमवाप्नुयुः ॥

O king of kings, Viśvāmitra, desiring to attain brāhmaṇa status, performed vast austerity that caused distress among the heavenly beings. Yet he did not obtain that status and encountered many obstacles. Then, learning which date was dear to Brahmā, he observed in many ways the foremost of lunar dates according to its discipline and fasted upon it. Thereupon Brahmā granted the wise Viśvāmitra the exceedingly difficult attainment of brāhmaṇa status here in this very body. This is indeed the foremost and highest of lunar dates. The source says that through it a kṣatriya, vaiśya, or śūdra may attain brāhmaṇa status.

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.16.61

हैहयैस्तालजङ्घैश्च तुरुष्कैर्यवनैः शकैः । उपोषिता इहात्रैव ब्राह्मणत्वमभीप्सुभिः ॥

The source says that Haihayas, Tālajaṅghas, Turks, Yavanas, and Śakas who desired brāhmaṇa status also fasted upon it here.

Remark on the Fast to Become a Brāhmaṇa

Viśvāmitra was not born into the brāhmaṇa jāti. In some accounts, he attains brāhmaṇa status through a prolonged period of tapas. In this account, however, his austerities do not achieve the desired result. He attains brāhmaṇa status only after observing the prescribed fast for a year. The account of Viśvāmitra is understood here as the attainment of brāhmaṇa status in the full sense, including ritual rights. The Purāṇa states that mlecchas and śūdras may also observe this fast to attain brāhmaṇa status. We should understand that attainment in the same sense as Viśvāmitra's.
