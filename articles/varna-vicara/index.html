Traditional Sarasvatī Vidyārambha prayer

सरस्वति नमस्तुभ्यं वरदे कामरूपिणि ।
विद्यारम्भं करिष्यामि सिद्धिर्भवतु मे सदा ॥

O Goddess Sarasvatī, I bow to you. You are the giver of boons and the fulfiller of wishes. As I begin my studies, please bless me so that I may attain success always.

Preface

Introduction

Before turning to varṇa in Sanātana Dharma, this inquiry begins with the means by which claims may be established in Hindu thought. It considers scriptural and relevant external evidence without assuming the conclusion.

Bhagavad Gītā 16.23–24

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः । न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥ तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ । ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥

The Supreme Lord said, “He who, discarding scriptural procedure, acts according to his own wishes does not attain success, happiness, or the highest goal. Scripture is therefore your basis for determining what should and should not be done. Having understood the scriptural injunction, perform your duty in this world.”

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.1.2

चोदनालक्षणोऽर्थो धर्मः।। १.१.२ ।।

That which is enjoined by the statement of scripture is dharma

Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva 1.1.204

इतिहासपुराणाभ्यां वेदं समुपबृंहयेत्। बिभेत्यल्पश्रुतावेदो मामयं प्रतरिष्यति।

One should explain the Vedas with the Itihāsas and Purāṇas. The Vedas fear that a person of little learning will misinterpret them.

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā 232

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात्॥

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Yoga Vāsiṣṭha 2.18.3

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि। अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना॥ ३॥

A reasonable statement should be accepted even when spoken by a child. An unreasonable statement should be discarded like a piece of straw, even if it was spoken by lotus-born Brahmā himself.

Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2

यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि। नो इतराणि॥ यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि। नो इतराणि॥

Only those actions of ours that are unobjectionable should be followed, not the others. Only those good deeds of ours should be emulated, not the others

Remark on Ācārya Consensus

Thus, with the support of pramāṇas as well as sound reasoning one may conclude that the consensus amongst ācāryas is no proof of a scriptural stance. The statements, conduct and conclusions of all ācāryas are to be subjected to

  1. Logic and reasoning
  2. Śāstra

Objection. But ācāryas dedicate many years to studying the Śāstras, and follow a strict paramparā. Hence the consensus of the paramparās is to be taken with confidence as one should interpret the scripture with the stance of an ācārya based on the fact that an ācārya is entirely based on the scripture.

Reply. This is not so for many issues. If consensus / agreement of the currently existing paramparās is to be taken as a valid stance in its own regard we reject this for many gurus have gone against the Paramparic consensus to establish their sampradaya. Śaṅkarācārya, Rāmānuja, Madhva etc. This objection will also be in conflict with the previously established pramāṇas that one should verify an ācāryas claim using the scriptures, THERE IS NO STATEMENT anywhere saying the consensus of ācāryas on a topic pertains to an established fact on that topic.

Objection. This is in regards to theological issues which we take the ācāryas to be infallible on, and not philosophical

Reply. This is not so, we reject this for the very obvious reason that Varṇa has direct philosophical implications meaning it is to be addressed by the scope of philosophy as well. This is because the only way to judge if a theological claim is consistent with other theological claims is through some metaphysical or logical laws. Also, theological claims that directly entail certain behaviours and actions in society crosses directly over into ethics, meta-ethics and epistemology. It is impossible to imagine a theological claim entirely devoid of interaction with philosophy or empirical data and is relevant to day to day life. Another obvious reason to reject the current statement is the basic fact that ācāryas conflict on matters of theology (and philosophy) all the time, including varṇa.

Remark on Paramparā and Circular Reasoning

The notion that one needs a paramparā to interpret scripture also may fall into a circular chain of reasoning

  1. Our sect is authoritative because the śāstras support it
  2. The guru paramparā tells us how the śāstra is to be interpreted, including the passages establishing its own authority

Śāstra validates a paramparā → Paramparā determines how to interpret śāstra → The prior interpretation validates the paramparā

The statements stated above are no actual issue in and of themselves as no system can prove its own fundamental foundations without some pre-existing assumptions within the particular system.

Hence it is proven that any notion of an infallible parmapara is simply a faith based claim from which their system is developed but there is no reason for any one else to accept this. Hence we should validate any claim using the maxim:

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.

Pūrvapakṣa

Pūrvapakṣa

It is said that Varṇa is directly correlated and in fact predicated upon by Jāti. One born as a brāhmaṇa-yoni belongs to the Brāhmaṇa jāti and Brāhmaṇa varṇa and likewise for the other varṇas. However one simply is not a Brāhmaṇa entirely by jāti alone but also by the Vedic karmas pertaining to initiation, vedic study etc. In short birth/jāti determines initial varṇa status and eligibility whereas saṃskāras and vedic karmas complete and actualise the status required for certain adhikāras. In other words one without a Brāhmaṇa-Kṣatriya-Vaiśya yoni can never attain dvija-hood and one should follow his jāti dharma. Let us provide some pramāṇa for the Purvapaksha:

Manusmṛti 10.4–5

ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यस्त्रयो वर्णा द्विजातयः। चतुर्थ एकजातिस्तु शूद्रो नास्ति तु पञ्चमः॥ ४॥ सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु। आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते॥ ५॥

The brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, and vaiśya are the three twice-born varṇas; the fourth, the śūdra, is once-born, and there is no fifth. Among all varṇas, those born in the natural order from wives of equal status are to be regarded as belonging to the same jāti as their fathers.

Padma Purāṇa 7.47.129

जन्मना ब्राह्मणो ज्ञेयः संस्कारैर्द्विज उच्यते॥ १२९॥

By birth he is known as a brāhmaṇa, and through the initiatory rites he is called a dvija, or twice-born

Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10.7

तद्य इह रमणीयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते रमणीयां योनिमापद्येरन् ब्राह्मणयोनिं वा क्षत्रिययोनिं वा वैश्ययोनिं वा। अथ य इह कपूयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते कपूयां योनिमापद्येरन् श्वयोनिं वा सूकरयोनिं वा चण्डालयोनिं वा॥

Those whose conduct in this world was good quickly attain a good birth: birth as a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, or vaiśya. Those whose conduct was evil quickly attain an evil birth: birth as a dog, a pig, or a caṇḍāla.

Remark on Birth-Based Brāhmaṇa Privileges

However being born as a Brāhmaṇa will provide certain benefits no matter how indulged in sin you are. Such as:

Manusmṛti 8.380

न जातु ब्राह्मणं हन्यात् सर्वपापेष्वपि स्थितम् । राष्ट्रादेनं बहिः कुर्यात् समग्रधनमक्षतम् ॥ ३८० ॥

Verily he shall not kill the Brāhmaṇa, even though he be steeped in all crimes; he should banish him from the kingdom, with all his property and unhurt.—(380).

Manusmṛti 8.20

जातिमात्रोपजीवी वा कामं स्याद् ब्राह्मणब्रुवः । धर्मप्रवक्ता नृपतेर् न तु शूद्रः कथं चन ॥ २० ॥

Even a so-called Brāhmaṇa, who makes a living by his caste only, may, at pleasure be the propounder of the Law for the king,—but not a Śūdra under any circumstances.—(20)

Manusmṛti 8.417

विस्रब्धं ब्राह्मणः शूद्राद् द्रव्योपादानमाचरेत् । न हि तस्यास्ति किं चित् स्वं भर्तृहार्यधनो हि सः ॥ ४१७ ॥

The Brāhmaṇa may confidently have recourse to seizing the goods of the Śūdra; as the latter has no property, and his property is meant to be seized by the master.—(417)

Refutation of Pūrvapakṣa

In order to refute the PurvaPaksha position we must make use of the maxim:

One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.”

Let us begin with Scriptural Pramāṇas against Jāti as a universal prerequisite of Varṇa.

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa on Varṇa

Mlecchas and Śūdras becoming Brāhmaṇa like Viśvāmitra

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.16.56–58

विश्वामित्रस्तु राजेन्द्र ब्राह्मणत्वजिगीषया । तपश्चचार विपुलं सन्तापाय दिवौकसाम् । । ब्राह्मणत्वं न लेभेऽसौ लेभे विघ्नाननेकशः । । ५६
ततस्तु नियमात्तेषां तिथीनां प्रवरा तिथिः । उपोषिता बहुविधा ज्ञात्वा ब्रह्मप्रियां तिथिम् । । ५७
ततो ददौ ब्रह्मा विश्वामित्राय धीमते । इहैव तेन देहेन ब्राह्मणत्वं सुदुर्लभम् । । ५८
तिथीनां प्रवरा ह्येषा तिथीनामुत्तमा तिथिः । क्षत्रियो वैश्यशूद्रौ वा ब्राह्मणत्वमवाप्नुयुः । । ५९

‘O king of kings, Viśvāmitra, desiring to attain brāhmaṇa status, performed vast austerity that caused distress among the heavenly beings; yet he did not obtain that status and encountered many obstacles.’

‘Then, learning which date was dear to Brahmā, he observed in many ways the foremost of lunar dates according to its discipline and fasted upon it.’

‘Thereupon Brahmā granted the wise Viśvāmitra the exceedingly difficult attainment of brāhmaṇa status here in this very body.’

This is indeed the foremost and highest of lunar dates. The source says that through it a kṣatriya, vaiśya, or śūdra may attain brāhmaṇa status.’

Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 1.16.61

हैहयैस्तालजङ्घैश्च तुरुष्कैर्यवनैः शकैः । उपोषिता इहात्रैव ब्राह्मणत्वमभीप्सुभिः १ । । ६१

‘The source says that Haihayas, Tālajaṅghas, Turks, Yavanas, and Śakas who desired brāhmaṇa status also fasted upon it here.’

Remark on the Fast to Become a Brāhmaṇa

Viśvāmitra was not born a Brāhmaṇa or Brāhmaṇa-Jāti. In some stories he does a long period of Tapas by which he attained Brahminhood. In this story too he does a long time of tapas, but that is stated to have been useless. Only by performing this fast for a year he attained Brahminhood. A basic understanding of the Viśvāmitra story tells us that he became a true Brāhmaṇa due to the attainment of ritual rights etc. The Purāṇa states that Mlecchas and Śūdras can also do this fast to attain Brahminhood. We should understand it to be in the same sense as Viśvāmitra.
