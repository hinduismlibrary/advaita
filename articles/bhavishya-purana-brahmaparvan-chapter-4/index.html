Bhaviṣya Purāṇa — Brāhmaparvan — Chapter 4: Rites of Passage, Marriage, and Student Duties

BP 1.4.1

सुमन्तुरुवाच
केशान्तः षोडशे वर्षे ब्राह्मणस्य विधीयते ।
राजन्यबन्धोर्द्वाविंशे वैश्यस्य त्र्यधिके ततः ॥ १

Sumantu said: The first shaving of the head is prescribed for a Brahmin in the sixteenth year, for a Kshatriya in the twenty-second, and for a Vaishya three years later.

BP 1.4.2

अमन्त्रिका सदा कार्या स्त्रीणां चूडा महीपते ।
संस्कारहेतोः कायस्य यथाकालं विभागशः ॥ २

For women, the tuft-cutting and other bodily rites should always be performed without mantras, O king, at the proper times and in due order.

BP 1.4.3

वैवाहिको विधिः स्त्रीणां संस्कारो नैगमः स्मृतः ।
निवसेद्वा गुरोर्वापि गृहे वाग्निपरिक्रिया ॥ ३

Marriage is remembered as the Vedic rite for women. Residence in the teacher's house, or a home with fire rites, is their observance.

BP 1.4.4

एष ते कथितो राजन्नौपनायनिको विधिः ।
द्विजातीनां महाबाहो उत्पत्तिव्यञ्जकः परः ॥ ४

Thus I have related to you the procedure of initiation, O king: the supreme rite that marks the birth of the twice-born classes, O mighty-armed one.

BP 1.4.5

कर्मयोगमिदानीं ते कथयामि महाबल ।
उपनीय गुरुः शिष्यं प्रथमं शौचमादिशेत् ॥ ५

I shall now tell you the discipline of action, O mighty one. Having initiated a pupil, the teacher should first instruct him in purity.

BP 1.4.6

आचारमग्निकार्यं च सन्ध्योपासनमेव च ।
अध्यापयेत्तु सच्छिष्यान्सदाचान्त उदङ्मुखः ॥ ६

He should teach good pupils proper conduct, tending the sacred fire, and worship at the two twilights, after sipping water for purification and facing north.

BP 1.4.7

ब्रह्माञ्जलिकरो नित्यमध्याप्यो विजितेन्द्रियः ।
लघुवासास्तथैकाग्रः सुमना सुप्रतिष्ठितः ॥ ७

While being taught, the pupil should always sit with palms joined, control his senses, wear light clothing, be one-pointed and serene, and sit firmly.

BP 1.4.8

ब्रह्मारम्भेऽवसाने च पादौ पूज्यौ गुरोः सदा ।
संहत्य हस्तावध्येयं स हि ब्रह्माञ्जलिः स्मृतः ॥ ८

At both the beginning and the end of Vedic study the teacher's feet should always be honored. Study is undertaken with joined hands; this is the hand-folding of reverence.

BP 1.4.9

व्यत्यस्तपाणिना कार्यमुपसङ्ग्रहणं गुरोः ।
सव्येन सव्यः स्प्रष्टव्यो दक्षिणेन तु दक्षिणः ॥ ९

He should grasp the teacher's feet with crossed hands: the left foot is touched with the left hand, the right with the right.

BP 1.4.10

अध्येष्यमाणं तु गुरुर्नित्यकालमतन्द्रितः ।
अधीष्व भो इति ब्रूयाद्विरामोऽस्त्विति वारयेत् ॥.4.१०

When the pupil is about to study, the ever-alert teacher should say, "Recite, sir." To stop the recitation, he should say, "Let there be a pause."

BP 1.4.11

ब्रह्मणः प्रणवं कुर्यादादावन्ते च सर्वदा ।
स्रवत्यनोङ्कृतं पूर्वं परस्ताच्च विशीर्यते ॥१

One should always utter Oṃ, the sound of Brahman, at the beginning and end. What is begun without Oṃ flows away; what ends without it falls apart.

BP 1.4.12

श्रूयतां चापि राजेन्द्र यथोङ्कारं द्विजोऽर्हति ।
प्राक्कूलान्पर्युपासीनः पवित्रैश्चैव पावितः ॥२

Hear how a twice-born person is qualified to utter Oṃ, O king of kings: he should sit on sacred grass pointing east and be purified by purifying rites.

BP 1.4.13

प्राणायामैस्त्रिभिः पूतस्ततस्त्वोङ्कारमर्हति ।
ॐकारलक्षणं चापि शृणुष्व कुरुनन्दन ॥३

Purified by three breath-controls, he then becomes qualified to utter Oṃ. Hear also the nature of Oṃ, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.4.14

अकारं चाप्युकारं च मकारं च प्रजापतिः ।
वेदत्रयात्तु निर्गृह्य भूर्भुवः स्वरितीति च ॥४

Prajāpati drew the sounds A, U, and M from the three Vedas, and also the utterances Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, and Svaḥ.

BP 1.4.15

त्रिभ्य एव तु वेदेभ्यः पादंपादमदूदुहत् ।
तदित्यृचोऽस्याः सावित्र्याः परमेष्ठी प्रजापतिः ॥५

From the three Vedas Prajāpati drew the three feet of the Sāvitrī, one foot at a time, beginning with the word tat.

BP 1.4.16

एतदक्षरमेतां च जपन्व्याहृतिपूर्विकाम् ।
सन्ध्ययोरुभयोर्विप्रो वेद पुण्येन युज्यते ॥६

A Brahmin who recites this syllable and the Sāvitrī preceded by the great utterances at both twilights is joined to the merit of Vedic knowledge.

BP 1.4.17

सहस्रकृत्वस्त्वभ्यस्य बहिरेतत्त्रिकं द्विजः ।
महतोऽप्येनसो मासात्त्वचेवाहिर्विमुच्यते ॥७

A twice-born person repeating this triad a thousand times outside the settlement is released within a month even from great sin, as a snake from its skin.

BP 1.4.18

एतयर्चा विसंयुक्तः काले च क्रियया स्वया ।
विप्रक्षत्रियविड्योनिर्गर्हणां याति साधुषु ॥८

One born a Brahmin, Kshatriya, or Vaishya who neglects this verse and its proper act at the appointed time incurs censure among the good.

BP 1.4.19

शृणुष्वैकमना राजन्परमं ब्रह्मणो मुखम् ।
ॐकारपूर्विकास्तिस्रो महाव्याहृतयोऽव्ययाः ॥९

Listen with one-pointed attention, O king, to the supreme utterance of Brahman: Oṃ together with the three imperishable great utterances.

BP 1.4.20

त्रिपदा चैव सावित्री विज्ञेया ब्रह्मणो मुखम् ।
योऽधीतेऽहन्यहन्येतां त्रीणि वर्षाण्यतन्द्रितः ॥.4.२०

The three-footed Sāvitrī is also to be known as the mouth of Brahman. Whoever recites it every day without weariness for three years—

BP 1.4.21

स ब्रह्म परमभ्येति वायुभूतः खमूर्तिमान् ।
एकाक्षरं परं ब्रह्म प्राणायामः परन्तप ॥१

—attains supreme Brahman, becoming like the wind and taking the sky as his form. The single syllable Oṃ is supreme Brahman; breath-control is the highest austerity, O scorcher of foes.

BP 1.4.22

सावित्र्यास्तु परं नास्ति मौनात्सत्यं विशिष्यते ।
तपः किया होमक्रिया तथा दानक्रिया नृप ॥२

Nothing is higher than the Sāvitrī, and truth is greater than silence. Austerity, fire-offerings, and giving, O king—

BP 1.4.23

अक्षयान्ताः सदा राजन्यथाह भगवान्मनुः ।
अवरं स्वक्षरं ज्ञेयं ब्रह्म चैव प्रजापतिः ॥३

—all come to an end, as blessed Manu declared. The imperishable syllable is to be known as Brahman itself and as Prajāpati.

BP 1.4.24

विधियज्ञात्सदा राजञ्जपयज्ञो विशिष्यते ।
नानाविधैर्गुणोद्देशैः सूक्ष्माख्यातैर्नृपोत्तम ॥४

The sacrifice of recitation is always superior to sacrifice by prescribed rites, O king, through its many distinct and subtly described qualities, O best of kings.

BP 1.4.25

पांशुः स्याल्लक्षगुणः सहस्रो मानसः स्मृतः ।
ये पाकयज्ञाश्चत्वारो विधियज्ञेन चान्विताः ॥५

Whispered recitation is a hundred thousand times greater; mental recitation is remembered as a thousandfold. The four domestic sacrifices, even when performed by rule—

BP 1.4.26

सर्वे ते जपयज्ञस्य कलां नार्हन्ति षोडशीम् ।
जपादेव तु संसिध्येद्द्ब्राह्मणो नात्र संशयः ॥६

—all together do not equal a sixteenth part of recitation-sacrifice. A Brahmin may attain perfection through recitation alone; of this there is no doubt.

BP 1.4.27

कुर्यादन्यन्न वा कुर्यान्मैत्रो ब्राह्मण उच्यते ।
पूर्वां सन्ध्यां जपंस्तिष्ठेत्सावित्रीमार्कदर्शनात् ॥७

Whether he performs other rites or not, a Brahmin devoted to goodwill is so called. At the morning twilight he should stand reciting the Sāvitrī until the sun appears.

BP 1.4.28

पश्चिमां तु समासीनः सम्यगृक्षविभावनात् ।
दिनस्यादौ भवेत्पूर्वा शर्वर्यादौ तथा परा ॥८

At the evening twilight he should sit until the stars are clearly seen. The first twilight comes at the beginning of day, and the later one at the beginning of night.

BP 1.4.29

सनक्षत्रा परा ज्ञेया अपरा सदिवाकरा ।
जपंस्तिष्ठन्परां सन्ध्यां नैशमेनो व्यपोहति ॥९

The later twilight is marked by stars, the earlier by the sun. Standing and reciting at the evening twilight, one removes the sin of night.

BP 1.4.30

अपरां तु समासीनो मलं हन्ति दिवाकृतम् ।
नोपतिष्ठति यः पूर्वां नोपास्ते पश्चिमां नृप ॥.4.३०

Sitting at the morning twilight, one destroys the stain made by day. One who does not attend the morning twilight or observe the evening one, O king—

BP 1.4.31

स शूद्रवद्बहिष्कार्यः सर्वस्माद्द्विजकर्मणः ।
अपां समीपे नियतो नैत्यकं विधिमास्थितः ॥१

—should be excluded from all twice-born rites like a Śūdra. A disciplined person should stay near water and follow the prescribed daily rule.

BP 1.4.32

सावित्रीमप्यधीयीत गत्याऽरण्यं समाहितः ।
वेदोपकरणे राजन्स्वाध्याये चैव नैत्यके ॥२

With collected mind he should go to the forest and recite the Sāvitrī. At the beginning of Vedic study, O king, and during daily recitation—

BP 1.4.33

नात्र दोषोस्त्यनध्याये होममन्त्रेषु वा विभो ।
नैत्यके नास्त्वनध्यायो ब्रह्मसत्रं हि तत्कृतम् ॥३

—there is no fault on a day when study is suspended, nor in fire-offering mantras, O lord. Daily recitation has no suspension, for it is a continual session of Brahman.

BP 1.4.34

ब्रह्माहुतिहुतं पुष्यमनध्यायवषट्कृतम् ।
ऋगेकां यस्त्वधीयीत विधिना नियतो द्विजः ॥४

The offering into Brahman remains nourishing even on a day when study is suspended and is consecrated by the ritual call. If a disciplined twice-born person recites even one verse of the Ṛgveda according to rule—

BP 1.4.35

तस्य नित्यं क्षरत्येषा पयो मेध्यं घृतं मधु ।
अग्निशुश्रूषणं भैक्षमधः शय्यां गुरोर्हितम् ॥५

—that verse continually pours forth pure milk, clarified butter, and honey for him. Tending the sacred fire, begging for food, sleeping on the ground, and acting for the teacher's welfare—

BP 1.4.36

आसमावर्तनात्कुर्यात्कृतोपनयनो द्विजः ।
आचार्यपुत्रशुश्रूषां ज्ञानदो धार्मिकः शुचिः ॥६

—an initiated twice-born should practise until the completion ceremony. The teacher's son, one eager to serve, one who teaches in return, the righteous and the pure—

BP 1.4.37

आप्तः शक्तोन्नदः साधुः स्वाध्याप्या दश धर्मतः ।
नापृष्टः कस्यचिद्ब्रूयान्न चान्यायेन पृच्छतः ॥७

—one trustworthy, capable, a giver of food, and virtuous: these ten kinds of people may be taught according to duty. One should not teach someone who has not asked, nor one who asks improperly.

BP 1.4.38

जानन्नपि हि मेधावी जडवल्लोक आचरेत् ।
अधर्मेण च यः प्राह यश्चाधर्मेण पृच्छति ॥८

Even when he knows, a wise man should act among people as though ignorant. One who teaches contrary to duty and one who asks contrary to duty—

BP 1.4.39

तयोरन्यतरः प्रैति विद्वेषं वा निगच्छति ।
धर्मार्थौ यत्र न स्यातां शुश्रूषा चापि तद्विधा ॥
न तत्र विद्या वप्तव्या शुभं बीजमिवोषरे ॥९

—one or the other perishes or falls into hatred. Where duty, a proper purpose, and a fitting willingness to serve are absent, knowledge should not be sown, like good seed in barren soil.

BP 1.4.40

विद्ययैव समं कामं मर्तव्यं ब्रह्मवादिना ।
आपद्यपि हि घोरायां न त्वेनामीरिणे वपेत् ॥४ ०

A speaker of Brahman should willingly die with his knowledge rather than sow it in barren ground, even in terrible distress.

BP 1.4.41

विद्या ब्राह्मणमित्याह शेवधिस्तेऽस्मि रक्ष माम् ।
असूयकाय मा प्रादास्तथा स्यां वीर्यवत्तमा ॥४ १

Knowledge said to the Brahmin: "I am your treasure; protect me. Do not give me to an envious person, so I may remain most powerful."

BP 1.4.42

शेवं सुखमुशन्तीह केचिज्ज्ञानं प्रचक्षते ।
तौ धारयति वै यस्माच्छेवधिस्तेन सोच्यते ॥४२

Some here understand treasure as happiness, others as knowledge; because it sustains both, it is called a treasure.

BP 1.4.43

यमेव तु शुचिं विद्यान्नियतं ब्रह्मचारिणम् ।
तस्मै मां ब्रूहि विप्राय निधिपायाप्रमादिने ॥४ ३

Knowledge continued: "Teach me only to a Brahmin known to you as pure, disciplined, and celibate, an attentive guardian of the treasure."

BP 1.4.44

ब्रह्म यस्त्वननुज्ञातमधीयानादवाप्नुयात् ॥४

One who studies sacred knowledge without authorization and thereby seeks to obtain Brahman—

BP 1.4.45

लौकिकं वैदिकं वापि तथाध्यात्मिकमेव च ।
स याति नरकं घोरं रौरवं भीमदर्शनम् ॥५

—whether worldly, Vedic, or spiritual, goes to the terrible hell called Raurava, dreadful to behold.

BP 1.4.46

अणुमात्रात्मकं देहं षोडशार्धमिति स्मृतम् ।
आददीत यतो ज्ञानं तं पूर्वमभिवादयेत् ॥६

Though the knowledge received be only an atom's measure or half a part, one should first salute the person from whom it was obtained.

BP 1.4.47

सावित्रीसारमात्रोऽपि वरो विप्रः सुयन्त्रितः ।
नायन्त्रितस्त्रिवेदोऽपि सर्वाशी सर्वविक्रयी ॥७

A disciplined Brahmin who knows only the essence of the Sāvitrī is superior to an undisciplined knower of the three Vedas who eats everything and sells everything.

BP 1.4.48

शय्यासनेध्याचरिते श्रेयसा न समाविशेत् ।
शय्यासनस्थश्चैवेनं प्रत्युत्थायाभिवादयेत् ॥८

One should not occupy a bed or seat used by a superior. If already seated or lying down when he approaches, one should rise and salute him.

BP 1.4.49

ऊर्ध्वं प्राणा ह्युत्क्रामन्ति यूनः स्थविर आगते ।
प्रत्युत्थानाभिवादाभ्यां पुनस्तान्प्रतिपद्यते ॥९

When an elder approaches a young person, the young person's vital breaths rise upward; by rising and saluting, one regains them.

BP 1.4.50

अभिवादनशीलस्य नित्यं वृद्धोपसेविनः ।
चत्वारि सम्यग्वर्धन्ते आयुः प्रज्ञा यशो बलम् ॥.4.५०

For one devoted to saluting and constantly serving elders, four things properly increase: life, wisdom, fame, and strength.

BP 1.4.51

अभिवादपरो विप्रो ज्यायांसमभिवादयेत् ।
असौ नामाहमस्मीति स्वनाम परिकीर्तयेत् ॥१

A Brahmin devoted to saluting should salute one senior and announce his own name, saying, "I am so-and-so."

BP 1.4.52

नामधेयस्य ये केचिदभिवादं न जानते ।
तान्प्राज्ञोऽहमिति ब्रूयात्स्त्रियः सर्वास्तथैव च ॥२

To people who do not understand a salutation with one's name, a wise person should say, "It is I"; the same applies to all women.

BP 1.4.53

भोः शब्दं कीर्तयेदन्ते स्वस्य नाम्नोभिवादने ।
नाम्नः स्वरूपभावो हि भो भाव ऋषिभिः स्मृतः ॥३

At the end of a salutation one should say bhoḥ after one's name; the sages remember bhoḥ as having the form of a name.

BP 1.4.54

आयुष्मान्भव सौम्येति वाच्यो विप्रोऽभिवादने ।
अकारश्चास्य नाम्नोऽन्ते वाच्यः पूर्वाक्षरः प्लुतः ॥४

In answering a Brahmin's greeting, one should say, "May you live long, gentle sir." The first vowel at the end of his name should be prolonged.

BP 1.4.55

यो न वेत्त्यभिवादस्य विप्रः प्रत्यभिवादनम् ।
नाभिवाद्यः स विदुषा यथा शूद्रस्तथैव सः ॥५

A Brahmin who does not know how to return a greeting should not be greeted by the learned; in this respect he is like a Śūdra.

BP 1.4.56

अभिवादे कृते यस्तु न करोत्यभिवादनम् ।
आशीर्वा कुरुशार्दूल स याति नरकं ध्रुवम् ॥६

One who does not return a greeting with a blessing after being greeted surely goes to hell, O tiger among the Kurus.

BP 1.4.57

अभीति भगवान्विष्णुार्वादयामीति शङ्करः ।
द्वावेव पूजितौ तेन यः करोत्यभिवादनम् ॥७

By saying “I approach,” one honors blessed Viṣṇu; by saying “I speak,” one honors Śaṅkara. One who offers a salutation thus honors both.

BP 1.4.58

ब्राह्मणं कुशलं पृच्छेत्क्षत्रबन्धुमनामयम् ।
वैश्यं क्षेमं समागम्य शूद्रमारोग्यमेव तु ॥८

On meeting them, one should ask a Brahmin about his well-being, a Kshatriya about freedom from illness, a Vaishya about security, and a Śūdra about health.

BP 1.4.59

न वाच्यो दीक्षितो नाम्ना यवीयानपि यो भवेत् ।
भो भवत्पूर्वकत्वेन इति स्वायम्भुवोऽब्रवीत् ।९

One who has been consecrated should not be addressed by name, even if younger; Manu said to address him with "sir" or a form beginning with "your honor."

BP 1.4.60

परपत्नी तु या राजन्नसम्बद्धा तु योनितः ।
वक्तव्या भवतीत्येवं सुभगे भगनीति च ॥.4.६०

Another man's wife who is unrelated by birth should be addressed as "lady," "fortunate one," or "sister," O king.

BP 1.4.61

पितृव्यान्मातुलान्राजञ्छ्वशुरानृत्विजो गुरून् ।
असावहमिति ब्रूयात्प्रत्युत्थाय जघन्यजः ॥१

A younger person should rise before his paternal uncles, maternal uncles, fathers-in-law, officiating priests, and teachers, O king, and identify himself by saying, "I am so-and-so."

BP 1.4.62

मातृष्वसा मातुलानी श्वश्रूरथ पितृष्वसा ।
सम्पूज्या गुरुपत्नी च समास्ता गुरुभार्यया ॥२

A mother's sister, maternal uncle's wife, mother-in-law, father's sister, and the teacher's wife should all be honored, each equal to the teacher's wife.

BP 1.4.63

ज्येष्ठस्य भ्रातुर्या भार्या सवर्णाहन्यहन्यपि ।
पूजयन्प्रयतो विप्रो याति विष्णुसदो नृप ॥३

A Brahmin who honors each day the wife of his elder brother, if she is of the same varṇa, reaches Viṣṇu's abode, O king.

BP 1.4.64

प्रवासादेत्य सम्पूज्या ज्ञातिसम्बन्धियोषितः ।
पितुर्या भगिनी राजन्मातुश्चापि विशाम्पते ॥४

On returning from a journey, one should honor women related through kinship and marriage, particularly one's father's sister and mother's sister, O lord of the people.

BP 1.4.65

आत्मनो भगिनी या च ज्येष्ठा कुरुकुलोद्वह ।
सदा स्वमातृवद्धृत्तिमातिष्ठेद्भारतोत्तम ॥५

One's own elder sister, O bearer of the Kuru lineage, should always be treated like one's mother, O best of the Bharatas.

BP 1.4.66

गरीयसी ततस्ताभ्यो माता ज्ञेया नराधिप ।
पुत्रमित्रभागिनेया द्रष्टव्या ह्यात्मना समाः ॥६

Yet one's mother is more venerable than all of them, O ruler of men. Sons, friends, and nephews should be regarded as equal to oneself.

BP 1.4.67

दशाब्दाख्यं पौरसख्यं पञ्चाब्दाख्यं कलाभृताम् ।
अब्दपूर्वं श्रोत्रियाणां स्वल्पेनापि स्वयोनिषु ॥७

Among fellow townsmen, a difference of ten years establishes seniority; among artisans, five years; among Vedic scholars, one year; among blood relatives, even a slight difference.

BP 1.4.68

ब्राह्मणं दशवर्षं च शतवर्षं च भूमिपम् ।
पितापुत्रौ विजानीयाद्ब्राह्मणस्तु तयोः पिता ॥८

A ten-year-old Brahmin and a hundred-year-old ruler should be regarded as father and son; of the two, the Brahmin is the father.

BP 1.4.69

इत्येवं क्षत्रियपिता वैश्यस्यापि पितामहः ।
प्रपितामहश्च शूद्रस्य प्रोक्तो विप्रो मनीषिभिः ॥९

Thus, the wise declare a Brahmin to be the father of a Kshatriya, grandfather of a Vaishya, and great-grandfather of a Śūdra.

BP 1.4.70

वित्तं बन्धुर्वयः कर्म विद्या भवति पञ्चमी ।
एतानि मान्यस्थानानि गरीयो यद्यदुत्तरम् ॥.4.७०

Wealth, kinship, age, ritual action, and knowledge as the fifth are grounds for honor; each succeeding ground is greater.

BP 1.4.71

पञ्चानां त्रिषु वर्गेषु भूयांसि गुणवन्ति च ।
यस्य स्युः सोऽत्र मानार्हः शूद्रोऽपि दशमीं गतः ॥ ७१

Of these five grounds, one who possesses abundant qualities in three is worthy of honor; so too is a Śūdra who has reached the tenth decade.

BP 1.4.72

चक्रिणो दशमीस्थस्य रोगिणो भारिणः स्त्रियाः ।
स्नातकस्य तु राज्ञश्च पन्था देयो वरस्य च ॥ ७२

The path should be yielded to one in a carriage, one a hundred years old, a sick person, a bearer of a load, a woman, a graduate of study, a king, and a bridegroom.

BP 1.4.73

एषां समागमे तात पूज्यौ स्नातकपार्थिवौ ।
आभ्यां समागमे राजन्स्नातको नृपमानभाक् ॥ ७३

When they meet, O child, the graduate and ruler are worthy of honor. When those two meet, O king, the graduate receives honor even from the ruler.

BP 1.4.74

अध्यापयेद्यस्तु शिष्यं कृत्वोपनयनं द्विजः ।
सरहस्यं सकल्पं च वेदं भरतसत्तम ॥
तमाचार्यं महाबाहो प्रवदन्ति मनीषिणः ॥ ७४

A twice-born who initiates a pupil and teaches him the Veda with its hidden teachings and ritual rules is called a teacher by the wise, O mighty-armed best of the Bharatas.

BP 1.4.75

एकदेशं तु वेदस्य वेदाङ्गान्यपि वा पुनः ।
योऽध्यापयति वृत्त्यर्थमुपाध्यायः स उच्यते ॥ ७५

One who teaches for a livelihood a portion of the Veda, or one or more auxiliary sciences, is called an instructor.

BP 1.4.76

निषेकादीनि कार्याणि यः करोति नृपोत्तम ।
अध्यापयति चान्येन स विप्रो गुरुरुच्यते ॥ ७६

A Brahmin who performs rites beginning with conception, O best of kings, and has another teach the Veda is called a spiritual teacher.

BP 1.4.77

अग्न्याधेयं पाकयज्ञानग्निष्टोमादिकान्मखान् ।
यः करोति वृतो यस्य स तस्यर्त्विगिहोच्यते ॥ ७७

One who is chosen and performs for another the establishment of the sacred fires, household sacrifices, and large rites beginning with the fire-and-Soma sacrifice is called that person's priest.

BP 1.4.78

य आवृणोत्यवितथं ब्रह्मणा श्रवणावुभौ ।
स माता स पिता ज्ञेयस्तं न द्रुह्येत्कथञ्चन ॥ ७८

One who fills both ears unfailingly with sacred knowledge should be known as both mother and father; one must never harm him in any way.

BP 1.4.79

उपाध्यायान्दशाचार्यं आचार्याणां शतं पिता ।
सहस्रेण पितुर्माता गौरवेणातिरिच्यते ॥ ७९

Ten instructors equal one teacher, a father equals one hundred teachers, and a mother exceeds a father a thousandfold in venerability.

BP 1.4.80

उत्पादकब्रह्मदात्रोर्गरीयान्ब्रह्मदः पिता ।
ब्रह्मजन्म हि विप्रस्य प्रेत्य चेह च शाश्वतम् ॥.4.८०

Of the physical father and the giver of sacred knowledge, the giver of sacred knowledge is the more venerable father; for a Brahmin, birth through Brahman is eternal here and after death.

BP 1.4.81

कामान्माता पिता चैनं यदुत्पादयतो मिथः ।
सम्भूतिं तस्य तां विद्याद्यद्योनावभिजायते ॥ ८१

When a mother and father unite from desire and generate a child, the birth that takes place in the womb is understood as the child's physical origin.

BP 1.4.82

आचार्यस्तस्य तां जातिं विधिद्वेदपारगः ।
उत्पादयति सावित्र्या सा सत्या साऽजरामरा ॥ ८२

The teacher who has mastered the Veda duly produces another birth for him through the Sāvitrī; that birth is true, undecaying, and immortal.

BP 1.4.83

उपाध्यायमादितः कृत्वा ये पूज्याः कथितास्तव ।
महागुरुर्महाबाहो सर्वेषामधिकः स्मृतः ॥ ८३

Beginning with the instructor, those worthy of honor have been described to you. The great teacher, O mighty-armed one, is considered superior to all.

BP 1.4.84

सहस्रशतसंख्योऽसावाचार्याणामिदं मतम् ।
चतुर्णामपि वर्णानां स महागुरुरुच्यते ॥ ८४

He is considered equal to a hundred thousand teachers; he is called the great teacher of all four varṇas.

BP 1.4.85

शतानीक उवाच
य एते भवता प्रोक्ता उपाध्यायमुखा द्विजाः ।
विदिता एव मे सर्वे न महागुरुरेव हि ॥ ८५

Śatānīka said: All the twice-born teachers you have described, beginning with the instructor, are known to me; only the great teacher is not.

BP 1.4.86

सुमन्तुरुवाच
जयोपजीवी यो विप्रः स महागुरुरुच्यते ।
अष्टादशपुराणानि रामस्य चरितं तथा ॥ ८६

Sumantu said: A Brahmin who lives by expounding the Jaya is called a great teacher. The eighteen Purāṇas and the story of Rāma—

BP 1.4.87

विष्णुधर्मादयो धर्माः शिवधर्माश्च भारत ।
कार्ष्णं वेदं पञ्चमं तु यन्महाभारतं स्मृतम् ॥ ८७

—the teachings concerning Viṣṇu, the teachings concerning Śiva, O descendant of Bharata, and the fifth Veda, remembered as the Mahābhārata—

BP 1.4.88

श्रौता धर्माश्च राजेन्द्र नारदोक्ता महीपते ।
जयेति नाम एतेषां प्रवदन्ति मनीषिणः ॥ ८८

—the Vedic rites, O king of kings, and those declared by Nārada: the wise call all of these Jaya.

BP 1.4.89

एवं विप्रकदम्बस्य धारकः२ प्रवरः स्मृतः ।
यस्त्वेतानि समस्तानि पुराणानीह विन्दति ॥ ८९

The foremost sustainer of this great body of Brahmin learning is the person who knows all these Purāṇas in their entirety—

BP 1.4.90

भारतं च महाबाहो स सर्वज्ञो मतो नृणाम् ।
तस्मात्स पूज्यो राजेन्द्र वर्णैर्विप्रादिभिः सदा ॥.4.९०

—and the Mahābhārata, O mighty-armed king. Such a person is considered all-knowing by people and should always be honored by all varṇas beginning with Brahmins, O king of kings.

BP 1.4.91

किं त्वया न श्रुतं वाक्यं यदाह भगवान्विभुः ।
अल्पं वा बहु वा यस्य श्रुतस्योपकरोति यः ॥
तमपीह गुरुं विद्याच्छ्रुतोपक्रियया तया३ ॥ ९१

Have you not heard the teaching spoken by the blessed, all-pervading Lord? Whoever helps in the transmission of sacred learning, little or much, should be recognized as a teacher for that service to revelation.

BP 1.4.92

ब्राह्मस्य जन्मनः कर्ता स्वधर्मस्य च शासिता ।
बालोऽपि विप्रो वृद्धस्य पिता भवति धर्मतः ॥ ९२

The teacher is an embodiment of Brahman, the father an embodiment of Prajāpati, the mother an embodiment of Aditi, and the brother an embodiment of one's own self.

BP 1.4.93

अध्यापयामास पितॄञ्छिशुराङ्गिरसः कविः ।
पुत्रका इति होवाच ज्ञानेन परिगृह्य ताम् ॥ ९३

Kavi, the youthful son of Aṅgiras, taught his forefathers. Having surpassed them in knowledge, he addressed them as "little sons."

BP 1.4.94

ते तमर्थमपृच्छन्त देवानागतमन्यवः ।
देवाश्चैतान्समेत्योचुर्न्याय्यं वै शिशुरुक्तवान् ॥ ९४

Angered, they questioned him about the matter and went to the gods. The gods assembled and told them, "The child has spoken justly."

BP 1.4.95

अज्ञो भवति वै बालः पिता भवति मन्त्रदः ।
अज्ञं हि बालमित्याहुः पितेत्येव तु मन्त्रदम् ॥ ९५

An ignorant person is truly a child, while one who gives sacred mantras is a father. The ignorant are called children, and the giver of mantras is called father.

BP 1.4.96

पितामहेति जयदमित्यूचुस्ते दिवौकसः ।
जयो मन्त्रास्तथा वेदा देहमेकं त्रिधा कृतम् ॥ ९६

The heavenly beings further called the giver of Jaya a grandfather. Jaya, the mantras, and the Vedas are one body formed in three ways.

BP 1.4.97

नहायनैर्न पलितैर्न मित्रेण न बन्धुभिः ।
ऋषयश्चक्रिरे धर्मं योऽनूचानः स नो महान् ॥ ९७

The seers did not establish greatness by years, grey hair, friendship, or kinship. They declared: "The one learned in recitation is great among us."

BP 1.4.98

ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियविशां शूद्राणां च विशांपते ।
ज्येष्ठं वन्दन्ति राजेन्द्र सन्देहं शृणु वै यथा ॥ ९८

Among Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Śūdras, O lord of the people, the senior is honored. Hear, O king, how seniority is determined.

BP 1.4.99

ज्ञानतो वीर्यतो राजन्धनतो जन्मतस्तथा ।
शीलतस्तु प्रधाना ये ते प्रधाना मता मम ॥ ९९

Precedence may arise from knowledge, strength, wealth, birth, or character, O king. In my view, those foremost in character are truly foremost.

BP 1.4.100

न तेन स्थविरो भवति येनास्य पलितं शिरः ।
यो वै युवाप्यधीयानस्तं देवाः स्थविरं विदुः ॥.4.१००

A person does not become an elder merely because his head is grey. The gods know even a young person learned in study to be an elder.

BP 1.4.101

यथा काष्ठमयो हस्ती यथा चर्ममयो मृगः ।
यश्च विप्रोऽनधीयानस्त्रयस्ते नाम बिभ्रति ॥०१

As an elephant made of wood and a deer made of hide bear only their names, so an unlearned Brahmin bears only the name.

BP 1.4.102

यथा योषाऽफला स्त्रीषु यथा गौर्गवि चाफला ।
यथा चाज्ञेऽफलं दानं यथा विप्रोऽनृचोऽफलः ॥०२

As a barren woman is fruitless among women, a barren cow among cattle, and a gift to an ignorant person is fruitless, so a Brahmin without Vedic verses is fruitless.

BP 1.4.103

वैश्वदेवेन ये हीना आतिथ्येन विवर्जिताः ।
सर्वे तु वृषला ज्ञेयाः प्राप्तवेदा अपि द्विजाः ॥०३

Twice-born people who omit the offering to all gods and are without hospitality should all be known as degraded, even if they have mastered the Vedas.

BP 1.4.104

नानृग्ब्राह्मणो भवति न वणिङ्न कुशीलवः ।
न शूद्रः प्रेषणं कुर्वन्नस्तेनो न चिकित्सकः ॥०४

One without Vedic verses is not a Brahmin, nor one who lives as a merchant or performer, acts as a servant, steals, or practises medicine.

BP 1.4.105

अव्रता ह्यनधीयाना यत्र भैक्षचरा द्विजाः ।
तं ग्रामं दण्डयेद्राजा चोरभक्तप्रदो हि सः ॥०५

Where twice-born people without vows and study wander for alms, the king should punish that village, for it gives sustenance to people likened to thieves.

BP 1.4.106

सन्तुष्टो यत्र वै विप्रः साग्निकः कुरुनन्दन ।
याति साफल्यतां वेदैर्देवैरेवं हि भाषितम् ॥०६

Where a contented Brahmin maintains the sacred fire, O delight of the Kurus, Vedic study reaches fulfilment; thus the gods have declared.

BP 1.4.107

वेदैरुक्तं यथा वीर सुरज्येष्ठमुपेत्य वै ।
वेपन्ते ब्राह्मणा भूमावभ्यस्यन्ति ह्यनग्निकाः ॥
क्लिश्यन्ते ते किमर्थं हि मूढा वै फलकाम्यया ॥०७

The Vedas themselves, approaching the eldest of the gods, spoke thus, O hero: "Brahmins on earth tremble and recite us while neglecting the sacred fire. Why do those deluded seekers of reward suffer?"

BP 1.4.108

अनुष्ठानविहीनानामस्मानभ्यसतां भुवि ।
क्लेशो हि केवलं देव नास्मदभ्यसने फलम् ॥०८

"For those who recite us on earth without ritual observance, O god, there is only hardship; no fruit comes from reciting us."

BP 1.4.109

अनुष्ठानं परं देवमस्मत्स्वभ्यसनात्सदा ।
इत्येवं राजशार्दूल वेदा ऊचुर्हि वेधसम् ॥
तस्माच्च वेदाभ्यसनादनुष्ठानं परं मतम् ॥०९

"Ritual observance is always higher than reciting us." Thus, O tiger among kings, the Vedas spoke to the Creator. Therefore ritual observance is considered higher than recitation alone.

BP 1.4.110

चत्वारो वा त्रयो वापि यद्ब्रूयुर्वेदपारगाः ।
स धर्म इति विज्ञेयो नेतरेषां सहस्रशः ॥.4.११०

Whatever four or even three masters of the Veda declare is to be recognized as duty, not what thousands of others say.

BP 1.4.111

यद्वदन्ति तमोमूढा मूर्खा धर्ममजानतः ।
तत्पापं शतधा भूत्वा वक्तॄनेवानुगच्छति ॥११

Whatever people deluded by darkness and ignorant of duty proclaim as duty, that sin becomes a hundredfold and follows the speakers themselves.

BP 1.4.112

शौचहीने व्रतभ्रष्टे विप्रे वेदविवर्जिते ।
दीयमानं रुदत्यन्नं कि मया दुष्कृतं कृतम् ॥१२

The food being given to a Brahmin lacking purity, who has broken his vows and is without Vedic learning, cries out, "What wrong have I done?"

BP 1.4.113

जपोपजीविने दत्तं यदात्मानं प्रपश्यति ।
नृत्यति स्म तदा राजन्करावुद्धृत्य भारत ॥१३

But when food sees itself given to one who lives by sacred recitation, it dances with raised hands, O king, O descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.4.114

विद्यातपोभ्यां सम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गृहमागते ।
क्रीडन्त्यौषधयः सर्वा यास्यामः परमां गतिम् ॥१४

When a Brahmin endowed with learning and austerity enters a house, all the food-plants rejoice, saying, "We shall attain the highest state."

BP 1.4.115

अव्रतानाममन्त्राणामजपानां च भारत ।
प्रतिग्रहो न दातव्यो न शिला तारयेच्छिलाम् ॥१५

A gift should not be given to those without vows, mantras, or recitation, O descendant of Bharata; one stone cannot carry another stone across.

BP 1.4.116

श्रोत्रियायैव देयानि हव्यकव्यानि नित्यशः ।
अश्रोत्रियाय दत्तानि न पितॄन्नापि देवताः ॥१६

Offerings to the gods and ancestors should always be given only to a Brahmin learned in the Veda. What is given to one who is not so learned reaches neither ancestors nor gods.

BP 1.4.117

यस्य चैव गृहे मूर्खो दूरे चापि बहुश्रुतः ।
बहुश्रुताय दातव्यं नास्ति मूर्खव्यतिक्रमः ॥१७

One should always keep separate a Brahmin seated in the house during a rite involving Brahmins. One who passes over him goes to the Raurava hell, O mighty-armed descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.4.118

ब्राह्मणातिक्रमो नास्ति मूर्खे जपविवर्जिते ।
ज्वलन्तमग्निमुत्सृज्य न हि भस्मनि हूयते ॥१८

There is no offence in passing over a Brahmin who is ignorant and without recitation; one does not leave a blazing fire to pour an offering into ashes.

BP 1.4.119

न चैतदेव मन्यन्ते .पितरो देवतास्तथा ।
सगुणं निर्गुणं वापि ब्राह्मणं दैवतं परम् ॥१९

The ancestors and gods consider a Brahmin, whether endowed with qualities or without them, a supreme divinity.

BP 1.4.120

नातिक्रमेद्गृहासीनं ब्राह्मणं विप्रकर्मणि ।
अतिक्रमन्महाबाहो रौरवं याति भारत ॥.4.१२०

During a rite involving Brahmins one should not pass over a Brahmin seated in the house. One who does so goes to the Raurava hell, O mighty-armed descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.4.121

गायत्रीमात्रसारोऽपि ब्राह्मणः पूज्यतां गतः ।
गृहासन्नो विशेषेण न भवेत्पतितस्तु सः ॥२१

Even a Brahmin whose learning consists only of the Gāyatrī is worthy of honor, especially when he lives nearby, provided he has not fallen from duty.

BP 1.4.122

धान्यशून्यो यथा ग्रामो यथा कूपश्च निर्जलः ।
ब्राह्मणश्चानधीयानस्त्रयस्ते नामधारकाः ॥२२

Instruction intended for the welfare of living beings should be given without violence. A person seeking duty should use speech that is sweet and gentle.

BP 1.4.123

यस्त्वेकपङ्क्त्यां विषमं ददाति स्नेहाद्भयाद्वा यदि वार्थहेतोः ।
वेदेषु दृष्टमृषिभिश्च गीतं तां ब्रह्महत्यां मुनयो वदन्ति ॥२३

One who distributes unequally among people seated in one row, whether from affection, fear, or hope of gain, commits what the Vedas and seers declare equal to killing a Brahmin.

BP 1.4.124

अहिंसयैव भूतानां कार्यं श्रेयोऽनुशासनम् ।
वाक्चैव मधुरा श्लक्ष्णा प्रयोज्या धर्ममीप्सता ॥२४

One whose speech and mind are pure and guarded by truth obtains the entire fruit taught in the concluding Vedic teaching, O descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.4.125

यस्य वाङ्मनसी शुद्धे सत्यगुप्ते च भारत ।
स वै सर्वमवाप्नोति वेदान्तोपगतं फलम् ॥२५

One whose speech and mind are pure and guarded by truth obtains the entire fruit taught in the Vedānta, O descendant of Bharata.

BP 1.4.126

नारुन्तुदः स्यादार्तोऽपि न परद्रोहकर्मधीः ।
ययास्यो द्विजते लोको न तां वाचमुदीरयेत् ॥२६

Even when afflicted, one should not speak cuttingly or intend harm to another. One should not utter speech that agitates the world.

BP 1.4.127

यत्करोति शुभं वाचा प्रोच्यमाना मनीषिभिः ।
श्रूयतां कुरुशार्दूल सदा चापि तथोच्यताम् ॥२७

Hear, O tiger among the Kurus, the good produced by speech as described by the wise, and let such beneficial speech always be spoken.

BP 1.4.128

न तथा शशी न सलिलं न चन्दनरसो न शीतलच्छाया ।
प्रह्लादयति च पुरुषं यथा मधुरभाषिणी वाणी ॥२८

Neither the moon, water, sandalwood, nor cool shade delights a person as much as sweet-speaking words.

BP 1.4.129

अर्हणाद्ब्राह्मणो नित्यमुद्विजेत विषादिव ।
अमृतस्येव चाकांक्षेदपमानस्य सर्वदा ॥२९

A Brahmin should recoil from honor as from poison and continually desire dishonor as though it were nectar.

BP 1.4.130

सुखं ह्यवमतः शेते सुखं च प्रतिबुध्यते ।
सुखं चरति लोकेस्मिन्नवमन्ता विनश्यति ॥.4.१३०

The person dishonored sleeps happily, wakes happily, and moves happily in this world; the one who dishonors is destroyed.

BP 1.4.131

अनेन विधिना राजन्संस्कृतात्मा द्विजः शनैः ।
गुरौ वसन्सेचिनुयाद्ब्रह्माधिगमिदं तपः ॥३१

A twice-born person whose inner self is gradually refined by this discipline should dwell with the teacher and accumulate the austerity that leads to Brahman.

BP 1.4.132

तपोविशेषैर्विविधैर्व्रतैश्च विविधोदितैः ।
वेदः कृत्स्नोधिगन्तव्यः सरहस्यो द्विजन्मना ॥३२

Through many kinds of austerity and the various prescribed vows, a twice-born person should master the entire Veda together with its mysteries.

BP 1.4.133

वेदमेवाभ्यसेन्नित्यं तपस्तप्यं द्विजोत्तमः ।
वेदाभ्यासो हि विप्रस्य तपः परमिहोच्यते ॥३३

The foremost twice-born should continually study the Veda and practise austerity, for Vedic study is declared the highest austerity of a Brahmin.

BP 1.4.134

आहैव स नखाग्रेभ्यः परमं तप्यते तपः ।
यः सुप्तोऽपि द्विजोऽधीते स्वाध्यायं शक्तितोऽन्वहम् ॥३४

From the tips of his nails throughout his body he performs the highest austerity: the twice-born person who studies each day to the extent of his ability, even while resting.

BP 1.4.135

योऽनधीत्य द्विजो वेदमन्यत्र कुरुते श्रमम् ।
स जीवन्नेव शूद्रत्वमाशु गच्छति सान्वयः ॥३५

A twice-born person who neglects study of the Veda and labors elsewhere quickly falls, while still alive, to the state of a Śūdra together with his descendants.

BP 1.4.136

न यस्य वेदो न जपो न विद्याश्च विशाम्पते ।
स शूद्र एव मन्तव्य इत्याह भगवान्विभुः ॥३६

One who has neither Vedic learning, recitation, nor knowledge should be considered a Śūdra, said the blessed Lord.

BP 1.4.137

मातुरग्रे च जननं द्वितीयो मौञ्जिबन्धनम् ।
तृतीयो यज्ञदीक्षायां द्विजस्य विधिरीरितः ॥३७

The first birth is before the mother, the second at the binding of the muñja girdle, the third at initiation into sacrifice; this threefold rule is declared for the twice-born.

BP 1.4.138

तत्र यद्ब्रह्म जन्मास्य मौञ्जीबन्धनचिह्नितम् ॥३८

The first birth is from the mother, the second at the binding of the sacred-grass girdle, the third at initiation into sacrifice; this threefold rule is declared for the twice-born.

BP 1.4.139

तत्रास्य माता सावित्री पिता त्वाचार्य उच्यते ।
वेदप्रदानात्त्वाचार्यं पितरं मनुरब्रवीत् ॥३९

Of these, his birth through Brahman is the one marked by the binding of the sacred-grass girdle.

BP 1.4.140

न ह्यस्य विद्यते कर्म किञ्चिदामौञ्जिबन्धनात् ।
नाभिव्याहारयेद्ब्रहम स्वधानिनयनादृते ॥.4.१४०

In that birth the Sāvitrī is his mother and the teacher is called his father. Manu said the teacher is the father because he gives the Veda.

BP 1.4.141

शूद्रेण तु समं तावद्यावद्वेदे न जायते ।
कृतोपनयनस्यास्य व्रतादेशनमिष्यते ॥
ब्रह्मणो ग्रहणं चैव क्रमेण विधिपूर्वकम् ॥४१

Before the binding of the sacred-grass girdle no rite is prescribed for him; he should not utter sacred Vedic speech except when making an ancestral offering.

BP 1.4.142

यत्सूत्रं चापि यच्चर्म या या चास्य च मेखला ।
वसनं चापि यो दण्डस्तद्वै तस्य व्रतेष्वपि ॥४२

Until he is born into the Veda, he remains on the same footing as a Śūdra. After initiation he receives instruction in the vows and sacred knowledge successively, according to rule.

BP 1.4.143

सेवेतेमांस्तु नियमान्ब्रह्मचारी गुरौ वसन् ।
सन्नियम्येन्द्रियग्रामं तपोवृद्ध्यर्थमात्मनः ॥४३

A celibate student living with his teacher should observe these disciplines, restraining all his senses to increase his own austerity.

BP 1.4.144

वृन्दारकर्षिपितॄणां कुर्यात्तर्पणमेव हि ।
नराणां च महाबाहो नित्यं स्नात्वा प्रयत्नतः ॥४४

After bathing carefully each day, O mighty-armed king, he should offer water-libations to the gods, seers, ancestors, and human beings.

BP 1.4.145

पुष्पं तोयं फलं चापि समिदाधानमेव च ।
नानाविधानि काष्ठानि मृत्तिकां च तथा कुशान् ॥४५

He should gather flowers, water, and fruit, provide fuel for the sacred fire, and bring the various woods, clay, and sacred grass.

BP 1.4.146

वर्जयेन्मधु मांसं च गन्धमाल्यरथान्स्त्रियः ।
शुक्लानि चैव सर्वाणि प्राणिनां चैव हिंसनम् ॥४६

He should avoid honey, meat, perfume, garlands, vehicles, women, all white substances, and the harming of living beings.

BP 1.4.147

अभ्यङ्गमञ्जनं चाक्ष्णोरुपानच्छत्रधारणम् ।
संकल्पं कामजं क्रोधं लोभं गीतं च वादनम् ॥४७

He should avoid oil massage, collyrium for the eyes, wearing shoes or carrying an umbrella, desire, anger, greed, singing, and instrumental music.

BP 1.4.148

नर्तनं च तथा द्यूतं जनवादं तथानृतम् ।
परिवादं चापि विभो दूरतः परिवर्जयेत् ॥४८

He should likewise keep far away from dancing, gambling, public gossip, falsehood, and slander, O lord.

BP 1.4.149

स्त्रीणां च प्रेक्षणालम्बो रपवीतं२ परस्य च ।
पुंश्चलीभिस्तथा सङ्गं न कुर्यात्कुरुनन्दन ॥४९

He should not gaze at or touch women, injure another person, or associate with promiscuous women, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.4.150

एकः शयीत सर्वत्र न रेतः स्कन्दयेत्क्वचित् ।
कामाद्धि स्कन्दयन्रेतो हिनस्ति व्रतमेव तु ॥.4.१५०

He should sleep alone everywhere and never discharge semen. If he discharges semen through desire, he destroys his vow.

BP 1.4.151

सुप्तः क्षरन्ब्रह्मचारी द्विजः शुक्रमकामतः ।
स्नात्वार्कमर्चयित्वा तु पुनर्मामित्यृचं जपेत् ॥५१

If a twice-born celibate student has an involuntary emission while asleep, he should bathe, worship the sun, and recite the Vedic verse beginning "Again to me."

BP 1.4.152

मनोरपि तथा चात्र श्रूयते परमं वचः ।
उदकुम्भं सुमनसो गोशकृन्मृत्तिकां कुशान् ॥
आहरेद्यावदर्थान्हि भैक्षं चापि हि नित्यशः ॥५२

The supreme teaching of Manu is heard on this matter: he should fetch a water-pot, flowers, cow dung, clay, and sacred grass as needed, and collect alms each day.

BP 1.4.153

गृहेषु येषां कर्तव्यं ताञ्छृणुष्व नृपोत्तम ।
स्वकर्मसु रता ये वै तथा वेदेषु ये रताः ॥
यज्ञेषु चापि राजेन्द्र ये च श्रद्धासमाश्रिताः ॥५३

Hear, O best of kings, the households from which alms should be sought: those whose residents are devoted to their duties, to the Vedas, to sacrifice, and to faith, O king of kings.

BP 1.4.154

ब्रह्मचार्या हरेद्भैक्षं गृहेभ्यः प्रयतोऽन्वहम् ।
गुरोः कुले न भिक्षेत् स्वज्ञातिकुलबन्धुषु ॥५४

A disciplined celibate student should obtain alms daily from such houses. He should not beg within the teacher's family, his own lineage, or the households of close relatives.

BP 1.4.155

अलाभे त्वन्यगोत्राणां पूर्वं पूर्वं विवर्जयेत् ।
सर्वं चापि चरेद्ग्रामं पूर्वोक्तानामसम्भवे ॥
अन्त्यवर्जं महाबाहो इत्याह भगवान्विभुः ॥५५

If alms are unavailable there, he should turn successively to other lineages, leaving the previously mentioned ones aside in order. If none is available, he may go through the whole village except the homes of outcasts, O mighty-armed king.

BP 1.4.156

वाचं नियम्य प्रयतस्त्वग्निं शस्त्रं च वर्जयेत् ।
चातुर्वर्ण्यं चरेद्भैक्षमलाभे कुरुनन्दन ॥५६

Restraining speech and remaining disciplined, he should avoid fire and weapons. If alms are unavailable, he may beg among the four varṇas, O delight of the Kurus.

BP 1.4.157

आरादाहृत्य समिधः. सन्निदध्याद् गृहोपरि ।
सायंप्रातस्तु जुहुयात्ताभिरग्निमतन्द्रितः ॥५७

He should bring fuel-sticks from a distance and store them by the house. Without weariness, he should use them to offer into the fire morning and evening.

BP 1.4.158

भैक्षाचरणमकृत्वा न तमग्निं समिध्य वै ।
अनातुरः सप्तरात्रमवकीर्णिव्रतं चरेत् ॥५८

If, while not ill, he fails for seven nights to go for alms, gather fuel, and kindle the fire, he should perform the vow prescribed for one who has broken celibate discipline.

BP 1.4.159

वर्तनं चास्य भैक्षेण प्रवदन्ति मनीषिणः ।
तस्माद्भैक्षेण वै नित्यं नैकान्नादी भवेद्यती ॥५९

The wise declare that his livelihood should come from alms. Therefore the disciplined student should beg daily and not become one who eats from a single household.

BP 1.4.160

भैक्षेण व्रतिनो वृत्तिरुपवाससमा स्मृता ।
दैवत्ये व्रतवद्राजन्पित्र्ये कर्मण्यथर्षिवत् ॥
काममभ्यर्थितोऽश्नीयाद्व्रतमस्य न लुप्यते ॥.4.१६०

The livelihood of one under vows through alms is remembered as equal to fasting. When invited to a rite for the gods or ancestors, O king, he may eat; his vow is not thereby broken.

BP 1.4.161

ब्राह्मणस्य महाबाहो कर्म यत्समुदाहृतम् ।
राजन्यवैश्ययोर्नैतत्पण्डितैः कुरुनन्दन ॥६१

The discipline just described, O mighty-armed delight of the Kurus, is prescribed for a Brahmin student; the learned do not apply all of it in the same way to Kshatriya and Vaishya students.

BP 1.4.162

चोदितोऽचोदितो वापि गुरुणा नित्यमेव हि ।
कुर्यादध्ययने योगमाचार्यस्य हितेषु च ॥६२

Whether instructed by his teacher or not, he should continually apply himself to study and to whatever promotes the teacher's welfare.

BP 1.4.163

बुद्धीन्द्रियाणि मनसा शरीरं वाचमेव हि ।
नियम्य प्राञ्जलिस्तिष्ठेद्वीक्षमाणो गुरोर्मुखम् ॥६३

Restraining understanding, the senses, mind, body, and speech, he should stand with joined palms looking toward the teacher's face.

BP 1.4.164

नित्यमुद्धृतपाणिः स्यात्साध्वाचारस्तु संयतः ।
आस्यतामिति चोक्तः सन्नासीताभिमुखं गुरोः ॥६४

He should always keep his hands joined, practise good conduct, and remain self-controlled. Only when told, "Be seated," should he sit facing the teacher.

BP 1.4.165

वस्त्रवेषैस्तथान्नैस्तु हीनः स्याद्गुरुसन्निधौ ।
उत्तिष्ठेत्प्रथमं चास्य जघन्यं चापि संविशेत् ॥६५

In the teacher's presence his clothing, appearance, and food should be humbler than the teacher's. He should rise before the teacher and lie down after him.

BP 1.4.166

प्रतिश्रवणसम्भाषे तल्पस्थो न समाचरेत् ।
न चासीनो न भुञ्जानो न तिष्ठन्न पराङ्मुखः ॥६६

He should not answer or converse with the teacher while lying on a bed, sitting, eating, standing, or turned away.

BP 1.4.167

आसीनस्य स्थितः कुर्यादभिगच्छंश्च तिष्ठतः ।
प्रत्युद्गन्ता तु व्रजतः पश्चाद्धावंश्च धावतः ॥६७

When the teacher is seated, the pupil should address him while standing; when the teacher stands, he should approach. When the teacher walks, he should go to meet him; when he runs, follow behind.

BP 1.4.168

पराङ्मुखस्याभिमुखो दूरस्थस्यैत्य चान्तिकम् ।
नमस्कृत्य शयानस्य निदेशे तिष्ठेत्सर्वदा ॥६८

If the teacher faces away, the pupil should move before him; if he is far away, approach near. If the teacher is lying down, the pupil should bow and remain awaiting his command.

BP 1.4.169

नीचं शय्यासनं चास्य सर्वदा गुरुसन्निधौ ।
गुरोश्च चक्षुर्विषये न यथेष्टासनो भवेत् ॥६९

After the teacher has gone to heaven, O best of the Bharatas, the pupil should treat a qualified son of the teacher, the teacher's wife, or a close blood-relative as the teacher, O king.

BP 1.4.170

नामोच्चारणमेवास्य परोक्षमपि सुव्रत ।
न चैनमनुकुर्वीत गतिभाषणचेष्टितैः ॥.4.१७०

He should not utter the teacher's bare name even when the teacher is absent, O keeper of good vows, nor imitate his walk, speech, or gestures.

BP 1.4.171

परीवादस्तथा निन्दा गुरोर्यत्र प्रवर्तते ।
कर्णौ तत्र पिधातव्यो गन्तव्यं वा ततोऽन्यतः ॥७१

Wherever disparagement or abuse of the teacher occurs, the pupil should cover both ears or leave that place.

BP 1.4.172

परीवादाद्रासभः स्यात्सारमेयस्तु निन्दकः ।
परिभोक्ता कृमिर्भवति कीटो भवति मत्सरी ॥७२

By disparaging the teacher one becomes a donkey; by reviling him, a dog; by consuming what belongs to him, a worm; by envying him, an insect.

BP 1.4.173

दूरस्थो नार्चयेदेनं न क्रुद्धो नान्तिके स्त्रियाः ।
यानासनगतो राजन्नवरुह्याभिवादयेत् ॥७३

He should not salute the teacher from a distance, while angry, or while near a woman. If riding or seated, O king, he should descend and then salute.

BP 1.4.174

प्रतिकूले समाने तु नासीत गुरुणा सह ।
अशृण्वंति गुरौ राजन्न किञ्चिदपि कीर्तयेत् ॥७४

He should not sit opposite or at the same level as his teacher. When the teacher cannot hear, O king, he should not say anything about him.

BP 1.4.175

गोश्वोष्ट्रयानप्रासादप्रस्तरेषु कटेषु च ।
आसीत गुरुणा सार्धं शिलाफलक नौषु च ॥७५

He may sit together with the teacher in a cart drawn by oxen, horses, or camels, on a terrace, mat, stone slab, board, or boat.

BP 1.4.176

गुरोर्गुरौ सन्निहिते गुरुवद्वृत्तिमाचरेत् ।
गुरुपुत्रेषु चार्येषु गुरोश्चैव स्वबन्धुषु ॥७६

When the teacher's own teacher is present, one should behave toward him as toward the teacher; the same applies to the teacher's sons, honorable people in his household, and close relatives.

BP 1.4.177

बालः समानजन्मा वा विशिष्टो यज्ञकर्मणि ।
अध्यापयन्गुरुसुतो गुरुवन्मानमर्हति ॥७७

A teacher's son who instructs the pupil deserves honor like the teacher, whether a child, of the pupil's own age, or distinguished in sacrificial work.

BP 1.4.178

उत्सादनमथाङ्गानां स्नापनोच्छिष्टभोजने ।
पादयोर्नेजन राजन्गुरुपुत्रेषु वर्जयेत् ॥७८

Toward the teacher's sons, one should not massage their limbs, bathe them, eat their leftovers, or wash their feet, O king.

BP 1.4.179

गुरुवत्प्रतिपूज्यास्तु सवर्णा गुरुयोषितः ।
असवर्णास्तु सम्पूज्याः प्रत्युत्थानाभिवादनैः ॥७९

The teacher's wives of the same varṇa should be honored like the teacher; those of another varṇa should be honored by rising and saluting.

BP 1.4.180

अभ्यञ्जनं स स्नपनं गात्रोत्सादनमेव च ।
गुरुपत्न्या न कार्याणि केशानां च प्रसाधनम् ॥.4.१८०

The pupil should not anoint, bathe, massage, or arrange the hair of the teacher's wife.

BP 1.4.181

गुरुपत्नीं तु युवतीं नाभिवादेत पादयोः ।
पूर्णविंशतिवर्षेण गुणदोषौ विजानता ॥८१

A student who has completed twenty years and understands virtue and fault should not greet a young wife of the teacher by grasping her feet.

BP 1.4.182

स्वभाव एव नारीणां नराणामिह दूषणम् ।
अतोर्थान्न प्रमाद्यन्ति प्रतिपाद्य विपश्चितः ॥८२

Here, women's nature itself is a fault for men; therefore the learned, having set forth this matter, do not become heedless.

BP 1.4.183

अविद्वांसमलं लोके विद्वांसमपि वा पुनः ।
प्रमदा ह्युत्पथं नेतुं कामक्रोधवशानुगम् ॥८३

Women may lead an ignorant person, and even a learned one, onto a wrong path when he has come under the power of desire and anger.

BP 1.4.184

मात्रा स्वस्रा दुहित्रा वा न विविक्तासनो भवेत् ।
बलवानिन्द्रियग्रामो विद्वांसमपि कर्षति ॥८४

One should not sit in seclusion even with one's mother, sister, or daughter; the collection of the senses is powerful and can draw away even the learned.

BP 1.4.185

राजेन्द्र गुरुपत्नीनां युवतीनां युवा भुवि ।
विधिवद्वन्दनं कुर्यादसावहमिति ब्रुवन् ॥८५

A young man on earth should greet a young wife of his teacher according to rule, O king of kings, respectfully announcing, "I am so-and-so."

BP 1.4.186

विप्रोऽस्य पादग्रहणमन्वहं चाभिवादनम् ।
गुरुदारेषु कुर्वीत सतां धर्ममनुस्मरन् ॥८६

Remembering the duty of the virtuous, a Brahmin should perform the daily salutation, and where proper, the respectful grasping of the feet of the teacher's wives.

BP 1.4.187

यथा खनन्खनित्रेण जलमाप्नोति मानवः ।
तथा गुरुगतां विद्यां शुश्रूषुरधिगच्छति ॥८७

Just as a person digging with a spade reaches water, so a pupil eager to serve reaches the knowledge within the teacher.

BP 1.4.188

मुण्डो वा जटिलो वा स्यादथ वा स्याच्छिखी जटी ।
नैनं ग्रामेऽभिनिम्लोचेदर्को नाभ्युदियात्क्वचित् ॥८८

Whether his head is shaved, his hair matted, or he wears a topknot with matted locks, he should not let the sun set while he is negligent in the village, nor rise while he remains asleep.

BP 1.4.189

तं चेदभ्युदियात्सूर्यः शयानं कामकारतः ।
निम्लोचेद्वाप्यभिज्ञानाज्जपन्नुपवसेद्दिनम् ॥८९

If the sun rises while he sleeps through wilfulness, or sets while he is heedless through lack of awareness, he should fast for a day while reciting sacred formulas.

BP 1.4.190

सूर्येण ह्यभिनिर्मुक्तः शयानोभ्युदितश्च यः ।
प्रायश्चित्तमकुर्वाणो युक्तः स्यान्महतैनसा ॥.4.१९०

One whom the sun catches asleep at sunrise or overtakes at sunset, and who performs no expiation, becomes burdened with great sin.

BP 1.4.191

उपस्पृश्य महाराज उभे सन्ध्ये समाहितः ।
शुचौ देशे जपञ्जप्यमुपासीत यथाविधि ॥९१

Having sipped water for purification with a collected mind, O great king, he should observe both twilights according to rule, reciting the prescribed prayer in a clean place.

BP 1.4.192

यदि स्त्री यद्यवरजः श्रेयः किञ्चित्समाचरेत् ।
तत्सर्वमाचरेद्युक्तो यत्र वा रमते मनः ॥९२

If a woman or a younger person performs something that leads to a better course, one should attentively adopt it wherever the mind finds wholesome delight.

BP 1.4.193

धर्मार्थावुच्यते श्रेयः कामार्थौ धर्ममेव च ।
अर्थ एवेह वा श्रेयस्त्रिवर्ग इति संस्थितिः ॥९३

Some call duty and wealth the highest good, others desire and wealth, others duty alone, and still others wealth alone; such are the views concerning the three aims.

BP 1.4.194

पिता माता तथा भ्राता आचार्याः कुरुनन्दन ।
नार्तेनाप्यवमन्तव्या ब्राह्मणेन विशेषतः ॥९४

Father, mother, brother, and teacher, O delight of the Kurus, should never be treated with contempt by one who is distressed, especially not by a Brahmin.

BP 1.4.195

आचार्यो ब्रह्मणो मूर्तिः पिता मूर्तिः प्रजापतेः ।
माताप्यथादितेर्मूर्तिर्भ्राता स्यान्मूर्तिरात्मनः ॥९५

The teacher is an embodiment of Brahman, the father of Prajāpati, the mother of Aditi, and the brother of one's own self.

BP 1.4.196

यन्माता पितरौ क्लेशं सहेते सम्भवे नृणाम् ।
न तस्य निष्कृतिः शक्या कर्तुं वर्षशतैरपि ॥९६

The hardship endured by mother and father in bringing a human being into existence cannot be repaid even by service for a hundred years.

BP 1.4.197

तयोर्नित्यं प्रियं कुर्यादाचार्यस्य च भारत ।
तेषु हि त्रिषु तुष्टेषु तपः सर्वं समाप्यते ॥९७

One should continually do what is pleasing to those two and to the teacher, O descendant of Bharata; when those three are satisfied, all austerity is brought to completion.

BP 1.4.198

तेषां त्रयाणां शुश्रूषा परमं तप उच्यते ।
न तैरनभ्यनुज्ञातो धर्ममन्यं समाचरेत् ॥९८

Service to those three is called the highest austerity. Without their permission, one should not undertake another religious observance.

BP 1.4.199

त एव हि त्रयो लोकास्त एव त्रय आश्रमाः ।
त एव च त्रयो वेदास्त एवोक्तास्त्रयोऽग्नयः ॥९९

Those three themselves are the three worlds, the three stages of life, the three Vedas, and the three sacred fires.

BP 1.4.200

माता वै गार्हपत्याग्निः पिता वै दक्षिणः स्मृतः ।
गुरुराहवनीयश्च साग्नित्रेता गरीयसी ॥.4.२००

The mother is the household fire, the father the southern fire, and the teacher the fire of offerings; this triad of sacred fires is most venerable.

BP 1.4.201

त्रिषु तुष्टेषु चैतेषु त्रींल्लोकाञ्जयते गृही ।
दीप्यमानः स्ववपुषा देववद्दिवि मोदते ॥०१

When these three are satisfied, a householder conquers the three worlds. Shining with his own radiance, he rejoices in heaven like a god.

BP 1.4.202

इमं लोकं पितृभक्त्या मातृभक्त्या तु मध्यमम् ।
गुरुशुश्रूषया चैव गच्छेच्छक्रसलोकताम् ॥०२

Through devotion to the father one gains this world, through devotion to the mother the middle world, and through service to the teacher one reaches the world of Indra.

BP 1.4.203

सर्वे तेनादृता धर्मा यस्यैते त्रय आदृताः ।
अनादृतास्तु येनैते सर्वास्तस्याफलाः ॥२०३

All duties are honored by the person who honors these three; for one who disregards them every religious act is fruitless.

BP 1.4.204

यावत्त्रयस्ते जीवेयुस्तावन्नान्यत्समाचरेत्।
तेष्वेव नित्यं शुश्रूषां कुर्यात्प्रियहिते रतः ॥०४

As long as those three live, one should undertake nothing else at their expense; one should always serve them, devoted to what pleases and benefits them.

BP 1.4.205

तेषामनुपरोधेन पार्थक्यं यद्यदाचरेत् ।
तत्तन्निवेदयेत्तेभ्यो मनोवचनकर्मभिः ॥०५

Whatever separate undertaking one performs without obstructing their welfare, one should report to them in thought, speech, and action.

BP 1.4.206

त्रिष्वेतेष्विति कृत्यं हि पुरुषस्य समाप्यते ।
एष धर्मः परः साक्षादुपधर्मोऽन्य उच्यते ॥०६

A person's duty is fulfilled through these three. This is directly the highest duty; every other observance is called subordinate duty.

BP 1.4.207

श्रद्धधानः शुभां विद्यामाददीतावरादपि ।
अन्त्यादपि परं धर्मं स्त्रीरत्नं दुष्कुलादपि ॥०७

With faith one should receive beneficial knowledge even from a person of lower standing, the highest duty even from the lowest, and a worthy woman even from a poor family.

BP 1.4.208

विषादप्यमृतं ग्राह्यं बालादपि सुभाषितम् ।
अमित्रादपि सद्वृत्तममेध्यादपि काञ्चनम् ॥०८

Nectar should be taken even from poison, a wise saying even from a child, good conduct even from an enemy, and gold even from an impure place.

BP 1.4.209

स्त्रियो रत्नं नयो विद्या धर्मः शौचं सुभाषितम् ।
विविधानि च शिल्पानि समादेयानि सर्वशः ॥०९

A worthy woman, a jewel, sound policy, knowledge, duty, purity, good speech, and the many crafts should be acquired from every suitable source.

BP 1.4.210

अब्राह्मणादध्ययनमापत्काले विधीयते ।
अनुव्रज्या च शुश्रूषा यावदध्ययनं गुरोः ॥.4.२१०

In a time of distress, study under a non-Brahmin is permitted. The pupil should accompany and serve that teacher for as long as the study continues.

BP 1.4.211

नाब्राह्मणे गुरौ शिष्यो वासमात्यन्तिकं वसेत् ।
ब्राह्मणे चाननूचाने काञ्क्षन्गतिमनुत्तमाम् ॥११

A pupil seeking the unsurpassed destination should not reside permanently with a non-Brahmin teacher, nor with a Brahmin who is not learned in recitation.

BP 1.4.212

यदि त्वात्यन्तिको वासो रोचते च गुरोः कुले ।
युक्तः परिचरेदेनमाशरीरविमोक्षणात् ॥१२

But if permanent residence in the teacher's household pleases him, he should remain disciplined and serve the teacher until release from the body.

BP 1.4.213

आ समाप्तेः शरीरस्य यस्तु शुश्रूषते गुरुम् ।
स गच्छत्यञ्जसा विप्रो ब्राह्मणः सद्य शाश्वतम् ॥१३

A Brahmin who serves the teacher until the end of his bodily life goes directly to the eternal abode of Brahman.

BP 1.4.214

न पूर्वं गुरवे किञ्चिदुपकुर्वीत धर्मवित् ।
स्नानाय गुरुणाज्ञस्तः शक्त्या गुर्वर्थमाहरेत् ॥१४

A knower of duty should not offer the teacher's fee beforehand. When the teacher authorizes the ceremonial bath completing study, he should procure a suitable gift according to his ability.

BP 1.4.215

क्षेत्रं हिरण्यं गामश्वं छत्रोपानहमेव च ।
धान्यं वासांसि शाकं वा गुरवे प्रीतमाहरेत् ॥१५

He may bring the teacher a pleasing gift such as land, gold, a cow, a horse, an umbrella, footwear, grain, garments, or vegetables.

BP 1.4.216

स्वर्गते गां परित्यज्य गुरौ भरतसत्तम ।
गुणान्विते गुरुसुते गुरुदारेऽथ वा नृप ॥
सपिण्डे वा गुरोश्चापि गुरुवद्वृत्तिमाचरेत् ॥१६

When the teacher has gone to heaven, O best of the Bharatas, the pupil should behave like a teacher's son, wife, or close blood-relative who is qualified, O king.

BP 1.4.217

एतेष्वविद्यमानेषु स्थानासनविहारवान् ।
प्रयुञ्जानोऽग्निशुश्रूषां साधयेद्देहमात्सनः ॥
वीरस्य कुर्वञ्छुश्रूषां याति वीरसलोकताम् ॥१७

If none of these exists, he should remain at the teacher's place, keep the prescribed seat and way of life, tend the sacred fire, and bring his own body to its completion. By serving that spiritual hero, he reaches the hero's world.

BP 1.4.218

चरत्येवं हि यो विप्रो ब्रह्मचर्यमविप्लुतः ।
स गत्वा ब्रह्मसदनं ब्रह्मणा सह मोदते ॥१८

A Brahmin who thus practises celibate studentship without deviation goes to Brahmā's abode and rejoices there with Brahmā.

BP 1.4.219

इत्येष कथितो धर्मः प्रथमं ब्रह्मचारिणः ।
गृहस्थस्यापि राजेन्द्र शृणु धर्ममशेषतः ॥१९

Thus the duty of the celibate student has been explained first. Now hear fully, O king of kings, the duty of the householder as well.

BP 1.4.220

काले प्राप्य व्रतं विप्र ऋतुयोगेन भारत ।
प्रपालयन्व्रतं याति ब्रह्मसालोक्यतां विभो ॥.4.२२०

A Brahmin who undertakes the vow at the proper time, O descendant of Bharata, and faithfully maintains it in the proper season reaches companionship with Brahmā, O lord.

BP 1.4.221

सदोपनयनं शस्तं वसन्ते ब्राह्मणस्य तु ।
क्षत्रियस्य ततो ग्रीष्मे प्रशस्तं मनुरब्रवीत् ॥२१

Initiation is always commended for a Brahmin in spring; Manu declared summer the approved season for a Kshatriya.

BP 1.4.222

प्राप्ते शरदि वैश्यस्य सदोपनयनं परम् ।
इत्येष त्रिविधः कालः कथितो व्रतयोजने ॥२२

For a Vaishya, initiation is best when autumn arrives. Thus the threefold season for undertaking the vow has been explained.
