VP 1.4.1

ब्रह्मा नारायणाख्योऽसौ कल्पादौ भगवान् यथा।
ससर्ज सर्व-भूतानि तदाचक्ष्व महामुने

Tell me, mighty sage, how, in the commencement of the (present) Kalpa, Nārāyaṇa, who is named Brahmā, created all existent things.

Śrīdhara commentary

The interrupted account of Brahmic creation is requested again, having been introduced earlier and displaced by another topic.

VP 1.4.3

अतीत-कल्पावसाने निशासुप्तोत्थितः प्रभुः।
सत्त्वोद्रिक्तस् तथा ब्रह्मा शून्यं लोकम् अवैक्षत

At the close of the past (or Pādma) Kalpa, the divine Brahmā, endowed with the quality of goodness, awoke from his night of sleep, and beheld the universe void.

Śrīdhara commentary

Before describing the promised creation of progeny, Parāśara first speaks of raising the earth, which is its support. Brahmā has awakened from sleep; hence sattva is predominant, as wakefulness brings clarity to the faculties.

VP 1.4.4

VP 1.4.5

इमं चोदाहरन्त्य् अत्र श्लोकं नारायणं प्रति।

VP 1.4.6

आपो नारा इति प्रोक्ता आपो वै नर-सूनवः।
अयनं तस्य ताः पूर्वं तेन नारायणः स्मृतः

The waters are called Nārā, because they were the offspring of Nara (the supreme spirit); and as in them his first (Ayana) progress (in the character of Brahmā) took place, he is thence named Nārāyaṇa (he whose place of moving was the waters).

Śrīdhara commentary

Nara is the eternal Supreme Self; nārāḥ are the waters born from him, and because those waters were his first abode, he is called Nārāyaṇa.

VP 1.4.7

तोयान्तःस्थां महीं ज्ञात्वा जगत्य् एकार्णवी-कृते।
अनुमानात् तद्-उद्धारं कर्तु-कामः प्रजापतिः

He, the lord, concluding that within the waters lay the earth, and being desirous to raise it up, created another form for that purpose;

Śrīdhara commentary

Seeing the lotus leaf upon the water, Brahmā inferred the earth as its support beneath the waters and wished to raise it; Nārāyaṇa therefore made a body suited to that task.

भू-विभागं ततः कृत्वा सप्त-द्वीपान् यथा-तथम्।
भूर्-आद्यांश् चतुरो लोकान् पूर्ववत् समकल्पयत्

Having then divided the earth into seven great portions or continents, as it was before, he constructed in like manner the four (lower) spheres, earth, sky, heaven, and the sphere of the sages (Maharloka).

Śrīdhara commentary

Yathā-tatham means exactly as before. The four worlds beginning with Bhūḥ are arranged as previously; Bhūḥ can also suggest Pātāla.

VP 1.4.50

ब्रह्म-रूप-धरो देवस् ततोऽसौ रजसा वृतः।
चकार सृष्टिं भगवांश् चतुर्-वक्त्र-धरो हरिः

Thus Hari, the four-faced god, invested with the quality of activity, and taking the form of Brahmā, accomplished the creation:

Śrīdhara commentary

The Varāha form has been described through the raising of earth and arranging of the worlds; now the text describes the creation of the gods and others by that same Lord in his form as Brahmā. Four faces follow from his Brahmā form.

VP 1.4.51

निमित्त-मात्रम् एवासौ सृष्टानां सर्ग-कर्मणि।
प्रधान-कारणी-भूता यतो वै सृज्य-शक्तयः

but he (Brahmā) is only the instrumental cause of things to be created; the things that are capable of being created arise from nature as a common material cause:

Śrīdhara commentary

The Lord in the form of Brahmā is only an instrumental cause. The prior karmic tendencies of living beings are the principal causes of the powers by which they are created.

VP 1.4.52

निमित्त-मात्रं मुक्त्वैवं नान्यत् किञ्चिद् अपेक्षते।
नीयते तपतां श्रेष्ठ स्व-शक्त्या वस्तु वस्तुताम्

with exception of one instrumental cause alone, there is no need of any other cause, for (imperceptible) substance becomes perceptible substance according to the powers with which it is originally imbued.

Śrīdhara commentary

When the instrumental cause alone is present, subtle matter becomes gross through its own power, as a seed develops into a sprout when rain is present; no additional cause is required.
