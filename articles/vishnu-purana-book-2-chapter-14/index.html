VP 2.14.1

निशम्य तस्येति वचः परमार्थ-समन्वितम्।
प्रश्रयावनतो भूत्वा तम् आह नृपतिर् द्विजम्॥१॥

Having heard these remarks, full of profound truth, the king was highly pleased with the Brahman, and respectfully thus addressed him:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
निशम्य (niśamya) — having heard;
तत् (tat) — that;
वचः (vacaḥ) — speech;
परमार्थ-समन्वितम् (paramārtha-samanvitam) — endowed with highest truth;
प्रश्रय-अवनतः (praśraya-avanataḥ) — bowed respectfully;
नृपतिः (nṛpatiḥ) — the king;
द्विजम् (dvijam) — to the Brahman
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

niśamya tasyeti vacaḥ paramārtha-samanvitam | praśrayāvanato bhūtvā tam āha nṛpatir dvijam

Śrīdhara

nāham ity-ādi vākyena pramāṇāsambhave gate | prameyāsambhavāśaṅkī bhūyo bhūyo'tha pṛcchati ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहम् इत्य्-आदि वाक्येन प्रमाणासम्भवे गते । प्रमेयासम्भवाशङ्की भूयो भूयोऽथ पृच्छति ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Having heard the statements beginning with “I do not carry [the palanquin],” and finding no difficulty in the means of knowledge, the king—still fearing that the object might be impossible to establish—asks again and again.

VP 2.14.2

राजोवाच— भगवन् यत् त्वया प्रोक्तं परमार्थ-मयं वचः।
श्रुते तस्मिन् भ्रमन्तीव मनसो मम वृत्तयः॥२॥

What you have said is no doubt the truth; but in listening to it my mind is much disturbed.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
भगवन् (bhagavan) — O venerable one;
यत् (yat) — what;
त्वया प्रोक्तम् (tvayā proktam) — spoken by you;
परमार्थ-मयम् (paramārtha-mayam) — full of highest truth;
श्रुते तस्मिन् (śrute tasmin) — when that was heard;
मनसः वृत्तयः (manasaḥ vṛttayaḥ) — movements of my mind;
भ्रमन्ति इव (bhramanti iva) — wander as if
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

rājovāca— bhagavan yat tvayā proktaṃ paramārtha-mayaṃ vacaḥ | śrute tasmin bhramantīva manaso mama vṛttayaḥ

Śrīdhara

nāhaṃ pīvetyādivākyaśravaṇataḥ pramāṇāsambhāvanāyāṃ nivṛttāyāmapi nānādarśanaprasiddhaśreyasāṃ paramārthatve sati ātmaiva paramārtha ityasya prameyasya asambhāvanayā pṛcchati—bhagavann iti daśabhiḥ ||2||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहं पीवेत्यादिवाक्यश्रवणतः प्रमाणासम्भावनायां निवृत्तायामपि नानादर्शनप्रसिद्धश्रेयसां परमार्थत्वे सति आत्मैव परमार्थ इत्यस्य प्रमेयस्य असम्भावनया पृच्छति—भगवन्न् इति दशभिः ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Although hearing the statement beginning “I do not [carry it]” removes doubt about the means of knowledge, he asks because he cannot conceive how the highest good taught by various schools can be the Supreme Truth, with the Self alone as that truth; he begins with “Bhagavan.”

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tasyātma-para-deheṣu sato’py ekamayaṃ hi yat | vijñānaṃ paramārtho’sau dvaitino’tathya-darśinaḥ

Śrīdhara

sa ca eka eva, na tu pratidehaṃ bhinnaḥ | vyāpī sākṣitayā sarva-vyāpakaḥ | samaḥ eka-rūpaḥ, na tu jñānādi-pariṇāmair aneka-rūpaḥ | śuddhaḥ kartṛtvādi lubya [?] śūnyaḥ | ato janma-vṛddhy-ādibhir dehādi-vikārair asaṃspṛṣṭaḥ | ata eva sarva-gato, na tu deha-pariṇāmaḥ | ato madhya-parimāṇatvābhāvād avyayaḥ ||29||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

स च एक एव, न तु प्रतिदेहं भिन्नः । व्यापी साक्षितया सर्व-व्यापकः । समः एक-रूपः, न तु ज्ञानादि-परिणामैर् अनेक-रूपः । शुद्धः कर्तृत्वादि लुब्य [?] शून्यः । अतो जन्म-वृद्ध्य्-आदिभिर् देहादि-विकारैर् असंस्पृष्टः । अत एव सर्व-गतो, न तु देह-परिणामः । अतो मध्य-परिमाणत्वाभावाद् अव्ययः ॥२९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The self’s consciousness remains one in its own and others’ bodies; apparent difference is caused by limiting adjuncts. Those who take the adjuncts as the self’s intrinsic nature see falsely.

VP 2.14.32

वेणु-रन्ध्र-विभेदेन भेदः षड्जादि-सञ्ज्ञितः।
अभेद-व्यापिनो वायोस् तथा तस्य महात्मनः॥३२॥

As the notes of a flute are distinguished by differences among its holes, so the one, undivided air pervades; in the same way the Supreme Self is spoken of through apparent distinctions.

Śrīdhara

etat dṛṣṭāntenopapādayati—veṇu-randhreti dvābhyām | abhedasya vyāpino vāyor yathā veṇu-randhropādhi-bhedena ṣaḍja-ṛṣabha-gāndhārādi-svarābhivyañjakatvāt, na tan-nāda-sañjñito bhedas tathā tasya mahato’paricchinnasya ātmanaḥ svata ekatvam ||32|| rūpa-bhedas tu deva-manuṣyādi-lakṣaṇo bāhya-karma-pravṛttijaḥ bāhyānām ātmanaḥ pṛthag-bhūtānāṃ dehendriyādīnāṃ yat karma, tasya pravṛttiḥ phalaṃ tato jātaḥ | pāṭhāntare, bāhyānāṃ dehānāṃ karmabhiḥ yā āvṛttiḥ āvaraṇaṃ, tataḥ prajāyate iti bāhya-karmāvṛtti-prajo bhedaḥ, na tu svataḥ | tatra hetuḥ--yasmāt devādi-bhede apadhvaste bhidyate aneneti bhedaḥ ahaṅkāraḥ devādi-bhede dehe, tāvat ahaṅkārāvaraṇe suṣuptādau vinaṣṭe sa bhedo nāsty eva |

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एतत् दृष्टान्तेनोपपादयति—वेणु-रन्ध्रेति द्वाभ्याम् । अभेदस्य व्यापिनो वायोर् यथा वेणु-रन्ध्रोपाधि-भेदेन षड्ज-ऋषभ-गान्धारादि-स्वराभिव्यञ्जकत्वात्, न तन्-नाद-सञ्ज्ञितो भेदस् तथा तस्य महतोऽपरिच्छिन्नस्य आत्मनः स्वत एकत्वम् ॥३२॥ रूप-भेदस् तु देव-मनुष्य्आदि-लक्षणो बाह्य-कर्म-प्रवृत्तिजः बाह्यानाम् आत्मनः पृथग्-भूतानां देहेन्द्रियादीनां यत् कर्म, तस्य प्रवृत्तिः फलं ततो जातः । पाठान्तरे, बाह्यानां देहानां कर्मभिः या आवृत्तिः आवरणं, ततः प्रजायते इति बाह्य-कर्मावृत्ति-प्रजो भेदः, न तु स्वतः । तत्र हेतुः--यस्मात् देवादि-भेदे अपध्वस्ते भिद्यते अनेनेति भेदः अहङ्कारः देवादि-भेदे देहे, तावत् अहङ्कारावरणे सुषुप्तादौ विनष्टे स भेदो नास्त्य् एव ।

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The flute analogy shows how one air appears as different notes when it passes through different holes. Likewise, differences in embodied experience do not alter the underlying nature of the self.

VP 2.14.33

एकत्वं रूप-भेदश् च बाह्य-कर्म-प्रवृत्तिजः।
देवादि-भेदम् अध्यास्ते नास्त्य् एवावरणो हि सः॥३३॥

The difference in form arises from external karma and its covering. When the distinctions of gods and other beings are destroyed, that covering is gone and the difference no longer remains.
