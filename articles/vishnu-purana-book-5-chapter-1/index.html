VP 5.1.1

नृपाणां कथितः सर्वो भवता वंश-विस्तरः ।

VP 5.1.2

अंशावतारो ब्रह्मर्षे योऽयं यदु-कुलोद्भवः ।
विष्णोस् तं विस्तरेणाहं श्रोतुम् इच्छामि तत्त्वतः ॥२॥

O Brahmarṣi, I wish to hear in detail and in truth of that partial incarnation of Viṣṇu, born in the Yadu lineage.

Śrīdhara translation

“Partial incarnation” means that Viṣṇu, the all-pervading Supreme Brahman, incarnates in a limited form. “In detail” means I wish to hear fully how that incarnation came to be, in what portion, and in what manner.

VP 5.1.3

चकार यानि कर्माणि भगवान् पुरुषोत्तमः ।
अंशांशेनावतीर्योर्व्यां तत्र तानि मुने वद ॥३॥

Tell, O sage, the deeds that Bhagavān, the Supreme Person, performed on earth, descending by a portion of a portion.

Śrīdhara translation

“Tell also his deeds in detail” is the sense of “and.” Tell what he did after descending in human form, as a portion of a portion of the one who reclines in the Milk Ocean, who is himself as though a portion of Supreme Brahman.

O First Lord, the speech that moves through inquiry into the Self, the self’s body, and qualities is also not other than you, whose nature is the Self.

Śrīdhara translation

The Upaniṣad section that teaches Supreme Brahman is also your form; this is stated by “the Self.” “Ātman” and “ātmā” denote the individual self and Supreme Self; “body” denotes the gross and subtle bodies; “possessing qualities” denotes the unmanifest, their cause. The Upaniṣadic sentence that investigates these—the two selves, two bodies, and the unmanifest—is also not other than you. In that inquiry, the inner nature indicated by “you” and the nature of the Self indicated by “that” are the result, Brahman. O First Lord, master of Brahmā and the rest, that Upaniṣadic sentence is not other than you. Alternatively, since the terms beginning with Ṛgveda also state the Upaniṣadic nature, the verse may be explained according to Sāṅkhya, Yoga, and the other systems, as teaching their meaning.

He says, “The one who sees the imperishable Brahman, the nature of the Lord, is liberated through that seeing,” with “subtler than the atom.” You, the Lord of inconceivable power, are subtler than the atom, extremely subtle because you are without limiting adjuncts; therefore you stand as though of unmanifest nature. Alternatively, for the one who sees the Self as the nature of the unreal universe, the highest and complete cessation of ignorance arises. Of what kind is that seer? He explains with “the intellect of the wise.” O Lord, O Supreme Self beyond the unmanifest, the intellect of this discerning person bears and meditates on nothing other than you, the most excellent form, the embodiment of supreme bliss. Through the cessation of latent impressions and ignorance, liberation arises.

If you are beyond Māyā’s covering, how can you be enclosed in many bodies such as a fish or tortoise? Śrīdhara translation

“He said” etc.: the idea is, “If my difficult task is to be accomplished, you should help me.” But this is unnecessary, since even in a great task such as removing Earth’s burden, my mere hair is capable. Do not think that I incarnate only as a hair: verses such as “shattered by my glance” and “I will be born on the eighth day” explicitly speak of my direct incarnation. The white and black hairs are only for beauty; gray hair cannot belong to one who is ageless and immortal, since time made of kāṣṭhās, muhūrtas, and the like is not the cause of change in my glory. My two hairs, descending upon Earth, will remove the distress caused by her burden.

VP 5.1.87

ते सर्वे सर्वदा भद्रा मत्-प्रसादाद् असंशयम् ।
असन्दिग्धं भविष्यन्ति गच्छ देवि यथोदितम् ॥८७॥

They will all be well, O auspicious one, through my grace, without doubt; their wishes will be unfailing. Go, goddess, as instructed.

Śrīdhara translation

“Those” etc.: all their desires, granted by you when pleased, will be unfailing through my grace; therefore go without doubt.
