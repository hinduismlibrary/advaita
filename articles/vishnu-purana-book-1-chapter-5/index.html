VP 1.5.1

यथा ससर्ज देवोऽसौ देवर्षि-पितृ-दानवान् ।
मनुष्य-तिर्यग्-वृक्षादीन् भू-व्योम-सलिलौकसः

Now unfold to me, Brahman, how this deity created the gods, sages, progenitors, demons, men, animals, trees, and the rest, that abide on earth, in heaven, or in the waters: how Brahmā at creation made the world with the qualities, the characteristics, and the forms of things.

Śrīdhara translation

The creation made by Brahmā in his form as the deity is now queried: how was it made? “Beings” are those whose abodes are earth, heaven, or water.

VP 1.5.2

यद् गुणं यत्-स्वभावं च यद्-रूपं च जगद् द्विज ।
सर्गादौ सृष्टवान् ब्रह्मा तन् ममाचक्ष्व कृत्स्नशः

What qualities, what characteristics, and what forms did Brahmā, at the beginning of creation, give to the world? Tell me all this, O twice-born.

Śrīdhara translation

The requested qualities are the guṇas such as sattva, the inherent dispositions such as peacefulness or fierceness, and forms such as two-footed and four-footed bodies.

VP 1.5.4

सृष्टिं चिन्तयतस् तस्य कल्पादिषु यथा पुरा ।
अबुद्धि-पूर्वकः सर्गः प्रादुर्भूतस् तमो-मयः

Whilst he (Brahmā) formerly, in the beginning of the Kalpas, was meditating on creation, there appeared a creation beginning with ignorance, and consisting of darkness.

Śrīdhara translation

Without avidyā, embodied beings could not have agency or experience; this is therefore the first appearance of avidyā. Abuddhi-pūrvaka means arising without deliberate attention, and tamomaya means enveloped in avidyā.

VP 1.5.5

तमो मोहो महामोहस् तामिस्रो ह्यन्धसञ्ज्ञितः ।
अविद्या पञ्चपर्वैषा प्रादुर्भूता महात्मनः

From that great being appeared fivefold Ignorance, consisting of obscurity, illusion, extreme illusion, gloom, utter darkness.

Śrīdhara translation

The five divisions of avidyā are tamas, self-identification with the body; moha, the sense of ownership toward relations; mahāmoha, desire for sense pleasures; tāmisra, anger when opposed; and andha-tāmisra, clinging to protection through fear of loss. These are the five inversions called kleśas.

VP 1.5.6

पञ्चधाऽवस्थितः सर्गो ध्यायतोऽप्रतिबोधवान् ।
बहिर् अन्तोऽप्रकाशश् च संवृतात्मा नगात्मकः

The creation of the creator thus plunged in abstraction, was the fivefold (immovable) world, without intellect or reflection, void of perception or sensation, incapable of feeling, and destitute of motion.

Śrīdhara translation

The first creation is the intensely dark immovable creation, in five kinds: trees, shrubs, creepers, climbing plants, and grasses. It lacks self-reflection, is without illumination outwardly or inwardly, and has consciousness covered by darkness.

VP 1.5.7

मुख्या नगा यतः प्रोक्ता मुख्यसर्गस् ततस् त्व् अयम्

Since immovable things were first created, this is called the first creation. Brahmā, beholding that it was defective, designed another;

Śrīdhara translation

Mukhya means primary, and naga means an immovable being. Although mountains are immovable, they were created by Varāha before Brahmā’s creation; they are therefore excluded here. Seeing that this creation could not serve the human aim, Brahmā contemplated another.

VP 1.5.8

तं दृष्ट्वाऽसाधकं सर्गम् अन्यद् अपरं पुनः

Having seen this creation to be incapable of purposeful activity, he contemplated another, superior creation.

Śrīdhara translation

Having seen the first creation to be incapable of purposeful activity, he contemplated another, superior creation. “Unproductive” means unable to undertake the activity of saṃsāra.

VP 1.5.63

नाम रूपं च भूतानां कृत्यानां च प्रपञ्चनम् ।
वेद-शब्देभ्य एवादौ देवादीनां चकार सः

he determined in the beginning, by the authority of the Vedas, the names and forms and functions of all creatures, and of the gods;

Śrīdhara translation

From the words of the Veda he established the names, forms, and functions of the gods and all other beings.

VP 1.5.65

VP 1.5.66

At the beginning of every Kalpa Brahmā creates this same kind of world again and again, endowed with the will to create and impelled by the power of what is to be created.
