VP 3.10.1

सगर उवाच— कथितं चातुराश्रम्यं चातुर्वर्ण्य-क्रियास् तथा।
पुंसः क्रियाम् अहं श्रोतुम् इच्छामि द्विज-सत्तम

Sagara: You have explained the four āśramas and the practices of the four varṇas. O best of the twice-born, I want to hear about the rites prescribed for a person.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
कथितम् (kathitam) — has been explained
चातुः-आश्रम्यम् (cātuḥ-āśramyam) — the four āśramas
चातुः-वर्ण्य-क्रियाः (cātuḥ-varṇya-kriyāḥ) — the practices of the four varṇas
तथा (tathā) — likewise
पुंसः क्रियाम् (puṃsaḥ kriyām) — the rites of a person
श्रोतुम् इच्छामि (śrotum icchāmi) — I wish to hear
द्विज-सत्तम (dvija-sattama) — O best of the twice-born.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kathitaṃ cāturāśramyaṃ cāturvarṇya-kriyās tathā|puṃsaḥ kriyām ahaṃ śrotum icchāmi dvija-sattama

Śrīdhara

puṃsaḥ ṣoḍaśasaṃskārairvarṇāśramaniveśanam । atastānāha daśame bāhulyena dvijātiṣu । catvāra āśramā eva cāturāśramyaṃ taddharmaḥ kathitaḥ, evañcāturvarṇyakriyā api puṃsaḥ kriyāṃ ṣoḍaśasaṃskārarūpām

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पुंसः षोडशसंस्कारैर्वर्णाश्रमनिवेशनम् । अतस्तानाह दशमे बाहुल्येन द्विजातिषु । चत्वार आश्रमा एव चातुराश्रम्यं तद्धर्मः कथितः, एवञ्चातुर्वर्ण्यक्रिया अपि पुंसः क्रियां षोडशसंस्काररूपाम्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Here “a person’s rites” means the sixteen saṃskāras by which one is established in varṇa and āśrama. The four āśramas and their dharmas, and likewise the duties of the four varṇas, have already been described; the text now turns to those sixteen saṃskāras, beginning with the rites commonly performed for the twice-born.

VP 3.10.2

नित्य-नैमित्तिकाः काम्याः क्रियाः पुंसाम् अशेषतः।
समाख्याहि भृगु-श्रेष्ठ सर्वज्ञो ह्य् असि मे मतः॥२॥

Tell me fully about the daily, occasional, and desire-motivated rites of human beings; I regard you as omniscient, O best of Bhṛgu’s line.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
नित्य-नैमित्तिकाः (nitya-naimittikāḥ) — daily and occasional
काम्याः क्रियाः (kāmyāḥ kriyāḥ) — desire-motivated rites
पुंसाम् (puṃsām) — of people
अशेषतः (aśeṣataḥ) — completely
समाख्याहि (samākhyāhi) — explain
भृगु-श्रेष्ठ (bhṛgu-śreṣṭha) — O best of Bhṛgu’s line
सर्व-ज्ञः (sarva-jñaḥ) — omniscient
असि (asi) — you are
मे मतः (me mataḥ) — in my view.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

nitya-naimittikāḥ kāmyāḥ kriyāḥ puṃsām aśeṣataḥ|samākhyāhi bhṛgu-śreṣṭha sarvajño hy asi me mataḥ||

Śrīdhara

nityāmaharahaḥ kriyamāṇāṃ pratyavāyaparihāraphalām, naimittikīṃ rāhudarśādau nimitte sati vihitānāmakaraṇāt pratyavāyapradāṃ kāmyāṃ kāmāya hitām agniṣṭomādikām

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नित्यामहरहः क्रियमाणां प्रत्यवायपरिहारफलाम्, नैमित्तिकीं राहुदर्शादौ निमित्ते सति विहितानामकरणात् प्रत्यवायप्रदां काम्यां कामाय हिताम् अग्निष्टोमादिकाम्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

A nitya rite is one performed every day, whose performance prevents the fault arising from omission. A naimittika rite is prescribed when a particular occasion occurs, such as an eclipse, and omission then produces a fault. A kāmya rite is undertaken for a desired result, such as the Agniṣṭoma.

VP 3.10.3

और्व उवाच— यद् एतद् उक्तं भवता नित्य-नैमित्तिकाश्रयम्।
तद् अहं कथयिष्यामि शृणुष्वैक-मना मम

You have asked about the basis of the daily and occasional rites. I shall explain it; listen to me with one-pointed attention.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
यत् एतत् उक्तम् (yat etat uktam) — what has been said
भवता (bhavatā) — by you
नित्य-नैमित्तिक-आश्रयम् (nitya-naimittika-āśrayam) — concerning the daily and occasional rites
तत् (tat) — that
अहम् कथयिष्यामि (aham kathayiṣyāmi) — I shall explain
शृणुष्व (śṛṇuṣva) — listen
एक-मनाः (eka-manāḥ) — with one-pointed mind

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yad etad uktaṃ bhavatā nitya-naimittikāśrayam|tad ahaṃ kathayiṣyāmi śṛṇuṣvaika-manā mama

Śrīdhara

nityanaimitikāśritam iti kāmyasyāpyupalakṣaṇam

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नित्यनैमितिकाश्रितम् इति काम्यस्याप्युपलक्षणम्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The expression “dependent on nitya and naimittika rites” also includes kāmya rites by implication.

VP 3.10.4

जातस्य जात-कर्मादि-क्रिया-काण्डम् अशेषतः।
पुत्रस्य कुर्वीत प्तिआ श्राद्धं चाभ्युदयात्मकम्॥४॥

For a newborn son, the father should perform the entire series of rites beginning with the birth ceremony, as well as the śrāddha performed for prosperity.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
जातस्य (jātasya) — of one born
जात-कर्म-आदि (jāta-karma-ādi) — beginning with the birth rite
क्रिया-काण्डम् (kriyā-kāṇḍam) — series of rites
अशेषतः (aśeṣataḥ) — completely
पुत्रस्य (putrasya) — of the son
कुर्वीत (kurvīta) — should perform
पिता (pitā) — the father
श्राद्धम् (śrāddham) — śrāddha
अभ्युदय-आत्मकम् (abhyudaya-ātmakam) — intended for prosperity.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

jātasya jāta-karmādi-kriyā-kāṇḍam aśeṣataḥ|putrasya kurvīta ptiā śrāddhaṃ cābhyudayātmakam||

Śrīdhara

jātasya putrasya jātakarma ca ādikriyākāṇḍañca janmanaḥ prācīnaṃ garbhādhānapuṃsavanādikriyāsamūhaṃ pitā kurvīta ityarthaḥ । taduktaṃ garbhādhānamṛtau puṃsaḥ savanaṃ spandanāt purā’ iti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

जातस्य पुत्रस्य जातकर्म च आदिक्रियाकाण्डञ्च जन्मनः प्राचीनं गर्भाधानपुंसवनादिक्रियासमूहं पिता कुर्वीत इत्यर्थः । तदुक्तं गर्भाधानमृतौ पुंसः सवनं स्पन्दनात् पुराऽ इति

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“Beginning with the birth-rite” includes both the jātakarma of the newborn son and the earlier group of rites performed before birth, such as garbhādhāna and puṃsavana. The father is to perform this whole series of rites.

VP 3.10.5

युग्मांस् तु प्राङ्-मुखान् विप्रान् भोजयेन् मनुजेश्वर।
यथा वृत्तिस् तथा कुर्याद् दैवं पित्र्यं द्विजन्मनाम्

He should feed twice-born men facing east in even numbers, O lord of men, and should perform the divine and ancestral rites so that they are satisfied.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
युग्मान् (yugmān) — even-numbered
प्राङ्-मुखान् (prāṅ-mukhān) — facing east
विप्रान् (viprān) — Brāhmaṇas
भोजयेत् (bhojayet) — should feed
मनुज-ईश्वर (manuja-īśvara) — O lord of men
यथा तृप्तिः (yathā tṛptiḥ) — as satisfaction
तथा कुर्यात् (tathā kuryāt) — should perform accordingly
दैवम् पित्र्यम् (daivam pitryam) — divine and ancestral rites
द्विजन्मनाम् (dvijanmanām) — for the twice-born.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yugmāṃs tu prāṅ-mukhān viprān bhojayen manujeśvara|yathā vṛttis tathā kuryād daivaṃ pitryaṃ dvijanmanām

Śrīdhara

dvijanmanāṃ brāhmaṇānāṃ yathāvṛtti yathopacāraṃ daivaṃ devasambandhikarma pitryaṃ pitṛsambandhikarma kuryāt । abhyudayaśrāddhoktena viśeṣeṇa pradakṣiṇopacārādinā daivapitryaśrāddhaṃ kuryāt ityarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

द्विजन्मनां ब्राह्मणानां यथावृत्ति यथोपचारं दैवं देवसम्बन्धिकर्म पित्र्यं पितृसम्बन्धिकर्म कुर्यात् । अभ्युदयश्राद्धोक्तेन विशेषेण प्रदक्षिणोपचारादिना दैवपित्र्यश्राद्धं कुर्यात् इत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The twice-born should perform the rites connected with the gods and the ancestors with the appropriate observances and offerings. In the abhyudaya-śrāddha this includes the special clockwise forms of service and the other prescribed details.

VP 3.10.6

दध्ना यवैः सबदरैर् मिश्रान् पिण्डान् मुदा युतः।
नान्दीमुखेभ्यस् तीर्थेन दद्याद् दैवेन पार्थिव॥६॥

With joy, he should offer to the Nandīmukhas balls mixed with curds, barley, and jujubes, using the prescribed sacred rite, O king.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
दध्ना (dadhnā) — with curds
यवैः (yavaiḥ) — with barley
सबदरैः (sabadaraiḥ) — with jujubes
मिश्रान् पिण्डान् (miśrān piṇḍān) — mixed balls
मुदा-युतः (mudā-yutaḥ) — filled with joy
नान्दी-मुखेभ्यः (nāndī-mukhebhyaḥ) — to the Nandīmukhas
तीर्थेन (tīrthena) — with the sacred rite
दद्यात् (dadyāt) — should give
दैवेन (daivena) — by the divine/right-hand rite

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

dadhnā yavaiḥ sabadarair miśrān piṇḍān mudā yutaḥ|nāndīmukhebhyas tīrthena dadyād daivena pārthiva||

Śrīdhara

tadevāha—dadhna iti । daivena karāgreṇa

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदेवाह—दध्न इति । दैवेन कराग्रेण

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

This is explained by the words beginning “with curd.” “By the divine tīrtha” means with the front or tip of the hand.

VP 3.10.7

प्राजापत्येन वा सर्वम् उपचारं प्रदक्षिणम्।
कुर्वीत तत् तथाशेष-वृद्धि-कालेषु भूपते

Then, at ceremonies of increase, O king, he should perform every offering and service in the proper order, using the Prajāpati rite.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
प्राजापत्येन (prājāpatyena) — with the Prajāpati rite
वा (vā) — or
सर्वम् उपचारम् (sarvam upacāram) — every service
प्रदक्षिणम् (pradakṣiṇam) — in the proper order
कुर्वीत (kurvīta) — should perform
तत् तथा (tat tathā) — so
अशेष-वृद्धि-कालेषु (aśeṣa-vṛddhi-kāleṣu) — at all ceremonies of increase
भूपते (bhūpate) — O king.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

prājāpatyena vā sarvam upacāraṃ pradakṣiṇam|kurvīta tat tathāśeṣa-vṛddhi-kāleṣu bhūpate

Śrīdhara

prājāpatyena kaniṣṭhāmūlena । taduktam— kaniṣṭhādeśinyaṅguṣṭhamūlānyagraṃ karasya ca । prajāpatipitṛ-brahma-devatīrthānyanukramāt । iti । aśeṣavṛddhikāleṣu kanyā putravivāheṣvityādinā vakṣyamāṇeṣu

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्राजापत्येन कनिष्ठामूलेन । तदुक्तम्— कनिष्ठादेशिन्यङ्गुष्ठमूलान्यग्रं करस्य च । प्रजापतिपितृ-ब्रह्म-देवतीर्थान्यनुक्रमात् । इति । अशेषवृद्धिकालेषु कन्या पुत्रविवाहेष्वित्यादिना वक्ष्यमाणेषु

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“By the Prājāpatya tīrtha” means at the root of the little finger. The cited rule assigns, in order, the hand-tīrthas of Prajāpati, the Pitṛs, Brahmā and the gods. The phrase “on every occasion of increase” refers to such occasions as the marriages of daughters and sons that are described later.

VP 3.10.8

ततश् च नाम कुर्वीत पितैव दशमेऽहनि।
देव-पूर्वं नराख्यं हि शर्म-वर्मादि-संयुतम्

On the tenth day the father should give the child a name, beginning with a divine name and ending in a human one, joined with Śarman, Varman, and the like.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
ततः (tataḥ) — then
नाम कुर्वीत (nāma kurvīta) — should give a name
पिता एव (pitā eva) — the father himself
दशमे अहनि (daśame ahani) — on the tenth day
देव-पूर्वम् (deva-pūrvam) — beginning with a divine name
नर-आख्यम् (nara-ākhyam) — a human name
शर्म-वर्म-आदि-संयुतम् (śarma-varma-ādi-saṃyutam) — joined with Śarman, Varman, and the like.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tataś ca nāma kurvīta pitaiva daśame'hani|deva-pūrvaṃ narākhyaṃ hi śarma-varmādi-saṃyutam

Śrīdhara

daśame’hani atīta iti śeṣaḥ । taccāśaucāntopalakṣaṇam । atraiva kālāntaramapyāha yathā— nāmadheyaṃ daśamyāñca kecidicchanti pārthiva । dvādaśyāmathavā rājyāṃ māse pūrṇe tathāpare । iti । devapūrvaṃ kuladevatānāmapūrvakaṃ kuladevatāsambaddhaṃ nāma kuryād iti śaṅkhokteḥ । narākhyaṃ puruṣavācakam । tatra prānte śarmavarmādisaṃyutaṃ yathā somaśarmā indravarmā, candravarmā, candraguptaḥ śivadāsaḥ ityādi

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

दशमेऽहनि अतीत इति शेषः । तच्चाशौचान्तोपलक्षणम् । अत्रैव कालान्तरमप्याह यथा— नामधेयं दशम्याञ्च केचिदिच्छन्ति पार्थिव । द्वादश्यामथवा राज्यां मासे पूर्णे तथापरे । इति । देवपूर्वं कुलदेवतानामपूर्वकं कुलदेवतासम्बद्धं नाम कुर्याद् इति शङ्खोक्तेः । नराख्यं पुरुषवाचकम् । तत्र प्रान्ते शर्मवर्मादिसंयुतं यथा सोमशर्मा इन्द्रवर्मा, चन्द्रवर्मा, चन्द्रगुप्तः शिवदासः इत्यादि

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“On the tenth day” has “having passed” understood after it; this indicates the end of the period of birth-impurity. Another permitted time is also stated here: some prescribe naming on the tenth day, O king; others on the twelfth, or when a month is complete. “Beginning with a divine name” means that the name should be preceded by, or connected with, the name of the family deity, according to the cited statement of Śaṅkha. “Narākhya” means a name denoting a person. At its end it should be joined with Śarman, Varman, and the like, as in Somaśarman, Indravarman, Candravarman, Candragupta, Śivadāsa, and so on.

The eight forms of marriage are said to be Brāhma, Daiva, Ārṣa, Prajāpatya, Āsura, Gāndharva, Rākṣasa, and Paiśāca.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara

tameva nyāyyaṃ vidhi darśayitum aṣṭau vivāhānāha—brāhma iti । yathā, brāhmo vivāha āhūya dīyate śaktyalaṅkṛtā । yajñasya ṛtvije daiva ādāyārṣastu godvayam । sahobhau caratāṃ dharmaṃ prājāpatyo vidhiḥ smṛtaḥ । āsuro draviṇādānād gāndharvaḥ samayānmithaḥ । rākṣaso yuddhaharaṇāt paiśācaḥ kanyakācchalāt । iti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तमेव न्याय्यं विधि दर्शयितुम् अष्टौ विवाहानाह—ब्राह्म इति । यथा, ब्राह्मो विवाह आहूय दीयते शक्त्यलङ्कृता । यज्ञस्य ऋत्विजे दैव आदायार्षस्तु गोद्वयम् । सहोभौ चरतां धर्मं प्राजापत्यो विधिः स्मृतः । आसुरो द्रविणादानाद् गान्धर्वः समयान्मिथः । राक्षसो युद्धहरणात् पैशाचः कन्यकाच्छलात् । इति

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

To explain the lawful forms of marriage, the text enumerates eight. The cited definition says: Brāhma is giving an adorned bride after inviting the bridegroom; Daiva is giving her to the officiating priest of a sacrifice; Ārṣa involves receiving a pair of cattle; Prājāpatya is given with the injunction that the couple perform dharma together; Āsura is based on the giving of wealth; Gāndharva on mutual agreement; Rākṣasa on seizure in battle; and Paiśāca on deceit toward the maiden.

Śrīdhara

eteṣām iti । yadāha devalaḥ—ādyā vivāhaścatvāro dharmyāstoyapradānikāḥ । aśulkā brāhmaṇārhāśca tārayanti dvayoḥ kulam । gāndharva-rākṣasau rājña āsuro vaiśya-śūdrayoḥ । sa pāpiṣṭho vivāhānāṃ paiśācaḥ prathito’ṣṭamaḥ iti। antyam paiśācam

एतेषाम् इति । यदाह देवलः—आद्या विवाहश्चत्वारो धर्म्यास्तोयप्रदानिकाः । अशुल्का ब्राह्मणार्हाश्च तारयन्ति द्वयोः कुलम् । गान्धर्व-राक्षसौ राज्ञ आसुरो वैश्य-शूद्रयोः । स पापिष्ठो विवाहानां पैशाचः प्रथितोऽष्टमः इति। अन्त्यम् पैशाचम्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Devala is cited to distinguish the forms: the first four marriages are righteous, accompanied by the ceremonial gift of water, without a bride-price, suitable for Brāhmaṇas, and said to benefit both families. Gāndharva and Rākṣasa are assigned to kings, Āsura to Vaiśyas and Śūdras, while Paiśāca is described as the most blameworthy and is counted as the eighth form.
