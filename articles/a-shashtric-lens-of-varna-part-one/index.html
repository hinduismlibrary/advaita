Invocation (Maṅgalācaraṇam)

॥ मङ्गलाचरणम् ॥

Show Sanskrit

अथातः संप्रवक्ष्यामि सद्वर्णार्थप्रकाशिनीम्। नत्वा श्वेताम्बरीं पुण्यां वीणापुस्तकधारिणीम्॥
गजवक्त्रं गणेशं च देवं शिवशिवात्मजम्। विष्णुरूपं तथा व्यासं विष्णुं च व्यासरूपिणम्॥

“Now I shall set forth Sadvarṇārthaprakāśinī, having bowed to the auspicious one clothed in white and holding a vīṇā and book; to elephant-faced Ganesha, the divine son of Shiva; and to Vyāsa, whose form is Vishnu, and Vishnu, whose form is Vyāsa.”

1. Preface

1.1 Introduction

One of the most misunderstood and propagandised topics in Hinduism is the varna system, often incorrectly called the “caste system” in English. Because it is a politically charged subject, people hold many different views and cite pragmatic concerns, tradition, history, and numerous other sources to support their claims.

Lord Krishna, however, states in the Gita:

Show Sanskrit

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः। न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम्॥ २३॥
तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ। ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि॥ २४॥

The Supreme Lord said: “He who discards scriptural procedure and acts according to his own wishes attains neither success, happiness, nor the highest goal. Therefore, scripture is your basis for determining what is and is not to be done. Having understood the scriptural injunction, it befits you to perform actions in this world.”

Bhagavad Gita 16.23–24

Varna concerns what is and is not to be done (karyakaryavyavasthiti). Therefore, scripture (shastra) alone is the pramana, or authoritative basis; nothing else can be an independent authority.

Two major sides nevertheless dominate the debate: varna by karma (action) and varna by janma (birth). Both appear to possess extensive scriptural support. To an outsider, the Hindu scriptures may therefore seem contradictory, but they are not.

The problem is that both sides tend to use shastra as an ad hoc justification for a predetermined view instead of attempting to understand the full scriptural position. Each side ignores the pramanas presented by the other rather than undertaking samanvaya, or synthesis. Samanvaya seeks an understanding that does not discard any scriptural injunction. This article therefore synthesises the scriptural references on varna and attempts to establish the orthodox Hindu position.

1.2 On pramāṇa

Because this is a matter of scripturally enjoined dharma, shastra alone is the pramana. This is stated in the second Mimamsa Sutra:

Show Sanskrit

चोदनालक्षणोऽर्थो धर्मः॥ १.१.२॥

“That which is enjoined by a statement of scripture is dharma.”

Mimamsa Sutra 1.1.2

Shastra includes the Vedas, whose four divisions are the Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, and Upanishad. The Vedas are interpreted with the aid of the Vedangas, particularly grammar, prosody, and etymology. Although the Vedas are the highest scriptural authority, the itihasas—the Ramayana and Mahabharata—and the eighteen Puranas assist in their proper interpretation. Mimamsa, the science of hermeneutics, supplies principles for interpreting scripture.

The interpretations of bona fide acaryas of the past will also be used as supporting evidence, but an acarya’s words are not an independent pramana. The Yajnavalkya Shiksha, a Vedanga text, states:

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात्॥

“One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.”

Yajnavalkya Shiksha

The Yoga Vasishtha, attributed to Valmiki, likewise states:

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि। अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना॥ ३॥

“A reasonable statement should be accepted even when spoken by a child. An unreasonable statement should be discarded like a piece of straw, even if it was spoken by lotus-born Brahma himself.”

Yoga Vasishtha 2.18.3

The Taittiriya Aranyaka further states:

Show Sanskrit

यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि। नो इतराणि॥ यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि। नो इतराणि॥

“Only those actions of ours that are unobjectionable should be followed, not the others. Only those good deeds of ours should be emulated, not the others.”

Taittiriya Aranyaka

Thus, even the statements and conduct of acaryas remain subject to scripture and must be rejected when they conflict with it. Scripture alone is an authority in its own right. In the final section, which concerns the practical implementation of varna, pratyaksha, or empirical evidence, will also be considered.

1.3 Summary view

The conclusion reached through a synthesis of all the scriptures, and demonstrated in this article, is as follows:

  1. There are two forms of varna: jati, or aupadhika varna, acquired through birth into a particular family; and guna-based, or svabhavika varna, arising from a person’s qualities.
  2. A person who belongs to one varna by birth can enter another on the basis of qualities or the absence of them. Thus, a shudra may become a brahmana, a brahmana may fall to the status of a shudra, and other changes are likewise possible.
  3. For these reasons, varna is predominantly based on guna, while birth-based jati-varna is secondary.

2. Varṇa by janma (jāti)

One error made by advocates of varna exclusively by qualities and actions is their denial that varna has any relation to jati. Although the predominant scriptural position supports varna by qualities, a system based solely on qualities cannot account for the numerous scriptural passages that speak of varna by birth. These must be considered first.

2.1 Indirect pramāṇa

There are first several indirect difficulties. Passages such as Bhagavad Gita 1.41 and portions of the Mahabharata and Manusmrti Chapter 10 describe varnasankaras, or mixed descendants of different varnas, as highly discouraged and outside ordinary varnashrama. An exclusively karma-based theory cannot explain this. Some organisations therefore mistranslate varnasankara in Gita 1.41 as “unwanted progeny,” avoiding the implications of the actual expression, “admixture of varnas.”

Other passages, such as Manusmrti 2.36, prescribe different ages of initiation for different varnas. Such rules are likewise inexplicable if birth-based varna is rejected altogether.

2.2 Direct pramāṇa

There are also explicit references. The Manusmrti states:

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यस्त्रयो वर्णा द्विजातयः। चतुर्थ एकजातिस्तु शूद्रो नास्ति तु पञ्चमः॥ ४॥
सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु। आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते॥ ५॥

“The brahmana, kshatriya, and vaishya are the three twice-born varnas; the fourth, the shudra, is once-born, and there is no fifth. Among all varnas, those born in the natural order from wives of equal status are to be regarded as belonging to the same jati as their fathers.”

Manusmrti 10.4–5

The Padma Purana similarly states:

Show Sanskrit

जन्मना ब्राह्मणो ज्ञेयः संस्कारैर्द्विज उच्यते॥ १२९॥

“By birth he is known as a brahmana, and through the initiatory rites he is called a dvija, or twice-born.”

Padma Purana 7.47.129

Finally, the Chandogya Upanishad, which is shruti, states:

Show Sanskrit

तद्य इह रमणीयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते रमणीयां योनिमापद्येरन् ब्राह्मणयोनिं वा क्षत्रिययोनिं वा वैश्ययोनिं वा। अथ य इह कपूयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते कपूयां योनिमापद्येरन् श्वयोनिं वा सूकरयोनिं वा चण्डालयोनिं वा॥

“Those whose conduct in this world was good quickly attain a good birth: birth as a brahmana, kshatriya, or vaishya. Those whose conduct was evil quickly attain an evil birth: birth as a dog, a pig, or a candala.”

Chandogya Upanishad 5.10.7

The word used is yoni. Brahmana, kshatriya, vaishya, and candala are all called yonis. The word generally refers to a class determined by birth, comparable here to the animal species named alongside them.

Many advocates of varna by birth stop at this point. Having shown that “brahmana” and “shudra” can name a yoni acquired at birth, they treat the question as settled. The matter, however, is not so simple. Even one born in a particular yoni may acquire another varna through qualities and actions, as the next section will demonstrate.

3. Change in varṇa

The primary aim of this treatise is to establish varna by qualities on the basis of scriptural evidence. We shall therefore begin with passages showing that the varna acquired at birth may change according to a person’s qualities.

3.1 Introductory pramāṇas

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रयोनौ हि जातस्य सद्गुणानुपतिष्ठतः। वैश्यत्वं लभते ब्रह्मन् क्षत्रियत्वं तथैव च॥ आर्जवे वर्तमानस्य ब्राह्मण्यमभिजायते॥

“O brahmana, one born in a shudra yoni who possesses good qualities may attain vaishyahood or, likewise, kshatriyahood; one who lives in rectitude may attain even brahmanahood.” The expression is explicitly shudrayonau, “in a shudra yoni.” Birth in the yoni of one varna therefore does not prevent the acquisition of another through qualities.

Mahabharata 3.203.11

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मणः पतनीयेषु वर्तमानो विकर्मसु। दाम्भिको दुष्कृतप्रायः शूद्रेण सदृशो भवेत्॥ ११॥
यस्तु शूद्रो दमे सत्ये धर्मे च सततोत्थितः। तं ब्राह्मणमहं मन्ये वृत्तेन हि भवेद्द्विजः॥ १२॥

“A brahmana who engages in degrading and improper actions, who is hypocritical and habitually commits evil deeds, becomes like a shudra. By contrast, a shudra who remains established in self-restraint, truth, and dharma I regard as a brahmana, for one becomes a dvija through conduct.” Both elevation and degradation are thus connected to qualities.

Mahabharata 3.215.11

3.2 Varṇa according to Śiva

In a conversation on varna found identically in the Mahabharata and the Brahma Purana, Shiva tells Parvati:

Show Sanskrit

वैश्यकर्म च यो विप्रो लोभमोहव्यपाश्रयः। ब्राह्मण्यं दुर्लभं प्राप्य करोत्यल्पमतिः सदा॥ १६॥
स द्विजो वैश्यतामेति वैश्यो वा शूद्रतामियात्। स्वधर्मात्प्रच्युतो विप्रस्ततः शूद्रत्वमाप्नुयात्॥ १७॥

“A brahmana who, overcome by greed or delusion, continually performs the work of a vaishya after attaining the rare state of brahmanahood—that foolish dvija becomes a vaishya. A vaishya may likewise become a shudra, and a brahmana who falls from his own dharma may attain shudrahood.”

Mahābhārata 13.131.16–17; Brahma Purāṇa 115

Show Sanskrit

न योनिर्नापि संस्कारो न श्रुतिर्न च सन्ततिः। कारणानि द्विजत्वस्य वृत्तमेव तु कारणम्॥ ५६॥
सर्वोऽयं ब्राह्मणो लोके वृत्तेन तु विधीयते। वृत्ते स्थितस्तु शूद्रोऽपि ब्राह्मणत्वं च गच्छति॥ ५७॥

“Neither yoni, initiatory rites, learning, nor lineage is the cause of dvijahood; conduct alone is its cause. Brahmana status in this world is determined by conduct. Even a shudra who abides in such conduct attains brahmanahood.” Yoni is therefore not the sole cause: a shudra may rise through conduct, while a brahmana may fall by abandoning his duties.

Mahābhārata 13.131.56–57; Brahma Purāṇa 115

3.3 Varṇa according to Nārada

When Yudhishthira asks Narada about varnashrama in Shrimad Bhagavatam 7.11, Narada presents the teaching not as his own opinion, but as what he heard from Narayana:

Show Sanskrit

नत्वा भगवतेऽजाय लोकानां धर्मसेतवे। वक्ष्ये सनातनं धर्मं नारायणमुखाच्छ्रुतम्॥ ५॥

“Having bowed to the unborn Lord, the guardian of dharma for all worlds, I shall explain sanatana-dharma as heard from the mouth of Narayana.”

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 7.11.5

The chapter then defines the characteristics of each varna:

Show Sanskrit

शमो दमस्तपः शौचं सन्तोषः क्षान्तिरार्जवम्। ज्ञानं दयाच्युतात्मत्वं सत्यं च ब्रह्मलक्षणम्॥ २१॥
शौर्यं वीर्यं धृतिस्तेजस्त्यागश्चात्मजयः क्षमा। ब्रह्मण्यता प्रसादश्च सत्यं च क्षत्रलक्षणम्॥ २२॥
देवगुर्वच्युते भक्तिस्त्रिवर्गपरिपोषणम्। आस्तिक्यमुद्यमो नित्यं नैपुण्यं वैश्यलक्षणम्॥ २३॥
शूद्रस्य सन्नतिः शौचं सेवा स्वामिन्यमायया। अमन्त्रयज्ञो ह्यस्तेयं सत्यं गोविप्ररक्षणम्॥ २४॥

The characteristics of a brahmana are control of mind and senses, austerity, purity, contentment, patience, rectitude, knowledge, compassion, devotion to Acyuta, and truthfulness. Those of a kshatriya are heroism, strength, fortitude, splendour, generosity, self-mastery, forgiveness, devotion to brahmanas, graciousness, and truth. Those of a vaishya are devotion to the gods, guru, and Acyuta; support of the three aims of life; faith; enterprise; and skill. Those of a shudra are humility, purity, sincere service of the master, sacrifice without Vedic mantras, non-stealing, truthfulness, and protection of cows and brahmanas.

At the end of the chapter, Narada gives the rule by which these characteristics are to be applied:

Show Sanskrit

यस्य यल्लक्षणं प्रोक्तं पुंसो वर्णाभिव्यञ्जकम्। यदन्यत्रापि दृश्येत तत्तेनैव विनिर्दिशेत्॥ ३५॥

“Whatever characteristic has been described as indicating a man’s varna, if it is seen elsewhere, he should be designated according to that very characteristic.”

Shridharacarya comments:

Show Sanskrit

शमादिभिरेव ब्राह्मणादिव्यवहारो मुख्यो न जातिमात्रादित्याह—यस्येति। यद्यदन्यत्र वर्णान्तरेऽपि दृश्येत तेनैव लक्षणनिमित्तेनैव वर्णेन विनिर्दिशेत्। न तु जातिनिमित्तेनेत्यर्थः॥

“The principal basis for designating someone a brahmana and so forth is the qualities beginning with shama, not birth alone. If a characteristic is found elsewhere, in another varna, that person should be designated by the varna of which that characteristic is the cause, not on the basis of jati.” The operative cause is thus lakshana, not birth.

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 7.11.21–24, 35; Śrīdhara commentary

3.4 Degradation in varṇa

Show Sanskrit

यस्य कायगतं ब्रह्म मद्येनाप्लाव्यते सकृत्। तस्य व्यपैति ब्राह्मण्यं शूद्रत्वं च स गच्छति॥ ९७॥

“When the Brahman residing in a man’s body is once flooded with intoxicating liquor, his brahmanahood departs and he becomes a shudra.”

Manusmrti 11.97

Show Sanskrit

सद्यः पतति मांसेन लाक्षया लवणेन च। त्र्यहेण शूद्रो भवति ब्राह्मणः क्षीरविक्रयात्॥ ९२॥

“By selling meat, lac, or salt, a brahmana becomes an outcaste at once; by selling milk, a brahmana becomes a shudra in three days.”

Manusmrti 10.92

Show Sanskrit

सन्ध्यां नोपासते यस्तु ब्राह्मणो हि विशेषतः। स जीवन्नेव शूद्रः स्यान्मृतः श्वा चैव जायते॥ ७८॥

“One who does not perform the daily sandhya—especially if he is a brahmana—becomes a shudra while living and is born as a dog after death.”

Daksha Smriti 2.18

A member of the three upper varnas who is not initiated at the prescribed time is described as fallen, a vratya or dvijabandhu. Shridhara defines the latter:

Show Sanskrit

द्विजबन्धवो यथोचितकालसंस्कारादिरहितास्त्रैवर्णिका भासाः॥

“Dvijabandhus are semblances of members of the three higher varnas who lack the proper samskaras at the prescribed time.” Grammatically, dvijabandhu is a tatpurusha compound meaning “a relative of the dvijas,” implying one who is merely connected with them rather than truly belonging among them.

One might object that statements about losing varna are non-literal arthavada, meaning only that a person becomes “like a shudra.” Even if that reading were granted, it would still show that qualities and conduct take precedence over birth. Moreover, the following passage cannot be reduced in the same way:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रो राजन् भवति ब्रह्मबन्धुर्दुश्चरित्रो यश्च धर्मादपेतः॥

“O king, a brahmabandhu who is of wicked conduct and has departed from dharma becomes a shudra.” If brahmabandhu already meant merely “like a shudra,” then to read this as another mere likeness would render it pointless. A person born a brahmana may first become a fallen brahmabandhu through lack of initiation and then become a shudra through reprehensible conduct.

Mahabharata 12.63.4

3.5 A pramāṇa concerning the right to the Vedas

A further objection is that when someone born in one jati acquires another varna through qualities or action, the acquisition is only figurative: he does not receive the actual rights of that varna, just as a powerful king called a lion does not literally become one. Shrimad Bhagavatam answers this directly:

Show Sanskrit

यन्नामधेयश्रवणानुकीर्तनाद्यत्प्रह्वणाद्यत्स्मरणादपि क्वचित्। श्वादोऽपि सद्यः सवनाय कल्पते कुतः पुनस्ते भगवन्नु दर्शनात्॥ ६॥

“By hearing or chanting whose name, bowing to whom, or even once remembering whom, a dog-eater immediately becomes eligible for the Soma rite—what, then, can be said of directly seeing You, O Lord?”

Shrimad Bhagavatam 3.33.6

Vallabhacarya explains:

Show Sanskrit

श्वादोऽपि चाण्डालोऽपि सद्यः सवनाय सोमाभिषवयोग्यत्वाय सद्यस्तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि अवकल्पते योग्यो भवति॥

“Even a dog-eater—that is, a candala—becomes eligible for the Soma pressing immediately, in that very birth.”

A word-by-word (dalakrtya) analysis removes the possible ambiguities:

  • shvado’pi, “even a dog-eating candala,” includes the lowest jati and therefore necessarily includes those above it. Vallabha writes: श्वादश्चाण्डालो नामाधमः। उत्कृष्टस्योत्तमत्वे कः सन्देह इत्यपि शब्दार्थः—“The dog-eating candala is the lowest; what doubt, then, can there be concerning those who are higher?”
  • sadyah, “immediately,” rules out the claim that the change occurs only in a future birth. Vallabha glosses it as तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि, “in that very birth.”
  • savanaya refers specifically to the Vedic Soma rite, not merely to a Puranic or Agamic sacrifice that might also be called a yajna.
  • kalpate, “becomes eligible,” establishes a genuine entitlement. Soma rites require upanayana, and upanayana belongs to the three upper varnas. Eligibility for Soma therefore entails eligibility for initiation and acquisition of the corresponding higher varna.

The objection that such a result is impossible and must therefore be mere praise of the divine name is also answered by Vallabha:

Show Sanskrit

अधर्मसंस्कृतभूतविशेषैर्हि चाण्डालादिशरीरमारभ्यते; सर्वोत्कृष्टधर्मसंस्कृतभूतविशेषैस्तु ब्राह्मणशरीरम्। भगवन्नाम्ना दीक्षया प्राप्तेन वा सर्वान् दोषानौत्पत्तिकान् परिहृत्य, तानि भूतानि उत्कृष्टसंस्कारेण संस्कृतानि क्रियन्त इति नाम्नो युक्तमेव माहात्म्यम्। अनेन सर्वदोषनाशनपूर्वकसर्वगुणोपपादकत्वं नाम्न उक्तम्॥

A candala body results from deeply adharmic impressions, while a brahmana body results from highly dharmic ones. The Lord’s name, received through initiation, destroys the former faults and establishes excellent samskaras. This is therefore a fitting glory of the name: it removes all faults and produces all virtues. Jiva Gosvami and other acaryas likewise accept that this verse grants brahmanical eligibility to one born even as a candala.

Vallabha then cites Valmiki as an example:

Show Sanskrit

स्मरणस्य तु वाल्मीकेः। स हि चाण्डाल एव मार्गघातकः, ऋषेरुपदेशात्पूर्वं महर्षिभावनां कृत्वा पश्चाद्रामस्मरणेन तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि वाल्मीकिर्जात इति॥

“Regarding remembrance, consider Valmiki. He was a candala highway robber, but through the instruction of a rshi and remembrance of Rama he became Maharshi Valmiki in that very birth.”

4. Varṇa by qualities

It might still be argued that changes of varna are exceptions and that the varna system remains primarily birth-based. The following scriptural conversations address the ideal basis of varna directly.

4.1 Bhṛgu and Bharadvāja

In Mahabharata, Vana Parva 188–189 in the Critical Edition, Bharadvaja asks Bhrgu about varna. The well-known commentary of Nilakantha is used here.

Issues with jāti

Bharadvaja asks:

Show Sanskrit

चातुर्वर्ण्यस्य वर्णेन यदि वर्णो विभज्यते। सर्वेषां खलु वर्णानां दृश्यते वर्णसङ्करः॥
कामः क्रोधो भयं लोभः शोकश्चिन्ता क्षुधा श्रमः। सर्वेषां नः प्रभवति कस्माद्वर्णो विभज्यते॥

“If the four varnas are divided according to jati-varna, admixture is nevertheless seen among all of them. Desire, anger, fear, greed, grief, anxiety, hunger, and fatigue arise in everyone. How, then, is varna divided?” Bodily characteristics are likewise common to all. Nilakantha summarises the difficulty: मनुष्येष्वपि न जातिभेदनिश्चय इत्यर्थः—“Among human beings there is no certain determination of distinctions of jati.” Jati is therefore an insufficient basis, especially where extensive intermixture has occurred.

Origin of the varṇas

Bhrgu replies:

Show Sanskrit

कामभोगप्रियास्तीक्ष्णाः क्रोधनाः प्रियसाहसाः। त्यक्तस्वधर्मा रक्ताङ्गास्ते द्विजाः क्षत्रतां गताः॥
गोषु वृत्तिं समाधाय पीताः कृष्युपजीविनः। स्वधर्मान्नानुतिष्ठन्ति ते द्विजा वैश्यतां गताः॥
हिंसानृतप्रिया लुब्धाः सर्वकर्मोपजीविनः। कृष्णाः शौचपरिभ्रष्टास्ते द्विजाः शूद्रतां गताः॥
इत्येतैः कर्मभिर्व्यस्ता द्विजा वर्णान्तरं गताः। धर्मो यज्ञक्रिया चैषां नित्यं न प्रतिषिध्यते॥

“Originally there was no distinction among the varnas; this whole world was brahmana. The brahmanas created at the beginning attained different varnas through their actions. Those fond of sensual pleasure, fierce and irascible, who delighted in valour and abandoned their own dharma, became kshatriyas. Those who adopted cattle-rearing and agriculture became vaishyas. Those fond of violence and falsehood, greedy, living by every kind of labour, and fallen from purity became shudras. In this way, through their actions, the dvijas entered other varnas; Vedic dharma and sacrifice were not invariably forbidden to them.”

The colours in this account signify gunas: sattva for brahmanas, rajas for kshatriyas, rajas mixed with tamas for vaishyas, and tamas for shudras. The passage explains why no physical difference decisively separates human jati groups: the distinction is one of qualities and actions. The Purusha Sukta’s description of the varnas as the mouth, arms, thighs, and feet of the cosmic Person is not a competing biological account. Medhatithi interprets the corresponding passage in Manusmrti as commendatory language expressing relative functions and status. Bhrgu’s answer therefore establishes guna and karma as the original and ideal basis of varna.

4.2 Yudhiṣṭhira on varṇa

Qualities as the basis of varṇa

In Mahabharata, Vana Parva 180, a serpent asks Yudhishthira who becomes a brahmana. Yudhishthira answers:

Show Sanskrit

सत्यं दानं क्षमा शीलमानृशंस्यं दमो घृणा। दृश्यन्ते यत्र नागेन्द्र स ब्राह्मण इति स्मृतः॥

“Where truthfulness, charity, forgiveness, good conduct, compassion, self-control, and mercy are found, O king of serpents, that person is remembered as a brahmana.”

Mahābhārata 3.180.4

The serpent objects that truth, charity, freedom from anger, nonviolence, and compassion may also occur in a shudra. Nilakantha explains that the serpent is treating “brahmana” as birth alone; in fact, if a shudra possesses these qualities, he too is a brahmana. Yudhishthira replies with the same decisive verse used by Bhrgu:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रे चैतद्भवेल्लक्ष्यं द्विजे तच्च न विद्यते। न वै शूद्रो भवेच्छूद्रो ब्राह्मणो न च ब्राह्मणः॥
यत्रैतल्लक्ष्यते सर्प वृत्तं स ब्राह्मणः स्मृतः। यत्रैतन्न भवेत्सर्प तं शूद्रमिति निर्दिशेत्॥

“If these characteristics occur in a shudra and are absent in a dvija, then that shudra is not a shudra, nor is that brahmana a brahmana. Where this conduct is seen, O serpent, that person is regarded as a brahmana; where it is absent, he should be designated a shudra.” Nilakantha is unequivocal: a shudra endowed with shama and the other virtues is a brahmana, while a brahmana endowed with lust and similar defects is a shudra.

Primacy of qualities over birth

The serpent understands the implication: “If a brahmana is determined by conduct, birth is useless until the requisite conduct is seen.” Yudhishthira does not deny this. Instead, he explains why birth is an unreliable test:

Show Sanskrit

जातिरत्र महासर्प मनुष्यत्वे महामते। सङ्करात्सर्ववर्णानां दुष्परीक्ष्येति मे मतिः॥
सर्वे सर्वास्वपत्यानि जनयन्ति यदा नराः। वाङ्मैथुनमथो जन्म मरणं च समं नृणाम्॥
इदमार्षं प्रमाणं च ये यजामह इत्यपि। तस्माच्छीलं प्रधानेष्टं विदुर्ये तत्त्वदर्शिनः॥

“Among human beings, jati is difficult to examine because all varnas have intermixed. Men produce offspring with women of every group; speech, intercourse, birth, and death are common to all. The Vedic expression ‘we who sacrifice’ is also authority for this. Therefore, those who see truth know conduct to be the principal criterion.” The account of Satyakama Jabali in the Chandogya Upanishad illustrates the same principle: although the boy did not know his paternal lineage, a sage accepted him as a brahmana solely because of his truthfulness.

Nilakantha explains the cited Vedic evidence:

Show Sanskrit

तथा च श्रुतिः—“न चैतद्विद्मो ब्राह्मणाः स्मो वयमब्राह्मणा वा” इति ब्राह्मण्ये संशयमुपन्यस्यति। अत्र “ये यजामहे” इत्यनेन च ये वयं स्मो ब्राह्मणा वा अब्राह्मणा वा ते वयं यजामहे इति ब्राह्मण्येऽनवधारणं दर्शितम्॥

“Shruti states, ‘We do not know whether we are brahmanas or non-brahmanas,’ presenting doubt concerning brahmanahood. ‘We who sacrifice’ likewise means, ‘Whatever we may be—brahmanas or non-brahmanas—we sacrifice,’ showing that brahmanahood is not conclusively ascertained.”

“The varṇas, which are only four in number, were created by Me, the Supreme Lord. 4.5 Vajrasūci Upaniṣad

Rejection of jāti

The Vajrasuci Upanishad asks: को वा ब्राह्मणो नाम किं जीवः किं देहः किं जातिः किं ज्ञानं किं कर्म किं धार्मिक इति—“Who is called a brahmana? Is it the jiva, body, birth, knowledge, action, or religiosity?” It rejects each insufficient criterion in turn. On birth, it states:

Show Sanskrit

तर्हि जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न। तत्र जात्यन्तरजन्तुष्वनेकजातिसम्भवात् महर्षयो बहवः सन्ति। ऋष्यशृङ्गो मृग्याः, कौशिकः कुशात्, जाम्बूको जाम्बूकात्, वाल्मीको वाल्मीकात्, व्यासः कैवर्तकन्यकायाम्, शशपृष्ठात् गौतमः, वसिष्ठ उर्वश्याम्, अगस्त्यः कलशे जात इति श्रुतत्वात्। एतेषां जात्या विनाप्यग्रे ज्ञानप्रतिपादिता ऋषयो बहवः सन्ति। तस्मान्न जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति॥

“If it is said that birth makes a brahmana, that is not so. Many great rshis arose from other classes of beings: Rshyashrnga from a doe, Kaushika from kusha grass, Jambuka from a jackal, Valmiki from an anthill, Vyasa from a fisherwoman’s daughter, Gautama from the back of a hare, Vasishtha from Urvashi, and Agastya from a jar. Although lacking brahmana birth, these many became rshis through supreme knowledge. Therefore, birth does not make a brahmana.” Valmiki and Vyasa, authors of the two itihasas, are especially significant human examples.

Show Sanskrit

शमदमादिसम्पन्नो भावमात्सर्यतृष्णाशामोहादिरहितो दम्भाहङ्कारादिभिरसंस्पृष्टचेताः समस्तलक्षणो यः स एव ब्राह्मण इति श्रुतिस्मृतीतिहासपुराणानामभिप्रायः। अन्यथा हि ब्राह्मणत्वसिद्धिर्नास्त्येव॥

“One endowed with shama, dama, and the other virtues, free from attachment, jealousy, craving, delusion, and the like, and whose mind is untouched by hypocrisy and ego—he alone is a brahmana. This is the purport of shruti, smrti, itihasa, and Purana. Brahmanahood is certainly not established otherwise.”

5. Examples of change in varṇa

The preceding sections established what the scriptures prescribe. We now turn to what they describe: examples of how varna operated in scriptural narratives. Vyasa and Valmiki have already been mentioned; the following cases provide additional evidence.

5.1 Pṛṣadhra

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 9.2 and Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.1 describe Manu’s lineage in similar terms. The Viṣṇu Purāṇa gives this sequence:

Show Sanskrit

ब्रह्मा भगवान् प्रागभूत्। ब्रह्मणश्च दक्षिणाङ्गुष्ठजन्मा दक्षप्रजापतिः। दक्षस्याप्यदितिरदितेर्विवस्वान् विवस्वतो मनुः॥ ६॥

“Brahmā came to be first. From Brahmā’s right thumb, Dakṣa Prajāpati was born. Dakṣa’s daughter was Aditi; from Aditi came Vivasvān, and from Vivasvān came Vaivasvata Manu.”

Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.1.6

The treatise identifies Dakṣa as a brāhmaṇa and notes that Manu’s descendants include kṣatriya kings such as Ikṣvāku and Kārūṣa. It presents the lineage as a transition from brāhmaṇa to kṣatriya, consistent with Bhṛgu’s account.

Show Sanskrit

पृषध्रस्तु मनुपुत्रो गुरुगोवधाच्छूद्रत्वमगमत्॥

“Pṛṣadhra, son of Manu, became a śūdra because he killed his guru’s cow.”

The Bhāgavatam explains that Pṛṣadhra was guarding his guru’s cows when a tiger attacked at night. In the darkness, he mistook a cow for the tiger and cut off its head:

“Seeing the tiger, he hastily struck with his sword. Although the sky was completely dark and the stars were hidden by clouds, he unknowingly cut off the cow’s head, thinking it was the tiger.”

The guru then cursed him:

Show Sanskrit

न क्षत्रबन्धुः शूद्रस्त्वं कर्मणा भवितामुना॥

“You shall not even remain a kṣatrabandhu; by this action you have become a śūdra.” The treatise notes that the phrase “not even a fallen kṣatriya” rules out reading “śūdra” here as only a comparison.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.1.17; Bhāgavatam 9.2.9

5.2 Nābhāga

Show Sanskrit

दिष्टपुत्रस्तु नाभागो वैश्यतामगमत्॥
नाभागो दिष्टपुत्रोऽन्यः कर्मणा वैश्यतां गतः॥

The Vishnu Purana says, “Nabhaga, son of Dishta, attained vaishyahood,” while the Bhagavata Purana makes the mechanism explicit: “Another son of Dishta was Nabhaga, who became a vaishya through his karma.”

5.3 The lineages of Dhṛṣṭa and Āgniveśya

Show Sanskrit

धृष्टाद्धार्ष्टमभूत्क्षत्रं ब्रह्मभूयं गतं क्षितौ॥
ततो ब्रह्मकुलं जातमाग्निवेश्यायनं नृप॥

“From Manu’s son Dhrshta arose the Dharshta kshatriyas, who attained brahmanahood on earth.” Shridharacarya glosses brahmabhuyam as brahmanatvam. Likewise, “From Agniveshya, a grandson of Manu, arose the brahmana lineage called Agniveshyayana.” Descendants of Manu’s kshatriya line thus became brahmanas, demonstrating flexibility not only for individuals but for lineages.

5.4 Viśvāmitra

The Ramayana and other scriptures describe Vishvamitra’s birth as a kshatriya king and his ascent to brahmanahood through austerity. Brahma himself confirms it:

Show Sanskrit

विश्वामित्रोऽपि धर्मात्मा लब्ध्वा ब्राह्मण्यमुत्तमम्। पूजयामास ब्रह्मर्षिं वसिष्ठं जपतां वरम्॥ कृतकामो महीं सर्वां चचार तपसि स्थितः। एवं त्वनेन ब्राह्मण्यं प्राप्तं राम महात्मना॥

“The righteous Viśvāmitra, having attained the highest brahmanhood, honoured Brahmarṣi Vasiṣṭha, foremost among those who chant. His wish fulfilled, he wandered the earth established in austerity. Thus, O Rāma, this great soul attained brahmanhood. This Viśvāmitra is the foremost among sages, an embodiment of austerity.”

The treatise considers a counter-account from the Mahābhārata: it says Viśvāmitra’s mother ate ritual food and bore a brāhmaṇa son despite being a kṣatriya. If so, his birth as a brāhmaṇa appears to conflict with this account of attaining brahmanhood. The author treats the two narratives as accounts from different cosmic cycles (kalpa-bheda), so the alternate birth story does not displace the Rāmāyaṇa account presented here.

Valmiki Ramayana 1.65.26

5.5 Ārṣṭiṣeṇa, Sindhudvīpa, and Devāpi

Show Sanskrit

एवं सिद्धः स भगवानार्ष्टिषेणः प्रतापवान्॥ तस्मिंस्तीर्थे तदा राजन् सिन्धुद्वीपः प्रतापवान्। देवापिश्च महाराज ब्राह्मण्यं प्रापतुर्महत्॥ तथा च कौशिकस्तात तपोनित्यो जितेन्द्रियः। तपसा वै सुतप्तेन ब्राह्मणत्वमवाप्तवान्॥

“In that place the radiant Ārṣṭiṣeṇa became accomplished. Sindhudvīpa and Devāpi also attained great brahmanhood. Likewise, the son of Kuśika, always engaged in austerity and self-control, attained brahmanhood through intense austerity.”

Mahābhārata 9.39.9

The treatise anticipates an objection: if these sages attained brahmanhood through austerity, how do their stories show the general operation of varṇa? It answers that tapas was the characteristic practice of Kṛta Yuga (Manusmṛti 1.86), while kīrtana is the corresponding practice in Kali Yuga (Bhāgavatam 12.3.5). What was attained through tapas in Kṛta Yuga may therefore be attained through chanting the Lord’s name in Kali Yuga, as in reference 10 above. The next example is offered as a case of attainment through worship of the Lord.

Manusmṛti 1.86; Bhāgavatam 12.3.5; see also reference 10

5.6 Mātaṅga

The Skanda Purāṇa 5.2.60 tells of Mātaṅga, who was raised in the household of Ṛṣi Sugati but, according to the narrative, was born from an affair between a barber and a brāhmaṇa woman. Gardabhī tells him:

Show Sanskrit

नापितेन प्रमत्तेन ब्राह्मण्यां वृषलेन हि। ततस्त्वमसि चाण्डालो ब्राह्मण्यां तेन ते गतम्॥ १॥

“Your brāhmaṇa mother was violated by a lowly barber. Thus you are a caṇḍāla, and your familial brahmanhood is lost; therefore, you are a caṇḍāla.”

Hearing this, Mātaṅga practises austerity in order to attain brahmanhood. Indra tells him that he cannot grant it and directs him to worship Śiva. Mātaṅga follows this instruction and travels to the Mahākāla forest, described as a pleasing place where siddhas dwell. He sees the Śiva liṅga, worships it with many kinds of flowers, and receives Śiva’s blessing:

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मण्यं दुर्लभं लब्धं लिङ्गस्यास्य प्रभावतः॥

“Mātaṅga attained brahmanhood merely by seeing the liṅga; the difficult-to-attain state of brahmanhood came to him through the power of this liṅga.”

Mātaṅga later became a great sage. The treatise offers this as an example of the change described in Bhāgavatam 3.33.6: a caṇḍāla attained the eligibility for Vedic sacrifice through worship of the Lord. It addresses a variant in Mahābhārata 13.3, which stops after Indra says brahmanhood is difficult to attain. The Skanda Purāṇa likewise recounts that earlier portion, then continues to Mātaṅga’s attainment through Śiva. The treatise suggests that Bhīṣma may have omitted the ending because it was not needed for his point; it also allows for kalpa-bheda, or differing accounts in different cosmic cycles.

Skanda Purāṇa 5.2.60; Mahābhārata 13.3

5.7 King Vitahavya

The treatise responds to the claim that Bhīṣma says a non-brāhmaṇa cannot attain brahmanhood. In Mahābhārata 13.3, Yudhiṣṭhira first asks:

“It befits you to tell me, O best of royalty: if a kṣatriya, vaiśya, or śūdra wishes to attain brahmanhood, how may he attain it—through great austerities, through action, or through learning? Tell me, grandfather.”

Bhīṣma answers that brahmanhood is difficult to attain for the other three varṇas, that it is the highest station among beings, and that one is born again and again through many births until at last one is born a brāhmaṇa.

“Brahmanhood is difficult to attain, O son, for people of the other three varṇas, beginning with kṣatriyas. This station is the highest among all beings. One is born again and again through many births until, in one of them, one is born a brāhmaṇa.”

The treatise reads this as arthavāda, praise of the rarity and stature of brahmanhood, rather than a literal impossibility. It points out that in Mahābhārata 13.31 Yudhiṣṭhira himself challenges Bhīṣma with two counterexamples:

“I have heard this great tale from you, O best of the Kuru clan, and you say that brahmanhood is very hard to attain, O best of speakers. But earlier Viśvāmitra attained brahmanhood—this too is heard. Why, then, do you say that it is hard to attain, O best of men? It is also heard that Vitahavya, the royal sage, attained brahmanhood.”

Bhīṣma then narrates how the kṣatriya king Vitahavya attained brahmanhood through the grace of sage Bhṛgu and concludes:

Show Sanskrit

एवं विप्रत्वमगमद्वीतहव्यो नराधिपः। भृगोः प्रसादाद्राजेन्द्र क्षत्रियः क्षत्रियर्षभ॥

“In this manner King Vitahavya, a kṣatriya, attained viprahood through the grace of Bhṛgu, O best of kṣatriyas.” The treatise therefore reads Bhīṣma’s earlier answer as praise, not a denial that varṇa can change.

Mahābhārata 13.3 and 13.31

5.8 Triśaṅku, Vasiṣṭhas, and Viśvāmitras

The Rāmāyaṇa gives three examples of degradation to caṇḍāla or niṣāda status. Each account links the change to an act against a guru or father.

Triśaṅku

After offending his guru, King Triśaṅku becomes a caṇḍāla:

Show Sanskrit

अथ रात्र्यां व्यतीतायां राजा चाण्डालतां गतः॥

“Then, when the night had passed, King Triśaṅku attained caṇḍāla status.”

Rāmāyaṇa 1.57.9 · reference 32

Mahodaya

Viśvāmitra curses Mahodaya, a son of Vasiṣṭha, for insulting him:

Show Sanskrit

महोदयश्च दुर्बुद्धिर्मद्वचो यदभाषत्। दूषितः सर्वलोकेषु निषादत्वं गमिष्यति॥

“That foolish Mahodaya, who spoke insultingly to me though I did not deserve insult, will be reviled in all the worlds and will attain niṣāda status.”

Rāmāyaṇa 1.59.20 · reference 33

Viśvāmitra’s sons

Viśvāmitra curses his own sons for insulting him:

Show Sanskrit

श्वमांसभोजिनः सर्वे वासिष्ठा इव जातिषु। पूर्णं वर्षसहस्रं तु पृथिव्यामनुवत्स्यथ॥

“You shall all roam the earth as caṇḍālas, like the sons of Vasiṣṭha, for a full thousand years.”

5.9 A verse of the Ṛgveda

Show Sanskrit

कारुरहं ततो भिषगुपलप्रक्षिणी नना। नानाधियो वसूयवोऽनु गा इव तस्थिमेन्द्रायेन्दो परि स्रव॥ ३॥

“I am a singer of hymns; my father is a physician, and my mother grinds herbs. Our occupations differ; seeking wealth, we remain together like cattle. Flow, O Soma, for Indra.”

The treatise identifies the speaker as a kāru, a Vedic hymn singer, and cites Ṛgveda 7.2.7 for its association of kāru with vipra. It then quotes the Manusmṛti to connect the father’s occupation with Ambashṭha jāti:

Show Sanskrit

सूतानामश्वसारथ्यमम्बष्ठानां चिकित्सनम्। वैदेहकानां स्त्रीकार्यं मागधानां वणिक्पथः॥ ४७॥

“For the Sūtas, tending horses; for the Ambashṭhas, medicine; for the Vaidehakas, service of women; and for the Māgadhas, trade.”

Manusmṛti 10.47

The source also cites a verse that excludes physicians from certain rites:

Show Sanskrit

चिकित्सकान् देवलकान् मांसविक्रयिणस्तथा। विपणेन च जीवन्तो वर्ज्याः स्युर्हव्यकव्ययोः॥ १५२॥

“Physicians, Devalakas, meat-sellers, and those who live by improper trade should be avoided in rites for the gods and ancestors.”

Manusmṛti 3.152

On the treatise’s reading, the hymn’s speaker is a brāhmaṇa while his father performs an occupation assigned here to a mixed jāti and is excluded from yajña rites. It presents the hymn as evidence that the son’s status follows qualities rather than simply the father’s occupation.

Ṛgveda 9.112.3; see also 7.2.7

5.10 Kavāṣa Ailuṣa

The eighth chapter of the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa recounts the story of Kavāṣa Ailuṣa. During a Soma sacrifice beside the Sarasvatī, the sages object:

“This man is a gambler, the son of a slave woman, and a non-brāhmaṇa. How may he be initiated among us?”

They send him into the desert, saying, “Let thirst kill him there; let him not drink from the Sarasvatī.”

Tormented by thirst, Kavāṣa perceives the Ṛgvedic mantra प्र देवत्रा ब्रह्मणे गातुरेत्वपः—“May the waters make a path to the gods for Brahman.” The Sarasvatī withdraws from the sacrificers and flows around him; the place is then called Pariṣāraka. The sages say, “The gods know him. Let us call him back.” They recall him to the sacrifice and perform the Apām Napāt rite, reciting the mantra revealed to Kavāṣa.

The treatise stresses the reversal: the sages first expelled him as a slave woman’s son and non-brāhmaṇa, then accepted him into the Soma sacrifice and performed a rite with the mantra he had seen. It presents this as recognition of Kavāṣa as a Vedic seer and as an example of a person becoming qualified for a rite the treatise describes as reserved for brāhmaṇas.

Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 8; Ṛgveda 10.30

These narratives supply thirteen individual examples of change and two examples of entire lineages changing varna. Many more could be cited, but these are sufficient to illustrate the principle already established by direct scriptural statements.

6. Views of other ācāryas

6.1 Madhva ācāryas

Madhvacarya’s position has already been established from his commentary on the Gita. Later teachers in his lineage express the same view.

Shri Vishvesha Tirtha, Gita Saroddhara

“Shri Madhvacarya has explained that the four divisions of varna do not belong to the human body but to the soul. Souls are categorised according to their nature, qualities, and propensities. The good souls worthy of salvation are divided into four groups. All possess a predominance of sattva; their differences arise from permutations of rajas and tamas.

“A shudra with noble qualities such as self-control and truthfulness is indeed a brahmana; a person is identified as a brahmana only through noble conduct. In answering the Yaksha, it is stated that virtuous character alone is the distinguishing cause of brahmanahood.

“The varna system of the Gita is not a classification based on accidental attributes of birth, but a classification of good souls in society. Such a system, based on essential and deeper qualities, produces harmony among different communities for the good of society as a whole.”

Bannanje Govindacarya, Madhvacharya: Life and Teachings

“The Gita’s idea of four varṇas is distinct from the four castes prevalent today. It concerns the soul’s innermost nature or personality. The soul’s true colour must be discovered; that is the right social order. In such an order, the son of a low-born person may be a noble brāhmaṇa, while a brāhmaṇa’s son may be a śūdra. Varṇa is not transmitted hereditarily; it is personal and determined by the individual’s own characteristics.”

The source adds that other Madhva ācāryas also accept the guṇa-based view taught by Madhvācārya.

6.2 Gauḍīya ācāryas

Jīva Gosvāmī

Commenting on Bhāgavatam 3.33.6 in the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, Jīva Gosvāmī explains that hearing or chanting the Lord’s name, bowing to Him, or remembering Him destroys the dog-eater’s prārabdha-karma—the karma of this life that caused his low birth and kept him from performing sacrifice. Devotional acts also produce the merit and qualities needed for sacrifice. A person born a brāhmaṇa still needs Vedic initiation with the Gāyatrī mantra, which gives the special birth required for sacrifice; the purified dog-eater likewise needs corresponding training. Jīva’s point is that devotion can elevate even a dog-eater by eradicating the karma that obstructs sacrifice. He concludes: if this is the power of hearing, chanting, and remembering, what must be the power of directly seeing the Lord—and how much more readily may others, who were not born so low, be elevated?

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu on Bhāgavatam 3.33.6

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa, Brahma-sūtra commentary

“The qualifications of a brāhmaṇa are required to engage in the study and practice of Vedānta. Consequently, very few people can begin, and even fewer can complete that study, because the elevated qualifications of a brāhmaṇa are rarely found in human society, especially today. These classifications are not determined by birth but by a person’s qualities and activities; they may change through experience and training. Thus even a śūdra who undertakes Vedic disciplines—abstaining from meat-eating, intoxication, illicit sex, and gambling—and chants the Lord’s holy name under the direction of a self-realised teacher may develop the purity and intelligence of a brāhmaṇa and become qualified to study Vedānta.”

Satya Nārāyaṇa Dāsa

Question: “I am not clear whether varṇa and jāti are different. Could you explain?”

Answer: “Varṇa and jāti are separate and should not be confused. Jāti is based on birth, while varṇa is based on guṇas. There are two opinions: some say that anyone who has the qualifications may be a brāhmaṇa regardless of birth. Indeed, this is how it was originally.”

Show Sanskrit

यथा काञ्चनतां याति कांस्यं रसविधानतः। तथा दीक्षाविधानेन द्विजत्वं जायते नृणाम्॥

“Just as base metal becomes gold through an alchemical process, a person attains dvijahood through the prescribed process of Vaishnava initiation.” This accords with Madhva’s view that Vaishnavas are sattva-predominant.

Sanātana Gosvāmī, Tattva-sāgara

Viśvanātha Cakravartin

Viśvanātha states that spiritual initiation according to Pāñcarātra gives even a śūdra or antyaja, one born in the lowest family, the samskāras of a dvija. He cites the Mahābhārata:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रोऽप्यागमसम्पन्नो द्विजो भवति संस्कृतः॥

“Even a shudra, perfected through the Agamic procedure, becomes a consecrated dvija.”

Mahabharata

6.3 Advaita ācāryas

Swami Krishnananda

“The classification of society into four varṇas is not a rigid, mechanical isolation of groups by birth and heredity alone, as it came to be viewed in later times. It is a logically developed cooperative system of living, instituted for the preservation and prosperity of society through division of labour. That division is based on people’s qualities and on the proportion of their contribution to society, according to aptitude, knowledge, and capacity. Svabhāva, one’s inherent nature, determines svadharma, one’s duty in society.”

Swami Chinmayananda

“Casteism is a perversion and has no place in Hinduism. The caste system is universal and cannot be removed: it shows how the mind is cast into four moulds—thinkers, leaders, entrepreneurs, and workers. It is based on the temperament or ‘colour’ (varna) of the mind—sattva, rajas, and tamas—and on the nature of individual actions, or karma. It is not based on birth.”

6.4 Śaiva ācāryas

Sivaya Subramuniyaswami, Living with Siva

“The original caste system was based on behaviour, as it is now in countries where no overt caste system is in effect. Its four divisions reflected an individual’s ability to manage body, mind, and emotions. If someone ceased to fulfil the dharma of his caste, society recognised that he had moved from one caste to another. The original system rested on self-discipline through education and personal sādhana, and on the unfolding of consciousness within each individual.”

Basava

“One who kills is a Madiga; one who eats filth is a Holeya. What has birth to do with a superior or inferior class? By birth, all are equal. Those who take refuge in Lord Kudalasangamadeva and seek the welfare of all beings are the truly superior class.”

Like Vaishnava teachers who describe devotees of Vishnu as sattvika, Basava describes those surrendered to Shiva as superior through qualities, while degraded status follows degraded action.

Abhinavagupta, Tantraloka 15.513

Show Sanskrit

योनिर्न कारणं तत्र शान्तात्मा द्विज उच्यते। मुनिना मोक्षधर्मादावेतच्च प्रविवेचितम्॥ ५१३॥

“Birth is not the cause; one of peaceful nature is called a dvija. This has been explained by the sage in the Mokshadharma and elsewhere.”

Tantraloka

Having assembled the evidence, the thesis may be restated:

  1. There are two forms of varna: jati-based or aupadhika varna, acquired through birth in a family; and guna-based or svabhavika varna, arising from a person’s qualities.
  2. One who belongs to a varna by birth may enter another through qualities or the absence of them. A shudra may become a brahmana, a brahmana may fall to shudrahood, and other transitions are likewise possible.
  3. Because of these principles—and because intermixture makes jati increasingly difficult to determine—varna is predominantly based on guna, while birth-based varna is secondary.

8. Pragmatic perspective

8.1 Economic prosperity and social harmony

Show Sanskrit

धर्मादर्थः प्रभवति धर्मात्प्रभवते सुखम्॥

“From dharma arises prosperity; from dharma arises happiness.”

Valmiki Ramayana 3.9.30

Scripture is the basis of dharma, but the soundness of an interpretation may also be tested by whether it produces the stated fruits: economic prosperity and enduring social harmony. “Happiness” here does not mean a person’s momentary preference, but the long-term welfare of society.

A system that ignores ability in favour of birth is economically inefficient. A person capable of being a merchant, warrior, or scholar may be barred from that vocation because of ancestry, while someone lacking the necessary aptitude may be compelled to perform it. The result is a larger number of unqualified people in every field and a corresponding loss of prosperity. Preventing qualified members of lower jati groups from advancing also creates resentment and social division.

The treatise argues that historical comparisons support its case for merit-based advancement. It contrasts Western Europe’s institutions with hereditary serfdom in parts of Eastern Europe, points to Revolutionary France’s meritocratic army and Napoleon’s advancement after inherited barriers weakened, and contrasts the Spanish Empire’s stratified colonial order with the success of its rivals.

For India, the treatise attributes weakened economic and military capacity, social discontent, and conversions under Muslim and British rule to rigid hereditary restrictions. It also argues that caste remains a frequent criticism of Hinduism and concludes that a birth-based system should be rejected for the sake of prosperity, social harmony, and the strength of the tradition.

8.2 Implementation

The final question is how a system of varna by qualities can be implemented. Birth-based classification may appear easier, but the scriptural arguments above say that jati becomes indeterminate through intermixture, whereas qualities can be observed directly. A genuine concern nevertheless remains: if varna were left entirely to personal choice, nearly everyone might claim to be a brahmana or kshatriya.

Assignment cannot therefore rest on self-declaration. It must be made by the guru, who examines a student’s qualities and grants an appropriate varna. As already noted, several ācāryas accept Pāñcarātrika or Āgamic initiation as capable of making a person a dvija irrespective of birth. The Mahābhārata states:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रोऽप्यागमसम्पन्नो द्विजो भवति संस्कृतः॥

“Even a śūdra who has completed the Āgamic procedure becomes a consecrated dvija.”

Mahābhārata 13.143

Before such samskāra, all are to be treated as śūdras, as stated in the Skanda Purāṇa 6.239.31:

“By birth one is born a śūdra; through samskāra one is called a dvija.” In a system where the guru assigns varṇa after examining qualities and performing the appropriate initiation, social roles can be filled by qualified people and the economy can prosper.
