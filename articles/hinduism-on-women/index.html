1. Introduction

1.1 Preface and Methodology

Purpose

Dharma is one of the most difficult Sanskrit words to translate into English. It refers simultaneously to innate qualities and appropriate duties. It is the ideal way that a thing ought to exist. Insofar as dharma is often discussed, however, it is discussed in the context of the dharmas of specific Varnas, Jatis, Ashramas, roles such as ‘king’,‘teacher’, ‘student’, etc, and the chief object of discussion is the men whom these categories divide. Rarely is the dharma of women, half of the population, ever discussed on its own.

Even when it is discussed, it is discussed mostly in two ways. Firstly, from the lens of Indology, a completely secular and outsider perspective, which seeks to understand Hinduism as a naturally developing social structure which changed and evolved based on human factors. Secondly is through the completely ‘Traditionalist’ perspective, which, though recognising scripture as divine in premise, is practically more focused on auxiliary social practices, ideas of later thinkers and even internalised foreign influence. Rarely, if ever, is the actual statement of the Hindu scriptures themselves engaged on its own.

This paper attempts to do just that, understand the position of the Hindu scriptures themselves, without seeking to project ideas of ‘Later’ or ‘older’ on them, without contextualising them as mere human productions based on human factors, without mixing in external practices and influences, completely on its own terms as divine pramana.

Methodology

As stated, only the statement of scriptures will be considered primary evidence for this analysis. Scripture includes Sruti or Vedas as the supreme section, containing 4 subsections, viz., Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka & Upanisad. These very rarely speak of social laws. Besides Sruti is the wide category of ‘Smrti’, the writings of the authoritative sages. Contained within are many sub-categories like Vedangas, Dharmasastras (Social-legal treatises), Itihasas (History) and Puranas (which focus on a broad range of topics and are sometimes considered distinct from smrtis), and a few others. It is these alone which are the sources of Dharma, as they themselves state:

Lord Krsna says in the Gita, 16.23

Show Sanskrit

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः ।
न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥
तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ ।
ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥

"He who, discarding scriptural procedure, proceeds as per his own wishes, he does not attain success, neither happiness, nor the highest goal. Therefore, the scripture is your basis in the determination of what is to be done and what is not to be done. Having known the scriptural injunction, it befits you to perform actions in this world."

Vasistha smrti 1.2 states :

Show Sanskrit

श्रुतिस्मृतिविहितो धर्मः

“That which is enjoined by Sruti or smrti is dharma”

Bhagavata Purana 6.1.40 states:

Show Sanskrit

वेदप्रणिहितो धर्मो ह्यधर्मस्तद्विपर्ययः

“That which is enjoined in Vedic scriptures is dharma and what is other than it is adharma.”

There is also a hierarchy within these scriptures (Vyasa smrti 1.4):

Show Sanskrit

श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानां विरोधो यत्र दृश्यते ।
तत्र श्रौतं प्रमाणं तु तयोर्द्वैधे स्मृतिर्वरा ॥

“when there is contradiction between Sruti, Smrti & Purana, Sruti (alone) shall be pramana. And between the two (Smrti and Purana), the Smrti is superior.”

However this hierarchy should only be invoked when all possible reconciliation has been ruled out.

In order to understand the scriptures , logic (Nyaya) and Hermeneutics (Mimamsa) are also needed. The opinion of learned scholars (Acaryas) are also considered, but only insofar as they are logical and based on the scripture, as the scriptures themselves say:

Yoga Vasistha — Valmiki

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि ।
अन्यतृणमिव त्याज्यमयुक्तं पद्मजन्मना ॥ ३

“That which is reasonable should be accepted even if it be stated by a child, and if it is not so, it should be given up like a piece of straw, even if it be stated by the lotus-born god brahma himself”

Yajnavalkya Siksa

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात्

One should accept (of the guru) what is reasonable or established (by scripture), he should not accept it merely by it having been stated by the guru.

Hence, even the statements and actions of Acharyas are subject to the authority of scriptures and are to be judged and discarded if they are not in line with them. Only scriptures are authority in their own right.

1.2 The Mahima of Women

In general

Before looking at the dharmas of women we must look at how the scriptures describe women and their status:

Manusmriti 3.56-58

Show Sanskrit

यत्र नार्यस्तु पूज्यन्ते रमन्ते तत्र देवताः ।
यत्रैतास्तु न पूज्यन्ते सर्वास्तत्राफलाः क्रियाः ॥
शोचन्ति जामयो यत्र विनश्यत्याशु तत्कुलम् ।
न शोचन्ति तु यत्रैता वर्धते तद्धि सर्वदा ॥
जामयो यानि गेहानि शपन्त्यप्रतिपूजिताः ।
तानि कृत्याहतानिव विनश्यन्ति समन्ततः ॥

“Where women are honoured, there the gods rejoice; where, on the other hand, they are not honoured, there all rites are fruitless. Where the female relations live in grief, the family soon wholly perishes; but that family where they are not unhappy ever prospers. The houses on which female relations, not being duly honoured, pronounce a curse, perish completely, as if destroyed by magic.”

Women as goddesses

Hinduism is well-known as the only major religion to still worship the divine as not only masculine but also feminine. In the Sakta sect, the feminine is even considered supreme, superior even to the masculine. The other sects each worship their own preferred deity’s wife as supreme after him, such as Laksmi and Parvati. This reverence for the feminine as divine also manifests as reverence for women, as they are called manifestations or forms of the goddess:

Markandeya Purana 91.5

Show Sanskrit

विद्याः समस्तास्तव देवि भेदाः स्त्रियः समस्ताः सकला जगत्सु ।

“O goddess! All the sciences are divisions of you, as also all the women in the world.”

Specifically, women are most often identified with the Goddess Laksmi, goddess of fortune, who is also called ‘Sri’.

Manusmriti 9.26

Show Sanskrit

प्रजनार्थं महाभागाः पूजार्हा गृहदीप्तयः ।
स्त्रियः श्रियश्च गेहेषु न विशेषोऽस्ति कश्चन ॥

There is no difference whatsoever between goddess Sri and the women. They secure many blessings for the sake of bearing children, they are worthy of worship and form the glory of their household.

Mahabharata 13.46.5-6

Show Sanskrit

पूज्या लालयितव्याश्च स्त्रियो नित्यं जनाधिप ।
अपूजिताश्च यत्रैताः सर्वास्तत्राफलाः क्रियाः ।
तदैव तत्कुलं नास्ति यदा शोचन्ति जामयः ॥
जामीशप्तानि गेहानि निकृत्तानीव कृत्यया ।
नैव भान्ति न वर्धन्ते श्रिया हीनानि पार्थिव ॥

At that very time that family is destroyed at which the women are unhappy. The households cursed by women are destroyed, as if by some magic. Such households, devoid of Sri (women) do not glow or prosper.

Mahabharata 5.38.11

Show Sanskrit

पूजनीया महाभागाः पुण्याश्च गृहदीप्तयः ।
स्त्रियः श्रियो गृहस्योक्तास्तस्माद्रक्ष्या विशेषतः ॥

Women are called worshippable, great, pure, the light of the household and goddess Sri herself. Therefore they are to be especially protected.

Mahabharata 13.46.14

Show Sanskrit

श्रिय एताः स्त्रियो नाम सत्कार्या भूतिमिच्छता ।
लालिता निगृहीता च स्त्री श्रीर्भवति भारत ॥

“Sri” is another name for women. They are always to be honoured, pleased with what they want, and being gratified, the woman becomes like goddess Laksmi, O descendant of Bharata.

Purity of women

Mahabharata 12.159.30

Show Sanskrit

अदूष्या हि स्त्रियो रत्नमाप इव धर्मतः ॥

Women, gemstones and water, these three are unpollutable by their nature.

Yajnavalkya Smriti 1.71; Garuda Purana 1.95.18; Skanda Purana 4.40.38

Show Sanskrit

सोमः शौचं ददावासां गन्धर्वश्च शुभां गिरम् ।
पावकः सर्वमेध्यत्वं मेध्या वै योषितो ह्यतः ॥

Soma gave them physical purity, Gandharva gave them pure speech, Agni gave them complete ritual purity, the Women are therefore completely pure.

Names of women

Sanskrit is one of the richest languages in terms of synonyms for a single thing. A synonym is not random but based on a specific quality of the thing it denotes. Fire, for example, while usually called ‘Agni’, is also called ‘Pavaka’ (purifier) as whatever is put in it is burnt and thereby purified.

The Amarakosa is a very famous lexicon which records such synonyms for each thing, arranged by topic in metrical verses. While not itself a scripture, its words almost always have examples of Scriptural use.

For ‘Men’ it contains only two lines, of mostly generic names deriving from ‘descendant of Manu’ (Manava, Manusa, Manusya, Manuja). The women, however, get five lines of highly descriptive names, a testament of how many qualities of women are recognised in scriptures. The following are a few select examples of the names it records:

Show Sanskrit

स्त्री । नारी । महिला । वधू । योषित् । योषा । वामा । रामा । मानिनी ।

  1. Stri: she who bears the womb
  2. Nari: she who leads
  3. Mahila: the great or honoured one
  4. Vadhu: she who is taken in marriage
  5. Yosit: she who is served by men
  6. Yosa: she who serves men
  7. Vama: she who embodies beauty
  8. Rama: delighted or who delights others
  9. Manini: she who has dignity

As mothers and daughters

Women, of course, do not exist in a vacuum. They are related to others as mothers, daughters, sisters, etc. of all relations, the one which everyone has and which is the root of all others, is that of the mother. The mother too is heavily glorified in the scriptures:

Taittiriya Upanishad, Sisyanusasana, 2

Show Sanskrit

मातृदेवो भव । पितृदेवो भव । आचार्यदेवो भव ।

“Be one for whom the mother is like god, be one for whom the father is like god, be one for whom the teacher is like god.”

Note how the Mother is mentioned first and foremost, then the father, then the teacher. This is because this is their order of superiority, as mentioned in the Manusmriti (2.145):

Show Sanskrit

उपाध्यायान् दशाचार्य आचार्याणां शतं पिता ।
सहस्रं तु पितॄणां माता गौरवेणातिरिच्यते ॥

"In venerability, the teacher excels ten Sub-teachers; the Father a hundred teachers, and the Mother a thousand Fathers.”

A famous section of Mahabharata is the Yaksa-prasna, a series of riddles by a creature called Yaksa to Yudhisthira, who is dharma incarnate, with the lives of his brothers on the line. A riddle in it is:

Yaksa asked: "What is greater than the earth? What is higher than the sky? What is faster than the wind? What is more numerous than blades of grass?"

Yudhisthira answered: "The mother is greater than the earth. The father is higher than the sky. The mind is faster than the wind and worries are more numerous than blades of grass."

The Padma Purana (1.18.353–355) states:

नास्ति मातृसमो बन्धुर्बलिनां क्षीरजीविनाम् ।
नास्ति मातृसमो नाथो नास्ति मातृसमा गतिः ।
नास्ति मातृसमः स्नेहो नास्ति मातृसमं मुखम् ।
नास्ति मातृसमो देव इहलोके परत्र च ।
एवं वै परमो धर्मः प्रजापतिविनिर्मितः ।
ये तिष्ठन्ति सदा पुत्रास्ते यान्ति परमां गतिम् ॥

"There is no friend like the mother to the child, who has lived off of her milk. There is no lord like the mother, nor any refuge like the mother. There is no love like that of the mother, nothing is like the mother's face. There is no god like the mother, neither in this world nor the other. This is the supreme dharma as stated by the creator, the sons who are established in it attain the supreme destination."

Even the great heroes and avataras of the epics are known primarily by the names of their mother, their matronymic, rather than their father. Lord Rama is called Kauslyeya, Laksmana is called Saumitri, Lord Krsna is called Devinandana, the Pandavas are called Kaunteya or Partha, Bhisma is called Gangaputra, Karna is called Radheya, Abhimanyu is called Saubhadra, etc.

It is a commonly held belief in India today amongst many Hindus that the Male child is preferred over the female. The scriptures, however, treat them equally or even the female as superior:

यथैवात्मा तथा पुत्रः पुत्रेण दुहिता समा ।

"The son is like one's own self, and the daughter is equal to the son."

Skanda purana 1.2.23.45

शास्त्रेषूक्तमसंदिग्धं वाक्यमेतन्महाफलम् ।
दशपुत्रसमा कन्या दशपुत्रान् प्रवर्धयन् ।
यत्फलं लभते मर्त्यस्तल्लभ्यं कन्ययैकया ॥

"This faultless statement said in the scripture is conducive to great reward : 'A single daughter is equal to ten sons. That merit which is attained by men by raising ten sons is attained by raising a single daughter'"

2. Marriage

2.1 Introduction and Consent

Importance of marriage

The most important legal and social institution tied to women (and men) is perhaps marriage. Marriage is for this reason its own section in this document. Marriage in Hinduism is not merely a social contract, but a divine obligation with few exceptions for both women and men. The Manusmriti (9.96) describes it as the very purpose of both:

प्रजनार्थं स्त्रियः सृष्टाः सन्तानार्थं च मानवाः ।
तस्मात्साधारणो धर्मः श्रुतौ पत्न्या सहोदितः ॥

"Women were created for the purpose of child-bearing, and men for the purpose of procreation. Hence it is that Religious Rites have been ordained in the Veda as common between the man and his wife.”

To understand why marriage is considered so important, one must understand the three debts. The Krsna Yajurveda ( 6.3.10.5) amongst various other sources, states:

त्रिभिरृणवा जायते ब्रह्मचर्येण ऋषिभ्यो यज्ञेन देवेभ्यः प्रजया पितृभ्यः

“One is born with three debts, of learning to the sages, of Vedic sacrifice to the gods, and of progeny to the ancestors.”

Similarly Manusmriti(6:36) :

अधीत्य विधिवद्वेदान् पुत्रांश्चोत्पाद्य धर्मतः । इष्ट्वा च शक्तितो यज्ञैर्मनो मोक्षे निवेशयेत् ॥

“Only after having studied the Vedas according to the rules, having begotten sons in the rightful manner, and having offered sacrifices to the rest of his ability, he shall turn his mind towards Liberation.”

Disqualification of the unmarried from religious observances

The second rna, to the gods, is paid by religious observances. However, as stated, religious observances have been stated to be common between the husband and wife, and so neither can perform them without the other :

Taittiriya Brahmana 3.3.3.1

अयज्ञो वा एषः । योऽपत्नीकः ।

"He is without the Vedic ritual, who is without a wife."

Skanda Purana 4.1.4.68, Siva Purana 2.6.54.66

देवपित्रतिथीज्यादि नाभार्यः कर्म चार्हति

"The man without a wife does not have the right to the rites of the gods, ancestors, guests or Vedic rituals."

Skanda Purana 4.1.4.19, Siva Purana 2.6.54.19

कुर्यात्पत्यननुज्ञाता नोपवासव्रतादिकम् ॥

"A Woman must not perform religious observances such as fasting without the prior knowledge of her husband."

Mahima of Grhastha asrama

The rna to the sages is paid by studying as a student (Brahmacarya), but for the remaining two, i.e., to procreate and perform religious activities, marriage is obligatory. Thus after Brahmacarya one should marry and enter the ‘Grhastha’ (Household) phase of life, and then procreate and perform religious activities , fulfilling the first three goals of life [ Righteousness (Dharma), Sensual pleasure (Kama), wealth (Artha) ] and the duties of his Varna until old age, only then should one take renunciation (Vanaprastha and eventually Samnyasa) and focus entirely on attaining on the final goal, liberation (Moksa).

This, in summary, is the entirety of human Dharma, and has marriage at its basis. Without marrying and entering Grhastha life one cannot fulfil two of the three rnas, cannot progress in the asramas, cannot perform the duties of his varna and cannot fulfill even a single one of the Purusarthas.

It is thus that married life is praised heavily in scriptures:

ब्रह्मचारी गृहस्थश्च वानप्रस्थो यतिस्तथा । एते गृहस्थप्रभवाश्चत्वारः पृथगाश्रमाः ॥
सर्वेषामपि चैतेषां वेदस्मृतिविधानतः । गृहस्थ उच्यते श्रेष्ठः स त्रीनेतान्बिभर्ति हि ॥

“Brahmacari (student), Grhastha (householder), Vanaprastha and Samnyasa, these four separate asramas have the Grhastha as the basis. As per the injunctions of the Veda and Smrti, amongst all of these, the Grhastha is said to be supreme, for he sustains the other three.”

गृहस्थ एव यजते गृहस्थस्तप्यते तपः । गार्हस्थ्यमस्य धर्मस्य मूलं यत्किञ्चिदर्जते ।

"The Grhastha performs Vedic rituals. The Grhastha performs austerities. Married life is the basis of all of dharma that proceeds.

गार्हस्थ्यात्परमो धर्मो द्वितीयो नास्ति भूतले गृहस्थस्य गृहः पुण्यः सत्यपुण्यसमन्वितः ।

"There is no superior dharma separate to married life on this earth. The householder's home is said to be endowed with purity and truth."

Consent

Regarding the marriage of the women, the Manusmriti & Mahabharata state :

Mahabharata 13.44.15 & Manusmriti 9.90

त्रीणि वर्षाण्युदीक्षेत कन्या ऋतुमती सती । चतुर्थे त्वथ सम्प्राप्ते स्वयं भर्तारमर्जयेत् ।

“Having reached her puberty, the righteous maiden shall wait for three years, with the beginning of the fourth, she may choose a suitable husband for herself”

Once the girl reaches a suitable age, the father should start looking for a good groom for her. If he fails in doing so, she may, after three years, herself choose a suitable husband.

Question : If the father arranges her marriage in the stipulated time, can he do so to whomever he wishes without the girl’s consent?

Answer : not so, for the same chapter of Mahabharata states a few verses ahead:

न ह्याकामेन संवासं मनुरेव प्रशंसति

“Manu does not praise the co-habitation of the girl with whom she does not desire.”

The fifth type of Marriage, Gandharva, which is characterized entirely by the mutual love and desire of the bride and groom and no involvement of the parents, is highly praised in scriptures and will be discussed shortly.

2.2 Forms of Marriage

The eight types

The eight types of marriage are summarised in short in the Garuda Purana, 1.95.7-10

आहूय दानं ब्राह्मः स्यात्कुलशीलगुणान्विताय तु ।
यज्ञस्थायर्त्विजे दैवमादायार्षस्तु गोयुगम् ॥
प्रार्थिता दीयते यस्य प्राजापत्यः स धर्मकृत् ।
शुल्केन चासुरी मन्दो गान्धर्वो वरयोर्मिथः ।
राक्षसो युद्धहरणात्पैशाचः कन्यकाच्छलात् ॥

  1. The marriage in which the father invites a man endowed with good qualities and lineage to give his daughter’s hand is called ‘Brahma’.
  2. That in which the daughter’s hand is given to an officiating priest is called ‘Daiva’.
  3. The one in which a pair of cow and bull is accepted as a symbolic gift is called ‘Arsa’.
  4. The one in which the groom himself asks for the daughter’s hand and is given it is called ‘Prajapatya’, a righteous form.
  5. The one in which there is an exchange of money is called ‘Asura’, an inferior form.
  6. The one which is done with mutual agreement of the bride and groom (and none else) is called ‘Gandharva’.
  7. The one in which martial force is used by the groom is called ‘Raksasa’.
  8. The one where the girl is deceived or cheated (by being intoxicated, asleep or lied to) is called ‘Paisaca’.

Amongst these, some are righteous whereas others are unrighteous. The classification given in Narada Smrti (12.44) is as follows :

एषां तु धर्म्याश्चत्वारो ब्राह्माद्याः समुदाहृताः । साधारणः स्याद्गान्धर्वोऽधर्मस्त्वितरे परे ॥

"Amongst these, the first four starting with brahma are dharmika (righteous). The Gandharva form is common to all, and all the subsequent three are Adharmika ( unrighteous).”

Asura marriage and dowry

Asura, the form characterised by exchange of money, is considered adharmic. It should be noted that the exchange referred to here is not The modern practice of dowry prevalent in India, where the bride’s family pays a certain amount in money or gifts to the groom’s family. That is entirely a medieval innovation with no basis in the scriptures. What the scriptures do mention is the exact opposite practice, where the groom pays a certain amount to the bride’s family. This is condemned in Manusmriti ( 9.98):

शुल्कं हि गृह्णते कन्यां छन्नं दुहितृविक्रयम् । एतत्तु न परे चक्रुर्नापरे जातु साधवः ॥

“By accepting dowry for the girl what he (the father) does is nothing but the covert sale of a daughter. This practice has never been performed by good men, neither of the present nor past.”

The modern practice, too, is thereby forbidden, firstly because of its complete lack of scriptural injunction (as seen, only the scriptures are the source of dharma) and by analogy, as what is condemned here is the sale of a child, whether it be of a daughter as done earlier or of a son as in modern times.

Greatness of the Gandharva form

Generally, the Brahma form is considered the best amongst the eight types. However , it is limited to only the Brahmana varna. The Gandharva form, where the bride and groom marry out of mutual love only without the involvement of the parents, is considered ‘Sadharana’ (may be practiced by all varnas).

In modern times, such a marriage which occurs only by the wish of the bride and groom and none else, will be termed ‘love marriage’ and considered ‘Un-traditional’ The scriptures, however, contain much praise for it, even as the best form:

Mahabharata 1.67.4 (also Skanda Purana 5.2.62.19)

विवाहानां हि सम्भोगो गान्धर्वः श्रेष्ठ उच्यते ॥

"Amongst the forms of marriages, O you with beautiful thighs, , the Gandharva is said to be the best"

पैशाच आसुरश्चैव न कर्तव्यो कथञ्चन । ब्राह्मः क्षात्रोऽथ गान्धर्व एते धर्म्या नरर्षभ ।
पृथग्वा यदि वा मिश्रा: कर्तव्या नात्र संशयः ॥

"The Paisaca and Aura forms should never be done under any circumstances. The Brahma, Ksatra (prajapatya) and Gandharva forms are considered righteous, O bull amongst men. They should be performed, either in their separate forms or mixed”

गान्धर्वमप्येके प्रशंसन्ति सर्वेषां स्नेहानुगत्वात्

"They also praise the Gandharva form for all (Varnas), because it has love as its basis"

Vatsyayana's Kama-sutra 3.5.30b

अनुरागात्मकत्वाच्च गान्धर्वः प्रवरो मतः

"Because its basis is love, the Gandharva form is considered the best."

Acarya Candesvara Thakura, in his work “Grhastha-ratnakara,” quotes the Harita-dharmasitra, a now-lost text:

हरीतोक्तन्तु सर्वान्प्रति गान्धर्वं श्रेष्ठत्वम्

"Harita has stated that ' for all (Varnas), there is superiority in the Gandharva type"

He further states that Gandharva, along with arsa and prajapatya, is permitted for all Varnas and is blameless:

आर्षप्राजापत्यगान्धर्वाणान्तु सर्ववर्णसाधारणत्वान्निन्दाविरहाच्च

"Because the Arsa , prajapatya and gandharva types are common to all Varnas, and because they are blameless.

An example of Gandharva marriage is that of King Duhsanta, father of King Bharata, after whom the Mahabharata and the country of India itself (Bharata) is named, and Sakuntala (Mahabharata 1.67):

सुकुमारं राजपुत्रि त्वं यथा कल्याणि भाषसे । भार्या मे भव सुश्रोणि ब्रूहि किं करवाणि ते ।

“O auspicious one of great form! From how you speak, you appear to be a princess. Become my wife, O beautiful one, tell me what your conditions are.”

Sakuntala hesitates, thinking she should wait for her father’s order:

Sakuntala said “My father, O king, who eats only fruit, has gone out of the residence. Wait just a moment, he shall give my hand to you.”

आत्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव गतिरात्मैव चात्मनः । आत्मनैवात्मनो दानं कर्तुमर्हसि धर्मतः ।
सा त्वं मम सकामस्य सकामा वरवर्णिनि । गान्धर्वेण विवाहेन भार्या भवितुमर्हसि ।

“One is one’s own friend and one is one’s own refuge. You may give your own self in marriage righteously. As I am desirous of you and you of me, O excellent-complexioned, you can become my wife by Gandharva marriage.”

Convinced, Sakuntala states her terms for marriage :

यदि धर्मपथस्त्वेष यदि चात्मा प्रभुर्मम । प्रदाने पौरवश्रेष्ठ शृणु मे समयं प्रभो ।
जायेत यः पुत्रः स भवेत्सदनन्तरम् । युवराजो महाराज सत्यमेतद्ब्रवीहि मे ॥

“If this is the way of dharma, If lam my own master in the matter of giving in marriage, O best of the Puru lineage, then hear my terms. The son born to me shall become king after you, O king, promise me this truthfully."

When her father, Kanva, returns, he also approves of the marriage :

Kanva said “What you have done today, viz, marrying a man in the manner fit for royalty, is not a violation of Dharma.”

Thus, Dharma gives women complete agency, both to choose their husband (Atma prabhur pradane) and to do so on their own terms which a suitor must accept to marry them.

On Raksasa marriage

The most controversial form of marriage is perhaps the Raksasa, not just among people but also in the scriptures. As seen, the Narada Smrti completely disapproves of it, classifying it alongside Asura and Paisaca as adharma. The Manusmriti, however, permits it for Ksatriyas (3.24). The Mahabharata lies somewhere in the middle, permitting it for kings but not ordinary Ksatriyas (1.67.11). Some object to this, mistakenly considering the “martial force” that characterizes the Raksasa marriage to be force against the bride’s consent. This is not so, as the commentators on Manu clarify:

कन्यापक्षादलङ्कारेण कन्याया हरणं राक्षसो विवाहः

“Taking the girl by using force against the family of the girl, this is called the Raksasa marriage”

कन्यापक्षः प्रतिपक्षतां याति तदा हननादिकमपि कर्तव्यमित्यर्थः

“Using martial force against the girl’s family if they come in opposition is the meaning .”

Thus the Raksasa vivaha is a form performed by Ksatriyas where, if the girl’s family opposes the marriage, the girl being ready for it, then the Ksatriya, in a display of valour, battles the girl’s family and takes her by force against them.

The exact form of Raksasa marriage will become clear if we consult the principal example of it given in the Itihasas & Puranas :

Bhagavatam 10.52.22-27

श्रीबादरायणिरुवाच राजासीद् भीष्मको नाम विदर्भाधिपतिर्महान् । तस्य पञ्चाभवन् पुत्राः कन्यैका च वरानना ॥
रुक्म्यग्रजो रुक्मरथो रुक्मबाहुरनन्तरः । रुक्मकेशो रुक्ममाली रुक्मिण्येषा स्वसा सती ॥
सोपश्रुत्य मुकुन्दस्य रूपवीर्यगुणश्रियः । गृहागतैर्गीयमानास्तं मेने सदृशं पतिम् ॥
तां बुद्धिलक्षणौदार्यरूपशीलगुणाश्रयाम् । कृष्णश्च सदृशीं भार्यां समुद्वोढुं मनो दधे ॥

“There was once a great king of the Vidarbha kingdom named Bhismaka, who had five sons and one beautiful daughter. The eldest was Rukmi, followed by Rukmaratha, Rukmabahu, Rukmesa , Rukmamili and finally the pious lady Rukmini. She, Rukmini, hearing of Krsna’s endowment of form, valour and other qualities being sung by the guests who came to her home, accepted him as a suitable husband for herself. Krsna too, considering her to be endowed with intelligence, good features, good form and other such qualities, accepted her as a suitable wife in his mind.”

बन्धूनामिच्छतां दातुं कृष्णाय भगिनीं नृप । ततो निवार्य कृष्णद्विड् रुक्मी चैद्यममन्यत ॥
तदवेत्यासितापाङ्गी वैदर्भी दुर्मना भृशम् । विचिन्त्याप्तं द्विजं कञ्चित् कृष्णाय प्राहिणोद् द्रुतम् ॥

“Her brothers too wished to marry her to Krsna, but Rukmi (the elder one) forbade them from doing so, as he envied Krsna, resolving to marry her to the king of Cedi, Sigupala. She, the dark-eyed princess of Vidarbha, came to know of this plan and quickly entrusted a trustworthy Brahmana with a message to Krsna.”

The contents of said message span 9 verses wherein Rukmini confesses her love to Krsna and urges him to come and take her in marriage, as she wishes to marry him and not Sisupala. The most important verse is this :

श्वो भाविनि त्वमजितोद्वहने विदर्भानुपागतः समेत्य पृतनापतिभिः परीतः ।
निर्मथ्य चैद्यमगधेन्द्रबलं प्रसह्य मां राक्षसेन विधिनोद्वह वीर्यशुल्काम् ॥

“O invincible one! Tomorrow at my wedding, come in secret with the leaders of your army, surround and crush the forces of Cedi and of the king of Magadha and take me by force in the Raksasa form of marriage.”

Thus, the use of force that characterises Raksasa marriage is against the family of the girl, if they oppose the marriage, and not against the girl herself, who, in this ideal example, is extremely eager to marry the suitor and in fact herself invites him to marry her in the Raksasa form. Far from being a mechanism by which the girl’s consent may be violated, it is a ‘safety net’ available to the girl in case of forced marriage by her family, that she may invite whom she loves to take her by force against her family.

पतिर्हि दैवतं स्त्रीणां पतिर्बन्धुः पतिः गतिः । नान्यां गतिमहं पश्ये प्रमदाया यथा पतिः ॥
जातिष्वपि च वै स्त्रीणां विशिष्टं मे मतिः प्रभो । काय क्लेशेन महता पुरुषः प्राप्नुयात्फलम् । तत्सर्वं लभते नारी सुखेन पतिपूजया ॥
विदेहराजदुहिता चात्र श्लोकमगायत । नास्ति यज्ञः स्त्रियाः कश्चित् श्राद्धं नोपवासकम् । धर्मः स्वभर्तृशुश्रूषया तथा स्वर्गं जयत्युत ॥

“The Husband is the deity of the wife, the husband is the friend and the husband is the refuge. I see no other refuge for a woman than her husband. In fact, I consider the birth as a woman superior to that of a man, before whatever fruit men attain by great bodily tribulations, all that is attainable by a woman merely by honouring her husband. The daughter of the king of Videha (Sita) stated the following verse in this regard: “there is no Vedic ritual obligatory for the women, neither ancestral rites nor fasts, merely by serving her own husband she wins over heaven.”

Valmiki Ramayana 2.118.2

सीतोवाच । विदितं तु ममाप्येतद्यथा नार्याः पतिर्गुरुः ।

“This too is known by me, that the husband is the Guru of the wife.”

Brahmavaivarta 1.9.64-66

पतिरेव गति: स्त्रीणां पतिः प्राणाश्च सम्पदः । धर्मार्थकाममोक्षाणां हेतु: सेतुर्भवार्णवे ॥
पतिर्नारायणः स्त्रीणां व्रतं धर्मः सनातनः । सर्वं कर्म वृथा तासां स्वामिनं विमुखाश्च याः ॥
स्नानं च सर्वतीर्थेषु सर्वयज्ञेषु दक्षिणा । सर्वदानानि पुण्यानि व्रतानि नियमाश्च ये ॥
देवार्चनं चानशनं सर्वाणि च तपांसि च । स्वामिनः पादसेवायाः कलां नार्हन्ति षोडशीम् ॥

“The husband is the refuge of the women, their very life and their wealth, the catalyst for Dharma, wealth, sensual pleasure and liberation, the bridge through the ocean of the material world. Husband is Narayana ( Lord Visnu) for the woman, he is the vow and eternal dharma. All deeds of those who turn away from their husbands are fruitless. Bathing at all the holy water bodies, giving alms at vedic sacrifices, charity, holy vows, offering unto the gods and all austerities, none of these match even the sixteenth part of serving the husband.”

Duties of the wife

Wife as ‘Queen’ of the household

The Rgveda calls on the bride to be a ‘Queen’ over her in-laws in the marriage hymn:

Rgveda Samhita 10.85.46

सम्राज्ञी श्वशुरे भव सम्राज्ञी श्वश्र्वां भव । ननान्दरि सम्राज्ञी भव सम्राज्ञी अधि देवृषु ॥

“Be a queen over your father-in-law and mother-in-law, be a queen over your sisters-in-law and brothers-in-law”

The Puranas too corroborate her leadership of the household:

गार्हस्थ्यस्य च धर्मस्य भार्या भवति स्वामिनी

“The wife is the mistress of the household dharma.”

As such, she is tasked with running the household:

• Managing accounts, cooking, and cleaning.

अर्थस्य संग्रहं चैव व्यये चैव नियोजयेत् । शौचे धर्मेऽन्नपाकं च पारिणाह्यस्य वेक्षणे ॥

“She shall be engaged in the accumulation and spending of money, in cleaning , cooking and taking care of household objects.”

Regarding cleaning, the Bhagavata purana (7.11.26) elaborates :

सम्मार्जनोपलेपाभ्यां गृहभाण्डपरिवर्तने । स्वयं च मण्डिता नित्यं परिमृष्टपरिच्छदा ॥

“A chaste wife should sweep, cleanse, and maintain the home so it remains ever pure and well adorned; she herself should be neatly dressed each day in clean, attractive garments”

Regarding food cooked by the wife, the Padma Purana (2.59.31–32) says:

भार्या पचति यदन्नं स्वहस्तेनामृतोपमम् । तदन्नमुपभुञ्जन्ति पितरो हृष्टमानसाः ।
तेनैव तृप्तिमायान्ति सन्तुष्टाश्च भवन्ति ते ॥

“The food which the wife cooks with her own hand is equal to the ambrosian nectar. The Ancestors partake of it and delight in their mind. They attain satisfaction and are content by it alone.”

Emotionally supporting and advising the husband

Mahabharata 12.250.17

कुपितं वाऽर्थहीनं वा श्रान्तं वाप्युपसेवितुम् । यथा स तुष्टः स्वस्थश्च तथा सन्तोषयेत्पतिम् ॥
यथा कुटुम्बचिन्तायां विवादे वार्थसञ्चये । आहूता तस्य सहायार्थं तथा प्रियहितं वदेत् ॥
अप्रियं च हितं ब्रूयात्तस्य धर्मार्थकाङ्क्षया

“When the husband is angry, poor or tired, she should appease him such that he may become content and healthy. In familial matters , in conflicts, or in matters of finance, she should pleasingly advise him on what is favourable for him, or she may even state it displeasingly, if that is favourable in view of his Dharma and wealth.”

Conclusion

We often hear only one side of the Scriptural statements on spousal status. We often hear that the husband is called the lord and god for the wife, compared to Narayana and Siva, the only dharma whom she is called on to serve and please.

Rarely are we told the other side, that the wife is also called lord and goddess , compared to Laksmi and Parvati, stated to be the supreme dharma for a man whom he is called on to provide for and please.

Valmiki Ramayana 2.118.2

Manusmriti 9.101–102

अन्योन्यस्याव्यभिचारो भवेदामरणान्तिकः । एष धर्मः समासेन ज्ञेयः स्त्रीपुंसयोः परः ॥
तथा नित्यं यतेयातां स्त्रीपुंसौ तु कृतक्रियौ । यथा नाभिचरेतां तौ वियुक्तावितरेतरम् ॥

“‘May mutual fidelity continue till death’, this, in summary, should be understood as the highest duty of Man and Woman. Husband and wife, after they have passed through the rites, should always exert themselves so that they may not become separated and be unfaithful to each other.”

Manusmriti 3.60

सन्तुष्टो भार्यया भर्ता भर्त्रा भार्या तथैव च । यस्मिन्नेव कुले नित्यं कल्याणं तत्र वै ध्रुवम् ॥

“In that family, where the husband is pleased with his wife and the wife with her husband, prosperity will assuredly be lasting.”

Garuda Purana 1.95.25

यत्राविरोधो दम्पत्योस्त्रिवर्गस्तत्र वर्धते

"Where there is no conflict between a couple, the three goals of life abound”

A beautiful verse on the relation of husband and wife in the Siva purana (2.3.54.69) :

उमाशिवसमौ साक्षात्तस्मात्तौ पूजयेद् बुधः ॥
तारः पतिः श्रुतिर्नारी क्षमा सा स स्वयं तपः ।
फलं पतिः सत्क्रिया सा धन्यौ तौ दम्पती शिवे ॥

“the two of them are Siva and Parvati (Uma) in manifest form, therefore the wise worship them; the husband is Omkara and the wife is the Vedas, she is forbearance and he is austerity itself, the husband is the fruit and the wife is the good deeds (which produce it) , together the couple are prosperous in auspiciousness.”

Aside from mere description, there are also plenty of examples in the Sastras of ideal couples. Chief among them are Rama and Sita. Their devotion to each other as demonstrated by the narrative of Ramayana itself is well-known, Sita willingly coming together with Rama in the exile instead of staying at the palace waiting for him, as he himself wanted her to, him mustering the force of the Vanaras, finding her and reaching Lanka to defeat Ravana and rescue her, all this requires no introduction. Aside from the narrative itself however, they both openly express their love and devotion for each other in similar terms:

Valmiki Ramayana 2.27.21

स्वर्गोऽपि च विना वासो भविता यदि राघव । त्वया विना नरव्याघ्र नाहं तदपि रोचये ॥

“If I were to live even in heaven without you, O descendant of Raghu! Then even that I would not desire, O tiger amongst men!”

Valmiki Ramayana 2.30.42

Rama said:

आरभ्य शुभश्रोणि वनवासक्षमाः क्रियाः । नेदानीं त्वदृते सीते स्वर्गोऽपि मम रोचते ॥

“O beautiful one, begin the preparations for the exile. Now even heaven does not interest me without you.

2.4 Dissolution of Marriage

No matter how perfect the marriage, it has to end in one way or the other. The principle verse on the dissolution of marriage is found in at least five different scriptures, the Parasara smrti (4.30) , the Narada smrti (12.97), Garuda purana (1.107.29), Agni purana (154.6) and the Visnu dharmottara purana (2.87.11), it is as follows :

नष्टे मृते प्रव्रजिते क्लीबे च पतिते पतौ । पञ्चस्वापत्सु नारीणां पतिरन्यो विधीयते

Remarriage

“When he is (I) Missing, (II) has died, (III) has taken renunciation, (IV) is impotent or (V) has fallen, in these five cases she is permitted to seek another husband (annulling the previous marriage).”

(I) Missing : in earlier days, men would often go out of town for earning, sometimes very far away. There being no means of quick communication, if the husband does not return for a long time, she is permitted to remarry. The exact lengths of time are mentioned in the Narada smrti (12.98-100)

अष्टौ वर्षाण्युदीक्षेत ब्राह्मणी प्रोषितं पतिम् । अप्रसूता तु चत्वारि परतोऽन्यं समाश्रयेत् ॥
क्षत्रिया षट्समास्तिष्ठेदप्रसूता समाश्रयेत् । वैश्यायां प्रसूता चत्वारि द्वे समे तु तितरा वसेत् ॥
न शूद्रायाः स्मृतः कालो न च धर्मव्यतिक्रमः । विशेषतोऽप्रसूता संवत्सरपरा स्थितिः ॥

“The Brahmana woman should wait eight years for her husband, four years if she is childless, and then take another husband. The ksatriya for six years with child and three without, the Vaisya four years with child and two without. No minimum time is enjoined for the Sudra, neither does she violate dharma (by remarrying). Especially if she is childless, she should not wait more than a year.”

(II) Has died: self-explanatory.

(II) Has taken renunciation : the husband is generally substantially older than the wife. If he has reached the time for renunciation and wishes to do so, and the wife does not, she may remarry after he renounces.

(IV) If he is impotent : as stated, the very purpose of marriage is primarily procreation. If the husband doesn't fulfill his sexual duties, or if he is infertile, the woman may annul the marriage and remarry.

(V) If he has fallen : “fallen” (Patita) is a technical term in Dharmasastra meaning one who has committed a major sin ( Pataka). In such cases too she may nullify and remarry.

Objection : this practice is Kali-varjya, as argued by Acarya Vidyaranya in his commentary on the Parasara smrti on this verse :

अयं च पुनरुद्वाहो युगान्तरविषयः । तथा च आदित्यपुराणम् ।
उढायाः पुनरुद्वाहं ज्येष्ठांशं गोवधं तथा । कलौ पञ्च न कुर्वीत भ्रातृजायां कमण्डलुम् ॥

“This matter of remarriage is of a separate yuga ( and not this one) , as stated in the Aditya-purana : “the remarriage of the married woman, the elder son’s extra share of property, sacrifice of cows, niyoga with the brother-in-law and use of the kamandalu, these five are not to be done in kaliyuga.”

(This verse has also been quoted by Hemadri’s Caturvargacintamani, Mitra misra’s Viramitrodaya , Smrtimuktavali of Krsnacarya and other works of acaryas, though it is absent from any extant Aditya-purana)

Answer : the Parasara smrti itself states in its very beginning (1.24):

कृते तु मानवाः धर्मास्त्रेतायां गौतमाः स्मृताः । द्वापरे शाङ्खलिखिताः कलौ पाराशराः स्मृताः ॥

“The Manusmriti is for the Krtayuga, the Gautama for the treta, the Sankhalikhita for the dvapara and the Parasara is for the Kaliyuga.”

The other Smrtis and sastras may contain content pertaining to other yuga, but the Parasara smrti, itself stated to be meant specifically for Kaliyuga, cannot contain any injunctions “pertaining to other Yugas” (Yugantaravisayah) as claimed by Vidyaranya and others. If a Purana claims anything enjoined by it to be forbidden in Kaliyuga, it would be in direct contradiction with the smrti itself, and must be rejected.

Thereby, to sustain the pramanya of the Aditya purana verse, a reconciliatory approach must be taken. ‘Marriage of one who is already married’ may plausibly be interpreted as referring to the practice of a woman marrying another man while her previous marriage remains valid, i.e., polyandry. This was indeed practiced in previous yugas, the most famous example being Draupadi in the dvapara yuga, and is now forbidden in kaliyuga.

Note that, the dissolution of the previous marriage in these 5 cases is mandatory before pursuing another marriage. This is stated not in this verse but in another parallel verse in the Devala smrti (1548):

नष्टः प्रव्रजितः क्लीबः पतितो राजकिल्बिषी । लोकान्तरगतो वापि परित्याज्यः स्त्रिया पतिः

“A husband who is lost, has taken samnyasa, is impotent, fallen, a traitor or has died, is to be denounced by the wife.”

Widowhood

Right after the verse in the Parasara smrti allowing for remarriage of a widow, it speaks of those women who do not wish to remarry. For them it states (4.31)

मृते भर्तरि या नारी ब्रह्मचर्यव्रते स्थिता । सा मृता लभते स्वर्गं यथा ते ब्रह्मचारिणः ॥

“The wife who, upon the death of her husband, takes a vow of celibacy, she attains heaven upon death, like other celibates.”

The chapter of Parasara concludes with one final option for the widow (4.32)

त्रिंशत्कोट्योर्धकोटिश्च यानि रोमाणि मानुषे । तावत्त्कालं वसेत्स्वर्गं भर्तारं यानुगच्छति ॥

On Sati

“For thirty-five million years , or for as many years as there are hairs on the human body, she stays in heaven, who follows her husband in death.”

This practice of following the husband in death and being burnt on the same pyre as him, is popularly called Sati, and more properly, Sahagamana. Similar injunctions are found in various other Puranas and smrtis. This verse itself has been quoted as being additionally from both Sankha and Angiras smrtis by Vijnanesvara on Yajnavalkya Smrti 1.86 Note that, the option of Sati is given last, first she is told to remarry, if she does not wish to do so, then live as a widow, and only finally is the option for Sahagamana. The mainstream traditional position accepts Sati as a Kamya ( desirable) act, but not even the most conservative of Acaryas consider it obligatory (Naimittika).

There is, however, a minority opinion amongst the traditional Acaryas that considers the act entirely forbidden (Nisiddha) . For example Medhatithi argues in his commentary of Manu 5.157

पुनस्त्रीणामपि प्रतिषिद्ध आत्मत्यागः । यद्यपि "पतिमनुम्रियेरन्" इत्युक्तं तदपि नित्यवदवश्यं कर्तव्यम् । फलस्तुतिस्तत्रास्ति । फलकामायाश्चाधिकारः श्येनतुल्यता । तथैव "श्येनेन हिंस्याद्भूतानि" इत्यधिकारस्यातिप्रवृद्धद्वेषान्धतया सत्यमपि प्रवृत्तो न धर्मत्वम् । एवमिहाप्यतिप्रवृद्धफलाभिलाषायाः सत्यपि प्रतिषेधे तदतिक्रमणेन मरणप्रवृत्त्युपपत्तेर्न शास्त्रीयत्वम् । अतोऽस्त्येव पतिमनुमरणेऽपि स्त्रियाः प्रतिषेधः । किंच "तस्माद्ध न पुरायुषः प्रयादिति" प्रत्यक्षश्रुतिविरोधे स्मृतिर्येषामन्यार्था शक्यते कल्पयितुम् । यथा "वेदमधीत्य स्नायात्" इत्यध्ययनानन्तरं कृतार्थबोधस्य स्नानस्मरणम् ।

“Suicide is forbidden for women, as it is for men. Regarding the Smrti text that ‘She shall die after her husband’ , this also is not an obligatory act that must be done. Also, it is connected with a specific mention of a fruit, and is only to be done by someone desirous of that fruit, and therefore is comparable to the Syena sacrifice. Just as it is said about it in the Vedas, ‘He shall kill beings by performing the Syena’, making its performance possible, but since it is only to be performed by one who is blinded by hatred, its performance cannot be stated to be dharma.

Similarly here, if a woman, desirous of the mentioned fruit, violates the prohibition on suicide and performs Sahagamana, this cannot be said to be Scripture-enjoined. Thus the act of killing herself after the husband is forbidden for the woman. There is also the Vedic text ‘one shall not die till his lifetime has run out’, a smrti which violates an extant Sruti should be assumed to have some other meaning.”

Medhatithi makes a multilayered argument. First, the example of the Syena sacrifice, which is a sacrifice is mentioned in the Vedas, and yet it is considered forbidden as per the Mimamsa sutras, because it is enjoined for the one who desires to kill, but desiring to kill is itself adharma, and thereby the performance of the rite is adharma. Similarly, the rite of Sahagamana, though it and it's fruit is mentioned in the sastra, can only be performed by one who desires to attain heaven by suicide, and since suicide itself is immoral, the practice is still adharma though it is mentioned by sastra.

Alternately, he cited the Sruti ‘He shall not die before his lifespan runs out’ from the Satapatha brahmana 10.2.6.7._ Another such sruti forbidding suicide is the ISa upanisad verse 3:

असुर्या नाम ते लोका अन्धेन तमसावृताः ।
ताँस्ते प्रेत्याभिगच्छन्ति ये के चात्महनो जनाः ॥

“Those realms are called sunless; they are filled with darkness. There go those, after passing away, who killed themselves.”

Since these extant Srutis clearly forbid suicide, Smrti, which derives it's pramanya from an inferred basis on lost sruti, cannot contradict it. If they appear to contradict it, such as by enjoining suicide for the woman if her husband dies, they must be either reinterpreted or considered apramana and rejected.

As stated however, before invoking the hierarchy and rejecting any lower prameana, reconciliation must be ruled out. The mainstream Acaryas have indeed attempted to reconcile this, Apararka for example writes in his commentary on Yajnavalkya smrti 1.87

(If it is objected that) This is forbidden by the sruti “He shall live out his full life” , then this is explained by this being a general rule, and the smrti of ascending the funeral pyre is a specific rule, the general injunction prohibits suicide except in the case of specific injunction . A smrti that contradicts a general sruti cannot be considered invalid, because they differ in scope, namely one general and one specific, and thus there is no contradiction”

Tryambaka in his Stridharmapaddhati adds the example of animal sacrifice. One sruti forbids killing any being, whereas another enjoins it in certain rites. The two are easily recognised as a general case (samanya or utsarga) and a specific case or exception (visesa or apavada).

This however has one obvious flaw. By the definition of general and exceptional injunctions, the exceptional is stronger than the general.

(उत्सर्गापवादयोरपवादो बलीयान्) In the example given, both injunctions were from the Vedas themselves, and so that doesn't matter.

However, in this case, the general injunction is a Vedic injunction and the exceptional would have to be from the smrti, i.e., the Smrti would have to be considered stronger than the Sruti in this case. Even Apararka acknowledges, stating “The specific smrti is stronger than the general sruti and thereby overrides it” (सा च सामान्यविषयश्रुतेर्बलीयसी सती तस्याः सङ्कोचहेतुर्भवति). For a smrti to be considered stronger than or overriding a Sruti is, in any case, an utter absurdity. That is why the

Visaya-bheda reconciliation is suggested for internal contradiction between Smrtis, not between Sruti and Smrti.

विरोधो यत्र तु भवेदत्रयाणां च परस्परम् । श्रुतिस्तत्र प्रमाणं स्याद् द्वयोरर्थे स्मृतिर्वरा ॥
श्रुतिद्वैधं भवेदत्र तत्र धर्मावुभौ स्मृती । स्मृतिद्वैधं तु यत्र स्याद्विषयः कल्प्यतां पृथक् ॥

“Upon contradiction between the three (Sruti, Smrti & purana), the Sruti is supreme. Between the two, Smrti is superior. When two Srutis conflict, both should be considered true (optionally). When two Smrtis conflict, one should consider them to be of different Visayas (General and exceptional)”

The other side also argues that Smrti may act as exception to Sruti, because Smrtis’ authority is based on assuming its basis in a lost Sruti. Thus there is no issue as it is that lost Smrti acting as the exception. This line of reasoning is addressed by Medhatithi while commenting on Manu 9.6:

किन्तु समकक्षत्वात् विकल्पकल्पितप्रयोगयोरव्याघातः । इह तु कल्प्यो वेदः । न च प्रत्यक्षविरोधकल्पनाया अवसरः । न च सम्भवमात्रेण तावता निश्चितः । निश्चितस्तु तद्विरुद्धप्रत्यक्षविधिः । अनिश्चितेन न वा निश्चितं बाध्यते ।

“but in the case of two extant Srutis, they are reconciled by considering optional application, because they are of equal strength. Here (in the case of Smrtis) however, the Vedic basis is merely assumed.

There is no prospect of equal conflict between the extant and the assumed, because it (the Vedic basis) is merely a possibility and not a certainty, whereas the extant Sruti is certain. Never does the uncertain act as the exception (Badhyate) to the certain.”

Therefore, though it is the minority position, it is the stronger position that Sati or Sahagamana is Nisiddha or forbidden.

Objection : it is seen in the Itihasa-purana that many great women performed Sahagamana, for example some wives of Krsna:

रुक्मिणी तथा गान्धारी शैब्या हैमवतीत्यपि । देवी जाम्बवती चैव विविशुर्जातवेदसम् ॥
सत्यभामा तथैवान्या देव्यः कृष्णस्य सम्मताः । वनं प्रविविशू राजंस्तापसे कृतनिश्चयाः ॥

“Rukmini, Gandhari, Saibya, Haimavati and goddess Jambavati, all these (wives of Krsna) entered the funeral pyre. Satyabhama and other goddesses, beloved of Krsna, entered the forest, O king, intent on performing austerities.”

Answer: the primary authority on determination of what is to be done and not to be done can only be direct injunction and not the actions of great personalities. On this the Apastamba dharmasitra (2.13.7-9) says:

दृष्टो धर्मव्यतिक्रमः साहसं च पूर्वेषाम् । तेषां तेजोविशेषेण प्रत्यवायो न विद्यते । तदन्वीक्ष्य प्रयुञ्जानः सीदत्यवरः ॥

“Transgression of dharma and bold acts are seen in men of the past. Due to their distinguished power, they incur no fault from this, but a later man, who imitates them, perishes (i.e., falls into sin)”

Similarly Gautama dharmasutra ( 1.1.3-4)

दृष्टो धर्मव्यतिक्रमः साहसं च महताम् । अवराद् दुर्बल्यात् ॥

“Transgression of dharma and bold acts are see amongst great men (but these are not to be followed) because of the relative weakness of ordinary people.”

Thus, great personalities of the past often, Rsis, Avataras, devas, etc, are seen to perform acts that are contrary to ordinary dharma, but they incur no fault as, due to their high status, the ordinary dharma does not bind them. But if one imitates them he falls into sin. Thereby their injunctions are primarily to be followed and not always their actions.

In this case, it is also seen that other Avataras like Laksmana gave up their body by immersing in water, and here the wives of Krsna did so by immersing in the funeral pyre. Both of these cases are similarly not to be imitated. They are part of the Lila of that Avatara and, if done by ordinary men, are nothing but suicide.

To summarise, Sahagamana (Sati) is given as a final option in the Smrtis like Parasara after remarriage and widowhood, and it is considered a voluntary permissible act by the majority of acaryas. A minority argue that it is forbidden, analogous to the Syena sacrifice, due to explicit prohibitions on suicide in the Vedas. The other side attempts to reconcile this by considering the Vedic passage as general statement and the Smrti passages as exceptions, however this is not tenable because exception is stronger than the general statement but Smrtis can never be stronger than Srutis, as the uncertain can't be exception for the certain. The argument from venerable figures in the itihasasas performing Sahagamana, is further refuted by the passages from Gautama and Apastamba stating that such figures of the past are not to be emulated as dharma did not fully bind them due to their distinguished power. Thus Sahagamana being forbidden is the stronger position.

3. Non-marital Topics

3.1 Property and Inheritance

One of the most frequently misinterpreted verses on women is the following verse from Manusmriti (9.3):

पिता रक्षति कौमारे भर्ता रक्षति यौवने । रक्षन्ति स्थविरे पुत्रा न स्त्री स्वातन्त्र्यमर्हति ॥

“The father should guard her in childhood, the husband in youth and the sons in old age. A woman is never fit for independence."

The issue is that people see only the last phrase and completely ignore that the preceding three phrases of the verse say “Shall protect”, Not “shall rule” or any such thing. In that context the word "Independence" (स्वातन्त्र्य) refers to her being left without protection. This was conveyed by the verse of the Garuda purana we saw earlier as well, which was almost the same except it ended with “and otherwise by other relatives”. The sense being that, failing the designated guardians, someone else should guard her, and she should not be left unprotected. This is stated by Medhatithi in his commentary as well:

नानेन सर्वक्रियाविषयमस्वातन्त्र्यं विधीयते, किं तर्हि नास्त्यत्र स्त्रियाः स्वात्मसंरक्षणाय प्रभावति शक्तिर्विकलत्वात् स्वतः ।

“By this it is not enjoined that she is not to be independent in any matter whatsoever, rather she isn't to be independent for her own protection as she lacks the physical strength to do so herself.”

As an example of matters in which she is independent, we come to the topic of Stridhana.

Stridhana

As stated, women are assigned to account for and spend the general funds of the house, provided by their husband. They possess also, however, their own separate property called Stridhana, defined as:

पितृमातृपतिभ्रातृदत्तमध्यग्न्युपागतम् । आधिवेदनिकाद्यच्च स्त्रीधनं परिकीर्तितम् ॥

“What is given by the father, mother, husband or brothers, what is given during the marriage (by other relations), what is given (as compensation) on supersession, and so on, is called ‘Stridhana’

Regarding ‘and so on’ (ādi), Vijñāneśvara comments:

आदिशब्देन रिक्थक्रयसंविभागपरिग्रहाधिगमप्राप्तम्

“‘and so on’ refers to inheritance, purchase, partition, and whatever else she finds or is given.”

All this is considered the property of the woman, on which she has complete rights. The Katyayana smrti (906) states :

सौदायिके सदा स्त्रीणां स्वातन्त्र्यं परिकीर्तितम् । विक्रये चैव दाने च यथेष्टं स्थावरेऽपि ॥

“Women’s independence with regard to their own property has always been stated, even to sell or give as they please, and even on immovable assets.”

For the male relatives to violate the sanctity of the woman’s right to her own property has both spiritual and legal consequence:

Manusmriti 3.52

स्त्रीधनानि तु ये मोहादुपजीवन्ति बान्धवाः । नारीयानानि वस्त्रं वा ते पापा यान्त्यधोगतिम् ॥

“Those relations who, through folly, live upon the Stridhana such as the bride’s conveyances and clothes, are sinners and fall into the lowest state.”

न भर्ता नैव च सुतो न पिता भ्रातरो न च । आदाने वा विसर्गे वा स्त्रीधने प्रभविष्णवः ॥
यदि ह्यकृततरोऽप्येषां स्त्रीधनं भक्षयेद्बलात् । सवृद्धिकं प्रदाप्यः स्यादण्डं चैव समाप्नुयात् ॥

“Neither husband, nor son, nor father nor also brothers have authority to take or dispose of the Stridhana. If any of them partake of it by force, they shall be punished and be liable to pay the sum with interest.”

On women working

The Hindu scriptures, though equally honouring the husband and wife, nevertheless recognise their roles as different and complementary. The husband is the provider and the wife is the administrator of the house. Thus she is not expected to work. However, if she wishes to work some craft from home, she may do so, but what she earns from this falls in the same common household funds as the earnings of her husband and not her Stridhana.

प्राप्तं शिल्पेषु यद्वित्तं प्रीत्या चैव यदन्यतः । भर्तुः स्वाम्यं भवेत् तत्र शेषं स्त्रीधनं स्मृतम् ॥

“What she earns by working crafts, and what is given by strangers, there the husband has authority also. The rest is Stridhana.”

This is in the case that the woman’s husband is providing for her. If he goes away without making monetary preparations for her, she may work without question ( Manusmriti 9.75) :

प्रोषिते त्वविधायैव जीवेत्शिल्पैरगर्हितैः ॥

“When the husband has gone abroad without providing for her, she shall subsist by unobjectionable industries.”

The Arthasastra of Kautilya also mentions that the state should employ the women without providers in such industries (2.23.2):

ऊर्णावल्ककार्पाससूत्रलक्षणक्षौमाणि च विधवाऽनङ्गकन्याप्रव्रजितादण्डप्रतिहारिणीभिः । रूपाजीवामातृकाभिर्वृद्धराजदासीभिर्व्युपरतकुलोपस्थानदेवदासीभिश्च कारयेत् ॥

“And the king should have wool, tree-bark fibre, cotton, floss, hemp, linen (and so on) manufactured by widows, women with disabilities, unmarried girls, female ascetics, women, women who are serving a sentence, prostitutes, women supported only by their mothers, elderly royal servants, retired attendants and temple women”

Such examples are also in Itihasa, such as Draupadi who worked as a Sairandhri ( hairdresser) for the queen of Virata during her incognito exile, mentioned in book 4 of the Mahabharata.

Inheritance

Of the man having sons

When a man dies, the first and foremost share is of his wife. Only after she has been given her share can there be any further partition:

एतावदेव साध्वीनां चोदितं विधवाधनम् । वसनानशनस्यैव तथैव राजकस्य च ।
धनं व्यपोह्य तच्छिष्टं दायादानां प्रकल्पयेत् ।

“Similarly the widow’s share is enjoined for her clothing, food, etc. only after setting aside her share may further partition be done.”

Regarding the exact size of her share, the Yajnavalkya smrti (2.114):

विभागं चेत्पिता कुर्यादिच्छया विभजेत्सुतान् । ज्येष्ठं वा श्रेष्ठभागेन सर्वे वा स्युः समांशिनः ॥
यदि कुर्यात्समानंशान् पत्नी च स्यादसमांशिका ।

“If the father does the partition amongst the sons, he may, as he wishes, give a special share to the elder son ( Jyesthamsa) or give everyone an equal part ( Samamsa) . If he makes an equal partition, then the share of the wife must also be made equal.”

The father may will his property to be divided in one of two ways, Jyesthamsa and samamesa. The former was a form where there wasn't really a partition, the elder son replaced the father, getting most of the property but also the responsibility of the mother and younger siblings. This form is, as briefly seen earlier, Kalivarjya. Hence only samamsa may be done in Kaliyuga. In that system the wife’s share must be equal to that of the sons.

For the married daughters, as they receive Stridhana from their family and others at marriage, and have their own share in the husband’s property, they receive no separate inheritance from the father upon death.

The case of unmarried daughters is different however. As they have neither been given their due Stridhana at marriage nor do they have a husband to maintain them and in whose property they have share, they must receive inheritance upon their father’s death :

Manusmriti 9.118

स्वेभ्योऽंशेभ्यस्तु कन्याभ्यः प्रदद्युर्भ्रातरः पृथक् । स्वात्स्वादंशाच्चतुर्भागं पतिताः स्युरदित्सवः ॥

“From their respective shares, each brother must separately give a fourth part to the unmarried girls, refusing to do so, they shall become fallen.”

If this 1/4th share from each brother is not enough for the required purpose of marriage and maintenance, then they shall receive the same share as a brother.

कन्यकानां तु दत्तानां चतुर्थो भाग इष्यते । पुत्राणां तु त्रयो भागाः साम्यं त्वल्पधने स्मृतम् ॥

“Of the unmarried girls, a fourth part is ordained; each son shall get only three parts (of his share). In the case that the share is insufficient, all shall receive equal shares.”

Finally, an illustration : let us say a man died leaving his widow, two sons and two daughters, one of whom is unmarried. In the first place the widow shall get her equal share with the two sons i.e., 33.3% and only then further partition will be done. After that, for the unmarried girl, each brother shall give a fourth part from his 33.3% share, i.e., 8.3% or around 16.6% in total for the girl, both brothers receiving 25%. If that is not sufficient for the marriage and maintenance of the girl, each sibling shall receive an equal share of 22.2% each. In any case no portion is set aside for the married girl.

Inheritance of the man having only daughters

If a man has only daughters and no sons, he may declare his daughter to be an ‘Appointed daughter’ (Putrika) :

अपुत्रोऽनेन विधिना सुतां कुर्वीत पुत्रिकाम् । यदपत्यं भवेदस्यां तन्मम स्यात्स्वधाकरम् ॥

“He who has no son may make his daughter an ‘appointed daughter’ in the following manner: [He shall mark the declaration]: ‘The child that may be born of her shall be the performer of my funeral rites’.”

In ancient times Daksa Prajapati himself made ‘appointed daughters’ in this same manner, for the purpose of multiplying his race

Note that declaring the daughter to be a putrika is a mere formality, and in actuality the daughters of a sonless man are Putrikas regardless of any declaration. The Brahma purana as quoted in Vivadaratnakara of acarya candesvara states (1589)

अपुत्रेण तु या कन्या मनसा पुत्रवत्कृता । राज्ञि वा निवन्धे वैश्य समक्षं वाथ कुत्रचित् ॥
प्राग्गर्भस्था वा शुल्कमुक्ता दत्ता वराय वा । मृते पितरि वा दत्ता सा विज्ञेया तु पुत्रिका ॥
पित्र्यादंशात्समं भागं लभते तादृशी सुता ॥

“A man without a son who declared his daughter to be like the son even mentally, or before the king, or the sacred fire, or relatives or anywhere else, or before her pregnancy, or when she is given to a suitor, or even if she is given in marriage after her father has died, should be known as a ‘Putrika’. From the paternal inheritance she receives an equal share (as a son).”

Thus, wherever the father declares her to be a putrika, or even if he does not do so and merely mentally considers her so, in all cases she is a putrika. She continues both the spiritual lineage by her son performing the funeral and ancestral rites and the legal lineage by inheriting the property just as a son would, though married.

Inheritance of the man having no children

If a man has neither sons nor daughters, his property goes entirely to his wife (Brhaspati smrti 1.26.92).

आम्नाये स्मृतितन्त्रे च लोकाचारे च सूरिभिः । शरीरार्धं स्मृता भार्या पुण्यापुण्यफले समा ॥
यस्य नोपहता भार्या देहार्धं तस्य जीवति जीवत्यर्धशरीरार्थ कथमन्यः समाश्रयात् ॥
सकुल्यैर्विद्यमानस्तु पितृभ्रातृसनाभिभिः । असुतस्य प्रपितस्य पत्नी तद्गृहहारिणी ॥

“In the Vedas, in the Smrtis, the Tantras, in worldly affair and by the wise, the wife is considered half of oneself in both good and bad. He whose wife is alive, half of his body lives on. When half of his body is alive, how can anyone else take anything? Even if there is his father, brothers and other relatives, the wife is the inheritor of the property of one who dies childless.”

Inheritance of a woman

Upon her death, the woman’s stridhana is inherited by her daughters:

Visnu smrti 17.21. In all cases, the property of the mother goes to her daughter(s).

मातुः पारिणेयं स्त्रियो विभजेरन् ॥

“The female (children) shall divide the property of the mother.”

If she does not have daughters, the sons may inherit:

मातुर्दुहितरो रिक्थं विभजेरन् ऋणं च यत् । अभावे तदुदासीनाः पुत्रास्तस्या: प्रजा तथा ॥

“The daughters shall divide the inheritance and debt of their mother (amongst themselves). Upon the lack of daughters, her sons (shall inherit).”

If she has no children, her Stridhana defaults either to her husband or her parents, depending on the type of marriage (Manusmriti 9.169-167):

ब्राह्मदैवार्षगान्धर्वप्राजापत्येषु यद्वसु । अप्रजायामतीतायां भर्तुरेव तदिष्यते ॥
यत् त्वस्याः स्याद् धनं दत्तं विवाहेष्वासुरादिषु । अप्रजायामतीतायां मातापित्रोस्तदिष्यते ॥

“It is ordained that the property of women married by the ‘Brahma,’ the ‘Daiva,’ the ‘Arsa,’ the ‘Gandharva,’ or the ‘Prajapatya’ form shall go to her husband alone if she dies childless. But the property given to a woman on the ‘Asura’ or other (inferior) forms of marriage has been held to belong to her parents upon her dying childless.”

3.2 Crimes against Women

It is good to hear the talks of the Mahima and the status of the women. In reality however, not all men respect it. Thus the scriptures provide not only talks of spiritual status but legally enforceable punishments when that status is violated.

Verbal assault

The Yajnavalkya smrti (2.204) and Agni Purana (258.3) both mention in their sections on verbal assault (Vakparusya) that the fine for insulting women is twice that of insulting a man of the same class:

अर्धोऽधर्मेषु द्विगुणः परस्त्रीषूत्तमेषु च ।

“The fine (on insulting) a person of inferior rank is half (that of an equal), and is double in the case of women and the superior.”

Not just insulting women directly, but insulting someone by bringing in their mother or sister is also punishable (Yajnavalkya smrti 2.205):

‘(Sexually) approach your sister or mother’; the one who curses in this way shall be fined 25 panas by the king.

That one shouldn't verbally upset the mother or should be clear by the earlier sections on their Mahima and status. This is also legally enforceable ( Manusmriti 8.275)

अभिगन्तास्मि भगिनीं मातरं वा तवेति ह । शपन्तं दापयेद्राजा पञ्चविंशतिकं दमम् ॥

“One who reviles the mother, the father, the wife, the child or the guru, he is to be fined a hundred. Also one who doesn't give way to elders…”

Abandonment

The condemnability of abandoning one’s wife and mother should be clear from the Mahima of the mother and the status of the wife, as seen earlier. These are not just idealistic statements; they are legally enforceable rights:

Manusmriti 8.389

न माता न पिता न स्त्री न पुत्रस्त्यागमर्हति । त्यजन्नपतितानेतान् राजा दण्ड्यः शतानि षट् ॥

“Neither the mother nor father nor wife nor son are fit to be abandoned. The one who abandons any of these, except if they are fallen, is to be fined six hundred panas by the king.”

Yajnavalkya 1.73

त्यजन्दाप्यस्तृतीयांशमद्रव्यो भरणं स्त्रियाः ॥

“If he abandons his wife, he must pay a third of his wealth (to her). If he does not have accumulated wealth, he should pay her maintenance.”

Devala smrti (as quoted in Vivadaratnakara 1229)

स्वदारांस्त्यजतो मोहात् प्रस्थस्यायमयोचिनः । धर्मवंशपरित्यक्तुर्निष्कृतिर्न विधीयते ॥

“No penance is possible for the man who, out of delusion, abandons his wife. He is the doer of grave injustice and gives up dharma and his lineage”

Adultery

The man who cheats on his wife and does adultery, is said to have performed an equivalent offence as killing his wife :

स्त्रीहत्यायां च यत्पापं परस्त्रीगमने तथा

“What sin one gets by murdering one’s wife, the same is that of approaching the wife of another.”

Visnu Purana (3.11.126) states that an adulterer goes to hell:

परदारान् गच्छेत् मनसापि कथञ्चन । मृतो नरकमभ्येति हीयतेऽत्रापि चायुषः ।
परदाररतिः पुंसामिह चामुत्र भीतिदा ॥

“one should not have recourse to a woman other than his wife, even mentally. The one who does so, his lifespan is shortened and he goes to hell upon death. Thus, interest in women other than his wife is terrible for men, here and hereafter.”

There are also legally enforceable punishments for it:

Manusmriti 8.352

परदाराभिमर्शेषु प्रवृत्तान्नृन्महीपतिः । उद्वेजनकरैर्दण्डैश्छिन्नयित्वा प्रवासयेत् ॥

“Those men who are addicted to intercourse with the wives of other men, the king shall banish after having branded them with terror-inspiring punishments.”

Apastamba dharmasitra 2.26.20

संनिपाते वृते शिश्नच्छेदनं सबीजस्य

“Upon the committing of adultery, (the punishment is) the castration of the penis along with the testicles.”

Violence

Women are, much like Brahmanas and cows, frequently declared to be Inviolable ( Avadhya), even if they had committed a fault.

Satapatha Brahmana 11.4.3.2

He (Prajapati) said :"Women are Sri. Indeed, one does not strike a woman."

Mahabharata 1.146.29

अवध्याः स्त्रिय इत्याहुर्धर्मज्ञा धर्मनिश्चये ।

"Women are inviolable, thus state the knowers of dharma as the conclusive dharma."

अवध्यास्तु स्त्रियः सृष्टा मन्यन्ते धर्मचिन्तकाः ।

“Women are created inviolable , thus accept the thinkers of dharma.”

प्रहरन्ति न वै स्त्रीषु कृतागाःस्वपि जन्तवः ।

"No being should strike women, even if they are at fault."

अवध्याश्च स्त्रियः प्राहुस्त्र्ययोनिगतापि वा ।

"Women are inviolable, even those amongst the animal species."

The Varaha Purana (126.38) also gives this legal force:

स्त्रियं चैवापि हिंसन्ति कामकाराश्च ये नराः । ते दण्ड्याश्चैव वध्याश्च राजधर्माद्यथार्हतः ॥

“Those men who, dominated by desire, do violence against women, are to be punished or even educated by the ruler, as per their crime.”

Domestic violence

Another frequently misinterpreted verse is Manusmriti 8.299:

भार्या पुत्रश्च दासश्च प्रेष्यो भ्राता तथानुजः । प्राप्तापराधास्ताड्याः स्यूरज्ज्वा वेणुदलेन वा ॥

“The wife, the son, the servant and the younger brother, if they have committed fault, are fit to be beaten (Tadana).”

In the case of the son, younger brother, and servant, one may take the standard meaning of “Tadana,” that is, physical beating, as corroborated by other dharmasastras (Yajnavalkya 1.155, ‘पुत्रं शिष्यं च ताडयेत्’). In the case of the wife, however, that meaning cannot be taken, as it would contradict the many pramanas, including Sruti, that women should never be struck, even if they are at fault.

मार्गस्थापनापायविधिप्रश्रयम्, न ताडनविधिरेव । तेन वाग्दण्डादिरपि कर्तव्यः ।

“Here merely guiding them to the right path is ordained, and not actually beating. By this it should be understood that verbal ‘beating’ should be done.”

Thus the word “Tadana” in this verse must be taken to mean verbal censure on when any of the listed members have committed a fault, and not actual physical beating.

Rape

Rape is considered one of the gravest offences. Immediate execution is enjoined for it across scriptures:

Manusmriti 8.364

योऽकामां दूषयेत् कन्यां स सद्यो वधमर्हति ।

“He who defiles an unwilling girl is to be executed immediately.”

Kātyāyana smṛti 830

स्त्रीषु वृत्तोपभोगः स्यात्प्रसह्य पुरुषो यदा । वधे तत्र प्रवर्तेत कार्यातिक्रमणं हि तत् ॥

“When a man has intercourse with a woman by force, he is to be executed, for that is a grave violation of appropriate behavior.”

Visnu dharmottara purana 2.72.108

योऽकामां दूषयेत्कन्यां स सद्यो वधमर्हति । यश्च संरक्षणे तत्र पुरुषः स तथा भवेत् ॥

“He who defiles an unwilling girl is to be executed immediately. Any man who assists him in that shall also receive the same punishment.”

Status of the victim

In modern India, there is a prevalent attitude of blaming the victim of rape, socially isolating her, etc. the scriptures however make it clear that no fault attaches to her:

बलात्संदूषयेद्यस्तु परभार्यां नरः क्वचित् । वधो दण्डो भवेत् तस्य नापराधो भवेत् स्त्रियाः ॥

“If a man forcibly defiles the wife of another, the punishment shall be execution. There is no fault of the woman.”

Atri smrti 194–195

बलात्कारेण भुक्ता वा चौरभुक्ता तथापि वा । न त्याज्या दूषिता नारी न कामोऽस्या विधीयते ॥
ऋतुकाल उपासीत पुष्पकालेन शुद्ध्यति ॥

“If a woman is violated forcibly, or by a thief, she is not to be abandoned. One should not have intercourse with her until her periods, at which point she is purified.”

Punishment of women

Thus the protections provided to women via punishments of the crime against them have been seen. In the case that a woman herself does a punishables act, the Katyayana smrti (487) states:

सर्वेषु चापराधेषु पुंसो योऽर्थदमः स्मृतः । तदर्धं योषितो दण्ड्याः पुंसोऽर्धं कर्तनम् ॥

“Whatever is the fine or punishment for a man for any crime, it shall be half in the case of women. If the punishment for the man is death, for the women it shall be cutting off of a limb.”

3.3 Clothing and Ornaments

Garments

The principle injunction regarding clothing for women comes from the Sankha-likhita dharmasitra, as quoted in Mitaksara, Viramitrodaya, Smrtimuktavali and many other texts. It states:

न नाभिं दर्शयेत् । आगुल्फाद् वासः परिदध्यात् । न स्तनौ विवृतौ कुर्यात् । नानुत्तरीयं निर्गच्छेत् ।

“She should not show her navel. She should drape the clothing till her ankles. Her breasts should not be open. She should not go out without her Uttariya”

These injunctions were fulfilled with three items of clothing, which find mention across scriptures and other literature. For example in Amarakosa 2.6. they are:

1. Antariya : also called Paridhana, it is a loose garment draped from the navel to the ankles as per the required covering. Modern Lehenga, Dhoti, Chaniya etc are examples of it.

2. Kurpasaka : also called Cola, Stanapatta, etc, it is a strap of clothing tied around the chest to cover the breasts. Modern Coli, blouse, etc are examples.

3. Uttariya: also called Sankhyana, it is a loose scarf draped on both shoulders, both arms or one shoulder and one arm. Only required when going out. Modern Dupatta is an example.

3.4 On Purdah

A very commonly accepted idea amongst Hindus, especially in the north and especially in the rural areas, is that women ought to cover their head, or even their face. One may see women of the house in the north rush to drape the ghunghat over their hair and face when a man other than their husband or blood relatives enters the home, or put their Saree’s dupatta over their hair when entering a temple. Even in the shows or films adapting stories from Hindu scriptures, all the good women cover their head, and only the demonic women are shown without such covering.

One may notice, however, that the sastras enjoin women to cover their lower body from the navel down to slightly above the ankles, and their chest, but nothing at all is stated about covering the head or face. In fact, the numerous head and face ornaments shown just above, would all become invisible and thereby useless, if the head or face were covered.

If this practice is not in the scriptures, then where has it come from? Both the word ‘Purdah’, which is a Persian word, and its geographic prevalence, the north of India, which was occupied by Muslim powers for far longer than the south, give us the answer. It is a borrowing of a muslim concept, and not a Hindu Scriptural one. Historian A.S. Altekar writes in his book, ‘the position of women in hindu civilization’ , pages 206-207:

“There are absolutely no traces of any Purdah observed within the family in the Hindu period. The general adoption of the Purdah system by the ruling and aristocratic families of Hindu community is subsequent to the advent of the Muslim rule. It was accepted by Hindu society in imitation of the manners of the conquerors.”

Further he states:

“This happened almost universally in northern India, where the Muslim rule and culture were in ascendancy for a long time. In the Deccan, the Muslim influence was superficial, and so the Purdah system got no footing in the Hindu society there”

Not only is the practice itself absent from Hindu scriptures and pre-islamic Hindu society as a whole, but even the rationale for it is explicitly rejected. In Islam, purdah is done to protect the women of the house from the male gaze. However, lord Rama himself states :

Valmiki Ramayana 6.117.23–24

न गृहाणि न वस्त्राणि न प्राकारास्तिरस्क्रियाः । नेदृशा राजसत्कारा वृतमावरणं स्त्रियाः ॥
व्यसनेषु न कृच्छेषु न युद्धेषु स्वयंवरे । न क्रतौ नो विवाहे वा दर्शनं दूष्यते स्त्रियाः ॥

“Neither the house nor clothes nor walls nor veils/curtains nor royal protection truly protects the woman, but only her character. Whether in times of crisis, in war, in a choosing ceremony, in religious events or in weddings, does public appearance ever pollute a woman.”

This sentiment is echoed in non-religious literature also. In the kathasaritasagara, 7.2, a character named Ratnaprabha, is told by her husband to stay within the house. She tells him:

रक्षा चान्तःपुरेणेदृङ्नैवमेतन्मतं मम । महता रक्षणे शीलेनैव कुलस्त्रियः ॥

“‘By staying in the inner quarters of the house there is protection’, this I do not accept. Only by the greatness of their good character are the women of the house protected.”

Thus, the practice of purdah, viz, compulsorily covering anything other than the lower body from navel to ankle and the chest, and general seclusion inside the home, is entirely absent from Hindu scriptures and ancient Hindu society, and even its base rationale is explicitly rejected.

3.5 Purity

General dharmas

Dharma, in general, is highly subject to the individual. It is different depending on one's Varna, Asrama and gender, as we have seen the special injunctions for women on many issues. A few dharmas, however, are common (Samanya) to all.

अहिंसा सूनृता वाणी सत्यं शौचं दया क्षमा । वर्णिनां लिङ्गिनां चैव सामान्यं धर्म उच्यते ॥

“Minimal violence (Ahimsa), pleasant speach, truthfulness, purity (Sauca), compassion and patience; these are common dharmas for people of every Varna and gender”

Most of these are self-explanatory. Only Ahimsa and Sauca require some elaboration.

Ahimsa: In the case of women, the primary application of ahimsa is with regard to diet. The Mahabharata states (13.116.25–26):

अहिंसा परमो धर्मस्तथाहिंसा परं तपः । अहिंसा परमं सत्यं ततो धर्मः प्रवर्तते ॥
न हि मांसं तृणात्काष्ठादुपलाद्वापि जायते । हत्वा जन्तुं ततो मांसं तस्माद्दोषोऽस्य भक्षणे ॥

“Ahimsa is the greatest dharma , ahimsa is the greatest tapas. Ahimsa is the greatest truth, from which dharma proceeds. Meat is not procured from grasses, trees or rocks, only by killing a being is there meat. Therefore there is fault in eating it.”

However the exceptions to this are also stated. (Manusmriti 5.27)

प्रोक्षितं भक्षयेन्मांसं ब्राह्मणानां च काम्यया । यथाविधि नियुक्तस्तु प्राणानामेव चात्यये ॥

“One may eat meat which is sacrificed with proper procedure, or for feeding brahmanas, or upon threat to his life.”

Regular purification (Nitya Sauca)

Purification can be divided based on two bases. Firstly, that which is to be done daily (Nitya) and secondly that which is to be done due to impurity from a particular occasion (Naimittika). Of these, we shall first see Nitya.

Internal purity

Further, purity is divided as internal and external (Daksa smrti 5.6):

शौचं तु द्विविधं प्रोक्तं बाह्यमाभ्यन्तरं तथा । अशौचाद्बाह्यं वरं बाह्यं तस्मादाभ्यन्तरं वरम् ॥

“Purity is of two types, viz, external and internal. Better than complete impurity is external purity, better than external purity is internal purity.”

Thus, internal purity is the most important. A very famous verse of the Brahmavaivarta Purana (1.17.17) states:

अपवित्रः पवित्रो वा सर्वावस्थां गतोऽपि वा । यः स्मरेत्पुण्डरीकाक्षं स बाह्याभ्यन्तरः शुचिः ॥

“Whether he is externally impure or pure, or whatever state he is in, the one who thinks of the supreme lord, is pure from inside and out”

For this reason both husband and wife are enjoined to focus their minds on the supreme lord, Visnu or siva or devi depending on sect, immediately after waking up (Vyasa smrti as quoted in Stridharmapaddhati and smrtimuktaphala):

षष्ठा विभज्य रजनीं चरमांशे प्रबोधितः । पत्न्या सह हरिं ध्यात्वा धर्ममर्थं च चिन्तयेत् ॥

“The night is divided into 6 sections (called Muhirtas), one should wake up in the final one (Brahma muhirta, around an hour before sunrise) and, along with his wife, focus his mind on the supreme lord. Only thereafter he should think of matters of ritual and business.”

The method of external purity is bathing, as stated in Daksa smrti 2.6-7 :

उषःकाले तु सम्प्राप्ते शौचं कुर्याद्यथाविधि । ततः स्नानं प्रकुर्वीत दन्तधावनपूर्वकम् ॥
अत्यन्तमलिनः कायो नवच्छिद्रसमन्वितः । स्रवत्येव दिवा रात्रौ प्रातःस्नानेन शुध्यति ॥

“With the approach of dawn one should perform Sauca (here, urination and defecation) as per the procedure. Thereafter, one should brush his teeth and bathe. The body, having nine openings, becomes extremely impure due to the secretions from these (sweat etc) day and night. By the morning bath, it is purified”

Occasional purification (Naimittika Sauca)

For women, there are primarily two occasions that warrant special purification. Menstruation and childbirth.

Menstruation

Earlier it was seen that, due to the gift of three gods, women are considered always pure. Yet now menstruation, a natural part of womanhood itself, is a state of impurity requiring purification? Isn't this contradictory? No, in fact, one is the direct consequence of the other. The Baudhayana dharmasitra states (2.2.4.3-5)

स्त्रियः पवित्रममृतं नैता दुष्यन्ति कर्हिचित् । मासि मासि रजो ह्यासां दुरितान्यपकर्षति ॥
सोमः शौचं ददौ तासां गन्धर्वः शिक्षितां गिरम् । अग्निश्च सर्वमेध्यत्वं तस्मान्निष्कल्मषाः स्त्रियः ॥

“Women have an unparalleled means of purification, because of which nothing renders them (permanently) impure. Every month, their menstrual blood removes all their impurity. ‘Soma gave them external purity, gandharva gave them pure speech, Agni gave them ritual purity. Therefore women have no impurity.’”.

मृत्तोयैः शुध्यते शौचं नदी वेगेन शुध्यति । रजसा स्त्री मनोदुष्टा संन्यासेन द्विजोत्तमाः ॥
अद्भिर्गात्राणि शुध्यन्ति मनः सत्येन शुध्यति । विद्यतपोभ्यां भूतात्मा बुद्धिर्ज्ञानेन शुध्यति ॥

“What needs purification is purified by clay and water; the river is purified by its current; A woman, if impure even by mind, by menstruation; and Brahmanas by renunciation.”

Therefore the women, whose menstruation is compared to a flowing river, are purer than men who are thereby like still water.

Thus menstruation is framed not as a defect of womanhood, but a great merit. It is not an impurity, rather it is supreme purity that manifests through temporary impurity.

Certain rules also have to be followed during this time, such as avoiding sex (Manusmriti 4.40):

नोपगच्छेत् प्रमत्तोऽपि स्त्रियमर्तवदर्शने ।

“one should never (sexually) approach a menstruating woman, even if he is mad with desire.”

It was earlier seen that the husband is enjoined to take care of the wife during menstruation by the Katyayana smrti. This is because at this time they are told to cease religious and household duties:

The Angiras Smrti states (37):

साध्वाचारो न तावत्त्याज्यो रजो यावत्प्रवर्तते । वृत्ते रजसि गम्या स्त्री गृहकर्मणि चैव हि ।
स्नात्वा रजस्वला चैव चतुर्थेऽह्नि विशुध्यति ॥

“She should not perform her religious duties (Sadhvacara) until there is flow of menstrual blood. When it has stopped, she may resume household work and other activities, having bathed on the fourth day (after the start of menstruation), she is purified.”

Childbirth

Many cultures observe a designated period of impurity of the mother after childbirth. The Hindu scriptures are unique, however, in that the impurity (called Sutaka) applies to the father as well.

जननमरणयोः संनिपाते समानो दशरात्रः ॥ जनने तावन्मातापित्रोर्दशाहमाशौचम् ॥
मातुरित्येके तदाहारात् ॥ पितुरित्यपरे शुक्रप्राधान्यात् ॥ मातापित्रोरेव तु संस्कारसामान्यात् ॥

“The impurity for both childbirth and death in the family is the same, ie., ten nights. In the case of childbirth there is a ten day impurity for both mother and father. Some say it is only for the mother, as she is the one who has given birth. Some others state it is the father, because of the contribution of semen. The correct view is that it is for both, due to their mutual contribution.”

3.6 Education

Education in the Vedas

The discussion of women’s education in the Hindu scriptures must be separated into two separate discussions: (i) education in the Vedas and (ii) education in all the topics other than the Vedas. These are very different.

The general restriction of women from Vedic study

The Vedas are the most sacred scriptures of Hinduism. They are not a book that is bought and read; rather, they are heard (hence the name Sruti, “that which is heard”) from a guru. This hearing is not simple: it begins with initiation through the sacred thread ceremony, which is performed only for the first three Varnas, not the Sudra. Afterward, the child lives with the Guru, serves him, and follows hundreds, if not thousands, of rules. During this time, he must beg for alms (Bhiksatana). Only then may he learn the Vedic mantras and their use in yajnas (fire rituals) from the guru.

In the case of general women, however, the Manusmriti(2.67) states:

वैवाहिको विधिः स्त्रीणां संस्कारो वैदिकः स्मृतः । पतिसेवा गुरौ वासो गृहकार्याग्निपरिक्रिया ॥

“For women, marriage is equivalent to the Vedic initiation rite, serving the husband equivalent to living with (and serving) the guru, and the performance of household duties equivalent to the performance of Vedic fire rituals.”

Therefore women in general are not enjoined to study the Vedas. It is for them (and the Sudras and uninitiated) that the other Vedic literatures, the itihasas and puranas, called the fifth Veda, have been composed.

Regarding this, the Bhagavata Purana (1.4.25) states:

स्त्रीशूद्रद्विजबन्धूनां त्रयी न श्रुतिगोचरा । कर्मश्रेयसि मूढानां श्रेय एवं भवेदिह ॥
इति भारतमाख्यानं कृपया मुनिना कृतम् ॥

“for the Women, Sudras and uninitiated men varnas there is not study of the Sruti. for their benefit, out of compassion the sage Vyasa composed the Mahabharata.”

Similarly Devi bhagavata purana 1.3.21:

स्त्रीशूद्रद्विजबन्धूनां न वेदश्रवणं मतम् । तेषामेव हितार्थाय पुराणानि कृतानि च ॥

“For women, Sudras and uninitiates amongst the other three varnas, study of the Vedas is not accepted. Is for their benefit that the Puranas were created.”

The exception (Brahmavadinis/Uttamastri)

As emphasised however, this restriction is a general rule and is subject to any exceptions that may be enjoined regarding it in the scriptures. And exceptions there must be, because we see in the scriptures that several hymns of the Vedas have themselves been composed by women, and in the Upanisads, Itihasas and puranas all mention women learned in Vedas, such as Gargi, Sulabha and Madalasa. Thus, the higher (Uttama) category of women, called Brahmavadinis, have been enjoined to study Vedas, as an exception to the general rule. The principle verse on this is Harita smrti 21.23:

द्विविधाः स्त्रियः ब्रह्मवादिन्यः सद्योवध्वश्च । तत्र ब्रह्मवादिनीनामुपनयनमग्न्याधानं वेदाध्ययनं स्वगृहे च भिक्षाचर्येति ॥
सद्योवधूनां चोपस्थिते विवाहे

“Women are of two types, Brahmavadinis and sadyovadhts. Amongst them, of the Brahmavadinis, the Vedic initiation, fire rituals, study of the Vedas and Bhiksatana within their own house is enjoined. Of the Sadhyovadhis, marriage (is the equivalent)”.

Thus all the earlier verses seen pertain to the Sadyovadhis, and not to the Brahmavadinis.

Amongst Acaryas, Madhvacarya, the founder of one of the three greatest schools of Vedanta and one of the most influential Acaryas of all time, has repeatedly mentioned the exception of Brahmavadinis in his writings. He writes in his Brahma sutra bhasya ,1.1.1:

स्त्रीशूद्रब्रह्मबन्धूनां तन्त्रज्ञानाधिकारिता । आहुर्युक्तमनस्त्रीणामधिकारं तु वैदिके ॥

“Of the Women, Sudras and uninitiates of the other three Varnas, there is right to the study of Tantra ( as opposed to Veda). Of the Higher category of women, however, right to Vedas is also stated.”

And in his Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (29.33):

जेयं सर्वत्र वर्णस्थस्त्रीभिर्वेदान् विनाखिलम् । वेदा अप्युत्तमस्त्रीभिः कृष्णादिभिराखिलाः ॥
देव्यो मुनिस्त्रियश्चैव नरादिकुलजा अपि ।

“Everything should be studied by the women of the three Varnas, except the Vedas. By the Higher category of women, such as Draupadi, even the Vedas are to be studied. This “Higher category” includes Goddesses, wives of the sages, and even those born in Human births.”

This should be noted for the later discussion of study of topics other than the Vedas as well. He also writes in the Apasudradhikarana (1.3.36) of the Brahma sutras, the section that restricts Vedas for Sudras:

उत्तमस्त्रीणां तु न शूद्रवत् । “सपत्नि मे पराधम” इत्याद्यधिकारदर्शनात् संस्काराभावेनाभावस्तु सामान्येन । अस्ति च तासां संस्कारः स्त्रीणां प्रधानकर्म यथोपनयनं तथेति स्मृतेः ।

“The case of the higher category of women is not the same as the Sudras, because by mantras such as “May my co-wife be destroyed” (Rgveda samhita 10.145.2) their right to the Vedas must be admitted.”

“If it is objected that they do not have the Initiatory ritual, then that is only the general rule. There is also initiation for them similar to the Upanayana enjoined in the Smrti (Harita).”

Madhva brilliantly argues from a Rgveda mantra that states “May my co-wife be destroyed”. This Mantra can only be used by someone who has co-wives in the first place, i.e., a woman, thereby at least some of them must be admitted to have right to the Vedas, because otherwise this mantra would have no application (Niravakasa) which can't be true, per Mimamsa. This is a more forceful argument than was discussed earlier.

Further he states that regarding the objection that the initiatory ritual (Samskara) is not enjoined for them ( due to the verse of the Manusmriti) then this is also just the general rule, and the exceptional initiation of brahmavadinis is stated in the smrti (i.e., Harita, though he has not mentioned it by name). Jayatirtha, the foremost sub-commentator on Madhva, states this in plainer terms:

वेदाधिकाराभावः सामान्यन्यायेनोक्तो न तु नियमेन । अतस्तस्य विशेषापवादे न दोष इति भावः ।

“The sense is that the lack of right to Vedic study is merely a general rule, and not an absolute one. Therefore by a more specific exception there is no issue.”

Thus the exception of Brahmavadini/Uttamastri to the general restriction, is well-established by both scriptures and Acaryas.

Examples of Brahmavadinis

There are ample examples of Brahmavadinis from Vedas to itihasas to puranas. In the Anukramant of the Rgveda samhita, 29 separate Rsikas are credited as seers of Mantras in it.

In the Brhadaranyaka Upanisad (2.4) :

अथ ह याज्ञवल्क्यस्य द्वे भार्ये बभूवतुः मैत्रेयी च कात्यायनी च । तयोर्ह मैत्रेयी ब्रह्मवादिनी बभूव । मैत्रेयीति होवाच याज्ञवल्क्यः प्रब्रजिष्यन् वा अरेऽहमस्मात्स्थानादस्मि हन्त तेऽनया कात्यायन्यान्तं करवाणीति ॥

“Now, Yajnavalkya had two wives, Maitreyi and Katyayani, of these Maitreyi was a Brahmavadini. Once Yajnavalkya said: “O maitreyi, I shall now renounce and leave this place. Therefore let me make a will for you and Maitreyi.”

सा होवाच मैत्रेयी यन्नु म इयं भगः सर्वा पृथिवी वित्तेन पूर्णा स्यात्स्यां न्वहं तेनामृता स्यामिति । नेति होवाच याज्ञवल्क्यः यथैवोपकरणवतां जीवितं तथैव ते जीवितं स्यादमृतत्वस्य तु नाशास्ति वित्तेनेति ।
सा होवाच मैत्रेयी येनाहं नामृता स्यां किमहं तेन कुर्यां यदेव भगवान्वेद तदेव मे ब्रूहीति ॥

“Maitreyi said, “If I were to attain this entire earth filled with enjoyments, shall I become immortal by them?” Yajnavalkya said, “No, no, your life will be like those of the wealthy people, but there is no hope of immortality by wealth.” Maitreyt said: “what shall I do with that by which I won't be immortal? let your lordship tell me only that by which I can be immortal.”” [ Whereafter Yajnavalkya narrated to her the Vedantic truths]

The Brhadaranyaka Upanisad (3.8) also contains a separate conversation involving Yajnavalkya where the Brahmavadini Garg Vacaknavi asks him questions on Vedantic topics.

In the Mahabharata(12.308). There is a conversation on Vedantic topics between the Brahmavadint Sulabha and the enlightened king Janaka. At the end of she states (verse 308-311):

साहं तस्मिन्कुले जाता भर्तर्यसति मन्दिरे । विनीतां मोक्षधर्मेषु चराम्येका मुनिव्रतम् ॥
नास्मि संप्रतिच्छन्ना न परस्वाभिमानिनी । न धर्मसङ्करकरी स्वधर्मेऽस्मि धृतव्रता ॥
मोक्षे ते भावितां बुद्धिं श्रुत्वाहं कुशलैषिणी । तव मोक्षस्य चायस्य जिज्ञासार्थमिहागता ॥

“I was born in the house of King Pradhana, finding no worthy husband, I became educated in the path of Moksa and took samnyasa. I am not a pretendress ascetic, neither do I take pride in others. I am not the confuser of dharmas, in fact I am devoted to my own dharma only. Having heard that your mind is towards Moksa, and being curious about this Moksa of yours, I had come here.”

Firstly stating her introduction as one who had received education in Moksa and taken samnyasa upon not finding a worthy husband, she addresses some possible allegations against her. She says she is not a pretendress, but a real, bona fide ascetic. She is not following the dharma of someone else, but own dharma, because moksa is the dharma of women too and not just men. She says that she does not take pride in anyone else, as other women take pride in their husbands, but only in herself. The Mahabharata (13.2.82) states regarding the princess Sudarsana:

एषा हि तपसा स्वेन संयुक्ता ब्रह्मवादिनी ।

“Indeed, endowed with her austerities, she is a Brahmavadini”

The Mahabharata (13.14.64) also speaks of the wife of Atri:

अत्रेर्भार्यापि भर्तारं सन्त्यज्य ब्रह्मवादिनी । नाहं तस्य मुनेर्भूयो वशगा स्यां कथञ्चन । इत्युक्त्वा सा महादेवमगच्छच्छरणं किल ॥

“The wife of Atri too, giving up her husband, became a Brahmavadini, saying ‘Never again shall I be under the control of that sage’ , and took the refuge of lord Siva (i.e., began to worship him)”

The Brahmanda Purana (2.8.82) and Vayu Purana (2.9.72) state:

दत्तात्रेयस्तस्य ज्येष्ठो दुर्वासास्तस्य चानुजः । यवीयसी सुता तस्य मबला ब्रह्मवादिनी ॥

“The eldest son (of atri) was Dattatreya, and the younger was Durvasa. His younger daughter was Abala, a brahmavadini.”

The Markandeya Purana narrates the story of queen Madalasa over multiple chapters (22–27), who educates her son in Vedanta at a young age. She is called “Madalasa, the higher woman” (26.34), similar to Madhvacarya’s “Uttamastri”.

These were only some of the examples present in the scriptures.

Objection by Vidyaranya and Devana

Vidyaranya in his commentary on the Parasara smrti and Devana in his Smrticandrika, however, object to the Validity of this practice in the current age. Their argument is roughly the same, and so I will present Vidyaranya’s version:

ननु—विवाहरहितानामपि ब्रह्मवादिनीनामुपनयनाध्ययनादिभिरुत्तमलोकसंभवात् । अत एव हारीतेनोक्तम्—“द्विविधाः स्त्रियः । ब्रह्मवादिन्यः सद्योवध्वश्च । तत्र ब्रह्मवादिनीनामुपनयनमग्न्याधानम्...” ।

“Some object that: “the sacred thread ceremony and the attainment of higher lokas may be done by unmarried Brahmavadinis, because Harita has stated: ‘Women are of two types, Brahmavadinis and sadyovadhis. Amongst them, of the Brahmavadinis, the Vedic initiation, fire rituals...’”.

मैवम्, तस्य कल्पान्तरविषयत्वात् । तथा च यमः—पुरा कल्पे कुमारिणां मौञ्जीबन्धनमिष्यते । अध्यापनं च वेदानां सावित्रीवाचनं तथा ॥
पिता पितृव्यो भ्राता वा नैनामध्यापयेत्परः । स्वगृहे चैव कन्याया भैक्षचर्या विधीयते ॥

“This is not so, because : this is a matter pertaining to a different age (and not this one). Yama smrti states —‘In the earlier age (purakalpe) , the tying of sacred thread is expected for women, as well as teaching them the Vedas and chanting Gayatri mantra. They may only be taught by their Father, uncle or brother and no one else. Performing Bhiksatana in her own house is also enjoined for the girl.’”

Firstly, the meaning of the term “Purakalpe” being “in an earlier age” is itself disputable, but let us discuss that later.

Assuming the meaning to be “In an earlier age”, this argument is similar to the one made earlier by Vidyaranya, regarding remarriage being forbidden in this yuga. That argument was decidedly stronger than this one, however, for that one had some injunctive force (“these are forbidden in Kali”) whereas this one has none.

All that can be inferred from something being practiced in an earlier age is that it has lost prevalence now, it does not necessitate an outright prohibition on it. Though this is obvious in and of itself, it may be demonstrated with the following example from Mahabharata (13.116.53) :

श्रूयते हि पुराकल्पे नृणां व्रीहिमयः पशुः । येनायजन्त यज्वानः पुण्यलोकपरायणाः ॥

“It is heard that in an earlier age (Purakalpe) amongst men that animals would be made out of grain, and officiaters, focused on attaining the higher realms, would perform sacrifice with them”

This practice is corroborated in the Manusmriti (5.37) :

कुर्याद्घृतपशुं सङ्घे कुर्यात्पिष्टपशुं तथा । न त्वेव तु वृथा हन्तुं पशुमिच्छेत्कदाचन ॥

“If there is occasion for sacrifice, one may make animals out of ghee or grains (for the sacrifice). Never should one wish to harm an animal needlessly”

This practice is recognised, even by those from the lineages of Devana or Vidyaranya, to be a valid practice even this age. No one objects to it on the basis of “Pura kalpe”. Even they recognise, here at least, that it has no injunctive force.

Let us see another verse from the Mahabharata (6.41.18)

श्रूयते हि पुराकल्पे गुरूनननुमान्य यः । युध्यते स भवेद्व्यक्तमपध्यातो महत्तरैः ॥

“It is heard in an earlier age (Purakalpe) that one who goes to war without permission of his preceptors, is cursed with evil thoughts by those great ones.”

The meaning of “in an earlier age” simply does not fit here. That is why the gloss given by Nilakantha in his commentary, Bharatabhavadipa, is “पुराकल्पे प्राचीनशास्त्रे” (Purakalpe means “in the ancient scriptures” [Vedas]).

Indeed, a careful reading of the Yama smrti would reveal that this alternate meaning is the one that fits in that verse as well. Because each and every verb in that verse is present tense and injunctive (isyate, adhyapayet, vidhtyate). None is past tense. It is clearly making an injunction in the current age, not stating one that was once practiced in a bygone era and is now forbidden.

Even if the Purvapaksa can argue that the earlier age meaning is superior, however, it does not matter. Because, even overlooking the lack of injunctive force in their meaning, there would be ambiguity. And an ambiguous passage can never be used to restrict an unambiguous one, such as that of Harita.

Topics other than the Vedas

The other topics have been touched upon just now, as Madhva has stated that women should learn all the topics except Vedas (except Brahmavadinis) and are entitled to study of Tantras and Puranas/itihasas as the alternative to Vedas as seen. The Tantric texts like Saktisangama tantra (58.8) corroborate this:

देवीपरम्पराप्राप्ता दीक्षा स्त्रीणां शुभा मता ।

“Initiation in the tradition of Devi is considered auspicious for women”

Mahanirvana 8.45-46

पुत्रश्चैव प्रयत्नतः पालनीयोऽनुशिक्षितः । जनकैस्त्रिस्त्रविभिश्च तावद्यावद् द्विषतिक्षमः ॥
ततः स्वकीयेषु तान् जन्यः कर्मणि विनियोजयेत् । ततो विभावयेद्धीमान् वयसा सह वर्धता ॥
कन्याप्येवं पालनीया शिक्षणीयातियत्नतः ।

“The fathers and mothers should raise and educate the son with effort, until he is of sixteen years of age, then he should be engaged in proper duties and occupation. As he grows in age he should be established in livelihood. The female child is to be raised and educated with effort in the same manner.”

While describing qualities of an ideal woman, the Skanda purana (5.2.61.4-5) states that they should be educated in the 64 arts:

दक्षा सुशीला धर्मिष्ठा गृहव्यापारकोविदा ॥ चतुःषष्टिकलायुक्ता सदा भर्तृहिते रता ।
पूर्णेन्दुवदना सौम्या सदा मधुरभाषिणी ॥

“Dextrous, Good natured, established in dharma, learned in matters of household and business, learned in the 64 arts, always engaged in the welfare of her husband, having a face like the full moon, calm, and soft-spoken”

Vatsyayana also states in his Kama-sutra (1.3.13)

अभ्यासप्रयोज्यांश्च चातुःषष्टिकान् योगान् कन्या रहस्येकाकिन्यभ्यसेत् ॥

“A girl should also learn and practice the 64 arts in private.”

The 64 arts are a wide array of subjects, including essentially all secular subjects, from arts like singing, dancing, playing instruments, painting, and poetry to sciences like chemistry, metallurgy, botany, and basic medicine, to subjects like politics, economics, and foreign languages. Lord Krsna is said to have mastered these arts as part of his education.

In summary, regarding the study of Vedas, women in general are restricted from it, but exceptional women may be initiated and taught the Vedas by their Father or other close male relative and perform the rituals. Regarding all the subjects other than the Vedas, women are encouraged to learn them just as much as men.

3.7 Religion and Moksha

Religious activities

In truth, several forms of religious activity have already been discussed. The household and marital duties are the highest religious duties of the women, even by their performance alone she can attain the highest good, all else is optional. Besides them we discussed purity, both external as well as internal, the latter consisting of meditation on God. The study of scriptures (Svadhayaya) is also a form of religious activity. Now let us see all the ones that remain, viz, (I) the obligatory occasional rituals (Samskaras), (II) Fasting (Vrata), (III) pilgrimage (Tirtha), (IV) chanting, (V) making offerings to the gods and (VI) charity (Dana).

Samskaras

There are a total of 16 obligatory occasional (Naimittika) rituals in Hindu scriptures. All 16 are performed for men of the three higher Varnas (and Brahmavadinis) using Vedic mantras. For general women, however, only ten are performed, without Vedic mantras. The excluded rites are those related to study of the Vedas (sacred thread, first alms, graduation, etc.), for which marriage is stated to be the equivalent. The Vyasa smrti states:

संस्काराः षोडश स्मृताः । नवैताः कर्णवेधान्ता मन्त्रवर्जं क्रियाः स्त्रियाः ।
विवाहो मन्त्रतस्तासां शूद्रस्यामन्त्रतो दश

“The samskaras are reckoned to be sixteen. Of these, nine, ending with ‘Karnavedhana’ are to be performed for women, without mantras. Marriage is performed for them with mantras, except the Sudras for whom it is without, thus totalling ten.”

It then lists their names and when they are to be performed:

  1. Garbhadhana: the rite to mark conception in the first month.
  2. Pumsava: prayers for the fetus in the third month.
  3. Simanta: ceremony of parting the mother’s hair in the eighth month.
  4. Jatakarma: the birth rites.
  5. Namakarana: naming ceremony on the 11th day.
  6. Niskramana: first outing in the fourth month.
  7. Annaprasana: first feeding in the sixth month.
  8. Cidakarma: first haircut, as per family tradition.
  9. Karnavedhana: ear-piercing after the Cudakarma.
Fasting (Vrata)

For all the topics henceforth there is pretty much no difference by gender. Any injunction for men is also valid for women, unless explicitly stated otherwise, because there is no restriction on women stated for these. However, for demonstration, we will see only those verses which explicitly enjoin the activity for women.

Bhavishya Purana 4.23

“Listen, O son of Prtha, I shall narrate to you that best vrata among vratas, doing which a fortunate woman may beget many children.”

Narada Purana 1.112.1

श्रुणु नारद वक्ष्यामि तृतीयाया व्रतानि ते । यानि संसेवितव्याशु नारी सौभाग्यमाप्नुयात् ॥

“Listen, O Narada, I shall narrate that fasts of the 3rd night of the fortnight, which a woman, by doing properly, attains great fortune.”

निवेद्यद्रव्यं चैव शिवाय परमेष्ठिने । स याति शिवसायुज्यं नारी देव्याः यदि प्रभो ॥

“One should perform fast for Siva, the supreme. Doing so, he attains oneness with Siva (if a man), or with Devi, if a woman, O lord.”

Pilgrimage (Tirtha)

Matsya 192.33 and Padma 3.19.31

शुक्लतीर्थं तु या नारी ददाति कनकं शुभम् । घृतेन स्नापयेद्देवं कुमारं चापि पूजयेत् ॥
एवं या कुरुते भक्त्या तस्याः पुण्यफलं शृणु । मोदते शर्वलोकेऽस्या यावदिन्द्रचतुर्दशम् ॥

“The woman who donates an auspicious golden ornament at the pilgrimage site ‘Suklatirtha’ and bathes the idol of Lord Kumara with ghee and worships it, all this with devotion, listen to her holy fruit. She enjoys in the realm of Siva for the reign of fourteen Indras.”

Skanda purana 7.1.157.4

स्नात्वा तद्वैष्णवं स्थानं नृणां पातकनाशनम् । माघे मासि तृतीयायां नारी वा पुरुषोऽपि वा ॥
यस्तं पूजयते भक्त्या स मुक्तः पातकैर्भवेत् ॥

“The one who bathes at that holy site of Visnu on the third day of the month of Magha and worships Visnu with devotion, whether man or woman, is freed of all sins.”

Skanda Purana (5.3.106.2) on pilgrimage to Prabhasa ksetra:

Moksa

The examples of Brahmavadinis like Maitreyi and Sulabha who received education on Moksa-sastra has already been seen. Even still some verses directly stating their eligibility shall be presented.

The most famous verse on the topic is perhaps Bhagavad Gita (9.32):

मां हि पार्थ व्यपाश्रित्य येऽपि स्युः पापयोनयः । स्त्रियो वैश्यास्तथा शूद्रास्तेऽपि यान्ति परां गतिम् ॥

“Whoever surrenders unto me, O son of Prtha! Be they those of sinful birth, Women, vaisyas or Sudras, they also attain the supreme destination.”

Thus the Moksa of women is stated plainly in the Gita itself.

On translation of Bhagavad Gita 9.32

This verse is, however, less famous as one which uplifts women and lower varnas by acknowledging their eligibility for Moksa, and more famous for the alleged accusation that it calls Women and Sudras as ‘those of sinful birth’ (Papayoni). The majority of translations, including Gita press, the most widely read translation in India, as well as Adidevananda, Shivananda, Tejomayananda, etc have translated it correctly ( Papayoni as a separate category). However, ISKCON’s ‘As it is’, the most widely read translation outside India, has mistranslated it, and a few others like A.M Shastri, by misuse of punctuation:

O son of Prtha, those who take shelter in Me, though they be of lower birth — women, vaisyas [merchants] and Sudras [workers] - can attain the supreme destination.

A.M. Shastri

For, finding refuge in Me, they also who, O son of Pritha, may be of a sinful birth-women, vaisyas as well as Sudras, even they attain to the Supreme Goal.

This reading is invalid grammatically, by the commentarial tradition, and by scripture. Grammatically, there is nothing in the verse to indicate that ‘Papayoni’ is the category and the other three terms mentioned come under it; it is stated as one continuous list. By the commentarial tradition, Acarya Ramanuja, founder of the Visistadvaita tradition, clarifies:

स्त्रियो वैश्याः शूद्राः च पापयोनयः अपि मां व्यपाश्रित्य परां गतिं यान्ति।

“Women, Vaisyas, Sudras and also (Ca api) the papayonis, surrendering unto me, attain the supreme destination.”

Acarya Sridhara of the Advaita tradition has also explained each term separately:

येऽपि पापयोनयः स्युः निकृष्टजन्मानोऽन्त्यजादयो भवेयुः । येऽपि वैश्याः केवलं कृष्यादिनिरताः स्त्रियः शूद्रादयश्चाध्ययनादिरहिताः तेऽपि मां व्यपाश्रित्य संसेव्य परां गतिं यान्ति । हि निश्चितम् ।

“Be they Papayonis, I.e those of sinful birth like the mixed castes, or be they Vaisyas, i.e. ones focused on agriculture and such activities, or be they Women and Sudras, who are devoid of study of the Vedas, all of them, surrendering to me, serving me, attain the supreme destination. This is certain”

As per Sridhara, ‘Papayoni’ refers to the mixed castes, Because intermarriage between two Varnas itself is sinful, and so those birthed by it are ‘of sinful birth’

भावमात्रवत्यः प्रसिद्धाः स्त्रियो व्रजपुरवनिताः वैश्यास्तुलाधारादयो भारते ख्याताः? नन्दादयो वा व्रजवासिन एव प्रसिद्ध एव? शूद्रामुत्पन्नाः शूद्रा विदुरादयश्च? ये वा पापयोनयः हीनजातयो हूणयवनशबरादयः पुलिन्द्यश्च मां पुरुषोत्तमं वात्सल्यजलधिं करुणावरुणालयं महापतितपावनमशरणशरणागतव्रजपालकं येन केनचिद्भावेनाश्रित्य साक्षात्कृतस्य मे आश्रयमात्रेण परां गतिं यान्ति।

“Women (such as) the ladies of Vraja, who had only love (Bhava) for krsna, Vaisyas such as those mentioned in Mahabharata like Nanda or other inhabitants of Vraja, and famous Sudras like Vidura, and others like the Papayonis, i.e., those born in lower races like the Huns, Greeks, Pulindas, etc, by taking refuge in me, the supreme being, the ocean of parental love, the ocean of compassion, the purifier of the most fallen, the refuge of the one who has no other refuge, the lord of Vraja, by whatever emotion, merely by that act of taking refuge attain the supreme destination”

Vallabha interprets ‘Papayoni’ to be the mlechha races, such as Huns and Greeks. This is supported by the Bhagavatam :

किरातहूणान्ध्रपुलिन्दपुल्कशा आभीरशुम्भा यवनाः खसादयः ।
येऽन्ये च पापा यदपाश्रयाश्रयाः शुध्यन्ति तस्मै प्रभविष्णवे नमः ॥

“Salutations to Visnu, who purifies even the Kirata, Hun, Andhra, Pulinda, Pulkasa, Abhira, Sumbha, Greek, Khasa, and other such sinful (Papa) races who are (called so because) of their addiction to sinful activities.”

The Puranas also clearly mention ‘Papayoni’ as a separate category from Sudras and Vaisyas while enumerating all the categories of people (Narada Purana 1.48.51 and Matsya Purana 180.19):

ब्राह्मणाः क्षत्रिया वैश्याः शूद्रा वै वर्णसङ्कराः । क्रिमयश्चैव ये म्लेच्छाः संकीर्णाः पापयोनयः

“Brahmanas, Ksatriyas, Vaisyas, Sudras, and the mixtures of (these) Varnas, also the demonic Mlechhas, and the mixed Papayonis”

Here ‘Papayoni’ has been indicated to be the mixture of People of the four Varnas and Mlechhas.

Thus, by grammar, by the commentarial tradition, and most importantly by the scripture itself, any notion of women being ‘Papayonis’ has been dispelled.

4. Addendum

4.1 Conclusion

Thus, almost every topic of a woman’s life—legal, social, and religious—has been explained, with scripture as the primary divine guide, without any historicisation, and with the Acaryas as supporting evidence. All the topics discussed may now be summarised in the order of a woman’s life.

When she is born, one ought to rejoice, for Devi herself has come to his home, and the daughter is equal or superior to the son. Her samskaras should be performed, and she should be given an education. If she is exceptional in religious matters, her sacred thread initiation should be performed, and she should be taught the Vedas as well, but not otherwise. When she reaches the appropriate age, she should be married in one of the higher forms of marriage, with her consent and without exchange of money, to a suitable husband. If a suitable husband is not found, she may remain unmarried, though marriage is superior for paying the rnas and entering the Grhastha stage.

When she is married, she should be loved, served, provided for and protected by her husband, for whom she is the supreme refuge. She too should serve him, perform the household work like cooking, cleaning, etc, and advise him on matters of family and business. When she has children she is more venerable to them than their father or teacher. If her husband has gone away for a long time, or is impotent, or has taken renunciation, or has fallen, then she may leave him and remarry. If he dies she may live as a widow or remarry, she should not perform sahagamana.

Whatever money she receives is her own and may not to be taken by anyone, or else they will be legally punished. If her husband has gone away without making arrangements for her she may work in respectable industries.

She receives inheritance from her father if there are no sons, or she is unmarried. She receives inheritance from her husband, equal to a son, or the entirety of it if there are no children. Upon her own death her property is inherited by her daughters, sons if no daughters or husband/parents depending on type of marriage.

She is to be guarded—not dominated— by her father in childhood, husband in youth and sons in old age. If not, by the government/king. She is inviolable. Any verbal, physical or sexual offence against her is a more serious crime than against a man. Abandonment and adultery are punishable offenses.

She should wear clothing such that it covers her chest, and lower body from navel to ankle. If she goes out, she should take an uttariya over arms and/or shoulder. All else is optional. She should wear the marital ornaments always, and other ornaments as per her wish. There is no fault in her going out in public.

Menstruation is the unparalleled means of purification for the women, like the flow of the river. Though it leaves them temporarily impure during that time, and they should cease religious household activities during it. At childbirth both she and her husband are impure for ten days. She should maintain internal purity by meditating on God with her husband, and external by bathing.

She may perform religious activities like fasting, pilgrimage, chanting, making offerings, charity, etc. though fulfilling her household duties itself is also sufficient. By performing bhakti of her preferred deity she attains Moksa.

Some may call the scriptural views ‘Progressive’, though this is a very bad label, because progress implies gradual improvement from a less than ideal state. The scriptures, however, are eternal. The only issue is the misinterpretation and addition. Thus what is to be done is not ‘progress’ but ‘purification’ to arrive at the original scriptural view.
