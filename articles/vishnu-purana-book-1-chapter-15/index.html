VP 1.15.1

तपश् चरत्सु पृथिवीं प्रचेतःसु महीरुहाः ।
अरक्ष्यमाणाम् आवव्रुः बभूवाथ प्रजाक्षयः ॥१॥

When they were deeply immersed in penance like this, the entire world was filled by trees, growing by great proportions. Mankind declined, and nobody could survive because the plants completely filled the world.

No commentary.

VP 1.15.3

तान् दृष्ट्वा जलनिष्क्रान्ताः सर्वे क्रुद्धाः प्रचेतसः ।
मुखेभ्यो वायुम् अग्निं च तेऽसृजन् जातमन्यवः ॥३॥

Seeing such a sorry state in the world, the Prachetasaas got very angry, and becoming very angry, they blew air and fire through their mouth.

Śrīdhara commentary

When the Pracetases were engaged in penance, their father had obtained knowledge of the truth, abandoned the kingdom, and gone to the forest; this should be understood. As the Bhāgavata says, Nārada compassionately instructed Prācīnabarhis, whose mind was attached to ritual works, through the story of Purañjana. Thus, because the earth was unprotected and there was no cultivation, the trees covered it.

VP 1.15.4

उन्मूलान् अथ तान् वृक्षान् कृत्वा वायुर् अशोषयत् ।
तान् अग्निर् अदहद् घोरस् तत्राभूद् द्रुमसङ्क्षयः ॥४॥

The air that they blew was so powerful that it uprooted all the trees, and the air dried everything. The fire which they blew burnt everything. Then, there was a severe lack of trees.

Śrīdhara commentary

He explains the purpose of the creation of air and fire: “they uprooted.”

VP 1.15.5

द्रुमक्षयम् अथो दृष्ट्वा किञ्चिच् छिष्टेषु शाखिषु ।

King Soma, having seen the destruction of the trees and that only a few branches remained, approached the Prajāpatis and spoke.

Śrīdhara commentary

Soma is the king of trees. By saying “Prajāpatis” he indicates a conciliatory approach: it is improper for you, protectors of creatures, to burn trees that nourish creatures with roots, fruits and fruits.

VP 1.15.7

रत्नभूता च कन्येयं वार्क्षेयी वरवर्णिनी ।
भविष्यज्जानता पूर्वं मया गोभिर् विवर्धिता ॥७॥

I also have this wonderful woman, gem of a woman, one who is born of the trees. I am protecting this gem of a woman, excellent among women. Through my grace, I have nurtured her, knowing the future what is going to happen.

Śrīdhara commentary

He reconciles them by offering penance: “ratna-bhūtā” means precious; “Vārkṣeyī” means born from trees. “She will be your wife; Dakṣa will be born from her, and the three worlds will be filled by his lineage”—not knowing this, I nourished her with my nectar-filled rays.

VP 1.15.8

मारिषा नाम नाम्नैषा वृक्षाणाम् इति निर्मिता ।
भार्या वोऽस्तु महाभागा ध्रुवं वंशविवर्धिनी ॥८॥

She is the daughter of the trees, vrukshaas, and is called as Maarishaa. She will become your wife and will continue the progeny, and help you in expanding the mankind.

Śrīdhara commentary

Her name is Māriṣā; the name means “this girl of the trees.” Thus “created” means produced, as is well known.

युष्माकं तेजसोऽर्धेन मम चार्धेन तेजसः ।

With half of your tejas, and half of my tejas, in her, one Prajaapati is going to be born, by name Dakṣa.

With half of your intense, ascetic radiance and half of my radiance, joined in her—she having grown by my cool rays—Dakṣa will be born.

Śrīdhara commentary

Having praised him in his pure form, he praises him as all things: “He is the cause.” The suffix -tas has the sense of the sixth case. Viṣṇu is the cause of the cause, and of that cause as well—the chain of causes from the universe to the primal cause. Likewise in effects, Viṣṇu is the chain of effects from prakṛti and mahat to the final effect. Having stated his all-pervasiveness as material cause and effect in creation, he states it in preservation as what is protected and as protector. “He alone” is emphatic: Viṣṇu, marked by all his forms as the objects and agents of protection, sustains everything. “Karma” here is the most desired result, from human joy up to Brahman-bliss; thus Viṣṇu alone is identified with all causes, effects, actions and agents connected with creation and dissolution.

VP 1.15.57

ब्रह्म प्रभुर् ब्रह्म स सर्वभूतो
ब्रह्म प्रजानां पतिर् अच्युतोऽसौ
ब्रह्माक्षरं नित्यम् अजं स विष्णुर्
अपक्षयाद्यैर् अखिलैर् असङ्गि ॥५७॥

He is Brahman, the Lord; He is Brahman, present in all beings; He is Brahman, the imperishable, eternal, unborn Viṣṇu, untouched by decay and all such changes.

Śrīdhara commentary

If he is pure Brahman, how can he exist in all forms? If all things, how can he remain pure amid every change? In answer: Viṣṇu is pure Brahman, Lord and controller of all, all beings, and protector of creatures. He is called Acyuta because he never departs from Brahman. Though all-pervading and the Self of all, he remains imperishable, eternal, unborn and free from decay, change, growth or attachment; through his inconceivable self-dependent māyā he appears as all. Thus there is no contradiction.
