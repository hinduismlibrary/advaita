VP 3.16.1

और्व उवाच— हविष्य-मत्स्य-मांसैस् तु शशस्य नकुलस्य च।
सौकर-च्छागलैणेय-रौरवैर् गवयेन च

The ancestors attain satisfaction for one month through havis, for two months through fish, and for three months through meat; likewise through rabbit, mongoose, boar, goat, antelope, ruru deer, and gavaya.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
हविष्य-मत्स्य-मांसैः तु (haviṣya-matsya-māṃsaiḥ tu) — with havis, fish, and meat
शशस्य नकुलस्य च (śaśasya nakulasya ca) — of rabbit and mongoose
सौकर-छागल-ऐणेय-रौरवैः (saukara-chāgala-aiṇeya-rauravaiḥ) — with boar, goat, antelope, and ruru deer
गवयेन च (gavayena ca) — and with gavaya
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

haviṣya-matsya-māṃsais tu śaśasya nakulasya ca|saukara-cchāgalaiṇeya-rauravair gavayena ca

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सदाचारप्रसङ्गानुप्रसङ्गेनाथ षोडशे । द्रव्याणि श्राद्धयोग्यानि तथायोग्यानि चादिशत् । तत्र श्राद्धे देयद्रव्यप्रशंसा हविष्यैर्व्रीह्यादिभिः मत्स्यादीनां नवानां मांसश्च प्रत्येकमेकैकमासवृद्ध्या पितामहाः पितरस्तृप्तिं प्रयान्ति इति द्वयोरन्वयः । व्रीह्यादिभिरेकमासं मत्स्यमांसैर्द्वौ मासावित्येवं दशमासान्तम् मत्स्यामिषे मांसत्वोपचारः । एणो हरिणविशेषः । रुरुः पृषतः तदीयैः । गवयो गोसदृशः पशुरारण्यकः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

As an incidental continuation of the discussion of proper conduct, this sixteenth chapter prescribes materials suitable and unsuitable for śrāddha. The first two verses are connected as follows: the ancestors attain satisfaction through havis made from rice and similar grains for one month, through fish for two months, and through the nine kinds of meat beginning with meat and ending with gavaya, with each increasing the satisfaction by one month. Here eṇa means a kind of deer, ruru means the spotted deer, and gavaya is a wild animal resembling a cow.

VP 3.16.2

औरभ्र-गव्यैश् च तथा मास-वृद्ध्या पितामहाः।
प्रयान्ति तृप्तिं मांसैस् तु नित्यं वार्ध्रीण-सामिषैः

The ancestors attain satisfaction through sheep and cow products with the satisfaction increasing month by month; through the meat of the Vārddhrīṇasa they are satisfied perpetually.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
औरभ्र-गव्यैः च तथा (aurabhra-gavyaiḥ ca tathā) — with sheep and cow products likewise
मास-वृद्ध्या पितामहाः प्रयान्ति तृप्तिम् (māsa-vṛddhyā pitāmahāḥ prayānti tṛptim) — with satisfaction increasing month by month the ancestors attain fulfillment
मांसैः तु नित्यम् वार्ध्रीणस-आमिषैः (māṃsaiḥ tu nityam vārdhrīṇasa-āmiṣaiḥ) — but with the meat of the Vārddhrīṇasa, they are satisfied perpetually
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

aurabhra-gavyaiś ca tathā māsa-vṛddhyā pitāmahāḥ|prayānti tṛptiṃ māṃsais tu nityaṃ vārdhrīṇa-sāmiṣaiḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उरभ्रो मेषः तदीयैश्च । गव्यन्तु पयः पायसं वा । संवत्सरन्तु गव्येन पयसा पायसेन च । इति स्मृतेः । मांसमध्यपाठात् मांसमवेत्यन्ते, तत्तु युगान्तरीणमित्यवधेयम् । वाध्रीणसमांसैस्तु नित्यं सर्वकालं तृप्यन्ति । वाध्रीणसः स्मृतिप्रसिद्धः । यथा— त्रिपिबन्त्विन्द्रियं क्षीणं श्वेतं वृद्धप्रजापतिम् । वाध्रीणसन्तु तं प्राहुर्याज्ञिकाः श्राद्धकर्मणि ॥ इति । जलपाने यस्य मुखवत् कर्णौ च जलं स्पृशतः स त्रिपिबः । यद्वा— कृष्णग्रीवो रक्तशिराः श्वेतपक्षो विहङ्गमः । स वै वाध्रीणसः प्रोक्त इत्येषा नैगमी श्रुतिः ॥ इति

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Urabhra means sheep and its meat. Gavya means milk or payasa; the tradition says that milk or payasa gives satisfaction for a year. Some say gavya means meat. The meat is understood at the end because it occurs in the middle of the list; this refers to a very long period. Through Vārddhrīṇasa meat the ancestors are satisfied at all times. Vārddhrīṇasa is known from tradition: it is described as a bird with a black neck, red head, and white wings; ritual authorities also describe it as a white, aged goat whose milk is drunk three times. If its ears are touched by water as it drinks, it is called Tripiba; alternatively, the first bird-description is the traditional account.

VP 3.16.3

खड्ग-मांसम् अतीवात्र काल-शाकं तथा मधु।
शस्तानि कर्मण्य् अत्यन्त-तृप्तिदानि नरेश्वर

Rhinoceros meat, Kāla greens, and honey are especially praised in the rite and give very great satisfaction, O king.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
खड्ग-मांसम् अतीव अत्र (khaḍga-māṃsam atīva atra) — rhinoceros meat here is especially [excellent]
काल-शाकम् तथा मधु (kāla-śākam tathā madhu) — likewise Kāla greens and honey
शस्तानि कर्मणि अत्यन्त-तृप्ति-दानि (śastāni karmaṇi atyanta-tṛpti-dāni) — are praised in the rite and give very great satisfaction
नरेश्वर (nareśvara) — O king
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

khaḍga-māṃsam atīvātra kāla-śākaṃ tathā madhu|śastāni karmaṇy atyanta-tṛptidāni nareśvara

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

No commentary.

VP 3.16.4

गयाम् उपेत्य यः श्राद्धं करोति पृथिवी-पते।
सफलं तस्य तज् जन्म जायते पितृ-तुष्टिदम्

O lord of the earth, whoever performs śrāddha after reaching Gayā makes his birth fruitful; it gives satisfaction to the ancestors.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
गयाम् उपेत्य यः श्राद्धं करोति (gayām upetya yaḥ śrāddhaṃ karoti) — whoever performs śrāddha after reaching Gayā
पृथिवी-पते (pṛthivī-pate) — O lord of the earth
सफलम् तस्य तत् जन्म जायते (saphalam tasya tat janma jāyate) — his birth becomes fruitful
पितृ-तुष्टि-दम् (pitṛ-tuṣṭi-dam) — giving satisfaction to the ancestors
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

gayām upetya yaḥ śrāddhaṃ karoti pṛthivī-pate|saphalaṃ tasya taj janma jāyate pitṛ-tuṣṭidam

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तज्जन्म पितृतुष्टिदं सत् तस्मिन् जन्मनि पितॄणामृणादृत्तीर्ण इत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The birth that reaches Gayā becomes fruitful and gives satisfaction to the ancestors; the meaning is that in that birth the ancestral debt has been repaid.

VP 3.16.5

प्रशान्तिकाः सनीवाराः श्यामका द्विविधास् तथा।
वन्यषधी-प्रधानास् तु श्राद्धार्हाः पुरुषर्षभ

Praśāntika, Sanīvāra, and the two kinds of Śyāmaka, being chiefly wild plants, are suitable for śrāddha, O best of men.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
प्रशान्तिकाः सनीवाराः श्यामकाः द्वि-विधाः तथा (praśāntikāḥ sanīvārāḥ śyāmakāḥ dvi-vidhāḥ tathā) — Praśāntika, Sanīvāra, and the two kinds of Śyāmaka
वन्य-औषधि-प्रधानाः तु (vanya-auṣadhi-pradhānāḥ tu) — chiefly wild plants
श्राद्ध-अर्हाः पुरुषर्षभ (śrāddha-arhāḥ puruṣarṣabha) — are suitable for śrāddha, O best of men
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

praśāntikāḥ sanīvārāḥ śyāmakā dvividhās tathā|vanyaṣadhī-pradhānās tu śrāddhārhāḥ puruṣarṣabha

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्यामाका द्विविधाः श्वेताः कृष्णाश्च वन्यौषधिप्रधाना वक्ष्यमाणाः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śyāmaka are of two kinds, white and black; the wild plants that will be listed are the principal ones.

VP 3.16.6

यवाः प्रियङ्गवो मुद्गा गोधूमा व्रीहयस् तिलाः।
निष्पावाः कोविदाराश् च सर्षयाश् चात्र शोभनाः

Barley, Priyaṅgu, mung beans, wheat, rice, sesame, beans, Kovidāra, and mustard are wholesome here.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
यवाः प्रियङ्गवः मुद्गाः गोधूमाः व्रीहयः तिलाः (yavāḥ priyaṅgavaḥ mudgāḥ godhūmāḥ vrīhayaḥ tilāḥ) — barley, Priyaṅgu, mung beans, wheat, rice, and sesame
निष्पावाः कोविदाराः च सर्षपाः च (niṣpāvāḥ kovidārāḥ ca sarṣapāḥ ca) — beans, Kovidāra, and mustard
अत्र शोभनाः (atra śobhanāḥ) — are wholesome here
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yavāḥ priyaṅgavo mudgā godhūmā vrīhayas tilāḥ|niṣpāvāḥ kovidārāś ca sarṣayāś cātra śobhanāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रियङ्गवः कङ्गवः । व्रीहयः शरत्पक्वानि धान्यानि । निष्पावा शिम्ब्यः । कोविदारा वृक्षास्तैश्च तत्फलानि लक्ष्यन्ते । सर्षपाः श्वेताः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Priyaṅgu means Kaṅgu; the rice grains are those ripened in autumn; niṣpāva means beans; Kovidāra refers to the trees and, by extension, their fruits; the mustard is white.

VP 3.16.7

अकृताग्रयणं यच् च धान्य-जातं नरेश्वर।
राज-माषान् अणूंश् चैव मसूरांश् च विसर्जयेत्

O king, one should avoid any grain for which the first-grain offering has not been performed, as well as Rājamāṣa, Aṇu, and lentils.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
अकृत-आग्रयणम् यत् च धान्य-जातम् (akṛta-āgrayaṇam yat ca dhānya-jātam) — any kind of grain for which the first-grain offering has not been performed
राज-माषान् अणून् च एव (rāja-māṣān aṇūn ca eva) — Rājamāṣa and Aṇu
मसूरान् च विवर्जयेत् (masūrān ca vivarjayet) — and lentils one should avoid
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

akṛtāgrayaṇaṃ yac ca dhānya-jātaṃ nareśvara|rāja-māṣān aṇūṃś caiva masūrāṃś ca visarjayet

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वर्ज्यानाह, अकृताग्रयणम् इत्य्-आदि ना पर्युषितमित्यन्तेन । नवशस्यागमे साग्नेर्विहिता इष्टिराग्रयणम् । तत्र कृतं यन्न तत् । राजमाषान् अकृष्टमासान् अणून् सूक्ष्मशालीन्

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

He now states the prohibited items, from akṛtāgrayaṇa through paryuṣita. When new grain arrives, the Āgrayaṇa offering is prescribed with fire; grain for which that has not been done is prohibited. Rājamāṣa means uncultivated māṣa, and Aṇu means fine-grained rice.

VP 3.16.8

अलाबुं गृञ्जनं चैव पलाण्डुं पिण्ड-मूलकम्।
गान्धारक-करम्बादि-लवणान्य् औषराणि च

Bottle gourd, green radish, onion, lump-root, Gāndhāraka, Karambha, and saline salts are to be avoided.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
अलाबुम् गृञ्जनम् च एव पलाण्डुम् पिण्ड-मूलकम् (alābum gṛñjanam ca eva palāṇḍum piṇḍa-mūlakam) — bottle gourd, green radish, onion, and lump-root
गान्धारक-करम्भाणि (gāndhāraka-karambhāṇi) — Gāndhāraka and Karambha
लवणानि औषराणि च (lavaṇāni auṣarāṇi ca) — and saline salts one should avoid
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

alābuṃ gṛñjanaṃ caiva palāṇḍuṃ piṇḍa-mūlakam|gāndhāraka-karambādi-lavaṇāny auṣarāṇi ca

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

गृञ्जनं हरितमूलकं, पलाण्डं लशुनभेदं, पिण्डमूलकं पिण्डाकारमूलकम् । एतेनान्यमूलकाभ्यनुज्ञा आयाति, गान्धारं शाकभेदः काञ्जिकं वा । करम्भाणि अविकासिता लाजाः शाकभेद इत्येके । औषराणि ऊषरभूमिजातानि

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Gṛñjana is green radish; palāṇḍu is a variety of garlic; piṇḍa-mūlaka is a root shaped like a lump. Gāndhāraka is a kind of vegetable, or fermented gruel; Karambha means unpuffed parched grain flour, according to some; auṣara means produced in saline soil.

VP 3.16.9

आरक्ताश् चैव निर्यासाः प्रत्यक्ष-लवणानि च।
वर्ज्यान्य् एतानि वै श्राद्धे यच् च वाचा न शस्यते

Naturally red tree resins, visible salts, and whatever is not praised by authoritative speech are to be avoided at śrāddha.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
आरक्ताः च एव निर्यासाः (āraktāḥ ca eva niryāsāḥ) — naturally red tree resins
प्रत्यक्ष-लवणानि च (pratyakṣa-lavaṇāni ca) — and visible salts
वर्ज्यानि एतानि वै श्राद्धे (varjyāni etāni vai śrāddhe) — these are indeed to be avoided at śrāddha
यत् च वाचा न शस्यते (yat ca vācā na śasyate) — and whatever is not praised by speech
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

āraktāś caiva niryāsāḥ pratyakṣa-lavaṇāni ca|varjyāny etāni vai śrāddhe yac ca vācā na śasyate

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निर्यासा वृक्षरसा आरक्ताः स्वभावतः । प्रत्यक्षलवणानि चक्षुर्देश्यलवणानि । यच्च विहितमपि वाचा न शस्यते निन्द्यतः इत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Niryāsa means tree resins that are naturally red; visible salts are salts perceptible to the eye. Whatever is prescribed but is not praised in authoritative speech is also meant to be avoided.

VP 3.16.10

नक्ताहृतम् अनुच्छिन्नं तृप्यते न च यत्र गौः।
दुर्गन्धि फेनिलं चाम्बु श्राद्ध-योग्यं न पार्थिव

Water brought at night, fresh water from an unestablished source, water where a cow is not satisfied, and foul-smelling or foamy water are not suitable for śrāddha, O king.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
नक्त-आहृतम् अनुच्छिष्टम् (nakta-āhṛtam anucchiṣṭam) — water brought at night and fresh water
तृप्यते न च यत्र गौः (tṛpyate na ca yatra gauḥ) — where a cow is not satisfied
दुर्गन्धि फेनिलम् च अम्बु (durgandhi phenilam ca ambu) — foul-smelling and foamy water
श्राद्ध-योग्यम् न पार्थिव (śrāddha-yogyam na pārthiva) — is not suitable for śrāddha, O king
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

naktāhṛtam anucchinnaṃ tṛpyate na ca yatra gauḥ|durgandhi phenilaṃ cāmbu śrāddha-yogyaṃ na pārthiva

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अनुत्सृष्टम् अप्रतिष्ठितकूपादिभवम् । गौर्यत्र न तृप्यते स्वल्पमित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Naktāhṛta means water brought at night. Anucchiṣṭa means fresh water, or milk from a cow that has not calved. “Where a cow is not satisfied” means where there is only a small amount.

VP 3.16.11

क्षीरम् एक-शफानां यद् औष्ट्रम् आविकम् एव च।
मार्गं च माहिषं चैव वर्जयेच् छ्राद्ध-कर्मणि

One should avoid in the śrāddha rite the milk of single-hoofed animals, camel milk, sheep milk, deer milk, and buffalo milk.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
क्षीरम् एक-शफानाम् (kṣīram eka-śaphānām) — milk of single-hoofed animals
यत् औष्ट्रम् आविकम् एव च (yat auṣṭram āvikam eva ca) — and camel and sheep milk
मार्गं च माहिषं च एव (mārgaṃ ca māhiṣaṃ ca eva) — and deer and buffalo milk
वर्जयेत् श्राद्ध-कर्मणि (varjayet śrāddha-karmaṇi) — one should avoid in the śrāddha rite
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kṣīram eka-śaphānāṃ yad auṣṭram āvikam eva ca|mārgaṃ ca māhiṣaṃ caiva varjayec chrāddha-karmaṇi

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एकशफा वडवाद्याः । आविकं मेषसम्बन्धि । मार्गं हरिणीसम्बन्धि

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Single-hoofed animals are horses and the like; āvika is connected with sheep; mārga is connected with deer.

VP 3.16.12

षण्ढापविद्ध-चाण्डाल-पापि-पषण्डि-रोगिभिः।
कृकवाकुश्वनग्नैश् च वानर-ग्राम-सूकरैः

If the śrāddha is seen by eunuchs, outcasts, sinners, heretics, diseased persons, cocks, dogs, naked persons, monkeys, or village pigs, [the rite is spoiled].

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
षण्ड-अपविद्ध-चण्डाल-पापि-पाषण्डि-रोगि-भिः (ṣaṇḍa-apaviddha-caṇḍāla-pāpi-pāṣaṇḍi-rogi-bhiḥ) — by eunuchs, outcasts, sinners, heretics, and diseased persons
कृकवाकु-श्वान-नग्न-वानर-ग्राम-सूकरैः (kṛkavāku-śvāna-nagna-vānara-grāma-sūkaraiḥ) — by cocks, dogs, naked persons, monkeys, and village pigs
वीक्षिते श्राद्धे (vīkṣite śrāddhe) — when the śrāddha is seen by them
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ṣaṇḍhāpaviddha-cāṇḍāla-pāpi-paṣaṇḍi-rogibhiḥ|kṛkavākuśvanagnaiś ca vānara-grāma-sūkaraiḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

षण्ढादिभिस्त्रयोदशभिर्वीक्षिते श्राद्धे देवाः पितरश्च न भुञ्जते । षण्ढो नपुंसकम्, अपविद्धो महाजनपरित्यक्तः, पाषण्डी वैदिककर्मपरित्यागी, रोगी महारोगीः कृकवाकुः कुक्कुटः, श्वा कक्कुरः, नग्ना अग्रिमाध्याये वक्ष्यमाणाः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

When the śrāddha is seen by the thirteen persons beginning with a eunuch, neither the gods nor the ancestors eat. A ṣaṇḍha is an impotent person; an apaviddha is one rejected by leading people; a pāṣaṇḍin has abandoned Vedic rites; a rogin is gravely diseased; a kṛkavāku is a cock; a śvan is a dog; the naked persons will be described in the next chapter.

VP 3.16.13

उदक्यासूतकाशौचि-मृत-हारैश् च वीक्षिते।
श्राद्धे सुरा न पितरो भुञ्जते पुरुषर्षभ

If the śrāddha is seen by menstruating women, women in birth impurity, those in death impurity, or corpse-carriers, the gods and ancestors do not eat it, O best of men.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
उदक्या-सूतिका-शौच-मृत-हारैः च वीक्षिते (udakyā-sūtikā-śauca-mṛta-hāraiḥ ca vīkṣite) — when seen by menstruating women, women in birth impurity, those in death impurity, and corpse-carriers
श्राद्धे सुराः न पितरः भुञ्जते (śrāddhe surāḥ na pitaraḥ bhuñjate) — the gods and ancestors do not eat at the śrāddha
पुरुषर्षभ (puruṣarṣabha) — O best of men
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

udakyāsūtakāśauci-mṛta-hāraiś ca vīkṣite|śrāddhe surā na pitaro bhuñjate puruṣarṣabha

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उदक्या रजस्वला, सूतकं जननाशौचमः अशौचं मरणनिमित्तं तद्युक्तः, मृतहारः शवनिर्हरणवृत्तिः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Udakyā is a menstruating woman; sūtaka is birth impurity; aśauca is impurity caused by death; a mṛtahāra is one whose occupation is removing corpses.

VP 3.16.14

उर्व्यां च तिल-विक्षेपाद् यातुधानान् निवारयेत्

Therefore one should perform śrāddha in an enclosed place, with faith, and by scattering sesame on the ground ward off the demons.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
तस्मात् परिश्रिते कुर्यात् श्राद्धम् श्रद्धा-समन्वितः (tasmāt pariśrite kuryāt śrāddham śraddhā-samanvitaḥ) — therefore, in an enclosed place, one should perform śrāddha endowed with faith
उर्व्याम् च तिल-विक्षेपात् (urvyām ca tila-vikṣepāt) — and by scattering sesame on the ground
यातुधानान् निवारयेत् (yātudhānān nivārayet) — one should ward off the demons

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

परिश्रिते सर्वतः परिवृते

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Pariśrita means enclosed on every side.

VP 3.16.15

नखादिना चोपपन्नं केश-कीटादिभिर् नृप।
न चैवाभिषवैर् मिश्रम् अन्नं पर्युषितं तथा

Food contaminated by nails or the like, containing hair or insects, mixed with fermented liquids, or stale is not to be used.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
नख-आदिना च उपपन्नम् (nakha-ādinā ca upapannam) — contaminated with nails and the like
केश-कीट-आदिभिः नृप (keśa-kīṭa-ādibhiḥ nṛpa) — with hair, insects, and the like, O king
न च एव अभिषवैः मिश्रम् अन्नम् (na ca eva abhiṣavaiḥ miśram annam) — nor food mixed with fermented liquids
पर्युषितम् तथा (paryuṣitam tathā) — nor stale food
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

nakhādinā copapannaṃ keśa-kīṭādibhir nṛpa|na caivābhiṣavair miśram annaṃ paryuṣitaṃ tathā

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पूर्ति दुर्गन्धि, केशटीकादिभिरुपपन्नं युक्तम् । अवपन्नम् इति पाठे दूषितमित्यर्थः । अभिषवैः काञ्जिकैः । पर्युषितं पक्वं रात्र्यन्तरितम् । एतत् सर्वमन्नं पितॄनुद्दिश्य न दद्यादित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Food that is spoiled or foul-smelling, contaminated with hair, insects, and the like, or, in the reading avapanna, polluted, is prohibited. Abhiṣava means fermented liquid or gruel; paryuṣita means cooked food left overnight. All of this food should not be given to the ancestors.

VP 3.16.16

श्रद्धा-समन्वितैर् दत्तं पितृभ्यो नाम-गोत्रतः।
यदाहारास् तु ते जातास् तद् आहारत्वम् एति तत्

Whatever is given with faith to the ancestors, addressed by name and lineage, becomes the kind of food appropriate to the state in which they have been born.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
श्रद्धा-समन्वितैः दत्तम् पितृभ्यः नाम-गोत्रतः (śraddhā-samanvitaiḥ dattam pitṛbhyaḥ nāma-gotrataḥ) — given with faith to the ancestors by name and lineage
यत् आहाराः तु ते जाताः (yat āhārāḥ tu te jātāḥ) — whatever kind of food they have become fit to receive
तद् आहारत्वम् एति तत् (tad āhāratvam eti tat) — that becomes that kind of food
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

śraddhā-samanvitair dattaṃ pitṛbhyo nāma-gotrataḥ|yadāhārās tu te jātās tad āhāratvam eti tat

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्रद्धासमन्वितैः पितॄनुद्दिश्य यद्दत्तं तदन्नञ्च ते पितरो यदाहारा यादृशाहारयोग्या जातास्तदाहारतामुपैतीत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Whatever food is given with faith to the ancestors, addressed to them by name and lineage, becomes food of the kind appropriate to the state in which those ancestors have been born. This means that it becomes fit for their enjoyment in that other world: if they are in heaven, it becomes nectar; if they are human, food; and if they are animals, grass and the like.

इक्ष्वाकोर् मनु-पुत्रस्य कलापोपवने पुरा

A song sung by the ancestors is also heard, O king, from long ago in the grove of Kalāpa, concerning Ikṣvāku, the son of Manu.

Will there perhaps be one born in our family who will give us, on the thirteenth day in the month of Varṣā under Maghā, rice pudding with honey and ghee?

VP 3.16.20

गौरीं वाप्य् उद्वहेत् कन्यां नीलं वा वृषम् उत्सृजेत्।
यजेत वाश्वमेधेन विधिवद् दक्षिणावता

Will he marry a Gaurī maiden, release a blue bull, or perform an Aśvamedha properly with the prescribed gifts?

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
गौरीम् वा अपि उद्वहेत् कन्याम् (gaurīm vā api udvahet kanyām) — or marry a Gaurī maiden
नीलम् वा वृषम् उत्सृजेत् (nīlam vā vṛṣam utsṛjet) — or release a blue bull
यजेत् वा अश्व-मेधेन विधि-वत् दक्षिणा-वता (yajet vā aśva-medhena vidhi-vat dakṣiṇā-vatā) — or perform an Aśvamedha properly, with the prescribed gifts
पितरः आशंसन्ते (pitaraḥ āśaṃsante) — the ancestors hope for these
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

gaurīṃ vāpy udvahet kanyāṃ nīlaṃ vā vṛṣam utsṛjet|yajeta vāśvamedhena vidhivad dakṣiṇāvatā

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

गौरीमष्टवर्षाम्— अष्टवर्षा भवेद् गौरी नववर्षा तु रोहिणी । दशवर्षा भवेत् कन्या अत ऊर्द्ध्वं रजस्वला । इति स्मृतेः । तामुद्वहेत् स्वीकुर्यात् । उद्वाहयेत् इति वा पाठः— गौरीं ददन्नाकपृष्टं वैकुण्ठं याति रोहिणीम् । कन्यां ददद् ब्रह्मलोकं रौरवन्तु रजस्वलाम् । इति संवत्तोक्तेः । नीलः स्मृत्युक्तः— लोहितो यस्तु वर्णेन मुखे पुच्छे च पाण्डुरः । श्वेतः खुरविषाणाभ्यां स नीलो वृष उच्यते

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Gaurī means an eight-year-old girl: tradition says that an eight-year-old is Gaurī, a nine-year-old Rohiṇī, and a ten-year-old Kanyā; after that she is menstruating. One should marry her, or the reading may mean “give her in marriage.” The blue bull is defined by tradition as red in color, pale on the face and tail, and white at the hooves and horns.
