VP 1.11.1

प्रियव्रतोत्तानपादौ मनोः स्वायम्भुवस्य तु।
द्वौ पुत्रौ तु महावीर्यौ धर्मज्ञौ कथितौ तव॥१॥

Parāśara said: I had told you about two sons, who had great valour, who were always following the path of dharma, as two sons of Svāyambhuva Manu, called Priyavrata and Uttānapāda.

Śrīdhara translation

The lineage of Manu’s daughters has been told; now the lineage of his sons is given. The great story of Dhruva is told in two chapters. Manu had two sons and two daughters, and the lineages of the two daughters have been stated. Now, after recounting the two sons, the account turns to Uttānapāda’s lineage, beginning with “Priyavrata.”

क्रियते किं वृथा वत्स महान् एष मनोरथः।
अन्य-स्त्री-गर्भ-जातेन ह्य् असम्भूत ममोदरे॥७॥

O son Dhruva, why are you trying to do this great effort, and having such a desire to go and sit on the lap of your father. You are born to the other woman, and were never born to me.

Śrīdhara translation

Unable to bear Suruci’s harsh words, Dhruva left his father, who was overpowered by his wife, and went to his grieving mother. With her permission he left the city; following the Seven Sages’ instruction, he worshipped the Lord in Madhuvana and attained his goal. These events are to be told in sequence; Suruci’s boastful words begin here, in four verses: “Why is this being done?”

One whose essential nature is pure jñāna, He is present in all these forms, Hiranya Garbha, Purusha, Pradhaana, Avyakta, means that He is antaryaami, everything is His shareera, being controlled by Him, being supported by Him. Such a Paramaatman who is Vāsudeva, I bow down and surrender unto Him. His essential nature is like that.

Śrīdhara translation

Hiraṇyagarbha is Brahmā; Puruṣa is Viṣṇu; Śiva is the unmanifest principle and cause of dissolution, into which the universe is cast in Prakṛti. Vāsudeva is the controller of those three and the inner cause of māyā’s three guṇas. In another reading, “the universe is manifest.” Salutation to him in whom these forms exist; to him whose pure nature of knowledge never declines, even while these conditioned forms exist; to Vāsudeva, indicated by Oṃ. This is the mantra’s meaning.

They also justify why this mantra can yield such great results. They give an example. They tell Dhruva that his grandfather, Svaayambhuva Manu, also did japa with this mantra, and Janaardana was extremely pleased with him.

That which cannot be easily obtained in all the three worlds, that Janaardana gave to Svaayambhuva Manu, who did japa with this mantra; Svaayambhuva Manu being your grandfather, you also do the same thing.
