1 Introduction

1.1 Preface & Core Objective

The Problem: While the Vedas are universally honored as Shruti (the highest revealed knowledge), the vast corpus of Agamas has frequently been dismissed or demeaned as non-Vedic (avaidika), delusory, or deceitful due to misinterpretation and isolated proof-texting.

Purpose: To systematically establish the objective scriptural position of the Agamas and demonstrate their inherent validity (Pramanya) across all traditional Hindu streams.

Primary Methodology: Rooted in the primary authority of Shruti (Veda-Samhitas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, and Upanishads) and extended through Itihasa, Purana, and Smrti. The framework subordinates post-scriptural human opinion to explicit scriptural injunctions and applies Mimamsa hermeneutics (Samanvaya) to reconcile apparent contradictions rather than discarding texts.

2 Scriptural Extension of Authority & The Scope of Revelation

2.1 Expansion of Valid Authority Beyond the Four Vedas

First, let us examine the claim that the only valid scripture for Hindus is the Vedas. This premise is proven incorrect by the Vedas themselves.

Extension to Smrti

In the Taittiriya Samhita of the Yajurveda (2.2.10.2), Manusmrti is explicitly referenced:

Show संस्कृतम्

यद् वै मनुर् अवदत् तद् भेषजम्

yad vai manur avadat tad bheshajam

"Whatever Manu said is medicine."

Because the authority and validity of the Manusmrti is established directly by the Veda, anyone rejecting the Manusmrti must simultaneously reject the validity of the Veda itself. This is how the category of Shastra (scripture) is extended to authoritative texts outside of Shruti.

Extension to Itihasa and Purana

In the Chandogya Upanishad (7.1.4), it is stated:

Show संस्कृतम्

नाम वा ऋग्वेदो यजुर्वेदः सामवेद आथर्वणश् चतुर्थ इतिहास-पुराणः पञ्चमो वेदानां वेदः पित्र्यो राशिर् दैवो निधिर् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्या ब्रह्मविद्या भूतविद्या क्षत्रविद्या नक्षत्रविद्या सर्पदेवजनविद्या नामैवैतन्नामोपास्स्वेति ॥ ७.१.४ ॥

naama vaa rigvedo yajurvedah saamaveda aatharvanash chaturtha itihaasa-puraanah panychamo vedaanaam vedah pitryo raashir daivo nidhir vaakovaakyam ekaayanam devavidyaa brahmavidyaa bhuutavidyaa kshatravidyaa nakshatravidyaa sarpadevajanavidyaa naamaivaitannaamopaassveti || 7.1.4 ||

"Name indeed is the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, the Atharvaveda as the fourth, the Itihasa-Purana as the fifth among the Vedas..."

Therefore, the category of the Veda-and Shruti-level authority by extension-includes the Itihasas (such as the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Shiva Rahasya Itihasa) as well as the 36 Puranas codified by Vedavyasa (consisting of the 18 Maha-Puranas and 18 Upa-Puranas).

Validation of Smrtis through Itihasa and Purana The validity of the Smrtis is further established across multiple Puranas and Itihasas. For instance, the Mahabharata (Book 14, Ashvamedhika Parva, Chapter 96) enumerates an extensive lineage of authoritative Smrtis:

  • Manu, Vasishtha, Kashyapa, Gargya, Gautama, and Gopalaka: Codes of law authored by these ancient sages.
  • Parashara, Maitreya, Uma, Maheshvara, and Nandi: Ancient teachings, including the holy doctrines of Uma and Maheshvara and the purifying laws of Nandi.
  • Brahma, Sanatkumara (Kaumara), Dhumayana, and Kanda-Vaishvanara: Divine laws and teachings spoken by Brahma and other exalted figures.
  • Bhargava, Yajnavalkya, Markandeya, Bharadvaja, and Brhaspati: Authoritative law codes composed by these prominent rishis.
  • Kuni, Kunibahu, Vishvamitra, Sumantu, Jaimini, and Shakuneya: Dharmic codes authored across various sage traditions.
  • Pulastya, Pulaha, Agni (Pavakiya), Agastya, Mudgala, Shandilya, and Shalabhayana: Sacred laws sung by primary sages and divine figures.
  • Valakhilyas, Saptarshis (Seven Sages), Apastamba, Shankha, and Likhita: Essential legal and moral frameworks codified by these revered groups and seers.
  • Prajapati, Yama, Mahendra, Vyaghra, Vyasa, and Vibhandaka: Traditions handed down through divine and patriarchal lineages.
  • Narada, Kapota, Vidura, Bhrigu, and Angiras: Wise sayings, dharmic traditions, and foundational moral codes.
  • Kraunca, Mrdanga, Surya (Solar Traditions), Harita, Pishanga, Katapaya, and Suvalaka: Ancient sung traditions and specialized legal lineages.
  • Uddalaka, Ushanas (Shukracarya), and Vaishampayana: Laws and teachings passed down through major classical lineages.

Furthermore, the Parashara Smrti-one of the Smrtis explicitly mentioned in the Mahabharata-enumerates additional canonical texts in verses 1.1.12-1.1.15:

Show संस्कृतम्

धर्मं कथय मे तात अनुग्राह्यो ह्यहं तव ।
श्रुता मे मानवा धर्मा वासिष्ठा काश्यपास्तथा ॥ १.१२ ॥
गर्गेया गौतमीयाश्च तथा चोशनसा स्मृताः ।
अत्रेर्विष्णोश्च संवर्ताद्दक्षादङ्गिरसस्तथा ॥ १.१३ ॥
शातातपाच्च हारीताद्याज्ञवल्क्यात्तथैव च ।
आपस्तम्बकृता धर्मा शङ्खस्य लिखितस्य च ॥ १.१४ ॥
कात्यायनकृताश्चैव तथा प्राचेतसान्मुनेः ।
श्रुता ह्येते भवत्प्रोक्ताः श्रुत्यर्था मे न विस्मृताः ॥ १.१५ ॥

dharmam kathaya me taata anugraahyo hyaham tava |
shrutaa me maanavaa dharmaa vaasishthaa kaashyapaastathaa || 1.12 ||
gargeyaa gautamiiyaashcha tathaa choshanasaa smritaah |
atrervishnoshcha samvartaaddakshaadanggirasastathaa || 1.13 ||
shaataatapaachcha haariitaadyaajnyavalkyaattathaiva cha |
aapastambakritaa dharmaa shangkhasya likhitasya cha || 1.14 ||
kaatyaayanakritaashchaiva tathaa praachetasaanmuneh |
shrutaa hyete bhavatproktaah shrutyarthaa me na vismritaah || 1.15 ||

"O Father, please tell me about Dharma (righteousness/duty), for I deserve your grace. I have already heard of the codes of law (dharmas) authored by Manu, Vasistha, and Kashyapa;"

"...as well as those remembered from Gargya, Gautama, Ushanas (Shukracharya), Atri, Vishnu, Samvarta, Daksha, and Angiras;"

"...and those proclaimed by Shatatapa, Harita, Yajnavalkya, Apastamba, Shankha, and Likhita;"

"...as well as those composed by Katyayana and the sage Prachetas. I have heard all these as taught by you, and the teachings of the Vedic texts have not been forgotten by me."

Because several of these Smrtis cross-reference even more text lineages, this canonical list is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

In the Annapurna Upanishad (Chapter 3, Verse 21)-which is Shruti, belongs to the Atharvaveda, and is part of the Muktika Canon of 108 Upanishads-the Shaiva Agamas are not subordinated, but are explicitly identified as the ultimate conclusion of all Shastras, including the Vedas themselves:

Show संस्कृतम्

शिवः शैवागमस्थाना कालः कालैकवादिनाम् । यत्सर्वशास्त्रसिद्धान्तं यत्सर्वहृदयानुगमम् ॥ २१ ॥

shivah shaivaagamasthaanaa kaalah kaalaikavaadinaam | yatsarvashaastrasiddhaantam yatsarvahridayaanugamam || 21 ||

"What Shiva is to the followers of the Shaiva Agamas, and as Time to those who uphold Time as the ultimate truth. It is the ultimate conclusion of all scriptures and that which resonates within every heart."

Furthermore, the Chandogya Upanishad (7.1.2) references the Agamas under another traditional designation, Ekayana:

Show संस्कृतम्

ऋग्वेदं भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदम् आथर्वणं चतुर्थम् इतिहास-पुराणं पञ्चमं वेदानां वेदं पित्र्यं राशिं दैवं निधिम् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्यां ब्रह्मविद्यां भूतविद्यां क्षत्रविद्यां नक्षत्रविद्यां सर्पदेवजनविद्याम् एतद् भगवोऽध्येमि ॥ ७.१.२ ॥

rigvedam bhagavo'dhyemi yajurvedam saamavedam aatharvanam chaturtham itihaasa-puraanam panychamam vedaanaam vedam pitryam raashim daivam nidhim vaakovaakyam ekaayanam devavidyaam brahmavidyaam bhuutavidyaam kshatravidyaam nakshatravidyaam sarpadevajanavidyaam etad bhagavo'dhyemi || 7.1.2 ||

In his work Agama Pramanya, Yamunacarya explicitly identifies Ekayana as referring to the Kashmir-Agamas, affirming them as apaurusheya (authorless/eternal) and treating them as Shruti when examining this verse:

Show संस्कृतम्

एकायनशाखाया अपौरुषेयत्वं तथा काश्मीरागमप्रामाण्ये प्रपञ्चितम् इति नेह प्रस्तूयते.

ekaayanashaakhaayaa apaurusheyatvam tathaa kaashmiiraagamapraamaanye prapanychitam iti neha prastuuyate.

"...the eternality/authoritativeness of the Ekayana Shakha has been detailed by me in [the work] Kashmira-Agama-Pramanya, so it is not discussed here."

Purva Paksha Objection: The Ekayana Shakha only refers to Vaishnava or Pancaratra works, not other Agamas.

Refutation & Validation: The Agamas are explicitly acknowledged and validated by Vedavyasa himself in the Mahabharata (Shanti Parva, Mokshadharma Parva, Chapter 349, Verse 64). Vyasa asserts that speculative reasoning (hetu) cannot be used to invalidate either the Pashupata or Pancaratra texts along with the Vedas, as they possess intrinsic validity (atmapramana):

Show संस्कृतम्

साङ्ख्यं योगः पाञ्चरात्रं वेदाः पाशुपतं तथा । आत्मप्रमाणान्य् एतानि न हन्तव्यानि हेतुभिः ॥

saangkhyam yogah paanycharaatram vedaah paashupatam tathaa | aatmapramaanaany etaani na hantavyaani hetubhih ||

"Sankhya, Yoga, Pancaratra, the Vedas, and the Pashupata-these are self-evident authorities (atmapramana) and should not be invalidated by logical arguments."

Furthermore, prominent Acaryas within Yamunacarya's own Shri Vaishnava lineage confirm that the scope of authoritative Agamas extends beyond Vaishnava texts. Vedanta Deshika, in his Pancaratra Raksha, explicitly accepts the validity of the Pashupata Agamas while commenting on the aforementioned Mahabharata verse:

Show संस्कृतम्

अत एव भगवतापि शिवादिरूपान्तरैर् यथार्हाधिकारिफलविशेषार्थिभ्यः तदुपदेशाः कृताः.

ata eva bhagavataapi shivaadiruupaantarair yathaarhaadhikaariphalavisheshaarthibhyah tadupadeshaah kritaah.

"Accordingly, the Lord (as Siva) revealed the Sastra for the sake of those who sought particular fruits (phala-vishesharthibhyah.) and in accordance with their respective fitness."

Show संस्कृतम्

ततश् च साङ्ख्यादीनां तत्र तत्र पृथगुपदिष्टफलविशेषप्राप्तिपरतया प्रामाण्यम् इति सिद्धम्.

tatash cha saangkhyaadiinaam tatra tatra prithagupadishtaphalavisheshapraaptiparatayaa praamaanyam iti siddham.

"...it follows, therefore, that the Sankhya and other systems are valid for those who seek the particular results (phala-visheshah.) mentioned in them."

To reinforce this point, Vedanta Deshika cites Lord Shiva directly from the Ahirbudhnya Samhita (12.4-5):

Show संस्कृतम्

साङ्ख्यं योगं तथैवान्यच्छास्त्रं यत् परमं तपः । विस्तरेण हि तस्योक्तं मयैव विदुरेति वै ॥

saangkhyam yogam tathaivaanyachchhaastram yat paramam tapah | vistarena hi tasyoktam mayaiva vidureti vai ||

"Sankhya, Yoga, and other systems which are supreme austerity - these were spoken at length by Me alone, O Vidura."

Thus, non-Vaishnava Agamas are explicitly acknowledged by Shri Vaishnava Acaryas as part of the broader Agamic tradition identified with the Ekayana Shakha.

In the Shiva Purana (Kailasa Samhita, Chapter 21, Verses 9-10), the Shaiva Agamas are explicitly prescribed by Lord Shiva through a Vidhi-Vakya (injunction) for aspirants seeking spiritual realization:

Show संस्कृतम्

अधैर्यचित्तो यः कश्चित् समाधिं न च विन्दति । तदुपायं प्रवक्ष्यामि सावधानतया शृणु ॥ ९ ॥

adhairyachitto yah kashchit samaadhim na cha vindati | tadupaayam pravakshyaami saavadhaanatayaa shrinu || 9 ||

"If there is such a person devoid of concentration who does not attain Samadhi, I shall mention the means of remedy for him. Listen attentively."

Show संस्कृतम्

त्रिपदार्थपरिज्ञानं वेदान्तागमवाक्यजम् । श्रुत्वा गुरोर्मुखाद् योगं अभ्यसेत् स यमादिकम् ॥ १० ॥

tripadaarthaparijnyaanam vedaantaagamavaakyajam | shrutvaa gurormukhaad yogam abhyaset sa yamaadikam || 10 ||

"On hearing from his preceptor the lore of Vedanta and Agamas along with the explanation, he shall practise Yoga by means of Yama, Niyama, etc."

Even if one attempts to dismiss references to the Shaiva Agamas within the Shiva Purana under the pretext of sectarian categorization, the Padma Purana (Bhumi-Khanda, Chapter 69, Verse 1) independently designates the Shivadharma Agamas as Uttama (supreme/excellent):

Show संस्कृतम्

अथ धर्माः शिवेनोक्ताः शिवधर्मागमोत्तमाः । ज्ञेया बहुविधास् ते च कर्मयोगप्रभेदतः ॥ हिंसादिदोषनिर्मुक्ताः क्लेशायासविवर्जिताः । सर्वभूतहिताः शुद्धाः सूक्ष्मायासा महत्फलाः ॥ १ ॥

atha dharmaah shivenoktaah shivadharmaagamottamaah | jnyeyaa bahuvidhaas te cha karmayogaprabhedatah || himsaadidoshanirmuktaah kleshaayaasavivarjitaah | sarvabhuutahitaah shuddhaah suukshmaayaasaa mahatphalaah || 1 ||

"Now, the supreme righteous duties (dharmas) spoken by Lord Shiva in the excellent Shivadharma Agamas are to be known in their many variations according to the divisions of Karma Yoga. They are free from faults such as violence (hisa), devoid of affliction and extreme exertion, beneficial to all living beings, pure, requiring subtle effort, yet yielding immense fruits."

In the Skanda Purana (Suta Samhita, Yajna-vaibhava Khanda, Chapter 22), the Bhairava, Shaiva, and Pashupata Agamas-along with the Vaishnava Agamas (referring to the Pancaratra Agamas) and even the Jain and Bauddha Agamas-are declared to possess validity (pramanya) as principal Agamas:

2.2 Recognition of the Agamas across Itihasa and Purana

Show संस्कृतम्

अथातः सम्प्रवक्ष्यामि मार्गप्रामाण्यनिर्णयम् । श्रद्धया सहिता यूयं शृणुध्व मुनिसत्तमाः ॥ वेदाश् च धर्मशास्त्राणि पुराण-भारतं तथा । वेदाङ्गाद्युपवेदाश् च कामिकाद्यागमान् अपि ॥ कापालं नाकुलम् चैव तथोपादानं द्विजर्षभाः । तथा पाशुपतं सौम्यं भैरवप्रमुखान् आगमान् ॥ तेषाम् एवोपभेदाश् च शतशोऽथ सहस्रशः । विष्ण्वागमांस् तथा बाह्यान् बुद्धार्हदागमान् अपि ॥ लोकायतं तर्कशास्त्रं बहुविस्तारसयुतम् । मीमांसामतिगम्भीराः साङ्ख्ययोगौ तथैव च ॥ ... तान्येव विस्तरेणैव सङ्ग्रहेण वा पुनः । कुर्वन्ति तानि नामानि कथितानि महर्षिभिः । अधिकारिविभेदेन नैकरूपा हि सदा द्विजाः । तर्कैरेते हि मार्गास्तु न हन्तव्या मनीषिभिः ॥

athaatah sampravakshyaami maargapraamaanyanirnayam | shraddhayaa sahitaa yuuyam shrinudhva munisattamaah || vedaash cha dharmashaastraani puraana-bhaaratam tathaa | vedaanggaadyupavedaash cha kaamikaadyaagamaan api || kaapaalam naakulam chaiva tathopaadaanam dvijarshabhaah | tathaa paashupatam saumyam bhairavapramukhaan aagamaan || teshaam evopabhedaash cha shatasho'tha sahasrashah | vishnvaagamaams tathaa baahyaan buddhaarhadaagamaan api || lokaayatam tarkashaastram bahuvistaarasayutam | miimaamsaamatigambhiiraah saangkhyayogau tathaiva cha ||... taanyeva vistarenaiva sanggrahena vaa punah | kurvanti taani naamaani kathitaani maharshibhih | adhikaarivibhedena naikaruupaa hi sadaa dvijaah | tarkairete hi maargaastu na hantavyaa maniishibhih ||

"Now I shall explain the decision regarding the authoritativeness of the paths (marga). O best of sages, listen with faith! The Vedas, Dharma-shastras, Puranas, the Bharata (Mahabharata), the Vedangas, Upavedas, the Kamika and other Agamas, the Kapala, Nakula, Upadana, the Pashupata, Saumya, and Bhairava-these major Agamas, along with their sub-sects by the hundreds and thousands; the Vaishnava Agamas, and even the external Buddhist and Jaina Agamas, the Lokayata system, Logic (Tarka-shastra), the deep Mimamsa, and Sankhya and Yoga-the great sages have given names to these paths and taught them in detail or in brief according to the varying eligibility (adhikara) of seekers. These paths should not be invalidated by wise men through logical arguments."

Specific emphasis is laid on the principle that each of these Agamic systems is articulated according to the varying adhikara (competence/qualification) of seekers. This framework of Adhikara-Bheda is central to addressing passages in certain Puranas that designate particular Agamas as mohaka or delusory.

Purva Paksha Objection: This verse from the Skanda Purana is merely Arthavada (praise/exaggeration) and cannot be taken literally.

Refutation: The notion that statements within the Puranas can be casually dismissed as Arthavada is explicitly rejected in scripture. The Narada Purana (Purva-Khanda, Chapter 1, Verses 57-58) strictly prohibits reading Arthavada into the Puranas:

Show संस्कृतम्

पुराणेष्व् अर्थवादत्वं ये वदन्ति नराधमाः ॥ १.५७ ॥ तैर् अर्जितानि पुण्यानि क्षयं यान्ति द्विजोत्तमाः । समस्तकर्मनिर्मूलसाधनानि नराधमाः ॥ १.५८ ॥ पुराणान्य् अर्थवादेन ब्रुवन्न् अरकम् अश्नुते ॥

puraaneshv arthavaadatvam ye vadanti naraadhamaah || 1.57 || tair arjitaani punyaani kshayam yaanti dvijottamaah | samastakarmanirmuulasaadhanaani naraadhamaah || 1.58 || puraanaany arthavaadena bruvann arakam ashnute ||

"Those lowest among men who claim that the Puranas contain Arthavada-all their accumulated merit and virtue are destroyed, O best of dvijas. By declaring the Puranas to be Arthavada, such wretched men go to hell."

Purva Paksha Objection: The Skanda Purana is classified as a Tamasika Purana (e.g., in the Padma Purana) and its statements are therefore non-authoritative.

Refutation: This line of objection creates a mutual contradiction. While the Padma Purana classifies the Skanda Purana as Tamasika, the Skanda Purana itself (Shankara Samhita, Daksha Khanda) conversely classifies the Vaishnava Puranas-including the Padma Purana-as Tamasika:

Show संस्कृतम्

वैष्णवानि च चत्वारि तामसानि मनीश्वराः.

vaishnavaani cha chatvaari taamasaani maniishvaraah.

"O foremost of sages! The four Vaishnava Puranas are considered Tamasic. In them, the duties of the Kshatriyas (warrior class) are heard, and Hari (Vishnu) is their presiding deity."

Relying on one text to invalidate another when both make reciprocal claims results in Atmashraya (circular reasoning/self-dependence), which is an unacceptable defect in classical hermeneutics. According to Purva Mimamsa hermeneutics, scriptures must be interpreted through inherent authority rather than selective rejection. As stated in the Mimamsa Sutras (1.1.5):

Show संस्कृतम्

औत्पत्तिकस् तु शब्दस्यार्थेन सम्बन्धः । तस्य ज्ञानम् उपदेशो ऽव्यतिरेकश् चार्थे ऽनुपलब्धे । तत्प्रमाणं बादरायणस्यानपेक्षत्वात् ॥ ५ ॥

autpattikas tu shabdasyaarthena sambandhah | tasya jnyaanam upadesho 'vyatirekash chaarthe 'nupalabdhe | tatpramaanam baadaraayanasyaanapekshatvaat || 5 ||

"On the other hand, the relation of the word with its meaning is inborn and eternal; consequently, injunction is the means of knowing dharma, and it is unfailing in regard to objects not perceived by other means of knowledge. It is authoritative, according to Badarayana, especially as it is independent and self-sufficient in its authority."

Therefore, discarding explicit scriptural statements by labeling them as "absurd" or Tamasika violates the foundational principle of Anapekshatva (independent self-sufficiency of scriptural authority).

Purva Paksha Objection: The Shankara Samhita and Suta Samhita are not legitimate parts of the Skanda Purana.

Context of the Objection: It is true that standard modern print editions (such as those from Gita Press) structure the Skanda Purana into seven Khandas totaling 81,000 verses. This structure

is referenced in the Narada Purana (Chapter 104, Verse 3), where Lord Brahma states:

Show संस्कृतम्

स्कन्दाह्वयस् तत्र खण्डाः सप्तैव परिकल्पिताः । एकाशीतिसहस्रं तु स्कान्दं सर्वोघकृन्तनम् ॥ ३ ॥

skandaahvayas tatra khandaah saptaiva parikalpitaah | ekaashiitisahasram tu skaandam sarvoghakrintanam || 3 ||

"In that Purana named Skanda, seven sections (khandas) have been designed. The Skanda Purana consists of eightyone thousand (ekashitisahasram) verses, and it destroys the entire accumulation of sins."

Refutation & Textual Evidence: Lord Brahma's statement in the Narada Purana enumerates a specific recension of 81,000 verses, but nowhere does it state that this represents the absolute limit or entirety of the Skanda Purana corpus. The internal testimony of the Skanda Purana's own Samhita tradition affirms a larger, expanded recension of 100,000 verses across six Samhitas:

Show संस्कृतम्

स्वतन्त्रम् एकम् अद्वैतं प्रवदन्ति निरङ्कुशम् । तत्र स्कान्दं प्रवक्ष्यामि पुराणेषु त्रयोदशम् ॥ ४४ ॥ सहिताभिः तथा षड्भिः लक्षैर् ग्रन्थैर् विराजितम् । दश चैव पुराणानि सात्त्विकानि विदुर् बुधाः ॥ ४५ ॥

svatantram ekam advaitam pravadanti nirangkusham | tatra skaandam pravakshyaami puraaneshu trayodasham || 44 || sahitaabhih tathaa shadbhih lakshair granthair viraajitam | dasha chaiva puraanaani saattvikaani vidur budhaah || 45 ||

"They declare Him to be independent, one without a second (non-dual), and completely unrestricted. Among those Puranas, I shall now narrate the Skanda Purana, which is the thirteenth. It shines brilliantly with six Samhitas and contains one hundred thousand verses. Wise scholars know that ten of the Puranas are Sattvika."

Combining the verse counts of the extant Samhitas (the Suta Samhita and Shankara Samhita) with the seven Khandas yields precisely 100,000 verses, matching the internal verse count specified within the Samhita text itself. Furthermore, this 100,000-verse recension is explicitly recognized and validated by other major Puranas. The Bhavishya Purana (Brahma Parva, 1.1.103-105) explicitly states:

Show संस्कृतम्

सर्वाण्य् एव पुराणानि संज्ञेयानि नरर्षभ । द्वादशैव सहस्राणि प्रोक्तानीह मनीषिभिः ॥ १०३ ॥ पुनर्वृद्धिं गतानीह आख्यानैर् विविधैर् वृष । यथा स्कान्दं तथा चेदं भविष्यं कुरुनन्दन ॥ १०४ ॥ स्कान्दं शतसहस्रं तु लोकानां ज्ञातम् एव हि । भविष्यम् एतद् ऋषिणा लक्षार्धं संख्यया कृतम् ॥ १०५ ॥

sarvaany eva puraanaani samjnyeyaani nararshabha | dvaadashaiva sahasraani proktaaniiha maniishibhih || 103 || punarvriddhim gataaniiha aakhyaanair vividhair vrisha | yathaa skaandam tathaa chedam bhavishyam kurunandana || 104 || skaandam shatasahasram tu lokaanaam jnyaatam eva hi | bhavishyam etad rishinaa lakshaardham samkhyayaa kritam || 105 ||

"All the Puranas were originally known to consist of twelve thousand verses by the wise, O best of men. Later, they expanded through various narratives-just like the Skanda Purana and this Bhavishya Purana, O descendant of Kuru. The Skanda Purana consists of one hundred thousand (shatasahasram) verses; indeed, this is well known among the people."

Historical transmission further corroborates the antiquity and authority of the Samhita tradition. The Kanda Puranam, authored in the 11th century CE by the Tamil Shaiva Acarya Kacchiyappa Shivachariyar, is a direct adaptation of the Shankara and Suta Samhitas of the Skanda Purana. This precedes the earliest citations of the Khanda recension found in the works of 13th-century commentators such as Ramanujacarya and Madhvacarya. Consequently, both the Khanda and Samhita recensions are authentic, historically transmitted branches of the Skanda Purana corpus, and both possess full scriptural authority (pramanya).

Purva Paksha Objection: Vedavyasa's Brahma Sutras mandate the complete rejection of the Pancaratra and Pashupata Agamas.

Refutation & Contextual Analysis: The text of the Brahma Sutras itself does not state that the Agamas are to be rejected as a whole. Rather, Brahma Sutra 2.2.42 specifically addresses a single cosmological point regarding origin:

Show संस्कृतम्

उत्पत्त्यसम्भवात् ॥ ४२ ॥

utpattyasambhavaat || 42 ||

"On account of the impossibility of origination."

The sutra targets a literal reading wherein the individual soul (Jiva) is generated or created-a notion contradicted by Shruti. While Adi Shankaracarya's commentary on the Brahma Sutras evaluates philosophical positions of the Pashupata and Pancaratra schools, Shankaracarya explicitly upholds the devotional and ritual validity (Pancakala Kriya) prescribed in the Pancaratra Agamas:

Show संस्कृतम्

...इति, तन् न निराक्रियते, “स एकधा भवति त्रिधा भवति” इत्यादिश्रुतिभ्यः परमात्मनोऽनेकधाभावस्याधिगतत्वात् । यद् अपि तस्य भगवतोऽभिगमनादिलक्षणम् आराधनम् अजस्रम् अनन्यचित्ततयाभिप्रेयते, तद् अपि न प्रतिषिध्यते, श्रुतिस्मृत्योर् ईश्वरप्रणिधानस्य प्रसिद्धत्वात् । यत् पुनर् इदम् उच्यते—वासुदेवात् सङ्कर्षण उत्पद्यते, सङ्कर्षणाच् च प्रद्युम्नः, प्रद्युम्नाच् चानिरुद्ध इति...

...iti, tan na niraakriyate, "sa ekadhaa bhavati tridhaa bhavati" ityaadishrutibhyah paramaatmano'nekadhaabhaavasyaadhigatatvaat | yad api tasya bhagavato'bhigamanaadilakshanam aaraadhanam ajasram ananyachittatayaabhipreyate, tad api na pratishidhyate, shrutismrityor iishvarapranidhaanasya prasiddhatvaat | yat punar idam uchyate-vaasudevaat sangkarshana utpadyate, sangkarshanaach cha pradyumnah, pradyumnaach chaaniruddha iti...

"...that is not refuted, from scriptural texts (Shruti) such as 'He becomes one-fold, he becomes three-fold...' (Chandogya Upanishad 7.26.2), because the state of the Supreme Self (Paramatman) existing in manifold forms is well understood. Even though worship of the Lord characterized by approaching Him continuously with undivided focus is intended, that too is not prohibited, because devotion to the Supreme Lord (Ishvara-pranidhana) is well-established in both Shruti and Smrti scriptures. As for what is further stated-that Sankarshana is born from Vasudeva, Pradyumna from Sankarshana, and Aniruddha from Pradyumna..."

Shankaracarya explicitly confirms that practical devotion, ritual methodology, and approach (abhigamana) prescribed by the Agamas are valid and non-prohibited, as they align with Shruti and Smrti. Furthermore, Adi Shankaracarya directly acknowledges the tradition of the 64 Bhairava Agamas in Verse 31 of the Saundarya Lahari:

चतुःषष्ट्या तन्त्रैः सकलम् अतिसन्धाय भुवनं स्थितस् तत्तत् सिद्धिप्रसवपरतन्त्रैः पशुपतिः । पुनस् त्वन्निर्बन्धाद् अखिलपुरुषार्थैकघटना-स्वतन्त्रं ते तन्त्रं क्षितितलम् अवातीतरद् इदम् ॥ ३१ ॥

chatuhshashtyaa tantraih sakalam atisandhaaya bhuvanam sthitas tattat siddhiprasavaparatantraih pashupatih | punas tvannirbandhaad akhilapurushaarthaikaghatanaa-svatantram te tantram kshititalam avaatiitarad idam || 31 ||

Purva Paksha Objection: The Brahma Sutras declare the Pashupata and Shaiva Agamas to be non-Vedic (Avaidika) and completely invalid.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Context: To properly address this objection, one must distinguish between the direct text of the Brahma Sutras and the later commentary traditions. The sutra frequently cited in this debate is Brahma Sutra 2.2.45:

Show संस्कृतम्

विप्रतिषेधाच् च ॥ ४५ ॥

vipratishedhaach cha || 45 ||

"And on account of contradictions."

When read in its proper contextual sequence (prakarana) alongside the preceding sutras (Brahma Sutras 2.2.42-44: utpattyasambhavat, na ca kartuh. karanam, and vijnanadibhave va tadapratishedhah.), this discussion addresses specific metaphysical formulations (such as the origination of the Jiva) rather than issuing a total rejection of the Agamic corpus itself.

This contextual interpretation aligns directly with the hermeneutical principles of Purva Mimamsa Sutra 1.2.24:

Show संस्कृतम्

प्रकरणे सम्भवन्न् अपकर्षो न कल्प्येत, विध्यानर्थक्यं हि सम्प्रति ॥ २४ ॥

prakarane sambhavann apakarsho na kalpyeta, vidhyaanarthakyam hi samprati || 24 ||

"In the case of that which is compatible with its own context, detachment or removal should not be assumed; for otherwise, the injunction itself would become meaningless."

Even in commentators who vigorously debate rival metaphysical positions, the validity of Agamic worship and practice remains intact. As Shankaracarya himself notes in his commentary on the adjacent sutras:

Show संस्कृतम्

तन्न निराक्रियते

tanna niraakriyate

"That is not refuted."

Furthermore, responding to apparent philosophical dualisms between Shruti and Agama, the Shaiva Acarya Jayaratha, in his commentary Tantralokaviveka on Abhinavagupta's Tantraloka, expounds on the secret nature of Brahman:

Show संस्कृतम्

नहि परस्यां संविदि तदुभयस्य स्थूलसूक्ष्मरूपस्य कश्चिद्विरोधः

nahi parasyaam samvidi tadubhayasya sthuulasuukshmaruupasya kashchidvirodhah

"In the Supreme Consciousness, there is no contradiction whatsoever between those two forms-the gross and the subtle."

The dialectical description of the Supreme Being as transcending standard logical categories is not a defect or contradiction, but a foundational feature of Brahman. In the Bhagavad Gita (Chapter 13, Verse 13), Lord Krshna Himself defines the nature of Brahman using these exact paradoxes:

Show संस्कृतम्

ज्ञेयं यत् तत् प्रवक्ष्यामि यज् ज्ञात्वामृतम् अश्नुते । अनादि मत्-परं ब्रह्म न सत् तन् नासद् उच्यते ॥ १३ ॥

jnyeyam yat tat pravakshyaami yaj jnyaatvaamritam ashnute | anaadi mat-param brahma na sat tan naasad uchyate || 13 ||

"I shall now describe that which is to be known, knowing which one attains immortality. It is the beginningless Supreme Brahman, which is said to be neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat)."

Similarly, the Kalika Purana (Chapter 1, Verses 19-20) employs identical language to describe the transcendent and immanent nature of the Supreme Reality:

Show संस्कृतम्

व्यक्ताव्यक्तस्वरूपाय सदसद्व्यक्तरूपिणे । स्थूलाय सूक्ष्मरूपाय विश्वरूपाय वेधसे ॥ १९ ॥ नित्याय नित्यज्ञानाय निर्विकाराय चेतसे । विद्याऽविद्यास्वरूपाय कलारूपाय वै नमः ॥ २० ॥

vyaktaavyaktasvaruupaaya sadasadvyaktaruupine | sthuulaaya suukshmaruupaaya vishvaruupaaya vedhase || 19 || nityaaya nityajnyaanaaya nirvikaaraaya chetase | vidyaa'vidyaasvaruupaaya kalaaruupaaya vai namah || 20 ||

"Salutations to the Creator, whose nature is both manifest (vyakta) and unmanifest (avyakta), who embodies both being (sat) and non-being (asat); who is gross (sthula) yet subtle (sukshma), and whose form is the entire universe; who is eternal, possessing eternal knowledge, unchanging consciousness, and who embodies both knowledge (vidya) and ignorance (avidya) as well as all creative arts (kala)."

Far from indicating incoherence, these descriptions express the absolute transcendence of Brahman/Shakti beyond dualistic conceptual frameworks. They affirm that empirical logic based on mundane categories cannot invalidate the self-evident authority (svatahpramanya) of divine revelation across the Shruti, Purana, and Agama literature.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Pashupata doctrine is inherently non-Vedic (Avaidika) and encourages practices opposed to Vedic orthodoxy.

Refutation & Canonical Evidence: This objection fails to account for the foundational texts of the Pashupata tradition itself, which explicitly uphold the authority of the Vedas and the traditional Ashrama system. In the Pashupata Sutras (4.20), accompanied by Kaundinya's commentary (Pancartha Bhashya), the trajectory of liberation for a Vedic practitioner is unambiguously defined:

Show संस्कृतम्

न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनरावर्तते ॥ २० ॥

na kashchid braahmanah punaraavartate || 20 ||

"No Brahmana whatsoever returns again [to mundane existence]."

In his authoritative commentary, Kaundinya details the exact scope and Vedic alignment of this sutra:

Show संस्कृतम्

अत्र नकारोपदेशोऽन्याचरणप्रतिपत्तिप्रतिषेधार्थः — कश्चिद् इति गृहस्थाद्यः — स्थानमात्रवैलक्षण्यदर्शनाद् ब्राह्मणेष्व् एव कश्चिच्छब्दः — गृहस्थो ब्रह्मचारी वानप्रस्थो भिक्षुर् एकवेदो द्विवेदस् त्रिवेदश् चतुर्वेदश् च गायत्रीमात्रसारो वानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं प्राप्तः सन् न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनर् आवर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — ब्राह्मणग्रहणं ब्राह्मण्यावधारणार्थम्, ब्राह्मण एव नान्य इत्य् अर्थः — क्षेत्रज्ञे च ब्राह्मणसंज्ञा — कस्मात्? उपचयजन्मयोगात् संस्कारयोगात्, श्रुतियोगाच् च ब्राह्मणः — पुनःशब्दः पुनरावृत्तिप्रतिषेधे — यथा पूर्वं संज्ञानादिभिर् गत्वा आवर्तते पुनः पुनः, तथानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं गत्वा न सकृद् आवर्तते — पुनः पुनः सर्वथापि नावर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — आङ् इति स्वशास्त्रोक्थमर्यादम् अधिकुरुते, अभिविध्यर्थं च — ये चानेन विधिना क्षपिताज्ञानकलुषपापमायादयः क्षीणास् ते पुनः पुनर् आवर्तन्ते — न तैः सह संयोगो भवति, न चापरं जन्म प्रतिपद्यत इत्य् अर्थः ॥ २० ॥

atra nakaaropadesho'nyaacharanapratipattipratishedhaarthah - kashchid iti grihasthaadyah - sthaanamaatravailakshanyadarshanaad braahmaneshv eva kashchichchhabdah - grihastho brahmachaarii vaanaprastho bhikshur ekavedo dvivedas trivedash chaturvedash cha gaayatriimaatrasaaro vaanena vidhinaa rudrasamiipam praaptah san na kashchid braahmanah punar aavartata ity arthah - braahmanagrahanam braahmanyaavadhaaranaartham, braahmana eva naanya ity arthah - kshetrajnye cha braahmanasamjnyaa - kasmaat? upachayajanmayogaat samskaarayogaat, shrutiyogaach cha braahmanah - punahshabdah punaraavrittipratishedhe - yathaa puurvam samjnyaanaadibhir gatvaa aavartate punah punah, tathaanena vidhinaa rudrasamiipam gatvaa na sakrid aavartate - punah punah sarvathaapi naavartata ity arthah - aang iti svashaastrokthamaryaadam adhikurute, abhividhyartham cha - ye chaanena vidhinaa kshapitaajnyaanakalushapaapamaayaadayah kshiinaas te punah punar aavartante - na taih saha samyogo bhavati, na chaaparam janma pratipadyata ity arthah || 20 ||

"Here, the instruction of the negative particle (na) is intended to prohibit the adoption of any other conduct. The word kashcid ('anyone') refers to householders and others. Because the distinction is seen only in their stage of life (Ashrama), the word kashcid applies exclusively among the Brahmanas. The meaning is that a householder (grhastha), a celibate student (brahmacari), a forest-dweller (vanaprastha), or a mendicant (bhikshu)-whether he knows one Veda, two Vedas, three Vedas, four Vedas, or has only the Gayatri mantra as his sole essence-having attained the proximity of Lord Rudra by this prescribed method, no such Brahmana returns. The use of the word Brahmana is to firmly restrict it to Brahmanahood; meaning, a Brahmana alone and no one else. Furthermore, the designation Brahmana is applied to the Kshetrajna (the conscious soul). Why? Because one becomes a Brahmana through the association with noble birth, the association with purifying sacraments (saskaras), and the association with Vedic learning (shruti). The word punah. ('again') is used to prohibit rebirth. Just as previously, having departed through mere worldly knowledge, one returns again and again, similarly, having approached Lord Rudra by this method, one does not return even once. Under no circumstances does one return again. The prefix An denotes the limit stated in one's own scriptures and also signifies inclusive extension. Those whose ignorance, impurities, and maya have been exhausted by this prescribed method do not return again. There is no further association with them, nor do they attain another birth. This is the meaning."

This foundational text proves that the Pashupata system explicitly integrates Vedic learning (shruti-yoga), sacraments (saskara-yoga), and traditional Ashrama stages as valid bases for spiritual practice and final liberation (duhkhanta). Consequently, labeling the Pashupata doctrine as fundamentally anti-Vedic contradicts its own primary canonical source.

"'O mighty-armed Rudra, create scriptures that delude [the unworthy]. Show them paths that require little effort yet promise great rewards. O Maheshvara, delude all people quickly by exhibiting tricks, magic, and contradictory practices... Indeed, for the delusion of those who have strayed from the path of the Vedas, scriptures under the names of logic and established doctrines (naya-siddhanta) were presented by me. This bond (pasha) is the state of animallike existence (pashubhava); when that falls away, then the Pashupata scripture becomes known as equal to the Vedas... O sage among Brahmanas, I am that which is to be known through the Vedas...'"

The core argument posed by Vaishnava critics rests on a specific deductive chain: Vishnu commands Rudra to produce deluding scriptures (moha-shastras) for those outside the Vedic fold; because the Pashupata doctrine appears within this discourse, the entire Shaiva/Pashupata Agamic corpus is inferred to be mohaka.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Resolution: This conclusion is untenable when subjected to rigorous textual and Mimasaka analysis: 1. Specificity vs. Universal Invalidation: The text explicitly uses terms such as moha-shastrani, kuhaka, indrajalani, and viruddhacarana. Nowhere does the text make a blanket assertion that the 28 canonical Shaiva Agamas broadly constitute these moha-shastras. 2. Exegesis of Verse 70.43 and Adhikara-Bheda: A close reading of Verse 70.43 reveals the core intent of the passage:

Show संस्कृतम्

तदा पाशुपतं शास्त्रं जायते वेदसंज्ञितम् ॥ ४३ ॥

tadaa paashupatam shaastram jaayate vedasamjnyitam || 43 ||

The term patita refers to an individual who has fallen or degraded from the standard Vedic social and religious order. For such a person who is outside ordinary Vedic eligibility, the Pashupata Shastra assumes a Veda-like function (Veda-sajnita). This provides primary Puranic evidence for the principle of Adhikara- Bheda (differential qualification), demonstrating that Agamic revelation accommodates seekers who are otherwise disconnected from direct Vedic injunctions. 3. Mimamsa Interpretation of Designation: The term Vedasajnita cannot be casually discarded as empty praise or figure of speech. As established in Purva Mimamsa Sutra 1.4.2:

Show संस्कृतम्

अपि वा नामधेयं स्याद् यदुत्पत्ताव् अपूर्वम् अविधायकत्वात् ॥ २ ॥

api vaa naamadheyam syaad yadutpattaav apuurvam avidhaayakatvaat || 2 ||

"Rather, it should be recognized as a proper name/designation (namadheya), since it applies to an original subject and does not function as an injunction."

Because Veda-sajnita serves as a formal designation (namadheya) within the verse, it possesses direct interpretative value and cannot be dismissed as mere arthavada-a position further fortified by the Narada Purana's explicit prohibition against reading arthavada into Puranic statements. Thus, far from disproving Agamic validity, the Varaha Purana affirms that the Pashupata doctrine functions with Veda-like authority for its intended practitioners, perfectly aligned with the framework of Adhikara-Bheda.

Purva Paksha Objection: The Kurma Purana classifies all Agamas/Tantras as Mohaka, where Bhairava, Pancaratra, and Pashupata Agamas are labeled as Mohaka.

Analysis of the Objection: The Kurma Purana contains passages that explicitly designate various Agamic traditions as Mohaka (delusory):

Show संस्कृतम्

तस्माद् धि वेदबाह्यानां रक्षणार्थाय पापिनाम् । विमोहनाय शास्त्राणि करिष्यामो वृषध्वज ॥ १११ ॥

tasmaad dhi vedabaahyaanaam rakshanaarthaaya paapinaam | vimohanaaya shaastraani karishyaamo vrishadhvaja || 111 ||

"Therefore, for the protection of those outside the Vedic fold, and for their delusion, we shall compose shastras, O Vrshadhvaja."

Show संस्कृतम्

एवं स बोधितो रुद्रो माधवेन मुरारिणा । चकार मोहशास्त्राणि केशवोऽपि शिवेरितः ॥ ११२ ॥

evam sa bodhito rudro maadhavena muraarinaa | chakaara mohashaastraani keshavo'pi shiveritah || 112 ||

"Thus addressed by Madhava, Rudra composed the deluding shastras, and Keshava too, prompted by Shiva, [composed them]."

Show संस्कृतम्

कापालं नाकुलं वामं भैरवं पूर्वपश्चिमम् । पाञ्चरात्रं पाशुपतं तथान्यानि सहस्रशः ॥ ११३ ॥

kaapaalam naakulam vaamam bhairavam puurvapashchimam | paanycharaatram paashupatam tathaanyaani sahasrashah || 113 ||

"Kapala, Nakula, Vama, Bhairava, Purvapashcima, Pancaratra, Pashupata, and thousands of others [are among them]."

Refutation & Hermeneutical Re-interpretation: However, in the same passage, the purpose of Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva creating these scriptures is explicitly explained:

Show संस्कृतम्

तस्माद् वै वेदबाह्यानां रक्षणार्थाय पापिनाम् । विमोहनाय शास्त्राणि करिष्यामो वृषध्वज.

tasmaad vai vedabaahyaanaam rakshanaarthaaya paapinaam | vimohanaaya shaastraani karishyaamo vrishadhvaja.

"Therefore, for the protection of sinful people who are outside the Veda, we shall create scriptures for delusion (mohaka)..."

Here, Mohaka does not mean malicious delusion; rather, Mohaka can also signify enchantment or allure. The correct interpretation of these verses is that Lord Shiva and Lord Vishnu, through their grace and mercy, created the Agamas to allure people back toward the Vedas (Veda-marga). This underlying harmony is expressed in the Agamas themselves, such as in the Sarvollasa Tantra (Chapter 7):

Show संस्कृतम्

शृणु देवि प्रवक्ष्यामि सुराशुद्धिं यथाक्रमम् । मकाराणां च सर्वेषां शोधनं सिद्धिवर्धनम् ॥ ३४ ॥ वेदो निष्कलशब्दो वै वेदार्थोऽस्त्य् आगमः शिवे । वेदार्थागमतत्त्वज्ञैः सुरा सेव्या मुमुक्षुभिः ॥ ३५ ॥ न वेदागमयोर् भेदः सर्वथेति विनिश्चयः । शास्त्राणां परमेशानि भ्रान्तिः कार्या न कौलिकैः ॥ ३६ ॥

shrinu devi pravakshyaami suraashuddhim yathaakramam | makaaraanaam cha sarveshaam shodhanam siddhivardhanam || 34 || vedo nishkalashabdo vai vedaartho'sty aagamah shive | vedaarthaagamatattvajnyaih suraa sevyaa mumukshubhih || 35 || na vedaagamayor bhedah sarvatheti vinishchayah | shaastraanaam parameshaani bhraantih kaaryaa na kaulikaih || 36 ||

"'Listen, O Goddess! I shall explain, step-by-step, the purification of wine (sura) and the purification/sanctification of all the five elements (makaras), which enhances spiritual achievements (siddhis). -- 34 --' 'O Shive! The Veda is indeed the flawless, partless sound (nishkala shabda), and the essence/meaning of the Veda is the Agama (scripture). Those who seek liberation (mumukshu) and understand the underlying truths of both the Vedas and the Agamas may partake in the ritual offering. -- 35 --' 'There is absolutely no difference between the Vedas and the Agamas-this is a certainty. O Supreme Goddess, practitioners of the Kaula tradition (kaulikas) should not entertain any confusion or delusion regarding the scriptures. -- 36 --'"

If Mohaka does not mean enchantment and indeed means delusion, Shiva and Vishnu creating Agamas as Mohaka-Shastras does not make logical sense, as the creation of these very Shastras is explicitly referred to as a form of Ishvara's protection (rakshana). Moreover, the same passage states:

Show संस्कृतम्

जायन्तो मानुषे लोके क्षीणपापचयास् ततः । ईश्वराराधनबलाद् गच्छध्वं सुकृतां गतिम्.

jaayanto maanushe loke kshiinapaapachayaas tatah | iishvaraaraadhanabalaad gachchhadhvam sukritaam gatim.

"Having been born in the human world, with their accumulation of sin diminished, by the power of worship of Ishvara, go to the destination of the righteous."

Show संस्कृतम्

वर्तध्वं मत्प्रसादेन नान्यथा निष्कृतिर् हि वः.

vartadhvam matprasaadena naanyathaa nishkritir hi vah.

"Act according to my grace; there is no other means of deliverance for you."

Furthermore, the passage goes on to say that Lord Shiva himself incarnates to "mohayant" or enchant the world for their own benefit:

Show संस्कृतम्

मोहयन्त इमं लोकम् अवतीर्य महीतले । चकार शङ्करो भिक्षां हितायैषा द्विजैः सह.

mohayanta imam lokam avatiirya mahiitale | chakaara shangkaro bhikshaam hitaayaishaa dvijaih saha.

"Having descended to the surface of the earth, enchanting this world, Shankara went about begging for alms along with the twice-born (Dvijas) for their benefit."

It would not make sense to delude someone to hell for their own benefit. The text explicitly frames this mohaka as rakshana (protection).

Purva Paksha Objection: The Kurma Purana asserts that Tantrikas attain Naraka for many kalpas.

Analysis of the Objection Verse: The objection relies on Verse 114 of the Kurma Purana:

Show संस्कृतम्

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ कुर्वाणाः शास्त्रचोदितम् । पतन्तो निरये घोरे बहून् कल्पान् पुनः पुनः ॥ ११४ ॥

srishtvaa taan uuchatur devau kurvaanaah shaastrachoditam | patanto niraye ghore bahuun kalpaan punah punah || 114 ||

"Having created them, the two gods said: 'Those who act according to the injunctions of these shastras, falling into terrible hell, [remain there] for many kalpas again and again.'"

Refutation & Hermeneutical Re-interpretation:

1. Semantic Re-evaluation via Ashtanga Hrdaya Consider this alternative textual reading (patha-antara): in medical treatises

such as the Ashtanga Hrdaya, the phrase bahu-kalpa does not denote "many kalpas" (cosmic time cycles), but rather "of many methods" or therapeutic procedures. As is well established, the Agamas function as an upaya (expedient means) and consist of diverse methods-including Pancaratra, Shaiva, Pashupata, etc. This usage is attested in Verse 28:

Show संस्कृतम्

बहुकल्पं बहुगुणं सम्पन्नं योग्यम् औषधम् ॥ २८ ॥

bahukalpam bahugunam sampannam yogyam aushadham || 28 ||

2. Re-ordering of Syntax (Patha-krama) via Mimamsa Rules We can re-arrange the word order of Verse 114 in accordance with Purva Mimamsa Sutra 5.4.1:

Show संस्कृतम्

क्रमकोपोऽर्थशब्दानां श्रुतिविशेषाद् अर्थपरत्वाच् च ॥ ५.४.१ ॥

kramakopo'rthashabdaanaam shrutivisheshaad arthaparatvaach cha || 5.4.1 ||

"There is a disruption (modification) of the textual sequence of action-denoting words/mantras, because of explicit scriptural statement and because these words exist primarily to serve the execution of the action."

However, a modification of textual sequence (patha-krama) can only be invoked if there is a logical impossibility (anupapatti) in the literal reading of the verse. In Chapter 6, Verse 12 of the Harita Smrti, it is stated:

Show संस्कृतम्

महाभागवतो विप्रस् तान्त्रिकः सर्वकर्मसु ॥ १२ ॥

mahaabhaagavato vipras taantrikah sarvakarmasu || 12 ||

"A most exalted devotee of the Lord, a learned scholar, and is a Tantric adept in all actions."

Furthermore, Chapter 1, Verse 26 states:

Show संस्कृतम्

अचक्रधारी यो विप्रो बहुवेदश्रुतोऽपि वा । स जीवन्नेव चण्डालो मृतो निरयम् आप्नुयात् ॥ २६ ॥

achakradhaarii yo vipro bahuvedashruto'pi vaa | sa jiivanneva chandaalo mrito nirayam aapnuyaat || 26 ||

"Whether a learned scholar or well-versed in many Vedas, if a person does not wear the mark of the Cakra (discus), they live as an outcast and, upon death, attain hell."

The practice of wearing the Cakra (Cakra-dharana) is exclusive to the Pancaratra Agamas. If Verse 114 from the Kurma Purana were taken literally to suggest that following the Pancaratra Agamas leads to hell, it would create a direct contradiction with the Harita Smrti. Therefore, the re-arranged and correct patha-krama of this verse is:

Show संस्कृतम्

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ — पुनः पुनः घोरे निरये पतन्तः [जनाः], शास्त्रचोदितम् कुर्वाणाः बहून् कल्पान् [लभन्ते] —

srishtvaa taan uuchatur devau - punah punah ghore niraye patantah [janaah], shaastrachoditam kurvaanaah bahuun kalpaan [labhante] -

"Having created them, the two gods declared: 'For those people who fall again and again into the terrible hell [of worldly suffering], performing that which is enjoined by these scriptures provides many therapeutic methods (bahun kalpan).'"

3. Resolution via Smrti Superiority Even if this contextual re-interpretation of the Kurma Purana is not accepted, Vyasa Smrti (Chapter 1, Verse 4) explicitly rules that when a conflict arises between a Purana and a Smrti, the statements of the Smrti take precedence:

Show संस्कृतम्

श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानां विरोधो यत्र दृश्यते । तत्र श्रौतं प्रमाणं तु तयोर् द्वैधे स्मृतिर् वरा ॥ ४ ॥

shrutismritipuraanaanaam virodho yatra drishyate | tatra shrautam pramaanam tu tayor dvaidhe smritir varaa || 4 ||

"Where a contradiction is observed among Shruti, Smrti, and Purana, the Shruti is the supreme authority; in a conflict between Smrti and Purana, the Smrti is superior."

Purva Paksha Objection: The Agamas are not the Vedas, and therefore they lack scriptural authority (pramanya).

Establishing Shabda-Pramana for the Agamas: To establish the validity of the Agamas, we must first examine the nature of valid knowledge (jnana) as the sole means to liberation, and then demonstrate how the Agamas qualify as valid verbal testimony (shabda-pramana).

Jnana as the Sole Means to Liberation: In the Atmabodha (Verse 2), Adi Shankaracarya defines jnana as the direct and indispensable cause of liberation:

Show संस्कृतम्

बोधोऽन्यसाधनेभ्यो हि साक्षान् मोक्षैकसाधनम् । पाकस्य वह्नितज्ज्ञानं विना मोक्षो न सिध्यति ॥ २ ॥

bodho'nyasaadhanebhyo hi saakshaan mokshaikasaadhanam | paakasya vahnitajjnyaanam vinaa moksho na sidhyati || 2 ||

"Compared with all other forms of discipline, Knowledge of the Self (bodha) is the one direct means for liberation. Just as cooking cannot be accomplished without fire, so Liberation cannot be attained without wisdom."

In Verse 3, he further clarifies the inherent limitations of ritual action (karma) in destroying fundamental ignorance (avidya):

Show संस्कृतम्

अविरोधितया कर्म नाविद्यां विनिवर्तयेत् । विद्याविद्यां निहन्त्य् एव तेजस्तिमिरसङ्घवत् ॥ ३ ॥

avirodhitayaa karma naavidyaam vinivartayet | vidyaavidyaam nihanty eva tejastimirasangghavat || 3 ||

"Action cannot destroy ignorance, for it is not in conflict with or opposed to ignorance. Knowledge (vidya) alone destroys ignorance, just as light destroys deep darkness."

Epistemological Framework of Nyaya: The Nyaya system defines valid knowledge through four foundational means of cognition (pramanas). As stated in Nyaya Sutra 1.1.3:

2.5 Agamas as valid testimony and the reliability of Shiva

Show संस्कृतम्

प्रत्यक्षानुमानोपमानशब्दाः प्रमाणानि ॥ १.१.३ ॥

pratyakshaanumaanopamaanashabdaah pramaanaani || 1.1.3 ||

"Perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana), comparison (upamana), and verbal testimony (shabda) are the valid means of knowledge."

Nyaya Sutra 1.1.7 specifically defines the nature of shabdapramana (which governs authoritative verbal testimony and revealed scriptures):

Show संस्कृतम्

आप्तोपदेशः शब्दः ॥ १.१.७ ॥

aaptopadeshah shabdah || 1.1.7 ||

"The instruction of an apta (a trustworthy/reliable authority) is shabda."

Acarya Vatsyayana, in his foundational commentary (Bhashya) on this sutra, explicitly defines the characteristics of an apta:

Show संस्कृतम्

यः साक्षात्कृतधर्मा यथादृष्टस्यार्थस्य चिख्यापयिषया प्रयुक्त उपदेष्टा स आप्तः.

yah saakshaatkritadharmaa yathaadrishtasyaarthasya chikhyaapayishayaa prayukta upadeshtaa sa aaptah.

"That person is called apta (reliable) who possesses direct and immediate perception of reality (sakshatkrtadharma), who is moved by the desire to impart the truth exactly as perceived, and who is fully capable of communicating it."

Furthermore, according to Nyaya Sutra 1.1.8 (drshtadrshtarthatvat), shabda-pramana applies to both perceptible (drshta) and imperceptible/transcendent (adrshta) entities. Because the Agamas originate directly from the Supreme Being (Ishvara)-the ultimate, omniscient Apta-their verbal instructions constitute an infallible Shabda-Pramana capable of bestowing liberating knowledge.

The Aptatva of Lord Shiva: To establish the Aptatva (absolute reliability and trustworthiness) of Lord Shiva as the author of the Agamas within the Nyaya framework, three criteria must be fulfilled: complete knowledge of reality (jnana), freedom from error or deception, and the benevolent desire to communicate truth (iccha-shakti).

1. Jnana: Does the Deity Know Reality As It Is? In the Kailasa Samhita of the Shiva Purana (Chapter 9, Verse 21), it is established that Sadashiva possesses direct, effortless, and omniscient perception of all existence:

Show संस्कृतम्

यद्यथावस्थितं वस्तु तत्तथैव सदाशिवः । अयत्नेनैव जानाति तस्मात् सर्वज्ञ उच्यते.

yadyathaavasthitam vastu tattathaiva sadaashivah | ayatnenaiva jaanaati tasmaat sarvajnya uchyate.

"Whatever object exists, exactly in whichever way it is situated, Sadashiva knows it precisely in that very manner without any effort; therefore, He is called 'Omniscient' (sarvajna)."

This passage directly aligns with Acarya Vatsyayana's criterion of yathadrshtasyarthasya (cognizing an object precisely as it exists in reality).

2. Freedom from Deception (Akhanda-Yatharthatva): Purva Paksha Objection: Omniscience alone is insufficient. Why assume Lord Shiva's statements are free from error, bias, or intentional deception? To establish absolute truthfulness (akhanda-yatharthatva), the source must be completely free from avidya (ignorance) and delusion. The Shvetashvatara Upanishad (4.18) defines Shiva as pure, untainted consciousness beyond all dualities and darkness:

Show संस्कृतम्

यदा तमस् तन् न दिवा न रात्रिर् न सन् न चासच् छिव एव केवलः । तद् अक्षरं तत् सवितुर् वरेण्यं प्रज्ञा च तस्मात् प्रसृता पुराणी ॥ १८ ॥

yadaa tamas tan na divaa na raatrir na san na chaasach chhiva eva kevalah | tad aksharam tat savitur varenyam prajnyaa cha tasmaat prasritaa puraanii || 18 ||

"When there is no darkness [ignorance], there is neither day nor night, neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat), but Shiva alone, absolute and pure (kevalah.). That is the Imperishable (aksharam), that is the adorable light of Savitr., and from That issued forth ancient Wisdom (prajna)."

Because Lord Shiva is the pure, imperishable source of primal wisdom (prajna) devoid of tamas (ignorance), his words are inherently free from error, delusion, or intent to deceive.

3. Iccha-Shakti: Shiva's Desire to Reveal Truth: The desire to make known the truth as perceived (cikhyapayisha) is demonstrated through Lord Shiva's Iccha-Shakti (will/desire to illuminate). As revealed in the Shvetashvatara Upanishad (3.18):

Show संस्कृतम्

यो ब्रह्माणं विदधाति पूर्वं यो वै वेदांश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै । तं ह देवम् आत्मबुद्धिप्रकाशं मुमुक्षुर् वै शरणम् अहं प्रपद्ये ॥ १८ ॥

yo brahmaanam vidadhaati puurvam yo vai vedaamsh cha prahinoti tasmai | tam ha devam aatmabuddhiprakaasham mumukshur vai sharanam aham prapadye || 18 ||

"To Him who at the beginning of creation brought forth Brahma, and who verily delivered (revealed) the Vedas unto him, to that Lord, the illuminator of self-knowledge, I, desirous of liberation (mumukshu), fly for refuge."

The compound atma-buddhi-prakasham specifically characterizes Lord Shiva as the illuminator of Self-knowledge (atmabuddhi) through revealed scriptures. If Lord Shiva is recognized as the ultimate revealer of atma-buddhi who imparted the Vedas, there is no logical ground to deny that His secondary revelations-such as the Shaiva, Pashupata, or Bhairava Agamas-likewise convey atma-buddhi. Given that illuminating spiritual knowledge is an essential attribute of Lord Shiva, and given that the Vedic corpus itself acknowledges the authority of Agamic revelations, the Agamas stand validated as self-authoritative (svatah-pramana) through the divine Aptatva of Lord Shiva.

4. Upadeshtrtva (Communication) & Application of Danda-pupa Nyaya: Purva Paksha Objection: Even if Lord Shiva possesses complete knowledge (jnana) and freedom from deception, He cannot be considered an Apta for the Agamas if He lacks the capacity to communicate (upadeshtrtva) or transmit scripture effectively to human beings.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Resolution: This objection is directly refuted by the Shvetashvatara Upanishad (3.18), which explicitly affirms Lord Shiva's active role in transmitting supreme revelation:

Show संस्कृतम्

यो वै वेदाश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै.

yo vai vedaash cha prahinoti tasmai.

"Who indeed revealed and sent forth the Vedas unto Him (Brahma)."

Because the Upanishad establishes Lord Shiva as the original revealer and communicator of the entire Vedic corpus, His capacity as an active upadeshta (teacher/communicator) is indisputable. To establish the authority of the Agamas from this premise, we apply the classical maxim of Danda-pupa Nyaya (the Maxim of the Stick and the Cake): Danda-pupa Nyaya dictates that if a stick and a cake are bound together, and a mouse is known to have eaten the hard stick, it is naturally and inevitably inferred that the softer cake tied to it has also been eaten. In this context, the vast, highly complex, and eternal Veda represents the danda (the greater/harder task), while the specialized Agamas represent the pupa (the secondary/easier task). Since it is authoritatively established that Lord Shiva revealed the monumental Vedic revelation-which is universally accepted as authoritative Shabda-Pramana-it follows a

fortiori (with much less effort) that His secondary, specialized revelations, such as the Shaiva and Pashupata Agamas, are also authoritative. If the primary and greater work (Veda) is recognized as His infallible utterance, the secondary and auxiliary work (Agama) originating from the exact same Apta cannot be logically rejected.

5. Identity of the Revealers (Abheda) & Extension of Aptatva to Shakta and Vaishnava Agamas: The Aptatva of Devi and the authority of the Shakta Agamas need not be established independently, as the Shiva Purana (Vayaviya Samhita, Uttarabhaga, Chapter 4, Verse 9) explicitly defines the complete non-difference (*abheda*) between Shiva and Devi:

Show संस्कृतम्

यथा शिवस् तथा देवी यथा देवी तथा शिवः । नानयोर् अन्तरं विद्याच् चन्द्रचन्द्रिकयोर् इव ॥ ९ ॥

yathaa shivas tathaa devii yathaa devii tathaa shivah | naanayor antaram vidyaach chandrachandrikayor iva || 9 ||

"As is Shiva, so is the Goddess (Devi); as is the Goddess, so is Shiva. One should perceive no difference between them, just as there is no separation between the moon and the moonlight."

Because there is absolute identity between Shiva and Devi, the Aptatva established for Lord Shiva applies inherently and identically to Devi, thereby validating the Shakta Agamas. Furthermore, the Rudra-hrdaya Upanishad (Verse 11) declares:

Show संस्कृतम्

उमाशङ्करयोगो यः स योगो विष्णुर् उच्यते । यस्तु तस्मै नमस्कारं कुर्याद् भक्तिसमन्वितः ॥ ११ ॥

umaashangkarayogo yah sa yogo vishnur uchyate | yastu tasmai namaskaaram kuryaad bhaktisamanvitah || 11 ||

"The union of Uma and Shankara is that very union called Vishnu. One should offer bowful respects to Him with devotion."

To establish the Aptatva of Lord Vishnu from this scriptural premise, we apply the classical maxim of Yatha-Karana- Guna-Tatha-Karya-Guna Nyaya (The Maxim that the Qualities of the Cause Manifest in the Effect): This Nyaya dictates that the qualities (gunas) inherent in the constituent causes must necessarily manifest in the resulting union or effect. Since the constituent principles of this divine union-Shiva and Uma-are established Aptas characterized by absolute omniscience (sarvajnatva) and freedom from error, the non-different union itself (Lord Vishnu) inherently possesses the complete quality of Aptatva. Consequently, the Vaishnava Agamas (Pancaratra) revealed by Lord Vishnu possess the exact same self-evident authority (atma-pramanya) as the Vedas and the Pashupata Agamas. This non-difference among manifestations extends equally to the fierce aspect of Bhairava. As stated in the Padma Purana (Uttara-khanda, Chapter 18, Verse 128):

Show संस्कृतम्

स्वयं च भैरवरूपं कृत्वा तन्मध्यगः पपौ । तीक्ष्णदंष्ट्रा महाकायास् तदासन् योगिनीगणाः ॥ १२८ ॥

svayam cha bhairavaruupam kritvaa tanmadhyagah papau | tiikshnadamshtraa mahaakaayaas tadaasan yoginiiganaah || 128 ||

"He Himself assumed the form of Bhairava, stood among them, and drank. The groups of Yoginis then became large-bodied with sharp fangs."

Therefore, in all matters concerning Aptatva and scriptural revelation, complete abheda (non-difference) must be recognized among the divine forms (Shiva, Devi, Vishnu, Bhairava) who revealed the Agama-shastras.

Mangalacarana & Conclusion To conclude this defense of Sarva-Agama-Pramanya, we offer our humble obeisances unto the Supreme Divine Manifestations and the Sacred Revealed Texts through the following concluding Mangalacarana:

Show संस्कृतम्

नमः शिवायाभयदाय नित्यं देव्यै च तस्यै जगदीश्वरायै । श्री-भैरवायात्म-विशुद्धि-दात्रे नमोऽस्तु सर्वागमपूजिताय ॥ १ ॥

namah shivaayaabhayadaaya nityam devyai cha tasyai jagadiishvaraayai | shrii-bhairavaayaatma-vishuddhi-daatre namo'stu sarvaagamapuujitaaya || 1 ||

"Salutations forever unto Shiva, the bestower of fearlessness; unto the Goddess (Devi), the Supreme Controller of the universe; unto Shri Bhairava, the purifier of the Self; and salutations unto the holy Agamas worshipped by all."

Thus, the Agamas stand united with the Vedas as eternal, infallible, and self-authoritative (svatah-pramana) guideposts leading all seeking souls (mumukshus) toward ultimate liberation (moksha).

Show संस्कृतम्

॥ इति श्री-सर्वागम-प्रामाण्य-वादः सम्पूर्णम् ॥

|| iti shrii-sarvaagama-praamaanya-vaadah sampuurnam ||
