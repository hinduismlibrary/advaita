VP 2.11.4

Śrīdhara commentary.

Is it not also said that the Āditya performs the work of raining, in the statement beginning “Vivasvān for eight months”? Therefore he says “if”: the sevenfold group is stated to be the common cause of snow, heat, water, and rain.

VP 2.11.5

VP 2.11.7

सर्व-शक्तिः परा विष्णोर् ऋग्-यजुः-साम-सञ्ज्ञिता।
सैषा त्रयी तपत्य् अंहो जगतश् च हिनस्ति या

The entire and mighty energy of Vishnu, which is called the three Vedas, or Rich, Yajush, and Saman, is that which enlightens the world, and destroys its iniquity.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sarva-śaktiḥ parā viṣṇor ṛg-yajuḥ-sāma-sañjñitā | saiṣā trayī tapaty aṃho jagataś ca hinasti yā

yad yasmād yā parā sarvārtha-prakāśikā viṣṇos trayī-rūpā śaktiḥ saivaiṣā ravi-rūpeṇa tapati ahaṃś ca pāpam upāsitā satī hinasti, ataḥ prādhānyenādhiko raviḥ, saiṣā trayeva vidyā tapati iti śruteḥ | sarvā śaktir iti pāṭhe sarvā śaktiḥ samagrā śaktir ity arthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यद् यस्माद् या परा सर्वार्थप्रकाशिका विष्णोस्त्रयीरूपा शक्तिः सैवैषा रविरूपेण तपति अहंश्च पापमुपासिता सती हिनस्ति, अतः प्राधान्येनाधिको रविः, सैषा त्रयेव विद्या तपति इति श्रुतेः । सर्वा शक्तिरिति पाठे सर्वा शक्तिः समग्रा शक्तिरित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Because that supreme power of Viṣṇu, which illuminates all things and has the form of the three Vedas, itself burns as the Sun and destroys sin when approached, the Sun is superior by primacy. “This burns as the threefold knowledge” is the Vedic statement. In the reading sarvā śaktiḥ, sarvā means “the complete power.”

VP 2.11.13

ṛgyajuḥsāmabhūto 'ntaḥ savitur dvija tiṣṭhati | viṣṇuśaktir avasthānaṃ sadāditye karoti saḥ || na kevalaṃ ravī śaktir ityādiṣu trayīśakti-rūpasya viṣṇoḥ pratimāsaṃ bhinneṣv āgamāpāyiṣu sūryeṣv avasthānokteḥ sūryādivat pratimāsaṃ viṣṇor apy āgamāpāyitvaṃ pratītaṃ vārayati—nodetā udayaṃ na gantā | nāstametā ataś ca na gantā | adhiṣṭhātā viṣṇuḥ pratimāsam āgamāpāyī na bhavati, kintu tad adhiṣṭheyaḥ saptamayaḥ saptabhiḥ sampādyo 'yaṃ sarvo 'py āgamāpāyī gaṇas tataḥ pṛthag eva

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ऋग्यजुःसामभूतोऽन्तःसवितुर्द्विज तिष्ठति । विष्णुशक्तिरवस्थानं सदादित्ये करोति सः ॥ न केवलं रवी शक्तिर् इत्यादिषु त्रयीशक्तिरूपस्य विष्णोः प्रतिमासं भिन्नेष्वागमापायिषु सूर्येष्ववस्थानोक्तेः सूर्यादिवत् प्रतिमासं विष्णोरप्यागमापायित्वं प्रतीतं वारयति—नोदेता उदयं न गन्ता । नास्तमेता अतञ्च न गन्ता । अधिष्ठाता विष्णुः प्रतिमासमागमापायी न भवति, किन्तु तदधिष्ठेयः सप्तमयः सप्तभिः सम्पाद्योऽयं सर्वोऽप्यागमापायी गणस्ततः पृथगेव

Śrīdhara commentary.

The verse quoted earlier says, “In the form of Ṛg, Yajur, and Sāman he stands within the Sun, O twice-born; he makes the Viṣṇu-power abide always in the Sun.” The statements that the power of the three Vedas is present each month in the changing Suns could suggest that Viṣṇu too rises and sets like the Sun; this is denied here. “Not rising” means not going to rise, and “not setting” means not going to set. Viṣṇu, the overseer, does not appear and disappear each month; the sevenfold group produced by its seven members is what appears and disappears, and it is separate from him.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

pitṛ-deva-manuṣyādīn sa sadāpyāyan prabhuḥ | parivartaty aho-rātra-kāraṇaṃ savitā dvija

evaṃ viṣṇuśaktyadhiṣṭhitasya raveḥ pitrāditarpakatvam āha—pitṛdeva iti | āpyāyayan tarpayan parivarttati paribhramatīty arthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं विष्णुशक्त्यधिष्ठितस्य रवेः पित्रादितर्पकत्वम् आह—पितृदेव इति । आप्याययन् तर्पयन् परिवर्त्तति परिभ्रमतीत्यर्थ

Śrīdhara commentary.

He now explains that the Sun, governed by Viṣṇu’s power, satisfies the ancestors and others: “pitṛ-deva” “He revolves” means that, while nourishing and satisfying them, he moves around.

VP 2.11.22

सूर्य-रश्मिः सुषुम्ना यस् तर्पितस् तेन चन्द्रमाः।
कृष्ण-पक्षेऽमरैः शश्वत् पीयते वै सुधामयः

The Moon is nourished by the Sun’s ray called Suṣumṇā; in the dark fortnight the nectar-filled Moon is continually drunk by the immortals.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तत्र देवपितृतर्पणे प्रकारम् आह—सूर्यरश्मिर् इति द्वाभ्याम् । तर्पितः शुक्लप्रतिपदमारभ्यापूरितः षोडशकलः सन् कृष्णपक्षे प्रतिपदादि प्रत्यहमेकैककलांशेनामरैः पीयते । प्रथमां पिबते वह्निर्द्वितीयां पिबते रविः इत्यादि स्मृतेः

Śrīdhara commentary.

He explains how the gods and ancestors are satisfied: “the Sun’s ray” The Moon, filled to sixteen kalās beginning with the bright fortnight’s first day, is drunk in the dark fortnight by the immortals, one kalā at a time each day, beginning with the first; this is taught by the traditional statement, “Fire drinks the first, the Sun drinks the second,” and so on.

VP 2.11.23

पीतं तं द्विकलं सोमं कृष्ण-पक्ष-क्षये द्विज।
पिबन्ति पितरस् तेषां भास्करोत्तर्पणं तथा

Out of the 16, for 14 days, they consume one by one all the kalaas of Chandra. There will be two parts which will be left at the end of the Krushna paksha. Out of that, one kalaa is consumed by the Pitrus; this is how they get tarpana from Sun.

Śrīdhara commentary.

Peetam iti | Atah krushna chaturdashyante dvi kalatvam, krushna paksha kshaye - darshe pibanti eka kalaa varjam; teshaam deva pitruunaam, tathaa ukta prakaarena | At the end of Krushna chaturdashi, he has got two kalaas. In Amaavaasya, leaving one kalaa, part, they drink the remaining. This is how the devaas and pitrus are being fed and then pleased by the Sun. This is how the Sun himself feeds everyone, is told.

VP 2.11.26

पक्ष-तृप्तिं तु देवानां पितॄणां चैव मासिकीम्।
शश्वत्-तृप्तिं च मर्त्यानां मैत्रेयार्कः प्रयच्छति

The Sun grants the gods satisfaction every fortnight, the ancestors satisfaction each month, and mortals perpetual satisfaction.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वृष्टिकृतायां तृप्तौ विशेषम् आह—पक्षतृप्तिम् इति । शश्वत्तृप्तिं स्वकृतवृष्टिनिष्पन्नैरन्नैः सकृद् भुक्तैः पक्षमासाहोरात्रपरिमितां तृप्तिं देवपितृमर्त्यानामर्कः करोतीत्यर्थः । अत एव श्रुतिः—तस्मादहरहर्मनुष्या अशनमिच्छन्तेऽर्धमासे देवा इत्यन्ते मासि पितृभ्यः क्रियते इति

Śrīdhara commentary.

He specifies the satisfaction produced by rain: “fortnightly satisfaction.” The Sun gives gods, ancestors, and mortals satisfaction measured by a fortnight, month, and day-and-night through food produced by his rain and eaten once. Hence the śruti: humans desire food every day, the gods at the half-month, and offerings are made to the ancestors at the month’s end.
