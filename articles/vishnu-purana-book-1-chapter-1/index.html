VP 1.1.1

जितं ते पुण्डरीकाक्ष नमस् ते विश्वभावन । नमस् तेऽस्तु हृषीकेश महापुरुष पूर्वज ॥१॥

Victory is yours, O Puṇḍarīkākṣa; salutation to you, O Viśvabhāvana. May there be salutation to you, O Hṛṣīkeśa, Mahāpuruṣa, Pūrvaja.

Śrīdhara translation

On the words “Victory to you.” In the first chapter of the first section, Maitreya puts twenty-two questions to Parāśara in order to set the Purāṇa in motion.

“Puṇḍarīkākṣa” means “he who pervades the lotus called the heart,” or “he whose eyes are like lotuses.” The whiteness of the lotus is not intended here, since the excellence of the eyes lies in their redness. It may also mean “he whose eye was made lotus-like for the worship of Śiva,” or be an address formed from the traditional derivation in which the lotus is the supreme abode and akṣa is the imperishable. “Te” is a genitive in a relation: “may victory be yours”; alternatively, “victory stands established through you.” Thus a salutation is implied.

“Viśvabhāvana” means the creator of the universe. “Hṛṣīkeśa” means lord of the senses, because he causes them to function, as the śruti says: “the life of life, the eye of the eye, the ear of the ear, and the mind of the mind.” “Mahāpuruṣa” marks him off from the individual soul, in keeping with the teaching of the two associated birds, and because his nature is eternally liberated. He is called puruṣa because he lies in the body. “Pūrvaja” explains his role as creator: he appeared before creation, independently, while all others depend upon him.

If one salutation would establish auspiciousness, why are many offered? Because this scripture is a supreme means to blessedness and many obstacles may arise; an abundance of auspiciousness is therefore necessary. Alternatively, the five epithets express the fivefold Hari: “Puṇḍarīkākṣa” is the elemental self, “Viśvabhāvana” the self identified with primordial nature, “Hṛṣīkeśa” the sensory self, “Mahāpuruṣa” the Supreme Self, and “Pūrvaja” the individual self.

VP 1.1.2

सद्अक्षरं ब्रह्म य ईश्वरं पुमान् गुणोर्मिसृष्टिस्थितिकालसंलयः । प्रधानसौभाग्य्आदिजगत्परम्पर्सूः स नोऽस्तु विष्णुर् मतिभूतिमुक्तिदः ॥२॥

May that Viṣṇu, who is the existent, imperishable Brahman, who is Īśvara and spirit; who with the waves of the three qualities is the cause of creation, preservation, and destruction; who is the parent of nature, intellect, and the other ingredients of the universe; be to us the bestower of understanding, wealth, and final emancipation.

Śrīdhara translation

To explain the listener’s engagement, the relation of the work to its subject, and its purpose, he says “the existent, imperishable Brahman.” Viṣṇu is supremely renowned and is one who pervades, since he is not delimited by place, time, or his own nature.

The name Viṣṇu may also derive from viś, “to enter,” for the śruti says that after creating the world he entered it. “May he grant understanding, prosperity, and liberation”: by understanding he grants liberation through the rise of knowledge of reality; or, according to the qualification of the aspirant, he gives excellent intellect, sovereignty, and liberation. If “Viṣṇu” in the sense of “one who enters” seems to imply a formed body, the word “Brahman” removes that implication and declares his fullness. “Sat” means that he is present throughout all things and therefore does not perish; “akṣara” denies modification; “Pumān” denotes the changeless one; and “Īśvara” means one able to create, not create, or create otherwise.

The qualities are sattva, rajas, and tamas. Their waves, produced by disturbance, are creation, preservation, time, and dissolution; he is that in whom their conjunction or superimposition occurs. As the support of all, his lordship is unobstructed. If creation is attributed to primordial nature alone, the verse says that he is the source of primordial nature, intellect, and the rest of the world. Although primordial nature is beginningless, during dissolution it is powerless; the giving of power by Viṣṇu is therefore figuratively called its generation. Alternatively, he produces intellect and the rest through primordial nature. For Vedāntins he is the existent imperishable Brahman; for the Pātañjalas, the Lord untouched by affliction, action, result, and latent impression; and for the Sāṅkhyas, the Person. The remaining construction is the same: may that Viṣṇu grant understanding, prosperity, and liberation.

VP 1.1.3

प्रणम्य विष्णुं विश्वेशं ब्रह्मादीन् प्रणिपत्य च । गुरुं प्रणम्य वक्ष्यामि पुराणं वेदसम्मितम् ॥३॥

Having bowed to Viṣṇu, Lord of the universe, and having paid obeisance to Brahmā and the others; having bowed to the guru, I will narrate a Purāṇa equal to the Vedas.

Śrīdhara translation

Now the sage Parāśara begins the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, which expounds Viṣṇu-tattva, taking Maitreya as his occasion and showing devotion to the guru and others as a means to the puruṣārthas. The prefix pra in praṇamya indicates an abundance of devotion and faith. Viśveśa means the īśa of all beings, from Brahmā down to a blade of grass, and the cause of their activity and withdrawal. In this śāstra he bows to the founders of the tradition: “Brahmā and the others”—Brahmā, Ṛbhu, Dakṣa, Puru, Kutsa, and Sārasvata Pratī. The guru is Kapila or Sārasvata. A Purāṇa has five limbs: sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, and vaṃśānucarita. As appropriate, the first aṃśa treats sarga and pratisarga; the second, the earth-globe and celestial sphere as they arise in the narrative; the third, manvantara; the fourth, lineage; the fifth, accounts of lineages. In the sixth, after setting out dissolution and the rest useful for dispassion, liberation in the form of final dissolution is taught. The Purāṇa is praised as “equal to the Vedas.”

VP 1.1.5

पराशरं मुनिवरं कृतपौर्वाह्निकक्रियम् । मैत्रेयः परिपप्रच्छ प्रणिपत्याभिवाद्य च ॥५॥

Maitreya, having saluted him reverentially, questioned Parāśara, the excellent sage who had performed the morning rites of devotion.

Śrīdhara translation

“Parāśara” is an indirect reference to himself, according to ordinary usage, and indicates that he does not identify himself with the body and its adjuncts. “Having bowed down” means bowing in full prostration and by such acts as grasping the feet.

VP 1.1.6

त्वत्तो हि वेदाध्ययनम् अधीतम् अखिलं गुरो । धर्मशास्त्राणि सर्वाणि तथाङ्गानि यथाक्रमम् ॥६॥

Master, through you I have studied the whole of the Veda, all the Dharmaśāstras, and the auxiliary disciplines in due order.

Śrīdhara translation

He restates the favor received from Maitreya in order to introduce the subject that Maitreya wishes to know. “From you I have studied the Veda” means that I have made it my own, just as one says, “he cooks the food he is cooking.” Other learned men will not say that I have mastered all the sciences without effort; rather, even those who dislike me will say that I have practiced them through your favor.

VP 1.1.35

विष्णोः सकाशाद् उद्भूतं जगत् तत्रैव च स्थितम् । स्थितिसंयमकर्तासौ जगतोऽस्य जगच् च सः ॥३५॥

The world was produced from Viṣṇu and exists in him; he is the cause of its continuance and cessation, and he is the world.

Śrīdhara translation

To make the Purāṇa’s meaning easy for the listener to grasp, its meaning is briefly stated as the answer to all the questions, in the verse beginning “from Viṣṇu.” Sakāśāt means “from the presence of”: kāśa is light, that is, seeing. The world arose from Viṣṇu endowed with that seeing. The phrases “He saw the worlds, intending to create them” and “He desired: ‘May I become many; may I be born’” establish this in śruti; īkṣaṇa, a word with terms such as “will” as synonyms, means deliberative seeing. The world arose in that way. This gives the answer to the question about the manner of creation, “How did it come to be?” “At the time of dissolution” answers the question about the support of dissolution. The word ca also says that the world’s maintenance is there. He alone is the agent of the maintenance and restraint of this world; birth is included as well. This answers the question about the instrumental cause: “From what has this world, consisting of the moving and unmoving, arisen?” “And the world is He” answers the question about the material cause, because an effect has the form of its material cause. By stating Viṣṇu’s agency as the world’s material cause, the answer to every question is understood: the world will arise from Viṣṇu alone and remain in Him alone. The first aṃśa answers how the world arose and will arise again, what it consists of, from what and where it dissolves, where it will dissolve again, the measures of the elements, and the origin of the gods and others. The second aṃśa answers four questions: the arrangement of oceans and mountains, the arrangement of the earth, the arrangement of the sun and the rest, and their measures. The third aṃśa answers six: the gods and their lineages, Manus, Manvantaras, the proper arrangement of the Veda branches by Vyāsa, and the dharmas of the Brāhmaṇas and others, as well as of those who dwell in āśramas. The fourth aṃśa answers the question about the histories of gods, ṛṣis, and kings, preceded by an account of their lineages. In it, the history of Hari, who descended in the Yadu lineage to lighten the earth’s burden, is told at length in the fifth aṃśa. The sixth aṃśa answers the questions about the Kalpas, their divisions, and the nature of a Kalpa’s end—answered briefly in the first aṃśa—along with the question about all the duties of the Yugas. This is the arrangement.

Śrīdhara

श्रोतुः सुखप्रतिपत्तये सङ्क्षेपतस्तावत् सर्वप्रश्नोत्तरतया पुराणार्थम् आह—विष्णोरिति श्लोकेन । सकाशात्काशः प्रकाशः ईक्षणम् इति यावत्, तत्सहिताद् विष्णोर्जगदुद्भूतम् । स ऐक्षत लोकान् उत्सृजा इति सोऽकामयत बहु स्यां प्रजायेय इत्य्-आदिश्रुतिसिद्धमिच्छादिशब्दपरपर्यायं यदीक्षणमालोचनात्मकं तेन प्रकारेण जगदुद्भूतम् । अनेन यथा वभूवेत्यस्य जन्मप्रकारप्रश्नस्योत्तरं तत्रैव स्थितम् । प्रलयकाल इति लयाधारप्रश्नस्योत्तरम् । चशब्दाज्जगतः स्थितिरपि तत्रैवेत्युक्तम् । अस्य जगतः स्थितिसंयमयोर् असावेव कर्ता । जन्मनोऽप्युपलक्षणम् । अनेन यतश्चैतच्चराचरात्मकं जगदित्यस्य निमित्तप्रश्नस्योत्तरम् । जगच्च स इत्युपादानप्रश्नस्योत्तरम् । उपादानकारणरूपत्वात् । कार्य्यस्य । अनेनैव विष्णोर्जगदुपादानरूपकर्तृत्वादिकथनेन विष्णोरेवोद्भविष्यति तत्रैव स्थास्यतीत्यादि—सर्वप्रश्नस्योत्तरमर्थाद् द्रष्टव्यम् । तत्र च यथा जगद् बभूव, यथा च पुनर्भविष्यति, यन्मयं च जगत् यतश् च यत्र पूंलीनम्, पुनश् च यत्र लयमेष्यति । यत्प्रमाणिनि भूतानि देवादीनाञ्च सम्भवमित्यष्टमप्रश्नत्योत्तरं प्रथमांशे । समुद्रपर्वतानाञ्च संस्थानं, भुवः संस्थानम्, सूर्यादीनाञ्च संस्थानम्, तेषां परिमाणञ्चेति चतुःप्रश्नोत्तरं द्वितीयांशे । देवादीनां तथा वशान् मनून् मन्वन्तराणि च । वेदशाखाप्रणयनं यथावद् व्यासकर्तृकम् । धर्माश् च ब्रह्माणादीनां तथा चाश्रमवासिनाम् । इतिषट् प्रश्नोत्तरं तृतीयांशे । देवर्षिपार्थिवानाञ्च चरितम् इति प्रश्नस्योत्तरं तद्वंशविस्तारोक्तिपूर्वकं चतुर्थांशे । तत्र च यदुवंशे भूभारहरणार्थमवतीर्णस्य हरेश्चरितं विस्तारेण पञ्चमांशे । कल्पान् कल्पविकल्पांश् च कल्पान्तस्य स्वरूपञ्चेति प्रश्नत्रस्य प्रथमांशेन सङ्क्षेपेण दत्तोत्तरस्य युगधर्मांश् च कृत्स्नश इत्यस्य चोत्तरं षष्ठांशे इति विवेकः ॥३५॥ इति श्रीधरस्वामिकृतायाम् आत्मप्रकाशाभिधायां विष्णुपुराणटीकायां प्रथमांशे प्रथमोऽध्यायः । विष्णुचित्तः- श्रीरित्य्-आदीति । सर्वपुराणानामादिभूते ब्रह्मकाण्डाख्ये महापुराण उद्धृतायां पाराशयसंहितायाम् इति रथमोध्याय इत्य् अन्वयः ॥ इति श्रीभगवद्रामानुजपदाब्जभृङ्गायमाणश्रीविष्णुचितार्यविरचिते विष्णुपुराणन्याख्याने श्रीविष्णुचित्तीये यमांशे प्रथमोऽध्यायः ॥१॥ इति श्रीविष्णुपुराणे प्रथमेऽंशे प्रथमोऽध्यायः ॥१॥ —ओ)०(ओ—
