VP 3.6.2

सुमन्तुस् तस्य पुत्रोऽभूत् सुकर्मास्याप्य् अभूत् सुतः । अधीतवन्तौ चैकैकां संहितां तौ महामती ॥२॥

Jaimini’s son was Sumantu. Sumantu’s son was Sutvā. Those two great-minded men each studied one Saṃhitā.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara identifies Sumantu and Sutvā as Jaimini’s son and grandson. Each studied one Saṃhitā divided by Jaimini in his own time.

VP 3.6.3

सहस्रसंहिताभेदं सुकर्मा तत्सुतस् ततः । चकार तं च तच्छिष्यौ जगृहाते महाव्रतौ ॥३॥

Then Sutvā’s son Sukarman made a thousandfold division of the Saṃhitā. His two steadfast pupils received it.

Śrīdhara translation

Śrīdhara identifies Sukarman as Sumantu’s son. The main verse’s reading of the preceding verse instead makes Sukarman the son of Sutvā. Wilson also identifies Sukarman as Sumantu’s son and understands this compound as the Sāhasra Saṃhitā, a collection of a thousand hymns. The English translation here follows Śrīdhara’s explanation of a division of Sumantu’s Saṃhitā.

VP 3.6.4

हिरण्यनाभः कौसल्यः पौष्पिञ्जिश् च द्विजोत्तम । उदीच्याः सामगाः शिष्यास् तस्य पञ्चशतं स्मृताः ॥४॥

Hiraṇyanābha, known as Kauśalya, and Pauṣpiñji were Sukarman’s disciples, O best of the twice-born. Five hundred northern Sāmagas are remembered as pupils of Hiraṇyanābha.

Śrīdhara translation

Parāśara names Sukarman’s disciples. Śrīdhara reads Kauśalya as an epithet of Hiraṇyanābha and says that fifteen pupils received fifteen Saṃhitās from him. The verse reads five hundred northern Sāmagas. Wilson’s translation instead gives fifteen northern pupils of Pauṣpiñji and fifteen eastern pupils of Hiraṇyanābha.

Those excellent twice-born recipients who received that many Saṃhitās from Hiraṇyanābha are also called eastern Sāmagas by the learned.

Śrīdhara translation

The Saindhavas were disciples of Saindhavāyana. Muñjikeśa is another name of Babhru. Babhru’s disciples divided the Veda into two kinds, each with its own Saṃhitā. The text then lists the Saṃhitā divisions. The Nakṣatra-kalpa concerns rites for the stars. The Veda-kalpa concerns Vaitāna rites and the duties of the Brahman priest. The Saṃhitā-kalpa concerns the rules for the Saṃhitās. The Āṅgirasa-kalpa concerns hostile rites and related practices. The Śānti-kalpa includes rites for horses and elephants, the eighteen great pacifications, and the like.
