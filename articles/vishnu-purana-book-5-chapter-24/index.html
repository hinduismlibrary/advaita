VP 5.24.1

इत्थं स्तुतस्तदा तेन मुचुकुन्देन धीमता ।
प्राहेशः सर्वभूतानामनादिनिधनो हरिः

Thus praised by the wise Muchukunda, Hari, the beginningless and endless Lord of all beings, spoke.

VP 5.24.2

यथाभिवाञ्छितान्दिव्यान्गच्छ लोकान्नराधिप ।
अव्याहतपरैश्वर्यो मत्प्रसादोपबृंहितः

Go, O king, to the divine worlds you desire; your supreme sovereignty will be unobstructed, strengthened by my grace.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

दिव्यान् लोकान् (divyān lokān) — divine worlds
गच्छ (gaccha) — go

अव्याहत (avyāhata) — unobstructed
परैश्वर्यः (paraiśvaryaḥ) — supreme sovereignty
मत्-प्रसाद (mat-prasāda) — by my grace
उपबृंहितः (upabṛṃhitaḥ) — strengthened.
Transliteration (IAST)
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yathābhivāñchitāndivyāngaccha lokānnarādhipa | avyāhataparaiśvaryo matprasādopabṛṃhitaḥ

Śrīdhara

kṛtānāṃ kāmyānāṃ karmaṇāṃ phalam ādau bhuṅkṣva janmāntare mokṣaṃ dadāmīty āha—yathābhivāñchitān ity-ādi dvābhyām || 2 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

कृतानां काम्यानां कर्मणां फलम् आदौ भुङ्क्ष्व जन्मान्तरे मोक्षं ददामीत्य् आह—यथाभिवाञ्छितान् इत्य्-आदि द्वाभ्याम् ॥२॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

The fruits of the desired actions performed should first be enjoyed; thereafter, in another birth, I grant liberation. This is taught by the two verses beginning “the divine worlds you desire.”

Śrīdhara commentary.

Thinking that the Lord had not granted him liberation because his inner purity was lacking, he went to perform austerity; this is taught by the two verses beginning “thus addressed.”

VP 5.24.8

Transliteration (IAST)

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तथा रामोऽप्य् एकाकी गोकुलं गत्वा कालिन्दी-कर्षणादिकं कृत्वा गत इति वक्ष्यन्न् आह—बलदेवोऽपि इत्य्-आदिना यावद् उत्तराध्याय-परिसमाप्ति । बलदेवोऽपीति । ज्ञातीनां दर्शने सोत्कण्ठः चातुर्यवान् ॥८॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

Likewise, Rāma too went alone to Gokula, performed the drawing of the Yamunā and other deeds, and departed; this is stated by the passage beginning “Baladeva too” and extending to the end of the following chapter. “Baladeva too” means he was clever and eager to see his kinsmen.

Śrīdhara commentary.

He presents the question of Kṛṣṇa’s beloveds in the seven verses beginning “the gopīs.” “Whose love is fickle” means one whose nature is only a small, unstable measure of love.
