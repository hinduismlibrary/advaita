ŚB 2.7.25

वक्षःस्थलस्पर्शरुग्नमहेन्द्रवाह-
दन्तैर्विडम्बितककुब्जुष ऊढहासम् ।
सद्योऽसुभिः सह विनेष्यति दारहर्तु-
र्विस्फूर्जितैर्धनुष उच्चरतोऽधिसैन्ये ॥ २५ ॥

Rāvaṇa had once broken the tusks of Indra’s elephant against his chest and proudly laughed as though he had conquered the directions. Yet while he strode among the armies, the resonant twang of Rāma’s bow would at once take away that wife-stealer’s laughter together with his life.

Word-for-word

vakṣaḥ-sthala-sparśa — by contact with the chest; rugna — broken; mahendra-vāha-dantaiḥ — tusks of Indra’s carrier Airāvata; viḍambita-kakup-juṣaḥ — one seeming to possess/conquer the directions; ūḍha-hāsam — proud laughter; sadyaḥ — immediately; asubhiḥ saha — together with life; vineṣyati — will remove; dāra-hartuḥ — of the wife-stealer; dhanuṣaḥ visphūrjitaiḥ — by the resounding bow; adhi-sainye — amid the armies.

Transliteration
vakṣaḥ-sthala-sparśa-rugna-mahendra-vāha-
dantair viḍambita-kakub-juṣa ūḍha-hāsam
sadyo ’subhiḥ saha vineṣyati dāra-hartur
visphūrjitair dhanuṣa uccarato ’dhisainye
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

किं च, युद्धे रावणस्य वक्षः-स्थल-स्पर्शेन रुग्णा भग्ना ये महेन्द्र-वाहस्य ऐरावतस्य दन्ताः, तैर् विडम्बिताः स्व-धवलिम्ना धवलीकृताः, तत्-तद्-दिक्षु पतितैः प्रकाशिता इत्य् अर्थः । या एवम्भूताः ककुभो दिशः, ता जुषते सेवते पालयतीति वा, तथा तस्य दार-हर्तू रावणस्य “अहो मत्-समः कोऽन्योऽस्ति” इति महा-गर्वेण ऊढं प्राप्तं हासम् असुभिः प्राणैः सह सद्यः शीघ्रं विनेष्यत्य् अपनेष्यति । कैः ? धनुषो विस्फूर्जितैः टङ्कार-घोषैर् एव ॥२५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Rāvaṇa’s pride came from his former display of strength against Airāvata. Śrīdhara explains that Rāma’s bow-string sound alone would immediately remove both that boastful laughter and Rāvaṇa’s life.

ŚB 2.7.26

To relieve the earth, oppressed by armies of ungodly rulers, the Lord appears together with His expansion, as the white and dark pair Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa. His way is beyond ordinary human perception, and His acts reveal and establish His own immeasurable glory.

bhūmeḥ — of the earth; sura-itara-varūtha — armies of ungodly rulers; vimarditāyāḥ — oppressed; kleśa-vyayāya — to remove distress; kalayā — with His expansion; sita-kṛṣṇa-keśaḥ — white and dark / Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa; jātaḥ — appeared; jana-anupalakṣya-mārgaḥ — whose way ordinary people cannot discern; karmāṇi — deeds; ātma-mahimā-upanibandhanāni — manifesting His own greatness.

Transliteration
bhūmeḥ suretara-varūtha-vimarditāyāḥ
kleśa-vyayāya kalayā sita-kṛṣṇa-keśaḥ
jātaḥ kariṣyati janānupalakṣya-mārgaḥ
karmāṇi cātma-mahimopanibandhanāni
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्री-कृष्णावतारम् आह—भूमेर् इति दशभिः । सुरेतरा असुरांश-भूता राजानस् तेषां वरूथैः सैन्यैर् विमर्दिताया भारेण पीडितायाः । कलया रामेण सह जातः सन् । कोऽसौ जातः? सित-कृष्णौ केशौ यस्य भगवतः स एव साक्षात् । सित-कृष्ण-केशत्वं शोभैव न तु वयः-परिणाम-कृतम्, अविकारित्वात् । ... कर्माणि कीदृशानि ? आत्मनो महिमा उपनिबध्यतेऽभिव्यज्यते येषु तानि ॥२६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara begins ten verses on Śrī Kṛṣṇa. He rejects the idea that the passage reduces Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to literal hairs of Viṣṇu: the wording indicates their white and dark forms and the ease with which the Lord can accomplish the earth’s relief; the deeds themselves disclose His supreme greatness.

Transliteration
tokena jīva-haraṇaṁ yad ulūki-kāyās
trai-māsikasya ca padā śakaṭo ’pavṛttaḥ
yad riṅgatāntara-gatena divi-spṛśor vā
unmūlanaṁ tv itarathārjunayor na bhāvyam

एतद् एव प्रपञ्चयति—तोकेनेत्य्-आदिना । बालेन पूतनाया जीव-हरणम् । यद् रिङ्गता जानुभ्यां गच्छता अन्तरं गतेन मध्यं प्राप्तेन । दिवि-स्पृशोर् अत्युच्चयोः । इतरथाऽनीश्वरत्वे तन् न भवितव्यम् ॥२७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. These childhood acts expand the previous statement about His divine glory. For Śrīdhara, their very impossibility for a non-Lord is the point: the crawling child’s effortless acts reveal who He is.

अन्ये च शाल्वकुजबल्वलदन्तवक्र-
सप्तोक्षशम्बरविदूरथरुक्मिमुख्याः ॥ ३४ ॥

Pralamba, Dhenuka, Baka, Keśī, Ariṣṭa, the wrestlers, Kuvalayāpīḍa, Kaṁsa, Yavana, Dvivida, Pauṇḍraka, Śālva, Naraka, Balvala, Dantavakra, the seven bulls, Śambara, Vidūratha, Rukmī and the others are all included in the Lord’s destruction of hostile powers; the sentence continues in 2.7.35.

Word-for-word

pralamba — Pralamba; khara — Dhenuka; dardura — Baka; keśī — Keśī; ariṣṭa — Ariṣṭa; malla — wrestlers; ibha — Kuvalayāpīḍa; kaṁsa — Kaṁsa; yavanāḥ — Yavana; kapi — Dvivida; pauṇḍraka — Pauṇḍraka; śālva — Śālva; kuja — Naraka; balvala — Balvala; dantavakra — Dantavakra; saptokṣa — seven bulls; śambara — Śambara; vidūratha — Vidūratha; rukmi — Rukmī; mukhyāḥ — chief among them.

Transliteration
ye ca pralamba-khara-dardura-keśy-ariṣṭa-

anye ca śālva-kuja-balvala-dantavakra-

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ये च प्रलम्बादयस् ते सर्वे हरिणा हेतु-भूतेन तदीयं निलयम् अदर्शनं दर्शनायोग्यं वैकुण्ठम् अलं यास्यन्ति इत्य् उत्तरेणान्वयः । खरो धेनुकः, दर्दुर इव दर्दुरो बकः, इभः कुवलयापीडः, कुजो नरकः, कपिर् द्विविदः ॥३४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara explicitly connects this verse syntactically with 2.7.35. He identifies Khara as Dhenuka, “Dardura” as Baka, the elephant as Kuvalayāpīḍa, Kuja as Naraka and the monkey as Dvivida. Their destination is explained in the next verse.
