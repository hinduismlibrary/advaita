ŚB 2.10.27

उत्सिसृक्षोर्धातुमलं निरभिद्यत वै गुदम् ।
ततः पायुस्ततो मित्र उत्सर्ग उभयाश्रयः ॥ २७ ॥

When He desired to expel the waste of the bodily constituents, the anus became manifest. Then arose the organ of evacuation and its presiding deity Mitra; excretion is the function dependent upon both.

Word-for-word

utsisṛkṣoḥ — desiring to discharge; dhātu-malam — bodily waste; gudam — anus; pāyuḥ — organ of evacuation; mitraḥ — Mitra; utsargaḥ — expulsion; ubhaya-āśrayaḥ — dependent upon both.

Transliteration
utsisṛkṣor dhātu-malaṁ
nirabhidyata vai gudam
tataḥ pāyus tato mitra
utsarga ubhayāśrayaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

धातु-मलं भुक्तान्नादीनाम् असारांशं त्यक्तुम् इच्छोः । गुद-पायु-मित्रोत्सर्गा अधिष्ठानेन्द्रिय-देवता-विषयाः ॥२७॥

dhātu-malaṁ bhuktānnādīnām asārāṁśaṁ tyaktum icchoḥ | guda-pāyu-mitrotsargā adhiṣṭhānendriya-devatā-viṣayāḥ ||27||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. dhātu-malam — nonessential bodily residue; guda — seat; pāyu — faculty; mitra — deity; utsarga — evacuation.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Anus, evacuative faculty, Mitra and excretion are the corresponding seat, organ, deity and function.

ŚB 2.10.28

आसिसृप्सोः पुरः पुर्या नाभिद्वारमपानतः ।
तत्रापानस्ततो मृत्युः पृथक्त्वमुभयाश्रयम् ॥ २८ ॥

When He desired to pass from one bodily city to another, the navel opening became manifest along the path of apāna. There appeared apāna and Death; separation from the body depends upon this conjunction.

Word-for-word

āsisṛpsoḥ — desiring to move onward; puraḥ — toward another city/body; puryāḥ — from the bodily city; nābhi-dvāram — navel opening; apānataḥ — through apāna; mṛtyuḥ — death; pṛthaktvam — separation.

Transliteration
āsisṛpsoḥ puraḥ puryā
nābhi-dvāram apānataḥ
tatrāpānas tato mṛtyuḥ
pṛthaktvam ubhayāśrayam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पुर्या देहात् पुरो देहान्तराणि आसिसृप्सोः सर्वतो गन्तुम् इच्छोः । नाभि-द्वारं निरभिद्यतेत्य् अनुषङ्गः । अपानतोऽपगच्छतः । पृथक्त्वं मरणम् । नाभ्यां हि प्राणापानयोर् बन्ध-विश्लेषे मृत्युर् इति प्रसिद्धम् ॥२८॥

puryā dehāt puro dehāntarāṇi āsisṛpsoḥ sarvato gantum icchoḥ | nābhi-dvāraṁ nirabhidyatety anuṣaṅgaḥ | apānato ’pagacchataḥ | pṛthaktvaṁ maraṇam | nābhyāṁ hi prāṇāpānayor bandha-viśleṣe mṛtyur iti prasiddham ||28||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. puryā dehāt — from the bodily city; dehāntarāṇi — toward other bodies; pṛthaktvam maraṇam — separation is death; prāṇa-apānayoḥ viśleṣaḥ — separation of prāṇa and apāna.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara interprets the verse as departure from one body toward another, with death associated with the separation of the life currents.

ŚB 2.10.29

आदित्सोरन्नपानानामासन्कुक्ष्यन्त्रनाडयः ।
नद्यः समुद्राश्च तयोस्तुष्टिः पुष्टिस्तदाश्रये ॥ २९ ॥

When He desired to take in food and drink, the abdomen, intestines and channels appeared. Rivers and the ocean became their corresponding presiding powers, and satisfaction and nourishment arose in dependence upon them.

Word-for-word

āditsoḥ — desiring to take in; anna-pānānām — food and drink; kukṣi — abdomen; antrāṇi — intestines; nāḍayaḥ — channels; nadyaḥ — rivers; samudrāḥ — oceans; tuṣṭiḥ — satisfaction; puṣṭiḥ — nourishment.

Transliteration
āditsor anna-pānānām
āsan kukṣy-antra-nāḍayaḥ
nadyaḥ samudrāś ca tayos
tuṣṭiḥ puṣṭis tad-āśraye
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अन्न-पानानाम् आदित्सोः संग्रहेच्छोः कुक्षिश् चान्त्राणि च नाड्यश् चासन् । तत्र कुक्षिर् अधिष्ठानम् । अन्त्राण्य् अन्न-संग्रहे करणम् इन्द्रिय-स्थानीयम् । नाड्यस् तु पानं ग्रहे । तयोर् नाड्य्-अन्त्र-वर्गयोः क्रमेण नद्यः समुद्राश् च देवते । तुष्टिर् उदर-भरणं, पुष्टिस् तु रस-परिणामतः स्थौल्यम् ॥२९॥

anna-pānānām āditsoḥ saṅgrahecchoḥ kukṣiś cāntrāṇi ca nāḍyaś cāsan | tatra kukṣir adhiṣṭhānam | antrāṇy anna-saṅgrahe karaṇam indriya-sthānīyam | nāḍyas tu pānaṁ grahe | tayor nāḍy-antra-vargayoḥ krameṇa nadyaḥ samudrāś ca devate | tuṣṭir udara-bharaṇaṁ, puṣṭis tu rasa-pariṇāmataḥ sthaulyam ||29||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. kukṣiḥ — abdomen; antrāṇi — intestines; nāḍyaḥ — channels; nadyaḥ/samudrāḥ — rivers/oceans; tuṣṭiḥ — fullness; puṣṭiḥ — nourishment.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara distinguishes the systems for food and drink and links them with river and ocean imagery; fullness is tuṣṭi and bodily nourishment is puṣṭi.
