2.10 Bhāgavatam Is the Answer to All Questions

ŚB 2.10.1

श्रीशुक उवाच
अत्र सर्गो विसर्गश्च स्थानं पोषणमूतयः ।
मन्वन्तरेशानुकथा निरोधो मुक्तिराश्रयः ॥ १ ॥

Śrī Śuka said: This Bhāgavata treats ten great subjects: primary creation, secondary creation, cosmic order, the Lord’s protection, impulses toward action, the reigns of the Manus, narrations of the Lord and His devotees, dissolution, liberation, and the ultimate shelter.

Word-for-word

atra — here, in this Bhāgavata; sargaḥ — primary creation; visargaḥ — secondary creation; sthānam — ordered establishment; poṣaṇam — protection and nourishment; ūtayaḥ — impulses or karmic tendencies; manvantara — the periods ruled by Manus; īśa-anukathāḥ — narrations concerning the Lord; nirodhaḥ — dissolution; muktiḥ — liberation; āśrayaḥ — the ultimate shelter.

Transliteration
śrī-śuka uvāca
atra sargo visargaś ca
sthānaṁ poṣaṇam ūtayaḥ
manvantareśānukathā
nirodho muktir āśrayaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततो भागवत-व्याख्या-द्वारैव दशमे स्फुटम् । राज-प्रश्नोत्तरं वक्तुम् आरेभे बादरायणिः ॥१॥ दश-लक्षणं पुराणं प्राहेत्य् उक्तम् । तानि दश लक्षानि दर्शयति—अत्रेति । मन्वन्तराणि ईशानुकथाश् चेति द्वन्द्वः । सर्गादयोऽत्र दशार्था लक्ष्यन्ते ॥१॥

tato bhāgavata-vyākhyā-dvāraiva daśame sphuṭam | rāja-praśnottaraṁ vaktum ārebhe bādarāyaṇiḥ ||1|| daśa-lakṣaṇaṁ purāṇaṁ prāhety uktam | tāni daśa lakṣāni darśayati—atreti | manvantarāṇi īśānukathāś ceti dvandvaḥ | sargādayo ’tra daśārthā lakṣyante ||1||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. bhāgavata-vyākhyā-dvārā — through explanation of the Bhāgavata; rāja-praśna-uttaram — the answers to the king’s questions; daśa-lakṣaṇam — having ten characteristics; manvantarāṇi īśa-anukathāḥ ca — manvantaras and narrations of the Lord are distinct topics; daśa-arthāḥ — ten subjects are indicated here.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śukadeva now answers Parīkṣit explicitly by unfolding the Bhāgavata. The ten terms name the organizing subjects through which the Purāṇa reveals its ultimate purpose.

ŚB 2.10.2

दशमस्य विशुद्ध्यर्थं नवानामिह लक्षणम् ।
वर्णयन्ति महात्मानः श्रुतेनार्थेन चाञ्जसा ॥ २ ॥

To reveal the tenth subject—the ultimate shelter—in its purity, the great teachers describe the characteristics of the other nine, sometimes through direct Vedic statement and sometimes through the intended meaning of narratives.

Word-for-word

daśamasya — of the tenth; viśuddhi-artham — for clear and pure understanding; navānām — of the nine; iha — here; lakṣaṇam — characteristics; varṇayanti — they describe; mahātmānaḥ — the great souls; śrutena — through revealed statement; arthena — through intended meaning; ca — and; añjasā — directly and clearly.

Transliteration
daśamasya viśuddhy-arthaṁ
navānām iha lakṣaṇam
varṇayanti mahātmānaḥ
śrutenārthena cāñjasā
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नन्व् एवम् अर्थ-भेदाच् छास्त्र-भेदः स्यात् ? तत्राह । दशमस्याश्रयस्य विशुद्ध्य्-अर्थं तत्त्व-ज्ञानार्थं नवानां लक्षणं स्वरूपम् । एकस्यैव प्राधान्यान् नायं दोष इत्य् अर्थः । नन्व् अत्र नैवं प्रतीयतेऽत आह । श्रुतेन श्रुत्यैव स्तुत्य्-आदि-स्थानेष्व् अञ्जसा साक्षाद् वर्णयन्ति । अर्थेन तात्पर्य-वृत्त्या च तत्-तद्-आख्यानेषु ॥२॥

nanv evam artha-bhedāc chāstra-bhedaḥ syāt? tatrāha | daśamasyāśrayasya viśuddhy-arthaṁ tattva-jñānārthaṁ navānāṁ lakṣaṇaṁ svarūpam | ekasyaiva prādhānyān nāyaṁ doṣa ity arthaḥ | nanv atra naivaṁ pratīyate ’ta āha | śrutena śrutyaiva stuty-ādi-sthāneṣv añjasā sākṣād varṇayanti | arthena tātparya-vṛttyā ca tat-tad-ākhyāneṣu ||2||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. daśamasya āśrayasya — of the tenth topic, the shelter; tattva-jñāna-artham — for true knowledge; navānām lakṣaṇam — the nature of the nine; ekasya eva prādhānyāt — because one subject is primary; śrutyā eva — by direct revelation; tātparya-vṛttyā — by the purport or intended sense of narratives.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The nine subjects are not rival centers of the text. They are described so that the tenth—the supreme shelter—can be understood distinctly, both by explicit statements and by the deeper purport of the Bhāgavata’s stories.

ŚB 2.10.3

भूतमात्रेन्द्रियधियां जन्म सर्ग उदाहृतः ।
ब्रह्मणो गुणवैषम्याद्विसर्गः पौरुषः स्मृतः ॥ ३ ॥

The arising of the elements, sense objects, senses, and the principles of cosmic intelligence and ego is called primary creation. The secondary creation is the production of embodied beings through the transformations and differentiation of the guṇas under the cosmic creator.

Word-for-word

bhūta — gross elements; mātrā — subtle sense objects; indriya — senses; dhiyām — of intelligence and related principles; janma — arising; sargaḥ — primary creation; udāhṛtaḥ — is defined; brahmaṇaḥ — from the supreme causal source / through Brahmā according to context; guṇa-vaiṣamyāt — from differentiation of the guṇas; visargaḥ — secondary creation; pauruṣaḥ — effected through the cosmic person; smṛtaḥ — is known.

Transliteration
bhūta-mātrendriya-dhiyāṁ
janma sarga udāhṛtaḥ
brahmaṇo guṇa-vaiṣamyād
visargaḥ pauruṣaḥ smṛtaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सर्गादीनां प्रत्येकं लक्षणम् आह । भूतान्य् आकाशादीनि, मात्राणि च शब्दादीनि, इन्द्रियाणि च, धी-शब्देन महद्-अहंकारौ । गुणानां वैषम्यात् परिणामाद् ब्रह्मणः परमेश्वरात् कर्तुर् भूतादीनां यद् विराट्-रूपेण स्वरूपतश् च जन्म स सर्गः । विसर्गम् आह । पुरुषो वैराजस् तत्-कृतः पौरुषश् चराचर-सर्गो विसर्ग इत्य् अर्थः ॥३॥

sargādīnāṁ pratyekaṁ lakṣaṇam āha | bhūtāny ākāśādīni, mātrāṇi ca śabdādīni, indriyāṇi ca, dhī-śabdena mahad-ahaṅkārau | guṇānāṁ vaiṣamyāt pariṇāmād brahmaṇaḥ parameśvarāt kartur bhūtādīnāṁ yad virāṭ-rūpeṇa svarūpataś ca janma sa sargaḥ | visargam āha | puruṣo vairājas tat-kṛtaḥ pauruṣaś carācara-sargo visarga ity arthaḥ ||3||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. bhūtāni ākāśādīni — elements beginning with ether; mātrāṇi śabdādīni — subtle objects beginning with sound; dhī-śabdena mahad-ahaṅkārau — dhī includes mahat and ahaṅkāra; guṇānāṁ vaiṣamyāt pariṇāmāt — through differentiated transformation of the guṇas; carācara-sargaḥ — creation of moving and unmoving beings; visargaḥ — secondary creation.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Primary creation supplies the elemental and psychological principles themselves; secondary creation combines and differentiates them into the multitude of moving and nonmoving embodied beings.

ŚB 2.10.4

स्थितिर्वैकुण्ठविजयः पोषणं तदनुग्रहः ।
मन्वन्तराणि सद्धर्म ऊतयः कर्मवासनाः ॥ ४ ॥

Cosmic establishment is the victory of the Lord of Vaikuṇṭha through preservation of the proper order of created beings. Protection is His grace toward His devotees; the manvantaras display true dharma, while the impulses to action are karmic dispositions.

Word-for-word

sthitiḥ — establishment and maintenance; vaikuṇṭha-vijayaḥ — the victory or supremacy of Vaikuṇṭha’s Lord; poṣaṇam — protection; tat-anugrahaḥ — His grace; manvantarāṇi — the periods of the Manus; sat-dharmaḥ — true dharma; ūtayaḥ — impulses and tendencies; karma-vāsanāḥ — dispositions produced by action.

Transliteration
sthitir vaikuṇṭha-vijayaḥ
poṣaṇaṁ tad-anugrahaḥ
manvantarāṇi sad-dharma
ūtayaḥ karma-vāsanāḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वैकुण्ठस्य भगवतो विजयः सृष्टानां तत्-तन्-मर्यादा-पालनेनोत्कर्षः । स्थितिः स्थानम् । स्व-भक्तेषु तस्यानुग्रहः पोषणम् । तद्-अनुगृहीतानां सतां मन्वन्तराधिपतीनां धर्मः सद्-धर्मः । कर्मणां वासनाः । ऊयन्ते कर्मभिः सन्तन्यन्त इत्य् ऊतयः । यद् वा, वृद्ध्य्-अर्थात् संश्लेषार्थाद् वावतेर् धातोर् इदं रूपम् । ऊयन्ते कर्मभिर् बृंह्यन्ते संश्लिष्यन्त इति वा ऊतय इत्य् अर्थः ॥४॥

vaikuṇṭhasya bhagavato vijayaḥ sṛṣṭānāṁ tat-tan-maryādā-pālanenotkarṣaḥ | sthitiḥ sthānam | sva-bhakteṣu tasyānugrahaḥ poṣaṇam | tad-anugṛhītānāṁ satāṁ manvantarādhipatīnāṁ dharmaḥ sad-dharmaḥ | karmaṇāṁ vāsanāḥ | ūyante karmabhiḥ santanyanta ity ūtayaḥ | yad vā, vṛddhy-arthāt saṁśleṣārthād vāvater dhātor idaṁ rūpam | ūyante karmabhir bṛṁhyante saṁśliṣyanta iti vā ūtaya ity arthaḥ ||4||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. maryādā-pālanena — by preserving proper limits and order; utkarṣaḥ — the Lord’s supremacy; sva-bhakteṣu anugrahaḥ — grace toward His own devotees; sad-dharmaḥ — the dharma of saintly Manu-period rulers; karmaṇāṁ vāsanāḥ — impressions and tendencies born from action; ūtayaḥ — that by which action is extended and carried forward.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara makes “protection” specifically the Lord’s favor toward His devotees and reads the cosmic order as an expression of His supremacy. Karmic impressions, by contrast, are the forces that extend conditioned activity forward.

ŚB 2.10.5

अवतारानुचरितं हरेश्चास्यानुवर्तिनाम् ।
पुंसामीशकथाः प्रोक्ता नानाख्यानोपबृंहिताः ॥ ५ ॥

Narrations of the Lord consist of the deeds of Hari’s incarnations and of those persons who faithfully follow Him, expanded through many histories and sacred accounts.

Word-for-word

avatāra-anucaritam — deeds of the incarnations; hareḥ — of Hari; ca — and; asya anuvartinām — of those who follow Him; puṁsām — of such persons; īśa-kathāḥ — narrations of the Lord; proktāḥ — are described; nānā-ākhyāna-upabṛṁhitāḥ — expanded through many narratives.

Transliteration
avatārānucaritaṁ
hareś cāsyānuvartinām
puṁsām īśa-kathāḥ proktā
nānākhyānopabṛṁhitāḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

हरेर् अवतारानुचरितम् अस्यानुवर्तिनां च सत्-कथा ईशानुकथाः प्रोक्ता इत्य् अर्थः ॥५॥

harer avatārānucaritam asyānuvartināṁ ca sat-kathā īśānukathāḥ proktā ity arthaḥ ||5||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. hareḥ avatāra-anucaritam — the deeds of Hari’s descents; asya anuvartinām — of those who follow Him; sat-kathāḥ — holy narratives; īśa-anukathāḥ — narrations centered on the Lord.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The Bhāgavata’s God-centered narrative includes both the Lord’s descents and the lives of His followers. Devotee history therefore belongs intrinsically to īśa-kathā.

ŚB 2.10.6

निरोधोऽस्यानुशयनमात्मनः सह शक्तिभिः ।
मुक्तिर्हित्वान्यथारूपं स्वरूपेण व्यवस्थितिः ॥ ६ ॥

Dissolution is the jīva’s lying merged with its conditioning powers after the Lord enters yogic repose. Liberation is the abandonment of an imposed, other-than-true identity and firm establishment in one’s own intrinsic nature.

Word-for-word

nirodhaḥ — dissolution; asya — of this jīva; anuśayanam — lying down afterward, merging; ātmanaḥ — of the self; saha śaktibhiḥ — together with its powers or conditioning adjuncts; muktiḥ — liberation; hitvā — giving up; anyathā-rūpam — an imposed or contrary form; sva-rūpeṇa — in one’s own true nature; vyavasthitiḥ — stable establishment.

Transliteration
nirodho ’syānuśayanam
ātmanaḥ saha śaktibhiḥ
muktir hitvānyathā rūpaṁ
sva-rūpeṇa vyavasthitiḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निरोधम् आह । अस्यात्मनो जीवस्य हरेर् योग-निद्राम् अनु पश्चाच् छक्तिभिश् चोपाधिभिः सह शयनं लयो निरोधः । अन्यथा रूपम् अविद्ययाध्यस्तं कर्तृत्वादि हित्वा स्वरूपेण ब्रह्मतया व्यवस्थितिर् मुक्तिः ॥६॥

nirodham āha | asyātmano jīvasya harer yoga-nidrām anu paścāc chaktibhiś copādhibhiḥ saha śayanaṁ layo nirodhaḥ | anyathā rūpam avidyayādhyastaṁ kartṛtvādi hitvā svarūpeṇa brahmatayā vyavasthitir muktiḥ ||6||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. jīvasya — of the jīva; śaktibhiḥ upādhibhiḥ saha — together with conditioning powers and adjuncts; layo nirodhaḥ — merger is dissolution; avidyayā adhyastam — superimposed by ignorance; kartṛtva-ādi — doership and similar identities; hitvā — abandoning them; svarūpeṇa vyavasthitiḥ — abiding in one’s own nature; muktiḥ — liberation.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Dissolution and liberation are not identical. In dissolution the conditioned complex becomes latent; in liberation the false, ignorance-based identity of independent doership is actually relinquished and the self is established in its true nature.

ŚB 2.10.7

आभासश्च निरोधश्च यतोऽस्त्यध्यवसीयते ।
स आश्रयः परं ब्रह्म परमात्मेति शब्द्यते ॥ ७ ॥

That from which manifestation and dissolution arise, and through which they are made known, is the ultimate shelter. It is spoken of as the Supreme Brahman and as the Paramātmā.

Word-for-word

ābhāsaḥ — manifestation, appearance of creation; ca — and; nirodhaḥ — dissolution; yataḥ — from whom; asti — exists or occurs; adhyavasīyate — is apprehended, manifested, known; saḥ — He; āśrayaḥ — the shelter; param brahma — the Supreme Brahman; paramātmā — the Supreme Self; iti śabdyate — is thus named.

Transliteration
ābhāsaś ca nirodhaś ca
yato ’sty adhyavasīyate
sa āśrayaḥ paraṁ brahma
paramātmeti śabdyate
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

आभासः सृष्टिर् निरोधो लयश् च यतो भवत्य् अध्यवसीयते प्रकाशते च स परं ब्रह्मेति परमात्मेति प्रसिद्ध आश्रयः कथ्यते ॥७॥

ābhāsaḥ sṛṣṭir nirodho layaś ca yato bhavaty adhyavasīyate prakāśate ca sa paraṁ brahmeti paramātmeti prasiddha āśrayaḥ kathyate ||7||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. ābhāsaḥ sṛṣṭiḥ — manifestation means creation; nirodhaḥ layaḥ — nirodha means dissolution; yataḥ bhavati — from whom it occurs; adhyavasīyate prakāśate — by whom it becomes known and manifest; paraṁ brahma — Supreme Brahman; paramātmā — Supreme Self; āśrayaḥ — the final shelter.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The shelter is not simply one item inside creation. It is the reality from which creation and dissolution arise and in whose light they become intelligible.

ŚB 2.10.8

योऽध्यात्मिकोऽयं पुरुषः सोऽसावेवाधिदैविकः ।
यस्तत्रोभयविच्छेदः पुरुषो ह्याधिभौतिकः ॥ ८ ॥

The experiencer associated with a particular sense is the adhyātmika person; its corresponding presiding deity is the adhidaivika person. The visible bodily instrument by which these two functions are distinguished is the adhibhautika person.

Word-for-word

yaḥ adhyātmikaḥ ayam puruṣaḥ — this person identified with the individual sensory apparatus; saḥ asau eva adhidaivikaḥ — that corresponding form is the presiding divine principle; yaḥ — that which; tatra — there; ubhaya-vicchedaḥ — provides the distinction of both; puruṣaḥ — the embodied person; hi — indeed; ādhibhautikaḥ — the material bodily manifestation.

Transliteration
yo ’dhyātmiko ’yaṁ puruṣaḥ
so ’sāv evādhidaivikaḥ
yas tatrobhaya-vicchedaḥ
puruṣo hy ādhibhautikaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

आश्रय-स्वरूपम् अपरोक्षानुभवेन स्पष्टं दर्शयितुम् अध्यात्मादि-विभागम् आह । योऽयम् आध्यात्मिकः पुरुषश् चक्षुर्-आदि-करणाभिमानी द्रष्टा जीवः स एवाधिदैविकश् चक्षुर्-आद्य्-अधिष्ठाता सूर्यादिः । तत्रैकस्मिन्न् एवोभयो द्वि-रूपो विच्छेदो यस्मात् स आधिभौतिकश् चक्षुर्-गोलकाद्य्-उपलक्षितो दृश्यो देहः पुरुष इति पुरुषस्य जीवस्योपाधिः । स वा एष पुरुषोऽन्न-रस-मयः इत्य्-आदि-श्रुतेः ॥८॥

āśraya-svarūpam aparokṣānubhavena spaṣṭaṁ darśayitum adhyātmādi-vibhāgam āha | yo ’yam ādhyātmikaḥ puruṣaś cakṣur-ādi-karaṇābhimānī draṣṭā jīvaḥ sa evādhidaivikaś cakṣur-ādy-adhiṣṭhātā sūryādiḥ | tatraikasminn evobhayo dvi-rūpo vicchedo yasmāt sa ādhibhautikaś cakṣur-golakādy-upalakṣito dṛśyo dehaḥ puruṣa iti puruṣasya jīvasyopādhiḥ | sa vā eṣa puruṣo ’nna-rasa-maya ity-ādi-śruteḥ ||8||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. adhyātmikaḥ — the individual experiencer; cakṣur-ādi-karaṇa-abhimānī draṣṭā jīvaḥ — the jīva identifying with sensory instruments such as the eye; adhidaivikaḥ — the presiding divine principle; sūryādiḥ — such as the sun for sight; ādhibhautikaḥ — the visible material basis; dṛśyaḥ dehaḥ — the body that is seen; jīvasya upādhiḥ — the limiting adjunct of the jīva.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Each act of embodied perception can be viewed through three interdependent aspects: individual experiencer, governing cosmic principle and bodily instrument. This analysis prepares the contrast with the truly independent shelter in the next verse.

ŚB 2.10.9

एकमेकतराभावे यदा नोपलभामहे ।
त्रितयं तत्र यो वेद स आत्मा स्वाश्रयाश्रयः ॥ ९ ॥

Because none of those three is apprehended independently when one of the others is absent, they are mutually dependent. The Supreme Self who knows and witnesses that entire triad is their shelter while Himself resting upon no shelter other than Himself.

Word-for-word

ekam — one member; ekatara-abhāve — when another is absent; yadā na upalabhāmahe — when we do not apprehend it; tritayam — the triad; tatra — in that relation; yaḥ veda — He who knows; saḥ ātmā — that Supreme Self; sva-āśraya — self-supported; āśrayaḥ — the shelter of all others.

Transliteration
ekam ekatarābhāve
yadā nopalabhāmahe
tritayaṁ tatra yo veda
sa ātmā svāśrayāśrayaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एकम् एकतराभाव इत्य् एतेषाम् अन्योन्य-सापेक्ष-सिद्धित्वेनानात्मत्वं दर्शयति । तथाहि । दृश्यं विना तत्-प्रतीत्य्-अनुमेयं करणं न सिध्यति, नापि द्रष्टा, न च तद् विना करण-प्रवृत्त्य्-अनुमेयस् तद्-अधिष्ठाता सूर्यादिः, न च तं विना कारणं प्रवर्तते, न च तद् विना दृश्यम् इत्य् एवम् एकतरस्याभावे एकं नोपलभामहे । तत्र तदा तत् त्रितयम् आलोचनात्मकेन प्रत्ययेन यो वेद साक्षितया पश्यति स परमात्मा आश्रयः । तेषाम् अपि परस्परम् आश्रयत्वम् अस्त्य् एवेति तद्-व्यवच्छेदार्थं विशेषणम् । स्वाश्रयोऽनन्याश्रयः स चासाव् अन्येषाम् आश्रयश् चेति ॥९॥

ekam ekatarābhāva ity eteṣām anyonya-sāpekṣa-siddhitvenānātmatvaṁ darśayati | tathāhi | dṛśyaṁ vinā tat-pratīty-anumeyaṁ karaṇaṁ na sidhyati, nāpi draṣṭā, na ca tad vinā karaṇa-pravṛtty-anumeyas tad-adhiṣṭhātā sūryādiḥ, na ca taṁ vinā kāraṇaṁ pravartate, na ca tad vinā dṛśyam ity evam ekatarasyābhāve ekaṁ nopalabhāmahe | tatra tadā tat tritayam ālocanātmakena pratyayena yo veda sākṣitayā paśyati sa paramātmā āśrayaḥ | teṣām api parasparam āśrayatvam asty eveti tad-vyavacchedārthaṁ viśeṣaṇam | svāśrayo ’nanyāśrayaḥ sa cāsāv anyeṣām āśrayaś ceti ||9||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. anyonya-sāpekṣa-siddhitvena — because their establishment depends mutually on one another; dṛśyam — the seen object; karaṇam — the sensory instrument; draṣṭā — the experiencer; adhiṣṭhātā sūryādiḥ — the presiding deity such as the sun; tritayam — the complete triad; sākṣitayā paśyati — sees as witness; paramātmā āśrayaḥ — Paramātmā is the shelter; ananyāśrayaḥ — dependent on no other shelter.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The three empirical factors can function only in relation to one another. Their mutual dependence disqualifies any of them from being the final ground. Paramātmā, who witnesses the entire relation without depending upon it, is the true āśraya.

ŚB 2.10.10

पुरुषोऽण्डं विनिर्भिद्य यदासौ स विनिर्गतः ।
आत्मनोऽयनमन्विच्छन्नपोऽस्राक्षीच्छुचिः शुचीः ॥ १० ॥

When the cosmic Puruṣa separated the universal egg and stood distinctly within it, He sought a resting place for Himself. Being pure, He created the pure waters known as Garbhodaka.

Word-for-word

puruṣaḥ — the cosmic Puruṣa; aṇḍam vinirbhidya — separating or opening the universal egg; yadā — when; asau saḥ — that very Lord; vinirgataḥ — came forth and stood distinctly; ātmanaḥ — for Himself; ayanam — a resting place; anvicchan — seeking; apaḥ — waters; asrākṣīt — created; śuciḥ — being pure; śucīḥ — pure waters.

Transliteration
puruṣo ’ṇḍaṁ vinirbhidya
yadāsau sa vinirgataḥ
ātmano ’yanam anvicchann
apo ’srākṣīc chuciḥ śucīḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उक्तम् एवाध्यात्मादि-विभागं प्रपञ्चयन्—“यद् उताहं त्वया पृष्टो वैराजात् पुरुषादिदम् । यथासीत् तद्-उपाख्यास्ये” इति यत् प्रतिज्ञातं तद् उत्पत्ति-प्रकारम् आह—पुरुष इत्य्-आदिना । पुरुषो वैराजोऽण्डं विनिर्भिद्य पृथक् कृत्य विनिर्गतः, पृथक् स्थित इत्य् अर्थः । अयनं स्थानम् अन्विच्छन् विमृशन् । यतः शुचिः स्वयम् । अतः शुचीः शुद्धा अपो गर्भोदक-संज्ञा अस्राक्षीत् ससर्ज ॥१०॥

uktam evādhyātmādi-vibhāgaṁ prapañcayan—“yad utāhaṁ tvayā pṛṣṭo vairājāt puruṣād idam | yathāsīt tad-upākhyāsye” iti yat pratijñātaṁ tad utpatti-prakāram āha—puruṣa ity-ādinā | puruṣo vairājo ’ṇḍaṁ vinirbhidya pṛthak kṛtya vinirgataḥ, pṛthak sthita ity arthaḥ | ayanaṁ sthānam anvicchan vimṛśan | yataḥ śuciḥ svayam | ataḥ śucīḥ śuddhā apo garbhodaka-saṁjñā asrākṣīt sasarja ||10||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. utpatti-prakāram āha — he now explains the manner of origination; puruṣaḥ vairājaḥ — the cosmic Puruṣa; aṇḍaṁ vinirbhidya — having opened/separated the cosmic egg; pṛthak sthitaḥ — stood distinctly; ayanam sthānam — a resting place; śuciḥ svayam — Himself pure; śucīḥ śuddhā apaḥ — pure waters; garbhodaka-saṁjñāḥ — called Garbhodaka.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The promised account of the virāṭ-puruṣa now begins in detail. The first setting is Garbhodaka: the pure waters created within the differentiated cosmic egg as the Puruṣa’s place of repose.
