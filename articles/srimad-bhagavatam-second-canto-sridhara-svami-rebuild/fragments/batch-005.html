ŚB 2.6.30

ततश्च मनवः काले ईजिरे ऋषयोऽपरे ।
पितरो विबुधा दैत्या मनुष्याः क्रतुभिर्विभुम् ॥ ३० ॥

Thereafter, each in its proper time, the Manus, other sages, the ancestors, the learned gods, the Daityas and human beings worshiped the all-pervading Lord through sacrifices.

Word-for-word

tataḥ — thereafter; manavaḥ — the Manus; kāle — in due time; ījire — worshiped; ṛṣayaḥ apare — other sages; pitaraḥ — ancestors; vibudhāḥ — learned gods; daityāḥ — Daityas; manuṣyāḥ — humans; kratubhiḥ — by sacrifices; vibhum — the all-pervading Lord.

Transliteration
tataś ca manavaḥ kāle
ījire ṛṣayo ’pare
pitaro vibudhā daityā
manuṣyāḥ kratubhir vibhum
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

काले स्व-स्वावसरे ॥३०॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “In time” means in the proper occasion belonging to each of them.

ŚB 2.6.31

नारायणे भगवति तदिदं विश्वमाहितम् ।
गृहीतमायोरुगुणः सर्गादावगुणः स्वतः ॥ ३१ ॥

This entire universe rests in Bhagavān Nārāyaṇa. For creation and the other cosmic functions He assumes the mighty qualities of Māyā, while in Himself He remains untouched by the guṇas.

Word-for-word

nārāyaṇe bhagavati — in Bhagavān Nārāyaṇa; tat idam viśvam — this whole universe; āhitam — situated; gṛhīta-māyā — having assumed Māyā; uru-guṇaḥ — possessing powerful qualities; sarga-ādau — in creation and related functions; aguṇaḥ — beyond the guṇas; svataḥ — in Himself.

Transliteration
nārāyaṇe bhagavati
tad idaṁ viśvam āhitam
gṛhīta-māyoru-guṇaḥ
sargādāv aguṇaḥ svataḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यद् अधिष्ठानम् इत्य् एतत् प्रश्नोत्तरार्थम् उपसंहरति—नारायण इति । आहितम् अधिष्ठितम् । भगवत्त्वे हेतुः—यः स्वतोऽगुणः । सर्गादौ गृहीता मायया उरवो गुणा येन स तस्मिन् ॥३१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This concludes the answer concerning the universe’s support. It is established in Nārāyaṇa; He is Bhagavān because He is intrinsically beyond the guṇas even while taking up Māyā’s powerful modes for creation and related functions.

ŚB 2.6.32

सृजामि तन्नियुक्तोऽहं हरो हरति तद्वशः ।
विश्वं पुरुषरूपेण परिपाति त्रिशक्तिधृक् ॥ ३२ ॥

Commissioned by Him, I create; under His control, Hara destroys; and He Himself, bearing the threefold power, preserves the universe in the form of the Puruṣa.

Word-for-word

sṛjāmi — I create; tat-niyuktaḥ — commissioned by Him; aham — I; haraḥ — Śiva; harati — destroys; tat-vaśaḥ — under His control; viśvam — universe; puruṣa-rūpeṇa — in the form of the Puruṣa; paripāti — maintains; tri-śakti-dhṛk — bearer of the threefold power.

Transliteration
sṛjāmi tan-niyukto ’haṁ
haro harati tad-vaśaḥ
viśvaṁ puruṣa-rūpeṇa
paripāti tri-śakti-dhṛk
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यत् परस् त्वम् इत्य् एतत् प्रश्नोत्तरं यद् उक्तं स एष भगवान् सर्वेषां मन चेश्वरः इति तद् उपसंहरति—सृजामि इति । पालनं तु स्वयम् एव करोतीत्य् आह—विश्वम् इति । पुरुष-रूपेण विष्णु-रूपेण । त्रि-शक्तिर् माया, तां धरतीति तथा सः ॥३२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā concludes the answer about who is above him: Brahmā creates by commission and Hara destroys under command, while the Lord Himself preserves as Viṣṇu. The “threefold power” is Māyā in her three guṇas.

ŚB 2.6.33

इति तेऽभिहितं तात यथेदमनुपृच्छसि ।
नान्यद्भगवतः किञ्चिद्भाव्यं सदसदात्मकम् ॥ ३३ ॥

My dear son, I have answered what you asked. Whatever exists as cause or effect should not be conceived as something independent or separate from Bhagavān.

Word-for-word

iti — thus; te abhihitam — explained to you; tāta — dear son; yathā idam anupṛcchasi — according to what you asked; na anyat — nothing separate; bhagavataḥ — from Bhagavān; kiñcit — anything; bhāvyam — should be conceived; sat-asat-ātmakam — consisting of cause and effect.

Transliteration
iti te ’bhihitaṁ tāta
yathedam anupṛcchasi
nānyad bhagavataḥ kiñcid
bhāvyaṁ sad-asad-ātmakam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यच् चेदं विश्वं यद्-आत्मकश् च त्वम् इत्य् एवम् आदि-सर्व-प्रश्नानां सामान्योत्तरं, यद् उक्तं वासुदेवात् परो ब्रह्मन् न चान्योऽर्थोऽस्ति तत्त्वतः इति पुरुष एवेदं सर्वम् इति श्रुत्या च यद् दृढीकृतं तद् उपसंहरति—इति इति । सद्-असद्-आत्मकं कार्य-कारणात्मकं भाव्यं सृज्यं भगवतः सकाशाद् अन्यत् पृथङ् न भवतीत्य् अभिहितम् ॥३३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This is Brahmā’s general conclusion to Nārada’s questions: all created reality, whether viewed as cause or effect, is not something independently separate from Bhagavān.

ŚB 2.6.34

न भारती मेऽङ्ग मृषोपलक्ष्यते
न वै क्वचिन्मे मनसो मृषा गतिः ।
न मे हृषीकाणि पतन्त्यसत्पथे
यन्मे हृदौत्कण्ठ्यवता धृतो हरिः ॥ ३४ ॥

O Nārada, my speech does not prove false, my mind does not move toward falsity, and my senses do not fall onto an unreal path, because with an intensely eager heart I hold Hari in meditation.

Word-for-word

bhāratī me — my speech; mṛṣā — false; na upalakṣyate — is not found; manasaḥ — of my mind; mṛṣā gatiḥ — false course; hṛṣīkāṇi — senses; asat-pathe — on an unreal or improper path; hṛdā autkaṇṭhyavatā — with an eagerly longing heart; dhṛtaḥ hariḥ — Hari is held in meditation.

Transliteration
na bhāratī me ’ṅga mṛṣopalakṣyate
na vai kvacin me manaso mṛṣā gatiḥ
na me hṛṣīkāṇi patanty asat-pathe
yan me hṛdautkaṇṭhyavatā dhṛto hariḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यद् उक्तम् एतावत् त्वं यतो हि मे, तम् अविज्ञाय माम् ईश्वरं प्रब्रवीषि इति तद् उपपाद्योपसंहरति—न भारती इति । यद् यस्मान् मे मया औत्कण्ठ्यं भक्त्य्-उद्रेकः, तद्-युक्तेन हृदा हरिर् धृतो ध्यातः । अङ्ग हे नारद ! अतो मे वाङ्-मन-इन्द्रियाणां वृत्तयः सत्यार्थाः, न तु मत्-प्रभावेणेत्य् अर्थः ॥३४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā attributes the reliability of his speech, mind and senses not to his own greatness but to concentrated devotion: Hari is held in his heart through an upsurge of longing bhakti.

ŚB 2.6.35

सोऽहं समाम्नायमयस्तपोमयः
प्रजापतीनामभिवन्दितः पतिः ।
आस्थाय योगं निपुणं समाहित-

Even I—steeped in Vedic revelation and austerity, honored as lord by the Prajāpatis, and deeply concentrated through accomplished yoga—could not fully comprehend Him from whom my own existence arose.

Word-for-word

saḥ aham — I myself; samāmnāya-mayaḥ — filled with Vedic revelation; tapaḥ-mayaḥ — filled with austerity; prajāpatīnām — of the Prajāpatis; abhivanditaḥ — honored; patiḥ — lord; āsthāya yogam — undertaking yoga; nipuṇam — skillfully; samāhitaḥ — concentrated; tam na adhyagaccham — I did not fully comprehend Him; yataḥ ātma-sambhavaḥ — from whom my own birth arose.

Transliteration
so ’haṁ samāmnāyamayas tapomayaḥ
prajāpatīnām abhivanditaḥ patiḥ
āsthāya yogaṁ nipuṇaṁ samāhitas
taṁ nādhyagacchaṁ yata ātma-sambhavaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत्र स्वानुभवम् एवान्वय-व्यतिरेकाभ्यां प्रमाणयति द्वाभ्याम् । सोऽहं समाम्नायादिभिः सर्वोत्कृष्टोऽपि योगम् आश्रित्यापि यत आत्मनो मम सम्भवो जन्म तम् एव न ज्ञातवान्, कृतोऽन्यस्य वार्ता । एतच् च तृतीय-स्कन्धे पद्मोद्भवे वक्ष्यते ॥३५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā appeals to his own experience as evidence: even though pre-eminent in scripture, austerity and yoga, he could not exhaustively know the very Lord from whom he was born—what then of others?

ŚB 2.6.36

नतोऽस्म्यहं तच्चरणं समीयुषां
भवच्छिदं स्वस्त्ययनं सुमङ्गलम् ।
यो ह्यात्ममायाविभवं स्म पर्यगाद्
यथा नभः स्वान्तमथापरे कुतः ॥ ३६ ॥

I therefore bow to His feet, the supremely auspicious refuge that cuts off worldly becoming for those who approach it. His own inconceivable potency has no measurable limit—just as the sky cannot reach its own end—so how could others do so?

Word-for-word

nataḥ asmi — I bow; tat-caraṇam — His feet; samīyuṣām — for those who approach or surrender; bhavat-chidam — cutting off worldly existence; svasti-ayanam — abode of well-being; su-maṅgalam — supremely auspicious; ātma-māyā-vibhavam — expansion of His own potency; paryagāt — reached the limit of; nabhaḥ — sky; sva-antam — its own end; apare kutaḥ — how then could others?

Transliteration
nato ’smy ahaṁ tac-caraṇaṁ samīyuṣāṁ
bhavac-chidaṁ svasty-ayanaṁ sumaṅgalam
yo hy ātma-māyā-vibhavaṁ sma paryagād
yathā nabhaḥ svāntam athāpare kutaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदा समीयुषां शरणागतानां भवच्-छिदं संसार-निवर्तकं सुमङ्गलं मङ्गलावहं सुसेव्यं च तस्य चरणं नतोऽस्मि । ततोऽचिन्त्य-महिमत्वेन ज्ञातवान् अस्मीति कैमुत्य-न्यायेनाह—यो हीति । स्व-माया-विस्तारं यः स्वयम् अपि पर्यगात् । परि-शब्दो निषेधे । एतावान् इति न ज्ञातवान् इत्य् अर्थः । अपरे कुतो जानीयुः ? तस्य चरणं नतोऽस्मीति पूर्वेण सम्बन्धः ॥३६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā bows to the feet that end saṁsāra for the surrendered. The statement that even He does not “reach” the limit of His own potency expresses its boundlessness, not a defect of knowledge; if there is no end, how could others know one?

ŚB 2.6.37

नाहं न यूयं यदृतां गतिं विदु-
र्न वामदेवः किमुतापरे सुराः ।
तन्मायया मोहितबुद्धयस्त्विदं
विनिर्मितं चात्मसमं विचक्ष्महे ॥ ३७ ॥

Neither I, nor you, nor Vāmadeva—much less the other gods—know His true extent. Our intelligence is bewildered by His Māyā, and we perceive this created universe only in proportion to our own capacity.

Word-for-word

na aham — not I; na yūyam — nor you; yat-ṛtām gatim — His true course or nature; na vāmadevaḥ — nor Śiva; apare surāḥ — other gods; tat-māyayā — by His Māyā; mohita-buddhayaḥ — with bewildered intelligence; idam vinirmitam — this created manifestation; ātma-samam — according to one’s own capacity; vicakṣmahe — we perceive or describe.

Transliteration
nāhaṁ na yūyaṁ yad-ṛtāṁ gatiṁ vidur
na vāmadevaḥ kim utāpare surāḥ
tan-māyayā mohita-buddhayas tv idaṁ
vinirmitaṁ cātma-samaṁ vicakṣmahe
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एतत् प्रपञ्चयति—नाहम् इति द्वाभ्याम् । वामदेवो रुद्रः । यस्य ऋतां गतिं परमार्थ-स्वरूपम् । किन्त्व् इदं प्रपञ्च-रूपं तस्य मायया विनिर्मितं विचक्ष्महे विद्मः । तद् अप्य् आत्म-समं स्व-ज्ञानानुरूपम् एव, न तु कृत्स्नम् । मोहित-बुद्धय इत्य् अयं तद्-अज्ञाने प्रपञ्च-ज्ञाने च हेतुः ॥३७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Vāmadeva is Rudra. What remains unknown is the Lord’s ultimate true nature; even the manifested cosmos fashioned by His Māyā is known only according to each knower’s capacity, never exhaustively.

ŚB 2.6.38

yasyāvatāra-karmāṇi

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The objection is that many do in fact describe Him. Brahmā’s answer is that singing His avatāras and deeds does not amount to knowing His reality exhaustively.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit
