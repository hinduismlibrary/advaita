ŚB 2.10.30

निदिध्यासोरात्ममायां हृदयं निरभिद्यत ।
ततो मनश्चन्द्र इति सङ्कल्पः काम एव च ॥ ३० ॥

When He desired deeply to contemplate His own māyā, the heart became manifest. Then came the mind with the moon as its presiding deity, along with conception and desire.

Word-for-word

nididhyāsoḥ — desiring to contemplate deeply; ātma-māyām — His own māyā; hṛdayam — heart; manaḥ — mind; candraḥ — moon; saṅkalpaḥ — conception/intention; kāmaḥ — desire.

Transliteration
nididhyāsor ātma-māyāṁ
hṛdayaṁ nirabhidyata
tato manaś candra iti
saṅkalpaḥ kāma eva ca
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निदिध्यासोर् नितरां चिन्तयितुम् इच्छोः । कामोऽभिलाषः । हृदय-मनश् चन्द्र-संकल्पा अधिष्ठानादयः ॥३०॥

nididhyāsor nitarāṁ cintayitum icchoḥ | kāmo ’bhilāṣaḥ | hṛdaya-manaś candra-saṅkalpā adhiṣṭhānādayaḥ ||30||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. nididhyāsoḥ — desiring intense contemplation; kāmaḥ — desire; hṛdaya — seat; manas — faculty; candra — deity; saṅkalpa — mental operation.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The familiar fourfold mapping continues: heart as seat, mind as faculty, moon as deity and saṅkalpa as the mental operation, with kāma as desire.

ŚB 2.10.31

त्वक्चर्ममांसरुधिरमेदोमज्जास्थिधातवः ।
भूम्यप्तेजोमयाः सप्त प्राणो व्योमाम्बुवायुभिः ॥ ३१ ॥

Skin, hide, flesh, blood, fat, marrow and bone are the seven bodily constituents, formed chiefly from earth, water and fire; the vital breath is sustained through ether, water and air.

Word-for-word

tvak — skin; carma — hide; māṁsa — flesh; rudhira — blood; medaḥ — fat; majjā — marrow; asthi — bone; dhātavaḥ — constituents; bhūmi-ap-tejas-mayāḥ — composed chiefly of earth, water and fire; prāṇaḥ — vital breath; vyoma-ambu-vāyubhiḥ — through ether, water and air.

Transliteration
tvak-carma-māṁsa-rudhira-
medo-majjāsthi-dhātavaḥ
bhūmy-ap-tejo-mayāḥ sapta
prāṇo vyomāmbu-vāyubhiḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवम् अधिदैवादि-भेदं विभज्योक्त्वा तद्-अंश-भूतानां धात्व्-आदीनं स्वरूपम् आह—त्वग् इति द्वाभ्याम् । त्वक् स्थूलम् । चर्म तद्-उपरि स्थितं सूक्ष्मम् । त्वग्-आदयोऽस्थ्य्-अन्ता द्वन्द्वैकवद् भावेन निर्दिष्टाः सप्त ये धातवस् ते भूम्य्-अप्-तेजो-मयाः । तेषां च पाञ्च-भौतिकत्वेऽपि वाय्व्-आकाशयोर् आहारादि-रूपेण संवर्धकत्वाभावाद् एवम् उक्तम् ॥३१॥

tad evam adhidaivādi-bhedaṁ vibhajyoktvā tad-aṁśa-bhūtānāṁ dhātv-ādīnaṁ svarūpam āha—tvag iti dvābhyām | tvak sthūlam | carma tad-upari sthitaṁ sūkṣmam | tvag-ādayo ’sthy-antā dvandvaikavad bhāvena nirdiṣṭāḥ sapta ye dhātavas te bhūmy-ap-tejo-mayāḥ | teṣāṁ ca pāñca-bhautikatve ’pi vāyv-ākāśayor āhārādi-rūpeṇa saṁvardhakatvābhāvād evam uktam ||31||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. sapta dhātavaḥ — seven bodily constituents; tvak sthūlam — outer skin is gross; carma sūkṣmam — the subtler covering lies upon it; bhūmy-ap-tejo-mayāḥ — principally constituted of earth, water and fire.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara now shifts from the sense-system to the bodily constituents and explains why earth, water and fire are named as their principal material bases.

ŚB 2.10.32

गुणात्मकानीन्द्रियाणि भूतादिप्रभवा गुणाः ।
मनः सर्वविकारात्मा बुद्धिर्विज्ञानरूपिणी ॥ ३२ ॥

The senses are directed toward their corresponding qualities, while those qualities arise from the elemental principle of ahaṅkāra. Mind is the field in which the many modifications arise, and intelligence takes the form of discriminative cognition.

Word-for-word

guṇa-ātmakāni — oriented toward qualities; indriyāṇi — the senses; bhūta-ādi-prabhavāḥ — arising from the elemental/ahaṅkāra principle; guṇāḥ — sound and the other qualities; manaḥ — mind; sarva-vikāra-ātmā — locus of all modifications; buddhiḥ — intelligence; vijñāna-rūpiṇī — formed as discriminative knowledge.

Transliteration
guṇātmakānīndriyāṇi
bhūtādi-prabhavā guṇāḥ
manaḥ sarva-vikārātmā
buddhir vijñāna-rūpiṇī
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

गुणात्मकानि गुणेषु शब्दादिष्व् आत्मा येषाम्, विषयाभिमुख-स्वभावानीत्य् अर्थः । गुणाः शब्दादयः । भूतादिर् अहंकारस् ततः प्रकर्षेण भवन्तीति तथा । अत्र हेतुः—यतो मन एव सर्व-विकाराणाम् आत्मा स्वरूपम् । बुद्धिस् तु तथाभूतार्थ-विज्ञान-रूपिणी ॥३२॥

guṇātmakāni guṇeṣu śabdādiṣv ātmā yeṣām, viṣayābhimukha-svabhāvānīty arthaḥ | guṇāḥ śabdādayaḥ | bhūtādir ahaṅkāras tataḥ prakarṣeṇa bhavantīti tathā | atra hetuḥ—yato mana eva sarva-vikārāṇām ātmā svarūpam | buddhis tu tathābhūtārtha-vijñāna-rūpiṇī ||32||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. viṣaya-abhimukha — turned toward objects; guṇāḥ śabdādayaḥ — qualities beginning with sound; manas — mind; sarva-vikārāṇām ātmā — basis of modifications; buddhiḥ — discriminating cognition.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The senses naturally face their objects; mind animates the shifting modifications, while buddhi provides discriminative cognition.
