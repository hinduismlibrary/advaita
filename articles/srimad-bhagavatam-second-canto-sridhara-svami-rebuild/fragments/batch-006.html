ŚB 2.6.40

विशुद्धं केवलं ज्ञानं प्रत्यक् सम्यगवस्थितम् ।
सत्यं पूर्णमनाद्यन्तं निर्गुणं नित्यमद्वयम् ॥ ४० ॥

Word-for-word

viśuddham — completely pure; kevalam — unmixed, absolute; jñānam — knowledge; pratyak — inward, all-pervading within; samyak avasthitam — perfectly established; satyam — true; pūrṇam — complete; anādi-antam — without beginning or end; nirguṇam — beyond the material guṇas; nityam — eternal; advayam — nondual.

Transliteration

pratyak samyag avasthitam
satyaṁ pūrṇam anādy-antaṁ
nirguṇaṁ nityam advayam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

न यं विदन्ति तत्त्वेन इत्य् उक्तं किं तत् तत्त्वम् ? इत्य् अपेक्षायाम् आह—ज्ञानं केवलं सत्यं तत्त्वम् । घटाद्य्-आकार-वृत्ति-ज्ञान-व्यवच्छेदार्थं विशेषणानि । विशुद्धं विषयाकार-शून्यम् । यतः प्रत्यक् सर्वान्तरम् । अत एव सम्यक् सन्देहादि-रहितम् । अवस्थितं स्थिरम् । यतो निर्गुणम् । अनाद्य्-अन्तं जन्म-नाश-रहितम् । यतः पूर्णम् । सर्वत्र हेतुः—नित्यम् अद्वयं नित्यं सर्वदा द्वैत-प्रतीति-समयेऽपि परमार्थतोऽद्वयम् इति ॥४०॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Asked what the reality is that cannot be fully known, Śrīdhara identifies it as pure knowledge itself. The adjectives exclude ordinary object-shaped mental cognition: it is inward, steady, free from material qualities and the six transformations, full, eternal and ultimately nondual.

दत्तो मयाहमिति यद् भगवान् स दत्तः ।
यत्पादपङ्कजपरागपवित्रदेहा
योगर्द्धिमापुरुभयीं यदुहैहयाद्याः ॥ ४ ॥

When Atri desired offspring, Bhagavān was pleased and said, “I Myself am given to you”; thus He became known as Datta. Purified by the dust of His lotus feet, Yadu, Haihaya and others attained both worldly and transcendent yogic attainments.

Word-for-word

atreḥ — of Atri; apatyam abhikāṅkṣataḥ — desiring offspring; tuṣṭaḥ — pleased; dattaḥ mayā aham — I Myself am given by Me; saḥ dattaḥ — He became Datta; yat-pāda-paṅkaja-parāga — dust of whose lotus feet; pavitra-dehāḥ — bodies purified; yoga-ṛddhim — yogic perfection; ubhayīm — of both kinds; yadu-haihaya-ādyāḥ — Yadu, Haihaya and others.

Transliteration
atrer apatyam abhikāṅkṣata āha tuṣṭo
datto mayāham iti yad bhagavān sa dattaḥ
yat-pāda-paṅkaja-parāga-pavitra-dehā
yogarddhim āpur ubhayīṁ yadu-haihayādyāḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

दत्तात्रेयावतारम् आह । मयाहम् एव तुभ्यं दत्त इति यद् यत आह ततः स नाम्ना दत्तो जातः । स्व-भक्तेभ्यो योगैश्वर्य-दानं तच्-चरितं च दर्शयति । यस्य पाद-पङ्कजयोः परागस् तेन पवित्रा देहा येषां ते । उभयीम् ऐहिकीम् आमुष्मिकीं च भुक्ति-मुक्ति-रूपां वा ॥४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This is Dattātreya. His name follows the Lord’s statement that He had “given” Himself to Atri. The verse also displays His gift of yogic opulence to devotees; “both kinds” can mean attainments in this world and the next, or enjoyment and liberation.
