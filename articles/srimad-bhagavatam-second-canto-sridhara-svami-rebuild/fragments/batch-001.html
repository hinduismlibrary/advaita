ŚB 2.5.32

यदैतेऽसङ्गता भावा भूतेन्द्रियमनोगुणाः ।
यदायतननिर्माणे न शेकुर्ब्रह्मवित्तम ॥ ३२ ॥

O best knower of Brahman, while these principles—the elements, senses, mind and their qualities—remained uncombined, they were unable to construct the bodily abode in which they could function.

Word-for-word

yadā — when; ete — these; asaṅgatāḥ — uncombined; bhāvāḥ — principles; bhūta — elements; indriya — senses; manaḥ — mind; guṇāḥ — qualities; yat-āyatana-nirmāṇe — in constructing their abode; na śekuḥ — were unable; brahma-vittama — O best knower of Brahman.

Transliteration
yadaite ’saṅgatā bhāvā
bhūtendriya-mano-guṇāḥ
yad-āyatana-nirmāṇe
na śekur brahma-vittama
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं कारणसृष्टिमुक्त्वा कार्यसृष्टिमाह । यदा एते असङ्गता अमीलिता आसन्, अत एव यदा आयतनस्य शरीरस्य निर्माणे न शेकुः ॥३२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Having described the creation of the causal principles, Brahmā now describes the creation of their effects. At first these principles were separate and had not combined; therefore they could not construct their abode, the body.

ŚB 2.5.33

तदा संहत्य चान्योन्यं भगवच्छक्तिचोदिताः ।
सदसत्त्वमुपादाय चोभयं ससृजुर्ह्यदः ॥ ३३ ॥

Then, impelled by Bhagavān’s power, they combined with one another. Taking up both primary and secondary, gross and subtle conditions, they produced the twofold cosmic embodiment.

Word-for-word

tadā — then; saṁhatya — having combined; anyonyam — with one another; bhagavat-śakti-coditāḥ — impelled by Bhagavān’s power; sat-asattvam — primary and secondary manifestation; upādāya — taking up; ubhayam — both; sasṛjuḥ — produced; adaḥ — that.

Transliteration
tadā saṁhatya cānyonyaṁ
bhagavac-chakti-coditāḥ
sad-asattvam upādāya
cobhayaṁ sasṛjur hy adaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदा सदसत्त्वं प्रधानगुणभावमुपादाय स्वीकृत्य । उभयं समष्टिव्यष्ट्यात्मकं शरीरम् ॥३३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “Sat-asattva” is taken as the condition of principal and subordinate manifestation. Accepting that condition, the principles produced the twofold body consisting of collective and individual forms.

ŚB 2.5.34

वर्षपूगसहस्रान्ते तदण्डमुदके शयम् ।
कालकर्मस्वभावस्थो जीवोऽजीवमजीवयत् ॥ ३४ ॥

After thousands upon thousands of years, the cosmic egg lay in the waters. The life-giving Supreme Person, working through time, karma and inherent nature, animated that which had been lifeless.

Word-for-word

varṣa-pūga-sahasra-ante — after thousands of groups of years; tat aṇḍam — that cosmic egg; udake — in the water; śayam — lying; kāla — time; karma — karma; svabhāva-sthaḥ — established in inherent nature; jīvaḥ — the life-giving Supreme Person; ajīvam — lifeless; ajīvayat — animated.

Transliteration
varṣa-pūga-sahasrānte
tad aṇḍam udake śayam
kāla-karma-svabhāva-stho
jīvo ’jīvam ajīvayat
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

कालकर्मस्वभावानधिष्ठाय स्थितो जीवयतीति जीवः परमात्मा अजीवमचेतनमजीवयच्चेतयति स्म ॥३४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The Supreme Self is called “jīva” here as the One who gives life. Presiding over time, karma and inherent nature, He caused the lifeless and unconscious cosmic egg to become animated.

ŚB 2.5.35

स एव पुरुषस्तस्मादण्डं निर्भिद्य निर्गतः ।
सहस्रोर्वङ्घ्रिबाह्वक्षः सहस्राननशीर्षवान् ॥ ३५ ॥

That very Puruṣa broke open the cosmic egg and emerged, possessing thousands of thighs and feet, arms and eyes, and thousands of faces and heads.

Word-for-word

saḥ eva — that very; puruṣaḥ — Cosmic Person; tasmāt — from that; aṇḍam — cosmic egg; nirbhidya — having broken open; nirgataḥ — emerged; sahasra — thousands; ūru — thighs; aṅghri — feet; bāhu — arms; akṣaḥ — eyes; ānana — faces; śīrṣavān — possessing heads.

Transliteration
sa eva puruṣas tasmād
aṇḍaṁ nirbhidya nirgataḥ
sahasrorv-aṅghri-bāhv-akṣaḥ
sahasrānana-śīrṣavān
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निर्भिद्य पृथक्कृत्य स्थित इत्यर्थः ॥३५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “Having broken it open and emerged” means that He divided it and then remained established distinctly.

ŚB 2.5.36

यस्येहावयवैर्लोकान् कल्पयन्ति मनीषिणः ।
कट्यादिभिरधः सप्त सप्तोर्ध्वं जघनादिभिः ॥ ३६ ॥

Wise seers conceive the worlds in relation to His limbs: seven lower realms from the waist downward and seven higher realms from the hips and regions above.

Word-for-word

yasya — whose; iha — here; avayavaiḥ — by the limbs; lokān — worlds; kalpayanti — conceive; manīṣiṇaḥ — the wise; kaṭi-ādibhiḥ — beginning from the waist; adhaḥ — downward; sapta — seven; ūrdhvam — upward; jaghana-ādibhiḥ — beginning from the front of the hips and above.

Transliteration
yasyehāvayavair lokān
kalpayanti manīṣiṇaḥ
kaṭy-ādibhir adhaḥ sapta
saptordhvaṁ jaghanādibhiḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदवयवैर्लोकरचनामाह—यस्येति । कटिरित्यूर्मूलयोः पश्चाद्भागः । जघनं पुरोभागः । अधः सप्तलोकानतलादीन् । ऊर्ध्वं भूरादीन् सप्त ॥३६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā describes the arrangement of the worlds upon the limbs of that form. The waist is the rear portion at the roots of the thighs, while jaghana is the front portion. Below are Atala and the other seven lower worlds; above are Bhū and the six higher worlds.

ŚB 2.5.37

पुरुषस्य मुखं ब्रह्म क्षत्रमेतस्य बाहवः ।
ऊर्वोर्वैश्यो भगवतः पद्भ्यां शूद्रो व्यजायत ॥ ३७ ॥

The brāhmaṇa is represented by the Puruṣa’s mouth, the kṣatriya by His arms, and the vaiśya by His thighs; from the feet of the Lord the śūdra was manifested.

Word-for-word

puruṣasya — of the Puruṣa; mukham — mouth; brahma — brāhmaṇa order; kṣatram — kṣatriya order; bāhavaḥ — arms; ūrvoḥ — from the thighs; vaiśyaḥ — vaiśya; bhagavataḥ — of Bhagavān; padbhyām — from the feet; śūdraḥ — śūdra; vyajāyata — manifested.

Transliteration
puruṣasya mukhaṁ brahma
kṣatram etasya bāhavaḥ
ūrvor vaiśyo bhagavataḥ
padbhyāṁ śūdro vyajāyata
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वर्णानां तत उत्पत्तिं दर्शयति—पुरुषस्येति । ब्रह्म ब्राह्मणो मुखम् इति कार्य-कारणयोर् अभेद-विवक्षयोक्तम् । बाहव इति च । क्षत्रं क्षत्रियः ॥३७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. He shows the origin of the social orders from Him. In saying that the brāhmaṇa is the mouth, effect and cause are spoken of as non-different; the same construction applies to the arms. “Kṣatram” means the kṣatriya.

ŚB 2.5.38

भूर्लोकः कल्पितः पद्भ्यां भुवर्लोकोऽस्य नाभितः ।
हृदा स्वर्लोक उरसा महर्लोको महात्मनः ॥ ३८ ॥

Bhūrloka is conceived in relation to His legs, Bhuvarloka to His navel, Svarloka to His heart, and Maharloka to the chest of the Great Being.

Word-for-word

bhūrlokaḥ — Bhūrloka; kalpitaḥ — conceived; padbhyām — from the feet or legs; bhuvarlokaḥ — Bhuvarloka; nābhitaḥ — from the navel; hṛdā — by the heart; svarlokaḥ — Svarloka; urasā — by the chest; maharlokaḥ — Maharloka; mahātmanaḥ — of the Great Being.

Transliteration
bhūrlokaḥ kalpitaḥ padbhyāṁ
bhuvarloko ’sya nābhitaḥ
hṛdā svarloka urasā
maharloko mahātmanaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इदानीम् उपासनार्थं लोक-कल्पना-भेदान् दर्शयन् सप्त-लोक-पक्षम् आह द्वाभ्याम् । भूर्-लोकः पातालम् आरभ्य । पद्भ्यां कटि-पर्यन्ताभ्याम् ॥३८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. For purposes of meditation, he presents different mappings of the worlds onto the cosmic form. These two verses give the seven-world scheme. Here Bhūrloka is reckoned beginning with Pātāla, and “from the legs” means the region from the feet up to the waist.

ŚB 2.5.39

ग्रीवायां जनलोकोऽस्य तपोलोकः स्तनद्वयात् ।
मूर्धभिः सत्यलोकस्तु ब्रह्मलोकः सनातनः ॥ ३९ ॥

Janaloka is placed at His neck, Tapoloka in relation to His two breasts, and Satyaloka at His head; Brahmaloka, however, is eternal.

Word-for-word

grīvāyām — at the neck; janalokaḥ — Janaloka; tapolokaḥ — Tapoloka; stana-dvayāt — from the pair of breasts; mūrdhabhiḥ — by the head; satyalokaḥ — Satyaloka; brahmalokaḥ — Brahmaloka; sanātanaḥ — eternal.

Transliteration
grīvāyāṁ janaloko ’sya
tapolokaḥ stana-dvayāt
mūrdhabhiḥ satyalokas tu
brahmalokaḥ sanātanaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

स्तन-द्वयाद् इत्य् उपासनार्थत्वाद् ऊर्ध्वाधो-भाग-वैपरीत्यं न दोषः । यद् वा, स्तनच्-छब्दं कुर्वद् यद् ओष्ठ-द्वयं तस्माद् इत्य् अर्थः । ब्रह्म-लोको वैकुण्ठाख्यः, सनातनो नित्यः, न तु सृज्य-प्रपञ्चान्तर्वर्तीत्य् अर्थः ॥३९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Because this arrangement is for meditation, a reversal of higher and lower bodily positions is no defect. Alternatively, the phrase can be understood with reference to the pair of lips. Brahmaloka here is Vaikuṇṭha: eternal and not contained within the created cosmos.

ŚB 2.5.40

तत्कट्यां चातलं क्लृप्तमूरुभ्यां वितलं विभोः ।
जानुभ्यां सुतलं शुद्धं जङ्घाभ्यां तु तलातलम् ॥ ४० ॥

Atala is assigned to the waist of the all-pervading Lord, Vitala to His thighs, pure Sutala to His knees, and Talātala to His shanks.

Word-for-word

tat-kaṭyām — at His waist; atalam — Atala; kḷptam — assigned; ūrubhyām — to the thighs; vitalam — Vitala; vibhoḥ — of the all-pervading Lord; jānubhyām — to the knees; sutalam — Sutala; śuddham — pure; jaṅghābhyām — to the shanks; talātalam — Talātala.

Transliteration
tat-kaṭyāṁ cātalaṁ kḷptam
ūrubhyāṁ vitalaṁ vibhoḥ
jānubhyāṁ sutalaṁ śuddhaṁ
jaṅghābhyāṁ tu talātalam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इदानीं चतुर्दश-लोक-पक्षं दर्शयति । तत्र जघनादिभिर् भूरादयः पूर्वोक्ता एव सप्त, कट्य्-आदिभिर् अधः सप्त-लोकान् आह—तत्-कट्याम् इति द्वाभ्याम् । शुद्धं हरि-भक्त-निवासत्वात् ॥४०-४१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. He now sets out the fourteen-world scheme. The seven worlds beginning with Bhū are associated with the upper portions; these two verses assign the seven lower worlds beginning from the waist. Sutala is called pure because it is the dwelling place of devotees of Hari.

ŚB 2.5.41

महातलं तु गुल्फाभ्यां प्रपदाभ्यां रसातलम् ।
पातालं पादतलत इति लोकमयः पुमान् ॥ ४१ ॥

Mahātala is placed at His ankles, Rasātala at the fronts of His feet, and Pātāla at their soles. In this way the Cosmic Person is contemplated as encompassing the worlds.

Transliteration
mahātalaṁ tu gulphābhyāṁ
prapadābhyāṁ rasātalam
pātālaṁ pāda-talata
iti lokamayaḥ pumān
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इदानीं चतुर्दश-लोक-पक्षं दर्शयति । तत्र जघनादिभिर् भूरादयः पूर्वोक्ता एव सप्त, कट्य्-आदिभिर् अधः सप्त-लोकान् आह—तत्-कट्याम् इति द्वाभ्याम् । शुद्धं हरि-भक्त-निवासत्वात् ॥४०-४१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This verse completes the same fourteen-world mapping begun in 2.5.40; Śrīdhara comments on the pair together.
