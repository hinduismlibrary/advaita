ŚB 2.6.10

पराभूतेरधर्मस्य तमसश्चापि पश्चिमः ।
नाड्यो नदनदीनां च गोत्राणामस्थिसंहतिः ॥ १० ॥

His back is the field of defeat, irreligion and ignorance. His channels correspond to rivers and streams, and the mass of His bones to the mountains.

Word-for-word

parābhūteḥ — of defeat or frustration; adharmasya — of irreligion; tamasaḥ — of ignorance; paścimaḥ — the back; nāḍyaḥ — channels or veins; nada-nadīnām — of rivers and streams; gotrāṇām — of mountains; asthi-saṁhatiḥ — mass of bones.

Transliteration
parābhūter adharmasya
tamasaś cāpi paścimaḥ
nāḍyo nada-nadīnāṁ ca
gotrāṇām asthi-saṁhatiḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तमसोऽज्ञानस्य । पश्चिमः पृष्ठ-भागः । गोत्राणां गिरीणाम् अस्थि-संघातः ॥१०॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Tamas means ignorance; “western” means the back portion. Gotra here means mountains, corresponding to the mass of bones.

ŚB 2.6.11

अव्यक्तरससिन्धूनां भूतानां निधनस्य च ।
उदरं विदितं पुंसो हृदयं मनसः पदम् ॥ ११ ॥

The belly of the Puruṣa is known as the place of the unmanifest, of nourishing essences and oceans, and of the dissolution of beings; His heart is the seat of mind and the subtle body.

Word-for-word

avyakta — the unmanifest principle; rasa — essence, especially of food; sindhūnām — of oceans; bhūtānām — of beings; nidhanasya — of dissolution; udaram — belly; puṁsaḥ — of the Puruṣa; hṛdayam — heart; manasaḥ — of mind or subtle body; padam — seat.

Transliteration
avyakta-rasa-sindhūnāṁ
bhūtānāṁ nidhanasya ca
udaraṁ viditaṁ puṁso
hṛdayaṁ manasaḥ padam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अव्यक्तं प्रधानम्, रसोऽन्नादि-सारः, सिन्धवः समुद्रास् तेषाम् । निधनस्य लयस्य । उदरं पदं स्थानं विदितं ज्ञानिभिः । मनसोऽस्मद्-आदि-लिङ्ग-शरीरस्य ॥११॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The unmanifest is primordial nature; rasa is the essence of food and similar things, and the sindhus are oceans. Dissolution is laya. The wise know the belly as their seat, while “mind” includes the subtle bodies of beings such as ourselves.

ŚB 2.6.12

धर्मस्य मम तुभ्यं च कुमाराणां भवस्य च ।
विज्ञानस्य च सत्त्वस्य परस्यात्मा परायणम् ॥ १२ ॥

The inner consciousness of the Supreme Person is the highest seat of dharma, of Brahmā and Nārada, of the Kumāras and Rudra, and of spiritual knowledge and purified consciousness.

Word-for-word

dharmasya — of dharma; mama — of me, Brahmā; tubhyam — of you, Nārada; kumārāṇām — of the Kumāras; bhavasya — of Rudra; vijñānasya — of realized knowledge; sattvasya — of purified consciousness; parasya ātmā — the inner self of the Supreme; parāyaṇam — supreme abode.

Transliteration
dharmasya mama tubhyaṁ ca
kumārāṇāṁ bhavasya ca
vijñānasya ca sattvasya
parasyātmā parāyaṇam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तुभ्यं तव । कुमाराणां सनकादीनाम् । भवस्य श्री-रुद्रस्य । आत्मा चित्तं परम् अयनम् ॥१२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “For you” means “of you”; the Kumāras are Sanaka and the others, and Bhava is Śrī Rudra. Ātman here is the inner consciousness, the supreme seat.

ŚB 2.6.13

अहं भवान् भवश्चैव त इमे मुनयोऽग्रजाः ।
सुरासुरनरा नागाः खगा मृगसरीसृपाः ॥ १३ ॥

I, you, Rudra, these elder sages, the gods and asuras, human beings, Nāgas, birds, beasts and reptiles—all belong within this manifestation of the Puruṣa.

Word-for-word

aham — I, Brahmā; bhavān — you, Nārada; bhavaḥ — Rudra; munayaḥ agrajāḥ — elder sages; sura-asura-narāḥ — gods, asuras and humans; nāgāḥ — Nāgas; khagāḥ — birds; mṛga — animals; sarīsṛpāḥ — reptiles.

Transliteration
ahaṁ bhavān bhavaś caiva
ta ime munayo ’grajāḥ
surāsura-narā nāgāḥ
khagā mṛga-sarīsṛpāḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं तावत् परमेश्वराज् जातं विश्वं ततो न भिन्नम् । यथा कुण्डलं सुवर्णान् न पृथक् । स तु सर्व-नियन्ता सर्व-प्रकाशको नित्यम् उक्त इत्य् अर्थाद् उक्तम् । एतद् एव पुरुष-सूक्तार्थ-कथनेन द्रढयति । पुरुष एवेदं सर्वम् इत्य् अस्यार्थं दर्शयति—अहं भवान् इति सार्ध-त्रिभिः । ते तव अग्रजा इमे सनकादयो मरीच्य्-आदयश् च ॥१३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara treats 2.6.13–16 as a continuous unit: the universe arising from the Supreme is not separate from Him, just as an ornament is not separate from gold. Verse 13 begins the exposition of the Puruṣa-sūkta statement that all this is the Puruṣa.

ŚB 2.6.14

गन्धर्वाप्सरसो यक्षा रक्षोभूतगणोरगाः ।
पशवः पितरः सिद्धा विद्याध्राश्चारणा द्रुमाः ॥ १४ ॥

Gandharvas, Apsarases, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas, hosts of spirits and serpents, animals, ancestors, Siddhas, Vidyādharas, Cāraṇas and trees are likewise included.

Word-for-word

gandharva-apsarasaḥ — Gandharvas and Apsarases; yakṣāḥ — Yakṣas; rakṣaḥ — Rākṣasas; bhūta-gaṇa — hosts of spirits; uragāḥ — serpents; paśavaḥ — animals; pitaraḥ — ancestors; siddhāḥ — Siddhas; vidyādharāḥ — Vidyādharas; cāraṇāḥ — Cāraṇas; drumāḥ — trees.

Transliteration
gandharvāpsaraso yakṣā
rakṣo-bhūta-gaṇoragāḥ
paśavaḥ pitaraḥ siddhā
vidyādhrāś cāraṇā drumāḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं तावत् परमेश्वराज् जातं विश्वं ततो न भिन्नम् । यथा कुण्डलं सुवर्णान् न पृथक् । २.६.१३–१६ इत्य् एतत् सार्ध-त्रिभिः पुरुष एवेदं सर्वम् इत्य् अस्यार्थं दर्शयति ॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This verse continues Śrīdhara’s joint explanation of 2.6.13–16, expanding the list of beings that are not separate from the cosmic Puruṣa.

ŚB 2.6.15

अन्ये च विविधा जीवा जलस्थलनभौकसः ।
ग्रहर्क्षकेतवस्तारास्तडितः स्तनयित्नवः ॥ १५ ॥

So too are the many other living beings whose homes are water, land or sky, together with planets, constellations, comets, stars, lightning and thunderclouds.

Word-for-word

anye — others; vividhāḥ jīvāḥ — diverse living beings; jala-sthala-nabha-okasaḥ — dwelling in water, land and sky; graha — planets; ṛkṣa — constellations; ketavaḥ — comets; tārāḥ — stars; taḍitaḥ — lightning; stanayitnavaḥ — thunderclouds or thunderings.

Transliteration
anye ca vividhā jīvā
jala-sthala-nabhaukasaḥ
graharkṣa-ketavas tārās
taḍitaḥ stanayitnavaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

जलं च स्थलं च नभश् चौकांसि येषां ते । नभौकस इति सन्धिर् आर्षः ॥१४॥ सर्वम् इदं पुरुष एव न ततः पृथग् इत्य् अर्थः ॥१५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Those described have water, land and sky as their respective abodes; Śrīdhara notes the archaic sandhi in nabhaukasaḥ. The point of the whole sequence is that all this is the Puruṣa and is not separate from Him.

ŚB 2.6.16

सर्वं पुरुष एवेदं भूतं भव्यं भवच्च यत् ।
तेनेदमावृतं विश्वं वितस्तिमधितिष्ठति ॥ १६ ॥

Whatever has been, will be, or now exists is this very Puruṣa. He pervades the universe and at the same time transcends it beyond every finite measure.

Word-for-word

sarvam — all; puruṣaḥ eva — the Puruṣa alone; idam — this; bhūtam — past; bhavyam — future; bhavat — present; tena — by Him; āvṛtam — pervaded; viśvam — universe; vitastim — a span, figuratively a measure beyond; adhitiṣṭhati — stands beyond or transcends.

Transliteration
sarvaṁ puruṣa evedaṁ
bhūtaṁ bhavyaṁ bhavac ca yat
tenedam āvṛtaṁ viśvaṁ
vitastim adhitiṣṭhati
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं तावत् परमेश्वराज् जातं विश्वं ततो न भिन्नम् । यथा कुण्डलं सुवर्णान् न पृथक् । स तु सर्व-नियन्ता सर्व-प्रकाशको नित्यम् उक्त इत्य् अर्थाद् उक्तम् । एतद् एव पुरुष-सूक्तार्थ-कथनेन द्रढयति । पुरुष एवेदं सर्वम् इत्य् अस्यार्थं दर्शयति—अहं भवान् इति सार्ध-त्रिभिः ॥१३–१६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This closes Śrīdhara’s joint exposition of verses 13–16: although the whole universe arises from the Supreme and is not separate from Him, He remains its eternal controller and illuminator and is not confined by it.

ŚB 2.6.17

स्वधिष्ण्यं प्रतपन् प्राणो बहिश्च प्रतपत्यसौ ।
एवं विराजं प्रतपंस्तपत्यन्तर्बहिः पुमान् ॥ १७ ॥

As the sun lights its own sphere and also shines outward, so the Supreme Person illuminates and enlivens the Virāṭ both within and without.

Word-for-word

sva-dhiṣṇyam — its own sphere; pratapan — illuminating; prāṇaḥ — the sun, identified with vital power; bahiḥ — outwardly; virājam — the Virāṭ; antaḥ bahiḥ — within and without; pumān — the Supreme Person.

Transliteration
sva-dhiṣṇyaṁ pratapan prāṇo
bahiś ca pratapaty asau
evaṁ virājaṁ pratapaṁs
tapaty antar bahiḥ pumān
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एतत् स-दृष्टान्तम् आह । असौ प्राण आदित्यः असौ वा आदित्यः प्राणः इति श्रुतेः । स्व-धिष्ण्यं मण्डलं प्रकाशयन् यथा बहिश् च प्रकाशयति एवं विराड् देहं प्रकाशयन् ब्रह्माण्डम् अन्तर् बहिश् च प्रकाशयति ॥१७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The teaching is given with an analogy. Scripture identifies the sun with prāṇa: just as the sun illuminates its own orb and what lies outside it, the Puruṣa illuminates the Virāṭ-body and the cosmic egg within and without.

ŚB 2.6.18

सोऽमृतस्याभयस्येशो मर्त्यमन्नं यदत्यगात् ।
महिमैष ततो ब्रह्मन् पुरुषस्य दुरत्ययः ॥ १८ ॥

He is Lord of deathlessness and fearlessness because He transcends what is mortal and the fruits of material action. Therefore, O brāhmaṇa, the greatness of the Puruṣa is immeasurable.

Word-for-word

saḥ — He; amṛtasya — of deathlessness; abhayasya — of fearlessness; īśaḥ — Lord; martyam — mortal; annam — the enjoyed fruit of action; atyagāt — transcended; mahimā — greatness; brahman — O brāhmaṇa; puruṣasya — of the Puruṣa; duratyayaḥ — boundless, not to be surpassed.

Transliteration
so ’mṛtasyābhayasyeśo
martyam annaṁ yad atyagāt
mahimaiṣa tato brahman
puruṣasya duratyayaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नित्यम् उक्तत्वं दर्शयितुम् उतामृतत्वस्य इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह—स इति । अभयस्येति मन्त्र-गतामृत-पद-व्याख्या । अन्नेनेति पदं विभक्ति-व्यत्ययेन व्याचष्टे । मर्त्यं मरण-धर्मकम् अन्नं कर्म-फलं यस्माद् अत्यगाद् अतिक्रान्तवान् । अतो न केवलं सर्वात्मकः, किन्त्व् अमृतत्वस्य निजानन्दस्यापीश्वर इत्य् अर्थः । प्रपञ्चात्मकस्य कुतो नित्यम् उक्तत्वम् इत्य् आशङ्क्य तत्-परिहाराय एतावान् अस्य इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह—महिमेति । प्रपञ्चात्मकस्याप्य् अमृतेशत्वम् इत्य् एष महिमा दुरत्ययोऽपारः । प्रपञ्चानभिभूतत्वाद् इति भावः ॥१८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The verse explains the Puruṣa-sūkta’s declaration of His immortality. He has crossed beyond mortal karmic fruit; He is not merely all-pervading but Lord of immortal, intrinsic bliss. His greatness is boundless precisely because the cosmos does not overpower or delimit Him.

ŚB 2.6.19

पादेषु सर्वभूतानि पुंसः स्थितिपदो विदुः ।
अमृतं क्षेममभयं त्रिमूर्ध्नोऽधायि मूर्धसु ॥ १९ ॥

All beings are known to dwell within the manifested portions of the Supreme Person. Above the threefold material realm are established deathlessness, well-being and fearlessness.

Word-for-word

pādeṣu — in His portions or quarters; sarva-bhūtāni — all beings; puṁsaḥ — of the Supreme Person; sthiti-padaḥ — whose portions are abodes of existence; amṛtam — deathlessness; kṣemam — secure well-being; abhayam — fearlessness; tri-mūrdhnaḥ — above the threefold realm; mūrdhasu — in the higher regions.

Transliteration
pādeṣu sarva-bhūtāni
puṁsaḥ sthiti-pado viduḥ
amṛtaṁ kṣemam abhayaṁ
tri-mūrdhno ’dhāyi mūrdhasu
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवम् ईश्वरो नित्य-मुक्त इत्य् उक्तं, तद्-आश्रितानां भूतानां बन्ध-मोक्षौ व्यवस्थया इति दर्शयन्, पादोऽस्य विश्वा भूतानि इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह—पादेष्व् इति । तिष्ठन्त्य् अत्रेति स्थितयो भूर्-आदि-लोकास् ते पादा इव पादा अंशा यस्य, स स्थिति-पात् । तस्य पादेष्व् अंश-भूतेषु लोकेषु सर्वाञ् जीवान् विदुः । अस्येश्वर-सम्बन्धि-त्रिपाद् अमृतं नित्य-सुखं दिवि ऊर्ध्व-लोकेषु, न त्रिलोक्याम् इत्य् अर्थः । तद् एव त्रिपाच्-छब्दोक्तं त्रैविध्यं दर्शयन्न् आह—त्रयाणां लोकानां मूर्धा महर्-लोकस् तस्य मूर्धानस् तद्-उपरितन-लोकास् तेषु यथाक्रमम् अमृतादिकम् अधायि निहितम् ॥१९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The Lord is eternally free, while beings dependent on Him experience bondage and liberation according to their station. The worlds are His “quarters” or portions; higher than the three-world material sphere, deathlessness and the other liberated conditions are established in the superior realms.
