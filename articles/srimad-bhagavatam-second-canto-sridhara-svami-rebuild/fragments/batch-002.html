ŚB 2.5.42

भूर्लोकः कल्पितः पद्भ्यां भुवर्लोकोऽस्य नाभितः ।
स्वर्लोकः कल्पितो मूर्ध्ना इति वा लोककल्पना ॥ ४२ ॥

Alternatively, Bhūrloka is contemplated in His legs, Bhuvarloka at His navel, and Svarloka in the upper region culminating in His head. This is another arrangement of the worlds for meditation.

Word-for-word

bhūrlokaḥ — Bhūrloka; kalpitaḥ — contemplated or assigned; padbhyām — at the legs; bhuvarlokaḥ — Bhuvarloka; asya — of Him; nābhitaḥ — at the navel; svarlokaḥ — Svarloka; mūrdhnā — at the head; iti vā — or thus; loka-kalpanā — conception of the worlds.

Transliteration
bhūrlokaḥ kalpitaḥ padbhyāṁ
bhuvarloko ’sya nābhitaḥ
svarlokaḥ kalpito mūrdhnā
iti vā loka-kalpanā
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

त्रि-लोक-पक्षम् आह । भूर्-लोकः पातालादि-सहितः ॥४२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. He now gives the three-world scheme. In this reckoning Bhūrloka includes Pātāla and the other lower worlds.

2.6 Puruṣa-sūkta Confirmed

ŚB 2.6.1

ब्रह्मोवाच
वाचां वह्नेर्मुखं क्षेत्रं छन्दसां सप्त धातवः ।
हव्यकव्यामृतान्नानां जिह्वा सर्वरसस्य च ॥ १ ॥

Brahmā said: His mouth is the field of speech and of Fire; the seven bodily constituents correspond to the Vedic metres. His tongue is the field of offerings for gods and ancestors, of food, and of every taste.

Word-for-word

brahmā uvāca — Brahmā said; vācām — of speech; vahneḥ — of Fire; mukham — mouth; kṣetram — field or seat; chandasām — of Vedic metres; sapta dhātavaḥ — the seven bodily constituents; havya-kavya — offerings to gods and ancestors; amṛta-annānām — food; jihvā — tongue; sarva-rasasya — of every taste.

Transliteration
brahmovāca
vācāṁ vahner mukhaṁ kṣetraṁ
chandasāṁ sapta dhātavaḥ
havya-kavyāmṛtānnānāṁ
jihvā sarva-rasasya ca
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इदानीं वैराजस्य विभूतिः स-प्रपञ्चम् अनुवर्ण्यते । यथाग्निशक्तिर्दहस्तेन दग्धोऽग्निर्दग्ध इत्युच्यते तद्वदनापीति । वैकुण्ठस्था वाग्वह्न्यादयस्तत्तदंशभूताश्चिन्मया नित्या एव तेषां चिद्विलासत्वात् । जगद्वर्तिनस्त्वमी तत्तद्विभूतयो मायिका अनित्या एवेति व्यवस्थितिः । एतस्य हरेः ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The manifestations of the Virāṭ are now described together with the cosmos. Śrīdhara distinguishes the eternal, conscious divine prototypes—speech, Fire and the rest—from their temporary material manifestations within the world.

ŚB 2.6.2

सर्वासूनां च वायोश्च तन्नासे परमायणे ।
अश्विनोरोषधीनां च घ्राणो मोदप्रमोदयोः ॥ २ ॥

His two nostrils are the supreme seat of the vital airs and of Vāyu. His faculty of smell is the seat of the Aśvins, medicinal plants, and both general and particular fragrance.

Word-for-word

sarva-asūnām — of all vital airs; vāyoḥ — of Vāyu; tat-nāse — His nostrils; parama-āyane — supreme seat; aśvinoḥ — of the Aśvins; oṣadhīnām — of medicinal herbs; ghrāṇaḥ — smell; moda-pramodayoḥ — of fragrance in general and particular.

Transliteration
sarvāsūnāṁ ca vāyoś ca
tan-nāse paramāyaṇe
aśvinor oṣadhīnāṁ ca
ghrāṇo moda-pramodayoḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सर्वासूनाम् अस्मद्-आदि-प्राणानां तस्य नासे नासारन्ध्रे परमायने उत्तम-क्षेत्रे । मोद-प्रमोदयोश् च सामान्य-विशेष-गन्धयोर् घ्राणो घ्राणेन्द्रियं परमायनम् ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “All vital airs” includes the life-breaths of beings such as ourselves. The nostrils are their excellent field; smell is likewise the supreme seat of ordinary and distinctive fragrances.

ŚB 2.6.3

रूपाणां तेजसां चक्षुर्दिवः सूर्यस्य चाक्षिणी ।
कर्णौ दिशां च तीर्थानां श्रोत्रमाकाशशब्दयोः ॥ ३ ॥

His faculty of sight is the field of forms and illumination, while His two eyes are the field of heaven and the sun. His ears correspond to the directions and sacred revelation, and hearing to ether and sound.

Word-for-word

rūpāṇām — of forms; tejasām — of lights; cakṣuḥ — sight; divaḥ — of heaven; sūryasya — of the sun; akṣiṇī — two eyes; karṇau — ears; diśām — of directions; tīrthānām — of sacred teachings; śrotram — hearing; ākāśa-śabdayoḥ — of ether and sound.

Transliteration
rūpāṇāṁ tejasāṁ cakṣur
divaḥ sūryasya cākṣiṇī
karṇau diśāṁ ca tīrthānāṁ
śrotram ākāśa-śabdayoḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

रूपाणां तेजसां रूप-प्रकाशानां च चक्षुर् इन्द्रियम् । अक्षिणी नेत्र-गोलके । सर्वत्र षष्ठ्य्-अन्तानां प्रथमान्तं क्षेत्रं द्रष्टव्यम् । कर्णौ श्रोत्राधिष्ठाने । श्रोत्रम् इन्द्रियम् ॥३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The eye-faculty is the field of form and illumination; the two eyes are the physical globes. Throughout this passage, the nominative bodily member is to be understood as the “field” of the items expressed in the genitive.

ŚB 2.6.4

तद्गात्रं वस्तुसाराणां सौभगस्य च भाजनम् ।
त्वगस्य स्पर्शवायोश्च सर्वमेधस्य चैव हि ॥ ४ ॥

His bodily surface is the receptacle of the essential powers of things and of auspiciousness. His skin is the field of touch, of moving air, and of sacrifice.

Word-for-word

tat-gātram — His bodily surface; vastu-sārāṇām — of the essences or powers of things; saubhagasya — of auspiciousness; bhājanam — receptacle; tvak — skin; sparśa — touch; vāyoḥ — of air; sarva-medhasya — of every sacrifice.

Transliteration
tad-gātraṁ vastu-sārāṇāṁ
saubhagasya ca bhājanam
tvag asya sparśa-vāyoś ca
sarva-medhasya caiva hi
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तस्य गात्रं शरीरं वस्तूनां ये सारांशास् तेषां भाजनं स्थानम् । अस्य त्वक्-स्पर्शस्य वायोश् चेत्य् अर्थः । सर्वस्य मेधस्य यज्ञस्य ॥४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. His body is the location of the essential portions or powers of things. The skin is the field of touch and air, and medha here means sacrifice.

ŚB 2.6.5

रोमाण्युद्भिज्जजातीनां यैर्वा यज्ञस्तु सम्भृतः ।
केशश्मश्रुनखान्यस्य शिलालोहाभ्रविद्युताम् ॥ ५ ॥

His body hairs are the field of plant life, especially the vegetation used to furnish sacrifice. His hair, beard and nails correspond respectively to clouds, lightning, and stones and metals.

Word-for-word

romāṇi — body hairs; udbhijja-jātīnām — of plant-born species; yaiḥ — by which; yajñaḥ — sacrifice; sambhṛtaḥ — furnished; keśa — head hair; śmaśru — facial hair; nakhāni — nails; śilā — stones; loha — metals; abhra — clouds; vidyutām — of lightning.

Transliteration
romāṇy udbhijja-jātīnāṁ
yair vā yajñas tu sambhṛtaḥ
keśa-śmaśru-nakhāny asya
śilā-lohābhra-vidyutām
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उद्भिज्ज-जातीनां सर्व-वृक्षाणाम् । यैर् वृक्षैर् यज्ञः सम्यक् साधितस् तेषाम् एव वा । केशा मेघानां क्षेत्रम्, पूर्वं तथोक्तेः । श्मश्रूणि विधुताम् । पाद-कर-नखानि शिलालोहानाम् इति सादृश्याद् ऊह्यम् ॥५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The plant-born class means all trees, or specifically those by which sacrifice is properly accomplished. The hair is the field of clouds, the beard of lightning, and the hand- and toenails of stones and metals, understood through resemblance.

ŚB 2.6.6

बाहवो लोकपालानां प्रायशः क्षेमकर्मणाम् ॥ ६ ॥

His arms are principally the field of the guardians of the worlds and of those whose function is protection and preservation.

Word-for-word

bāhavaḥ — arms; loka-pālānām — of the guardians of the worlds; prāyaśaḥ — principally; kṣema-karmaṇām — of those engaged in protection and preservation.

Transliteration
bāhavo loka-pālānāṁ
prāyaśaḥ kṣema-karmaṇām
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

क्षेम-कर्मणां पालन-कर्तृणाम् । विक्रमः पादन्यासो भूर्-आदि-लोकानाम् आस्पदम् आश्रयः । भूर्-आदि-शब्दानाम् अव्ययत्वात् षष्ठ्या लुक् । क्षेमो लब्ध-रक्षणम् ॥६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. “Those whose work is kṣema” are protectors. Śrīdhara also anticipates the next half of the construction: the Lord’s stride is the supporting field of the worlds beginning with Bhū, and kṣema means preservation of what has been obtained.

ŚB 2.6.7

विक्रमो भूर्भुवः स्वश्च क्षेमस्य शरणस्य च ।
सर्वकामवरस्यापि हरेश्चरण आस्पदम् ॥ ७ ॥

Word-for-word

vikramaḥ — stride; bhūḥ bhuvaḥ svaḥ — Bhū, Bhuvar and Svar; kṣemasya — of preservation; śaraṇasya — of refuge or protection from fear; sarva-kāma — every desire; varasya — blessing or boon; hareḥ — of Hari; caraṇaḥ — foot; āspadam — seat, shelter.

Transliteration
vikramo bhūr bhuvaḥ svaś ca
kṣemasya śaraṇasya ca
sarva-kāma-varasyāpi
hareś caraṇa āspadam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

शरणं भयाद् रक्षणम् । सर्वेषां कामानां वरस्य वरणस्यापि हरेर् अङ्घ्रिर् आस्पदम् ॥७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śaraṇa means protection from fear. Hari’s foot is also the seat of the choosing or granting of all desired boons.

ŚB 2.6.8

अपां वीर्यस्य सर्गस्य पर्जन्यस्य प्रजापतेः ।
पुंसः शिश्न उपस्थस्तु प्रजात्यानन्दनिर्वृतेः ॥ ८ ॥

The Lord’s generative organ is the field of water, semen, generation, rain and Prajāpati; the corresponding sense faculty is the field of the pleasure and relief associated with procreation.

Word-for-word

apām — of water; vīryasya — of semen; sargasya — of generation; parjanyasya — of rain; prajāpateḥ — of Prajāpati; puṁsaḥ — of the Puruṣa; śiśnaḥ — generative organ; upasthaḥ — generative sense; prajāti — procreation; ānanda — pleasure; nirvṛteḥ — of relief or satisfaction.

Transliteration
apāṁ vīryasya sargasya
parjanyasya prajāpateḥ
puṁsaḥ śiśna upasthas tu
prajāty-ānanda-nirvṛteḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वीर्यस्य शुक्रस्य । शिश्नोऽधिष्ठानम् । उपस्थस् त्व् इन्द्रियम् । प्रजात्य्-आनन्दः सन्तानार्थः सम्भोगस् तेन या निर्वृतिस् तापहानिस् तस्याः ॥८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Vīrya means semen. The śiśna is the physical seat, while upastha is the sense faculty. The pleasure connected with procreation is intercourse for the sake of offspring, and nirvṛti is the relief of distress produced through it.

ŚB 2.6.9

पायुर्यमस्य मित्रस्य परिमोक्षस्य नारद ।
हिंसाया निरृतेर्मृत्योर्निरयस्य गुदं स्मृतः ॥ ९ ॥

Word-for-word

pāyuḥ — evacuating faculty; yamasya — of Yama; mitrasya — of Mitra; parimokṣasya — of evacuation; nārada — O Nārada; hiṁsāyāḥ — of violence; nirṛteḥ — of Nirṛti, misfortune; mṛtyoḥ — of death; nirayasya — of hell; gudam — rectal region; smṛtaḥ — is described.

Transliteration
pāyur yamasya mitrasya
parimokṣasya nārada
hiṁsāyā nirṛter mṛtyor
nirayasya gudaṁ smṛtaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

परिमोक्षस्य मल-त्यागस्य पायुर् इन्द्रियम् । गुदः स्थानम् । निरृतेर् अलक्ष्म्याः ॥९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Parimokṣa is the discharge of waste; pāyu is the corresponding sense faculty, while guda is its bodily location. Nirṛti is interpreted as misfortune or inauspiciousness.
