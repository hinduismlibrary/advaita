ŚB 2.6.20

पादास्त्रयो बहिश्चासन्नप्रजानां य आश्रमाः ।
अन्तस्त्रिलोक्यास्त्वपरो गृहमेधोऽबृहद्व्रतः ॥ २० ॥

The three higher quarters lie beyond the three material worlds and are the destinations of those who do not generate further worldly progeny; the remaining quarter, within the three worlds, is the sphere of the householder who has not taken the greater vow of celibacy.

Word-for-word

pādāḥ trayaḥ — three quarters; bahiḥ — beyond; aprajānām — of those not producing progeny; āśramāḥ — stations of life; antaḥ tri-lokyāḥ — within the three worlds; aparaḥ — the other quarter; gṛha-medhaḥ — householder attached to household life; abṛhat-vrataḥ — without the greater vow of celibacy.

Transliteration
pādās trayo bahiś cāsann
aprajānāṁ ya āśramāḥ
antas tri-lokyās tv aparo
gṛha-medho ’bṛhad-vrataḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एतद् एव वैचित्र्यम् अधिकारि-भेदेनोपपादयन् त्रिपाद्-ऊर्ध्व इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह । न प्रजायन्ते पुत्रादि-रूपेणेत्य् अप्रजा नैष्ठिक-ब्रह्मचारि-वान-प्रस्थयतयस् तेषाम् आश्रमास् त्रिलोक्या बहिर् आसन्, गृह-मेधस् त्व् अन्तः । यस्माद् अबृहद्-व्रतो ब्रह्मचर्य-व्रत-रहितः ॥२०॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara explains the diversity by differences of qualification. The lifelong celibate, forest-dweller and renunciant are called apraja; their destinations are beyond the three worlds, whereas the ordinary householder remains within them.

ŚB 2.6.21

सृती विचक्रमे विश्वङ् साशनानशने उभे ।
यदविद्या च विद्या च पुरुषस्तूभयाश्रयः ॥ २१ ॥

The all-pervading conscious being traverses both paths—the path connected with enjoyment and the path connected with release. Their respective causes are ignorance and knowledge, while the Supreme Person is the ultimate support of both.

Word-for-word

sṛtī — two paths; vicakrame — traverses; viśvaṅ — one moving everywhere; sāśana-anaśane — connected with enjoyment and non-enjoyment; ubhe — both; avidyā — ignorance; vidyā — knowledge; puruṣaḥ — Supreme Person; ubhaya-āśrayaḥ — support of both.

Transliteration
sṛtī vicakrame viśvaṅ
sāśanānaśane ubhe
yad avidyā ca vidyā ca
puruṣas tūbhayāśrayaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अयं चाधिकार-भेद एकस्यैवावस्था-भेदेन न त्व् अत्यन्त-भिन्न-विषय इति दर्शयन् ततो विष्वङ् इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह । विविधं सुष्ठु अञ्चतीति विष्वङ् पुरुषः क्षेत्र-ज्ञः सृती मार्गौ दक्षिणोत्तरौ विचक्रमे चलति स्म । कथम्भूते सृती, साशनानशने भोगापवर्ग-प्राप्ति-साधन-भूते । अत्र हेतुत्वेन पुनर् विशेषणम् । यद्य् अतः अविद्या कर्म-रूपा एका । विद्या च तत्-साधनोपासना-रूपान्या ॥२१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The distinction is one of conditions and qualification, not of wholly separate subjects. Śrīdhara understands the two courses as the southern and northern paths, serving respectively enjoyment and liberation, with karmic ignorance and liberating knowledge as their causes.

ŚB 2.6.22

यस्मादण्डं विराड् जज्ञे भूतेन्द्रियगुणात्मकः ।
तद् द्रव्यमत्यगाद् विश्वं गोभिः सूर्य इवातपन् ॥ २२ ॥

From Him arose the cosmic egg and the Virāṭ composed of elements, senses and qualities; yet the Lord transcends that entire material manifestation while illuminating it, like the sun shining by its rays.

Word-for-word

yasmāt — from whom; aṇḍam — cosmic egg; virāṭ — universal form; bhūta-indriya-guṇa-ātmakaḥ — composed of elements, senses and qualities; tat dravyam — that material manifestation; atyagāt — transcended; viśvam — universe; gobhiḥ — by rays; sūryaḥ iva — like the sun; ātapan — illuminating.

Transliteration
yasmād aṇḍaṁ virāḍ jajñe
bhūtendriya-guṇātmakaḥ
tad dravyam atyagād viśvaṁ
gobhiḥ sūrya ivātapan
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवं विराड् अन्तर्वर्तिनां फल-वैचित्र्यम् उक्त्वा तत्-कारणेश्वरस्य तद्-वैलक्षण्यं दर्शयितुं तस्माद् विराड् अजायत इत्य् अस्यार्थम् आह । यस्माद् अण्डं जज्ञे, तत्र च भूतेन्द्रिय-गुणात्मको विराट् च जज्ञे, स ईश्वरस् तद् विश्वं विराड् देहं द्रव्यम् अण्डं च अतिक्रान्तवान् । सूर्य इवेति पूर्वोक्त एव दृष्टान्तः ॥२२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. After describing the differing results experienced within the Virāṭ, Śrīdhara stresses the transcendence of its cause: the Lord gives rise to the cosmic egg and universal body but remains beyond both.

ŚB 2.6.23

यदास्य नाभ्यान्नलिनादहमासं महात्मनः ।
नाविदं यज्ञसम्भारान् पुरुषावयवानृते ॥ २३ ॥

When I arose from the lotus growing from the navel of that Great Person, I found no ingredients for sacrifice apart from the limbs of the Puruṣa Himself.

Word-for-word

yadā — when; asya nābhyāt — from His navel; nalināt — from the lotus; aham āsam — I came to be; mahātmanaḥ — of the Great Person; na avidam — I did not find; yajña-sambhārān — ingredients of sacrifice; puruṣa-avayavān ṛte — apart from the limbs of the Puruṣa.

Transliteration
yadāsya nābhyān nalinād
aham āsaṁ mahātmanaḥ
nāvidaṁ yajña-sambhārān
puruṣāvayavān ṛte
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नन्व् एवं पुरुष एव चेत् सर्वं तर्हि यज्ञस्य तत्-साधनानां चापृथग्भावाद् यज्ञैः पुरुषाराधनं न सिध्येद् इत्य् आशङ्क्य तत्-परिहाराय यत् पुरुषेण हविषा इत्य् आदि-मन्त्रार्थं संगृह्य दर्शयति—यदेत्य् आदिना । अस्येति विराड्-अन्तर्यामिणः । तस्यैव प्रकृतत्वात् । नाभौ भवं नाभ्यं तस्मात् । यज्ञस्य संभारान् साधनानि नापश्यम् ॥२३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. An objection is raised: if everything is the Puruṣa, how can sacrifice worship Him using supposedly separate materials? Brahmā’s own example answers it—the sacrificial means themselves arise from Him.

ŚB 2.6.24

तेषु यज्ञस्य पशवः सवनस्पतयः कुशाः ।
इदं च देवयजनं कालश्चोरुगुणान्वितः ॥ २४ ॥

Among the required sacrificial provisions were the sacrificial animals, wooden posts and kuśa grass, together with the sacrificial ground and the proper season endowed with its many suitable qualities.

Word-for-word

teṣu — among those ingredients; yajñasya paśavaḥ — sacrificial animals; sa-vanaspatayaḥ — wooden sacrificial posts; kuśāḥ — kuśa grass; idam deva-yajanam — this sacrificial ground; kālaḥ — proper time or season; uru-guṇa-anvitaḥ — endowed with many appropriate qualities.

Transliteration
teṣu yajñasya paśavaḥ
sa-vanaspatayaḥ kuśāḥ
idaṁ ca deva-yajanaṁ
kālaś coru-guṇānvitaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तदा तेषु सम्भारेषु साध्येषु सत्सु पुरुषावयवैर् एते संभाराः सम्भृताः इति चतुर्थेनान्वयः । वनस्पतयो यूपाः । देव-यजनं यज्ञ-भूमिः । इदं चेति वचनाद् यज्ञार्हे स्थाने उपविष्टः कथयतीति गम्यते । बहु-गुणान्वितो वसन्तादि-कालः ॥२४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. These are part of the provisions later said to have been assembled from the Puruṣa’s limbs. Vanaspatayaḥ are the sacrificial posts, deva-yajana the sacrificial ground, and the qualified time includes seasons such as spring.

ŚB 2.6.25

वस्तून्योषधयः स्नेहा रसलोहमृदो जलम् ।
ऋचो यजूंषि सामानि चातुर्होत्रं च सत्तम ॥ २५ ॥

Other requirements were utensils, grains and herbs, clarified butter, honey and other essences, metals, earth and water, together with the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas and the fourfold priestly service, O best of the good.

Word-for-word

vastūni — utensils; oṣadhayaḥ — grains and herbs; snehāḥ — clarified butter and other fats; rasa — honey or essences; loha — metals; mṛdaḥ — earth; jalam — water; ṛcaḥ — Ṛg Veda hymns; yajūṁṣi — Yajur Veda formulas; sāmāni — Sāma Veda chants; cātuḥ-hotram — fourfold priestly service; sattama — O best of the good.

Transliteration
vastūny oṣadhayaḥ snehā
rasa-loha-mṛdo jalam
ṛco yajūṁṣi sāmāni
cātur-hotraṁ ca sattama
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वस्तूनि पात्रादीनि । ओषधयो व्रीह्य्-आदयः । स्नेहा घृतादयः । रसा मधुरादयः । सुवर्णादीनि लोहानि । मृदश् च जलं च । चातुर्होत्रं होत्रादिकं कर्म ॥२५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara specifies vessels, rice and other grains, ghee and related substances, sweet essences, gold and other metals, earth and water; the fourfold hotṛ-service denotes the ritual work of the four principal priests.

ŚB 2.6.26

नामधेयानि मन्त्राश्च दक्षिणाश्च व्रतानि च ।
देवतानुक्रमः कल्पः सङ्कल्पस्तन्त्रमेव च ॥ २६ ॥

Also required were the names of the sacrifices and deities, mantras, priestly gifts, vows, the prescribed sequence of deities, ritual manuals, the formal resolve and the detailed method of performance.

Word-for-word

nāma-dheyāni — ritual names; mantrāḥ — mantras; dakṣiṇāḥ — priestly gifts; vratāni — vows; devatā-anukramaḥ — sequence of deities; kalpaḥ — ritual manual; saṅkalpaḥ — formal resolve; tantram — prescribed procedure.

Transliteration
nāma-dheyāni mantrāś ca

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नामधेयानि ज्योतिष्टोमादीनि । ऋग्-आदीनाम् उक्तत्वान् मन्त्रा इति स्वाहा-कारादयः । देवतानाम् अनुक्रम उद्देशः । कल्पो बौधायनादि-कर्म-पद्धति-ग्रन्थः । अनेनाहं यक्ष्य इति संकल्पः । तन्त्रम् अनुष्ठान-प्रकारः ॥२६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The names include rites such as Jyotiṣṭoma; since the Vedic collections were already mentioned, “mantras” here includes formulas such as svāhā. Kalpa is a ritual manual, saṅkalpa the resolve to sacrifice, and tantra the procedure.

ŚB 2.6.27

गतयो मतयश्चैव प्रायश्चित्तं समर्पणम् ।

The ritual movements and contemplations, expiation and final dedication were also required. All these sacrificial provisions I assembled from the limbs of the Puruṣa.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The gatis include ritual steps such as the Viṣṇu-strides; the matis are meditations on the deities, and samarpaṇa is the dedication of the completed rite to Bhagavān.

ŚB 2.6.28

Thus equipped with sacrificial materials derived from the Puruṣa’s own limbs, I worshiped that very Supreme Person—the Lord who is sacrifice itself—by means of those same materials.

Transliteration
iti sambhṛta-sambhāraḥ

tenaivāyajam īśvaram
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सम्भृताः सम्पादिताः सम्भारा येन सोऽहम् । तेनैव यज्ञेनैव । अनेन यज्ञेन यज्ञम् अयजन्त इति मन्त्रार्थः सूचितः ॥२८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Brahmā had obtained and assembled the provisions; with the sacrifice itself he worshiped the sacrificial Puruṣa. Śrīdhara identifies this as an exposition of the Puruṣa-sūkta statement that the gods worshiped sacrifice by sacrifice.

ŚB 2.6.29

Thereafter these nine brothers of yours, the lords of created beings, performed sacrifice with concentrated attention to the Puruṣa, who is manifest through forms such as Indra yet in Himself remains unmanifest.

Word-for-word

tataḥ — thereafter; te bhrātaraḥ — your brothers; ime — these; prajānām patayaḥ — lords of created beings; nava — nine; ayajan — worshiped by sacrifice; vyaktam — manifest; avyaktam — unmanifest; puruṣam — the Puruṣa; su-samāhitāḥ — with full concentration.

Transliteration
tatas te bhrātara ime
prajānāṁ patayo nava

तेन देवा अयजन्त इत्य् अस्यार्थं स-प्रपञ्चं दर्शयति—तत इति द्वाभ्याम् । व्यक्तम् इन्द्रादि-रूपेण । अव्यक्तं स्वतः । अनेन पुरुषं जातम् अग्रतः इत्य् अस्यार्थो दर्शितः ॥२९॥
