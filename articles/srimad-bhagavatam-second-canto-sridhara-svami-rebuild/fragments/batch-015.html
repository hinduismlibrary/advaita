ŚB 2.9.3

यर्हि वाव महिम्नि स्वे परस्मिन् कालमाययोः ।
रमेत गतसम्मोहस्त्यक्त्वोदास्ते तदोभयम् ॥ ३ ॥

When the jīva, freed from bewilderment, delights in its own true glory situated beyond time and Māyā, it gives up both false notions—“I” and “mine”—and abides in its fullness.

Word-for-word

yarhi — when; vāva — indeed; mahimni sve — in one’s own true glory; parasmin — in that which is beyond; kāla-māyayoḥ — time and Māyā; rameta — one delights; gata-sammohaḥ — freed from bewilderment; tyaktvā — abandoning; udāste — abides; tadā — then; ubhayam — both, “I” and “mine.”

Transliteration
yarhi vāva mahimni sve
parasmin kāla-māyayoḥ
rameta gata-sammohas
tyaktvodāste tadobhayam
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत एव भक्ति-योगेन तन्-निरासे सति मोक्षोऽपि घटत इत्य् आह—यर्हीति । वाव-शब्द एवार्थे । स्व एव महिम्नि यदा रमेत । तद् एवाह । काल-माययोः पुरुष-प्रकृत्योः परस्मिन् । तदोभयम् अहं ममेति च त्यक्त्वा उदास्ते पूर्ण-रूपेणावतिष्ठते ॥३॥

ata eva bhakti-yogena tan-nirāse sati mokṣo ’pi ghaṭata ity āha—yarhīti | vāva-śabda evārthe | sva eva mahimni yadā rameta | tad evāha | kāla-māyayoḥ puruṣa-prakṛtyoḥ parasmin | tadobhayam ahaṁ mameti ca tyaktvā udāste pūrṇa-rūpeṇāvatiṣṭhate ||3||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. bhakti-yogena tan-nirāse — when that illusion is removed by bhakti-yoga; mokṣaḥ api ghaṭate — liberation becomes possible; svake mahimni — in one’s own true glory; kāla-māyayoḥ parasmin — beyond time and Māyā; aham mama iti — “I” and “mine”; tyaktvā — abandoning; pūrṇa-rūpeṇa avatiṣṭhate — one stands in one’s fullness.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Bhakti removes the bewilderment responsible for material identification. Liberation is therefore not the destruction of the self but its full establishment beyond the false pair of “I” and “mine.”

ŚB 2.9.4

आत्मतत्त्वविशुद्ध्यर्थं यदाह भगवानृतम् ।
ब्रह्मणे दर्शयन् रूपमव्यलीकव्रतादृतः ॥ ४ ॥

For purification of understanding concerning the self, Bhagavān—honored by Brahmā’s sincere vow—revealed His true form to Brahmā and taught him the truth.

Word-for-word

ātma-tattva-viśuddhi-artham — for purification of knowledge of the self; yat āha — what He spoke; bhagavān — Bhagavān; ṛtam — truth, reality; brahmaṇe — to Brahmā; darśayan — showing; rūpam — His form; avyalīka-vrata — sincere, nondeceptive vow; ādṛtaḥ — honored, worshiped.

Transliteration
ātma-tattva-viśuddhy-arthaṁ
yad āha bhagavān ṛtam
brahmaṇe darśayan rūpam
avyalīka-vratādṛtaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यच् चोक्तं परमेश्वरस्यापि देह-सम्बन्धाविशेषात् कथं तद्-भक्त्या मोक्षः स्याद् इति ... तत्राह—आत्मनो जीवस्य तत्त्व-विशुद्ध्य्-अर्थं तत्त्व-ज्ञानार्थं तद् भवत्य् एव । ... ऋतं सत्यं चिद्-घनं रूपं दर्शयन् । दर्शने हेतुः—अव्यलीकेन व्रतेन तपसा आदृतः सेवितः सन् । अयं भावः—जीवस्याविद्यया मिथ्या-रूप-देह-सम्बन्धः, ईश्वरस्य तु योगमायया चिद्-घन-लीला-विग्रहाविर्भाव इति महान् विशेषः ॥४॥

yac coktaṁ parameśvarasyāpi deha-sambandhāviśeṣāt kathaṁ tad-bhaktyā mokṣaḥ syād iti ... tatrāha—ātmano jīvasya tattva-viśuddhy-arthaṁ tattva-jñānārthaṁ tad bhavaty eva | ... ṛtaṁ satyaṁ cid-ghanaṁ rūpaṁ darśayan | darśane hetuḥ—avyalīkena vratena tapasā ādṛtaḥ sevitaḥ san | ayaṁ bhāvaḥ—jīvasyāvidyayā mithyā-rūpa-deha-sambandhaḥ, īśvarasya tu yogamāyayā cid-ghana-līlā-vigrahāvirbhāva iti mahān viśeṣaḥ ||4||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. ātmano jīvasya — of the jīva; tattva-jñāna-artham — for true knowledge; ṛtam satyam cid-ghanam rūpam — the true, wholly conscious form; avyalīkena vratena — by a sincere vow; jīvasya avidyayā — for the jīva, through ignorance; īśvarasya yogamāyayā — for the Lord, through Yogamāyā; cid-ghana-līlā-vigraha — His wholly conscious pastime-form.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. This supplies the decisive distinction requested by Parīkṣit: the jīva’s material body-relation is produced by ignorance, whereas the Lord’s manifested form is a wholly conscious līlā-vigraha revealed through Yogamāyā.

ŚB 2.9.5

स आदिदेवो जगतां परो गुरुः
स्वधिष्ण्यमास्थाय सिसृक्षयैक्षत ।
तां नाध्यगच्छद् दृशमत्र सम्मतां
प्रपञ्चनिर्माणविधिर्यया भवेत् ॥ ५ ॥

That first deva, the exalted teacher of the worlds, sat upon his own lotus-seat and contemplated creation. Yet he could not discover the faultless insight by which the ordered construction of the cosmos could be carried out.

Word-for-word

saḥ — he; ādi-devaḥ — the first deva, Brahmā; jagatām paraḥ guruḥ — exalted teacher of the worlds; sva-dhiṣṇyam āsthāya — taking his own lotus-seat; sisṛkṣayā — with desire to create; aikṣata — considered; tām na adhyagacchat — did not attain that; dṛśam — insight; sammatām — reliable, approved; prapañca-nirmāṇa-vidhiḥ — method of constructing the cosmos; yayā bhavet — by which it might occur.

Transliteration
sa ādi-devo jagatāṁ paro guruḥ
svadhiṣṇyam āsthāya sisṛkṣayaikṣata
tāṁ nādhyagacchad dṛśam atra sammatāṁ
prapañca-nirmāṇa-vidhir yayā bhavet
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

भगवद्-भजनाद् एव तत्त्व-ज्ञानम् इत्य् एतत् प्रपञ्चयिष्यन् ब्रह्मणोऽपि तत्त्व-ज्ञानं तत्-प्रसादाद् एवेति दर्शयितुम् इतिहासम् आह—स इत्य्-आदिना । परो गुरुर् भक्ति-रहस्योपदेष्टा । स्वधिष्ण्यं पद्मम् आस्थाय अधिष्ठाय ... ऐक्षत तत् कथं स्रष्टव्यम् इत्य् आलोचितवान् । तां दृशं प्रज्ञाम् अत्र सृष्टि-विषये संमताम् अव्यभिचारिणीम् । विधिः प्रकारः ॥५॥

bhagavad-bhajanād eva tattva-jñānam ity etat prapañcayiṣyan brahmaṇo ’pi tattva-jñānaṁ tat-prasādād eveti darśayitum itihāsam āha—sa ity-ādinā | paro gurur bhakti-rahasyopadeṣṭā | svadhiṣṇyaṁ padmam āsthāya adhiṣṭhāya ... aikṣata tat kathaṁ sraṣṭavyam ity ālocitavān | tāṁ dṛśaṁ prajñām atra sṛṣṭi-viṣaye sammatām avyabhicāriṇīm | vidhiḥ prakāraḥ ||5||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. bhagavad-bhajanāt eva tattva-jñānam — true knowledge comes from worship of Bhagavān; tat-prasādāt eva — only by His grace; paraḥ guruḥ — the supreme teacher; bhakti-rahasya-upadeṣṭā — instructor in the secret of bhakti; svadhiṣṇyam padmam — his lotus-seat; dṛśam prajñām — the needed insight; vidhiḥ prakāraḥ — method means procedure.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Even Brahmā does not obtain complete creative knowledge merely from his exalted office. Śrīdhara introduces the episode to show that tattva-jñāna comes by the Lord’s grace through bhajana.

ŚB 2.9.6

स चिन्तयन् द्व्यक्षरमेकदाम्भस्युपाशृणोद् द्विर्गदितं वचो विभुः ।
स्पर्शेषु यत् षोडशमेकविंशं निष्किञ्चनानां नृप यद् धनं विदुः ॥ ६ ॥

While Brahmā was thus reflecting, he heard nearby in the waters a two-syllable command repeated twice: the sixteenth and twenty-first consonants joined as tapa—the treasure of those who possess nothing.

Word-for-word

saḥ cintayan — he, while thinking; dvi-akṣaram — two syllables; ekadā — once; ambhasi — in the water; upāśṛṇot — heard nearby; dviḥ-gaditam — spoken twice; vacaḥ — a word; sparśeṣu — among the consonants; ṣoḍaśam — the sixteenth; ekaviṁśam — the twenty-first; niṣkiñcanānām — of those without possessions; dhanam — treasure; viduḥ — they know.

Transliteration
sa cintayan dvy-akṣaram ekadāmbhasy
upāśṛṇod dvir-gaditaṁ vaco vibhuḥ
sparśeṣu yat ṣoḍaśam ekaviṁśaṁ
niṣkiñcanānāṁ nṛpa yad dhanaṁ viduḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सृष्टिं चिन्तयन् कदाचित् द्व्य्-अक्षरं वचोऽम्भसि उपाशृणोत् उप समीपे श्रुतवान् । ते अक्षरे दर्शयति । कादयो मावसानाः स्पर्शाः तेषु यत् षोडशं त-कारो यच् चैकविंशं प-कारः ... निष्किंचनानां त्यक्त-धनानां धनं ... तच् च द्विर्-गदितं तप तपेति । लोट्-मध्य-पुरुषैकवचनम् ॥६॥

sṛṣṭiṁ cintayan kadācit dvy-akṣaraṁ vaco ’mbhasi upāśṛṇot upa samīpe śrutavān | te akṣare darśayati | kādayo māvasānāḥ sparśāḥ teṣu yat ṣoḍaśaṁ ta-kāro yac caikaviṁśaṁ pa-kāraḥ ... niṣkiñcanānāṁ tyakta-dhanānāṁ dhanaṁ ... tac ca dvir-gaditaṁ tapa tapeti | loṭ-madhya-puruṣaika-vacanam ||6||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. dvy-akṣaram vacaḥ — a two-syllable utterance; upa samīpe — nearby; ṣoḍaśam ta-kāraḥ — the sixteenth is ta; ekaviṁśam pa-kāraḥ — the twenty-first is pa; niṣkiñcanānām dhanam — the wealth of the possessionless; tapa tapa — “perform tapas, perform tapas”; loṭ — imperative form.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The mysterious two syllables resolve into the imperative tapa. Repeated with emphasis, it directs Brahmā toward tapas—the true wealth of one who has renounced all lesser possessions.

ŚB 2.9.7

निशम्य तद्वक्तृदिदृक्षया दिशो विलोक्य तत्रान्यदपश्यमानः ।

Hearing it, Brahmā looked in every direction, wishing to see the speaker, but saw no one else. Returning firmly to his lotus-seat and recognizing the instruction as beneficial, he fixed his mind upon tapas.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं निशम्य तस्य वचसो वक्तुर् दिदृक्षया ततः प्रचलितः सन् दिशो विलोक्य पुनः स्वधिष्ण्यम् आस्थाय केनचित् प्रत्यक्षं नियुक्त इव तच् चात्मनो हितं विमृश्य तपसि मनो धृतवान् इत्य् अर्थः ॥७॥

Śrīdhara word-for-word. vaktur didṛkṣayā — wishing to see the speaker; diśaḥ vilokya — having looked in the directions; punaḥ svadhiṣṇyam āsthāya — returning to his own seat; pratyakṣam niyukta iva — as though directly commissioned; ātmano hitam — beneficial for himself; tapasi mano dhṛtavān — he fixed his mind on tapas.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Although the speaker remained unseen, Brahmā accepted the command as though personally delivered, recognized its good purpose, and gave his mind wholly to the prescribed tapas.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तयोस् ताभ्यां मिश्रं सत्त्वं च न प्रवर्तते, किन्तु शुद्धम् एव सत्त्वम् । काल-विक्रमो नाशः । अपरे राग-लोभादयो न सन्तीति, किम् उ वक्तव्यम् । अनुव्रताः पार्षदाः ॥१०॥

tayos tābhyāṁ miśraṁ sattvaṁ ca na pravartate, kintu śuddham eva sattvam | kāla-vikramo nāśaḥ | apare rāga-lobhādayo na santīti, kim u vaktavyam | anuvratāḥ pārṣadāḥ ||10||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. miśram sattvam na pravartate — mixed material goodness does not operate; śuddham eva sattvam — only pure sattva; kāla-vikramaḥ — time’s destructive transformation; rāga-lobha-ādayaḥ — attachment, greed and the like; anuvratāḥ — devoted followers; pārṣadāḥ — personal attendants.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Vaikuṇṭha is not simply a refined material realm. Mixed sattva and the destructive operation of time are absent; its residents are the Lord’s personal attendants established in pure existence.

ŚB 2.9.11

श्यामावदाताः शतपत्रलोचनाः पिशङ्गवस्त्राः सुरुचः सुपेशसः ।
सर्वे चतुर्बाहव उन्मिषन्मणिप्रवेकनिष्काभरणाः सुवर्चसः ॥ ११ ॥

The residents there are dark yet radiant, lotus-eyed, clad in yellow, beautiful and exceedingly delicate. All possess four arms and shine with superb jewels, pendants and ornaments.

Word-for-word

śyāma-avadātāḥ — dark-hued and radiant; śata-patra-locanāḥ — lotus-eyed; piśaṅga-vastrāḥ — clothed in yellow; su-rucaḥ — very attractive; su-peśasaḥ — very delicate; sarve — all; catuḥ-bāhavaḥ — four-armed; unmiṣat-maṇi-praveka — brilliant with excellent gems; niṣka-ābharaṇāḥ — adorned with pendants and ornaments; su-varcasaḥ — highly radiant.

Transliteration
śyāmāvadātāḥ śata-patra-locanāḥ
piśaṅga-vastrāḥ surucaḥ supeśasaḥ
sarve catur-bāhava unmiṣan-maṇi-

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्यामाश् च ते अवदाता उज्ज्वलाश् च, पद्म-नेत्राः, पीताम्बराः, सुरुचोऽतिकमनीयाः, सुपेशसोऽतिसुकुमाराः । उन्मिषन्त इव प्रभावन्तो मणि-प्रवेका मण्य्-उत्तमा येषु तानि निष्काणि पदकान्य् आभरणानि च येषां ते । सुवर्चसोऽतितेजस्विनः ... ॥११॥

śyāmāś ca te avadātā ujjvalāś ca, padma-netrāḥ, pītāmbarāḥ, suruco ’tikamanīyāḥ, supeśaso ’tisukumārāḥ | unmiṣanta iva prabhāvanto maṇi-pravekā maṇy-uttamā yeṣu tāni niṣkāṇi padakāny ābharaṇāni ca yeṣāṁ te | suvarcaso ’titejasvinaḥ ... ||11||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. śyāmāḥ — dark-hued; avadātāḥ ujjvalāḥ — radiant and bright; padma-netrāḥ — lotus-eyed; pītāmbarāḥ — yellow-clad; atikamanīyāḥ — exceedingly lovely; atisukumārāḥ — exceedingly delicate; maṇy-uttamāḥ — finest gems; atitejasvinaḥ — intensely effulgent.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara reads every feature positively as spiritual beauty and luminosity. Vishvasa’s source groups Vedabase 2.9.11–12 together as one extended verse, so this commentary continues into the next card.

ŚB 2.9.12

प्रवालवैदूर्यमृणालवर्चसः ।
परिस्फुरत्कुण्डलमौलिमालिनः ॥ १२ ॥

Their splendor shines like coral, vaidūrya gems and lotus stalks, and they are adorned with glittering earrings, crowns and garlands.

Word-for-word

pravāla — coral; vaidūrya — vaidūrya gem; mṛṇāla — lotus stalk; varcasaḥ — possessing such splendor or hue; parisphurat — glittering all around; kuṇḍala — earrings; mauli — crowns; mālinaḥ — garlanded.

Transliteration
pravāla-vaidūrya-mṛṇāla-varcasaḥ
parisphurat-kuṇḍala-mauli-mālinaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रवालादिवद् वर्चो वर्णो येषां ते, परितः स्फुरन्ति कुण्डलानि मौलयो मालाश् च सन्ति येषां ते ॥११॥

pravālādivad varco varṇo yeṣāṁ te, paritaḥ sphuranti kuṇḍalāni maulayo mālāś ca santi yeṣāṁ te ||11||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. pravāla-ādivat varcaḥ varṇaḥ — splendor or hue like coral and the other substances; paritaḥ sphuranti — glittering all around; kuṇḍalāni — earrings; maulayaḥ — crowns; mālāḥ — garlands. Textual alignment: Vishvasa numbers these two lines as the continuation of its 2.9.11, whereas Vedabase gives them their own number, 2.9.12.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The same description continues: the Vaikuṇṭha attendants possess jewel-like radiance, with earrings, crowns and garlands flashing around them. The numbering follows Vedabase here while preserving Śrīdhara’s grouped reading.
