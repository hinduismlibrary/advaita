ŚB 2.10.21

यदात्मनि निरालोकमात्मानं च दिदृक्षतः ।
निर्भिन्ने ह्यक्षिणी तस्य ज्योतिश्चक्षुर्गुणग्रहः ॥ २१ ॥

When the cosmic person found Himself and the created forms without illumination and desired to see them, His two eyes became manifest; with them appeared the sun, the faculty of sight, and the apprehension of visible form.

Word-for-word

yadā — when; ātmani — in Himself; nirālokam — without illumination; ātmānam — Himself; ca — and; didṛkṣataḥ — desiring to see; akṣiṇī — the two eyes; jyotiḥ — the luminous sun; cakṣuḥ — sight; guṇa-grahaḥ — apprehension of visible form.

Transliteration
yadātmani nirālokam
ātmānaṁ ca didṛkṣataḥ
nirbhinne hy akṣiṇī tasya
jyotiś cakṣur guṇa-grahaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निरालोकं प्रकाश-शून्यम् आसीद् इति शेषः । निर्मक्षिकम् इतिवद् अव्ययीभावः । तदा आत्मानं देहं च-काराद् अन्यच् च वस्तु दिदृक्षतः । अक्षिणी स्थानम्, ज्योतिर् आदित्यो देवता, चक्षुर् इन्द्रियम्, ततो गुणस्य रूपस्य ग्रहो ग्रहणम् । अनेन रूपं विषयो दर्शितः ॥२१॥

nirālokaṁ prakāśa-śūnyam āsīd iti śeṣaḥ | nirmakṣikam itivad avyayībhāvaḥ | tadā ātmānaṁ dehaṁ ca-kārād anyac ca vastu didṛkṣataḥ | akṣiṇī sthānam, jyotir ādityo devatā, cakṣur indriyam, tato guṇasya rūpasya graho grahaṇam | anena rūpaṁ viṣayo darśitaḥ ||21||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. nirālokam — devoid of light; akṣiṇī sthānam — the eyes are the seat; ādityaḥ devatā — the sun is the presiding deity; cakṣuḥ indriyam — sight is the faculty; rūpasya grahaḥ — apprehension of form.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The eyes are the bodily seat, the sun the presiding deity, sight the faculty, and visible form its object.

ŚB 2.10.22

बोध्यमानस्य ऋषिभिरात्मनस्तज्जिघृक्षतः ।
कर्णौ च निरभिद्येतां दिशः श्रोत्रं गुणग्रहः ॥ २२ ॥

When the self, being instructed by the seers, desired to receive that instruction, the two ears appeared; with them came the directions, the faculty of hearing, and the apprehension of sound.

Word-for-word

bodhyamānasya — of one being instructed; ṛṣibhiḥ — by the seers; tat jighṛkṣataḥ — desiring to receive that; karṇau — the ears; diśaḥ — the directions; śrotram — hearing; guṇa-grahaḥ — apprehension of sound.

Transliteration
bodhyamānasya ṛṣibhir
ātmanas taj jighṛkṣataḥ
karṇau ca nirabhidyetāṁ
diśaḥ śrotraṁ guṇa-grahaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ऋषिभिर् वेदैर् बोध्यमानस्य । तद् आत्मनः प्रबोधनं ग्रहीतुम् इच्छतः । ततो गुण-ग्रहः शब्दस्य ग्रहणम् ॥२२॥

ṛṣibhir vedair bodhyamānasya | tad ātmanaḥ prabodhanaṁ grahītum icchataḥ | tato guṇa-grahaḥ śabdasya grahaṇam ||22||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. ṛṣibhiḥ vedaiḥ — by the Vedas as seers; prabodhanam — instruction; grahītum icchataḥ — desiring to receive; śabdasya grahaṇam — apprehension of sound.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara understands the ṛṣis here as the Vedas; the faculty that receives their instruction is hearing, whose object is sound.

ŚB 2.10.23

वस्तुनो मृदुकाठिन्यलघुगुर्वोष्णशीतताम् ।
जिघृक्षतस्त्वङ्निर्भिन्ना तस्यां रोममहीरुहाः ।
तत्र चान्तरबहिर्वातस्त्वचा लब्धगुणो वृतः ॥ २३ ॥

When He desired to apprehend softness and hardness, lightness and heaviness, warmth and cold in objects, the skin became manifest. Hair arose upon it, with vegetation as its presiding correspondence, while air pervaded within and without as the carrier of touch.

Word-for-word

vastunaḥ — of an object; mṛdu-kāṭhinya — softness and hardness; laghu-guru — lightness and heaviness; oṣṇa-śītatām — warmth and cold; tvak — skin; romā — hair; mahī-ruhāḥ — vegetation; vātaḥ — air; guṇaḥ — touch-quality.

Transliteration
vastuno mṛdu-kāṭhinya-
laghu-gurv-oṣṇa-śītatām
jighṛkṣatas tvaṅ nirbhinnā
tasyāṁ roma-mahī-ruhāḥ
tatra cāntar-bahir vātas
tvacā labdha-guṇo vṛtaḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

मृदुत्वं च काठिन्यं च लघुत्वं च गुरुत्वं च आ उष्णत्वम् ईषद् उष्णत्वं च शीततां चेत्य् अर्थः । वस्तुन एतान् धर्मान् जिघृक्षतस् त्वङ् निर्भिन्ना । त्वग्-इन्द्रियाधिष्ठानं चर्म जातम् इत्य् अर्थः । तस्यां रोमाणीन्द्रियं, मही-रुहाश् च देवता जाताः । तत्र त्वच्य् अन्तर् बहिश् च वातो वृत आवृत्य स्थितः । कथम्भूतः ? त्वचा लब्धो गुणः स्पर्शो येन ॥२३॥

mṛdutvaṁ ca kāṭhinyaṁ ca laghutvaṁ ca gurutvaṁ ca ā uṣṇatvam īṣad uṣṇatvaṁ ca śītatāṁ cety arthaḥ | vastuna etān dharmān jighṛkṣatas tvaṅ nirbhinnā | tvag-indriyādhiṣṭhānaṁ carma jātam ity arthaḥ | tasyāṁ romāṇīndriyaṁ, mahī-ruhāś ca devatā jātāḥ | tatra tvacy antar bahiś ca vāto vṛta āvṛtya sthitaḥ | kathambhūtaḥ? tvacā labdho guṇaḥ sparśo yena ||23||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. mṛdutvam — softness; kāṭhinyam — hardness; laghutvam/gurutvam — lightness/heaviness; tvak — tactile seat; mahī-ruhāḥ — vegetation as deity; vātaḥ — air; sparśaḥ — touch.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara explains the tactile complex: skin is the seat, air the presiding deity, and the tactile objects include softness, hardness, weight, warmth and cold.
