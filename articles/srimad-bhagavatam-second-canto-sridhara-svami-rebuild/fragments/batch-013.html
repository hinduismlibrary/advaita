ŚB 2.8.12

यावान् कल्पो विकल्पो वा यथा कालोऽनुमीयते ।
भूतभव्यभवच्छब्द आयुर्मानं च यत् सतः ॥ १२ ॥

Please explain the extent of a kalpa and of its subsidiary periods, how time itself is inferred through the notions of past, future and present, and how the spans of life of embodied beings are measured.

Word-for-word

yāvān — how extensive; kalpaḥ — a great cosmic period; vikalpaḥ vā — or a subsidiary period; yathā — how; kālaḥ anumīyate — time is inferred or measured; bhūta — past; bhavya — future; bhavat — present; śabdaḥ — designation, word; āyuḥ-mānam — measure of lifespan; yat sataḥ — that which belongs to living beings.

Transliteration
yāvān kalpo vikalpo vā
yathā kālo ’numīyate
bhūta-bhavya-bhavac-chabda
āyur-mānaṁ ca yat sataḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं सन्देह-विपर्ययाभ्यां पृष्टम्, इदानीम् अज्ञातान् बहून् अर्थान् पृच्छति—यावान् इत्य्-आदिना । कल्पो महान् । विकल्पोऽवान्तरः । भूतादिः शब्दो यस्मात्, यस्य वाचक इति वा । सतः स्थूल-देहाभिमानिनो मनुष्य-पितृ-देवादेर् आयुषः प्रमाणम् ॥१२॥

evaṁ sandeha-viparyayābhyāṁ pṛṣṭam, idānīm ajñātān bahūn arthān pṛcchati—yāvān ity-ādinā | kalpo mahān | vikalpo ’vāntaraḥ | bhūtādiḥ śabdo yasmāt, yasya vācaka iti vā | sataḥ sthūla-dehābhimānino manuṣya-pitṛ-devāder āyuṣaḥ pramāṇam ||12||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. ajñātān bahūn arthān pṛcchati — he now asks many matters not yet known; kalpaḥ mahān — a kalpa is the great period; vikalpaḥ avāntaraḥ — a vikalpa is a subordinate period; bhūtādiḥ śabdaḥ — terms such as past; sataḥ — of embodied beings; manuṣya-pitṛ-devādeḥ — humans, ancestors, gods and others; āyuṣaḥ pramāṇam — the measure of their lifespan.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Parīkṣit now moves from doubtful points to a broad body of unknown subjects. Śrīdhara distinguishes the great cosmic period from its subordinate divisions and takes the final phrase as a request for the lifespans of humans, Pitṛs, devas and other embodied beings.

ŚB 2.8.13

कालस्यानुगतिर्या तु लक्ष्यतेऽण्वी बृहत्यपि ।
यावत्यः कर्मगतयो यादृशीर्द्विजसत्तम ॥ १३ ॥

O best of the twice-born, please explain the course of time as it is observed in both minute and vast measures, and describe the many destinations reached through karma and what each of them is like.

Word-for-word

kālasya — of time; anugatiḥ — course, progression; yā tu — that which; lakṣyate — is observed; aṇvī — minute; bṛhatī api — and also vast; yāvatyaḥ — however many; karma-gatayaḥ — destinations attained through action; yādṛśīḥ — of what kind; dvija-sattama — O best of the twice-born.

Transliteration
kālasyānugatir yā tu
lakṣyate ’ṇvī bṛhaty api
yāvatyaḥ karma-gatayo
yādṛśīr dvija-sattama
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अनुमान-प्रकारः पृष्टो विशेषं पृच्छति—कालस्येति । अनुगतिः प्रवृत्तिः । कर्म-गतयः कर्म-प्राप्याणि स्थानानि । यादृशीः यादृश्यः ॥१३॥

anumāna-prakāraḥ pṛṣṭo viśeṣaṁ pṛcchati—kālasyeti | anugatiḥ pravṛttiḥ | karma-gatayaḥ karma-prāpyāṇi sthānāni | yādṛśīḥ yādṛśyaḥ ||13||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. anumāna-prakāraḥ pṛṣṭaḥ — the mode of inference having been asked; viśeṣam pṛcchati — he asks for particulars; anugatiḥ — progression; pravṛttiḥ — movement or operation; karma-gatayaḥ — karmic destinations; karma-prāpyāṇi sthānāni — places attained by karma; yādṛśīḥ — what they are like.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Verse 12 asked how time is inferred; this verse requests the particulars. The “paths of karma” are the actual states or places reached as the results of action.

ŚB 2.8.15

भूपातालककुब्व्योमग्रहनक्षत्रभूभृताम् ।
सरित्समुद्रद्वीपानां सम्भवश्चैतदोकसाम् ॥ १५ ॥

Please describe the arising of the earth and subterranean regions, the directions and sky, planets and stars, mountains, rivers, oceans and islands, together with the beings whose homes these places are.

Word-for-word

bhū-pātāla — earth and subterranean regions; kakub — directions; vyoma — sky; graha — planets; nakṣatra — stars; bhūbhṛtām — of mountains; sarit — rivers; samudra — oceans; dvīpānām — islands; sambhavaḥ — origin, manifestation; ca — and; etat-okasām — of those whose dwellings these are.

Transliteration
bhū-pātāla-kakub-vyoma-
graha-nakṣatra-bhūbhṛtām
sarit-samudra-dvīpānāṁ
sambhavaś caitad-okasām
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

भू-पातालादीनां सम्भवः । एतान्य् ओकांसि येषां प्राणिनां, तेषां च सम्भवः । यथेति सर्वत्रानुषङ्गः ॥१५॥

bhū-pātālādīnāṁ sambhavaḥ | etāny okāṁsi yeṣāṁ prāṇināṁ, teṣāṁ ca sambhavaḥ | yatheti sarvatrānuṣaṅgaḥ ||15||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. bhū-pātālādīnām sambhavaḥ — the origin of earth, Pātāla and the rest; etāni okāṁsi — these are dwellings; yeṣām prāṇinām — for whichever living beings; teṣām ca sambhavaḥ — their origin as well; yathā — “how”; sarvatra anuṣaṅgaḥ — is to be supplied throughout.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. The request concerns not merely cosmic geography but also the origin of the beings suited to each environment. Śrīdhara notes that the implied question “how?” carries through every item in the list.

ŚB 2.8.21

सम्प्लवः सर्वभूतानां विक्रमः प्रतिसङ्क्रमः ।
इष्टापूर्तस्य काम्यानां त्रिवर्गस्य च यो विधिः ॥ २१ ॥

Please explain the arising, continuance and reabsorption of all beings, and the proper procedure for Vedic sacrifice, public works and desired rites, as well as for the three aims of dharma, prosperity and enjoyment.

Word-for-word

samplavaḥ — dissolution, or alternatively full emergence; sarva-bhūtānām — of all beings; vikramaḥ — continuance, stable progression; pratisaṅkramaḥ — return, great dissolution; iṣṭa — Vedic sacrificial work; pūrtasya — meritorious public works; kāmyānām — desired rites; tri-vargasya — of the three aims, dharma, artha and kāma; yaḥ vidhiḥ — the proper rule or method.

Transliteration
samplavaḥ sarva-bhūtānāṁ
vikramaḥ pratisaṅkramaḥ
iṣṭā-pūrtasya kāmyānāṁ
tri-vargasya ca yo vidhiḥ
Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सम्प्लवोऽवान्तर-प्रलयः । यद् वा, सम्यक् प्लवनम् उद्भवः, विक्रमः स्थितिः, प्रतिसंक्रमो महा-प्रलयः, इष्टं वैदिकं कर्म, पूर्तं स्मार्तं— “वापी-कूप-तडागादि देवता-यतनानि च । अन्न-प्रदानम् आरामः पूर्तम् इत्य् अभिधीयते ॥” इति वा । काम्यानां काम्य-कर्मणाम् । त्रि-वर्गस्य धर्मार्थ-कामस्य । विधिर् अविरोध-प्रकारः ॥२१॥

samplavo ’vāntara-pralayaḥ | yad vā, samyak plavanam udbhavaḥ, vikramaḥ sthitiḥ, pratisaṅkramo mahā-pralayaḥ, iṣṭaṁ vaidikaṁ karma, pūrtaṁ smārtaṁ—“vāpī-kūpa-taḍāgādi devatā-yatanāni ca | anna-pradānam ārāmaḥ pūrtam ity abhidhīyate ||” iti vā | kāmyānāṁ kāmya-karmaṇām | tri-vargasya dharmārtha-kāmasya | vidhir avirodha-prakāraḥ ||21||

Śrīdhara word-for-word. samplavaḥ — a subsidiary dissolution; or samyak plavanam udbhavaḥ — full emergence; vikramaḥ sthitiḥ — continuation means maintenance; pratisaṅkramaḥ mahā-pralayaḥ — return means the great dissolution; iṣṭam — Vedic ritual action; pūrtam — meritorious works prescribed in smṛti, such as wells, ponds, shrines, food-giving and gardens; kāmyānām — desire-motivated rites; tri-vargasya — dharma, artha and kāma; vidhiḥ — their non-conflicting proper procedure.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary. Śrīdhara records two ways of reading samplava: a subordinate dissolution, or—together with the next terms—the sequence of emergence, maintenance and final dissolution. He also distinguishes Vedic iṣṭa rites from pūrta, the public-benefit works of smṛti tradition.
