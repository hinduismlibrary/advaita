VP 3.15.1

ब्राह्मणान् भोजयेच् छ्राद्धे यद् गुणांस् तान् निबोध मे ॥१॥

O King, learn from me the qualifications of the Brāhmaṇas who are to be fed at a śrāddha.

Śrīdhara translation

The sage describes here the prescribed acts and procedures, the Brāhmaṇas fit for śrāddha, and what the eater and performer must avoid. Now, while about to explain the procedure of the fortnightly śrāddha, he first describes the Brāhmaṇas fit for it, beginning with “Brāhmaṇas,” in three verses. “Their qualifications” means those endowed with these qualities. Triṇāciketa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas of the second section of the Kāṭhaka. Trimadhu means one who has studied and practices the three ṛcs beginning “Madhuvātā”; alternatively, one devoted to the Madhu-vidyā taught in the Chāndogya. Trisuparṇa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas beginning “Brahmametu mām”; alternatively, the three ṛcs. Ṣaḍaṅgavit is one who studies or knows the Veda together with its six limbs.

VP 3.15.2

त्रिणाचिकेतस् त्रिमधुस् त्रिसुपर्णः षडङ्गवित् । वेदविच् छ्रोत्रियो योगी तथा वै ज्येष्ठसामगः ॥२॥

They are one who has mastered and practiced the Tri-Nāciketa teaching, one who has mastered and practiced the Tri-Madhu teaching, one versed in the Tri-Suparṇa passages, one who knows the six Vedāṅgas, a knower of the Veda, a Śrotriya, a yogin, and a singer of the Jyeṣṭha-sāman.

Śrīdhara translation

The sage describes here the prescribed acts and procedures, the Brāhmaṇas fit for śrāddha, and what the eater and performer must avoid. Now, while about to explain the procedure of the fortnightly śrāddha, he first describes the Brāhmaṇas fit for it, beginning with “Brāhmaṇas,” in three verses. “Their qualifications” means those endowed with these qualities. Triṇāciketa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas of the second section of the Kāṭhaka. Trimadhu means one who has studied and practices the three ṛcs beginning “Madhuvātā”; alternatively, one devoted to the Madhu-vidyā taught in the Chāndogya. Trisuparṇa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas beginning “Brahmametu mām”; alternatively, the three ṛcs. Ṣaḍaṅgavit is one who studies or knows the Veda together with its six limbs. Vedavit is an investigator of the meaning of the Veda; a Śrotriya practices that meaning. A yogin practices yoga. The Jyeṣṭha-sāman is the special ṛg-verse beginning “Mūrdhānaṃ diva” sung as a sāman; its singer is a Jyeṣṭha-sāmaga. The three fires beginning with Āhavanīya together with the two fires Sajya and Āvasathya are the five fires; one devoted to them is their worshipper. Alternatively, he is one who practices the five-fire meditation taught in the Vedānta, consisting of heaven, rain, earth, man, and woman.

VP 3.15.3

ऋत्विक्स्वस्रेयदौहित्रजामातृश्वशुरास् तथा । मातुलोऽथ तपोनिष्ठः पञ्चाग्न्यभिर् अतस् तथा । शिष्याः सम्बन्धिनश् चैव मातापितृरतश् च यः ॥३॥

The three fires beginning with Āhavanīya together with Sajya and Āvasathya are the five fires; one devoted to them is their worshipper. Alternatively, he is one who practices the five-fire meditation taught in the Vedānta, consisting of heaven, rain, earth, man, and woman.

VP 3.15.4

एतान् नियोजयेच् छ्राद्धे पूर्वोक्तान् प्रथमे नृप । ब्राह्मणान् पितृतुष्ट्य्अर्थम् अनुकल्पेष्व् अनन्तरान् ॥४॥

O King, invite these previously mentioned Brāhmaṇas first for the satisfaction of the ancestors; thereafter invite the others as alternatives.

He explains the distinction between the principal and alternative choices: the persons stated in the first verse are the Jyeṣṭha-sāmaga and the others. Invite them first as the principal choice, and the Ṛg-vedins and others thereafter as alternatives.

VP 3.15.5

मित्रध्रुक्कुनखी क्लीबः श्यावदन्तस् तथा द्विजः । कन्यादूषयिता वह्निवेदोज्झः सोमविक्रयी ॥५॥

A betrayer of a friend, one with malformed nails, an impotent man, one with dark teeth, one who violates a maiden, one who abandons the sacred fire or Veda without cause, and one who sells Soma are not fit for the śrāddha invitation.

Śrīdhara translation

In the absence of these, he states the prohibited persons in order to prohibit their selection. A kunakhī is one whose nails are naturally deformed; one with dark teeth has naturally black teeth. A person who is capable but abandons the Agnihotra and Vedic study is a fire-and-Veda abandoner. One who sells the Soma creeper is a Soma seller.

VP 3.15.6

अभिशस्तस् तथा स्तेनः पिशुनो ग्रामयाजकः । भृतकाध्यापकस् तद्वद् भृतकाध्यापितश् च यः ॥६॥

One accused of a grave sin, a thief, a backbiter, a village priest, a teacher who teaches for wages, and one who has been taught for wages are also unfit.

Śrīdhara translation

Abhiśasta means one accused of a great sin, whether truly or falsely; stena means a thief. Piśuna is one who secretly speaks of another’s faults. A grāmayājaka is one who officiates for ordinary people, or one maintained by a village. A hired teacher teaches for wages, and a hired student is taught for wages.

VP 3.15.52

त्रीणि श्राद्धे पवित्राणि दौहित्रः कुतपस् तिलाः । रजतस्य तथा दानं कथासङ्कीर्तनादिकम् ॥५२॥

Three things are sacred at a śrāddha: the daughter’s son, the Kutapa time, and sesame; and the mention, gift, sight, and other acts concerning silver.

Śrīdhara translation

A daughter’s son is a dauhitra; Kutapa is the eighth muhūrta. With the reading “dauhitra,” it means a special kind of ghee, explained by the verse about the cow that eats grass on the new moon. Some say dauhitra means a vessel shaped like a sword; some say Kutapa is a blanket made from goat hair. Speaking of silver, seeing it, and similar acts are also included. A silver or silver-coated vessel is called a pure vessel for the ancestors; the gift and sight of silver are praised in the Matsya and Vāyu texts.

Śrīdhara translation

The ancestral host has Soma as its support: it is the group including the Agniṣvāttas, who depend on Soma. The Moon has yoga as its support, because through the power of their yoga the Moon is replenished again. The Harivaṃśa says: “These are our ancestors, dear one, increasers of the yogins’ yoga; they first replenish Soma through the power of yoga.” The Vāyu likewise says: “The ancestors, pleased at the śrāddha and abiding in yoga, again replenish Soma through yoga, by which the three worlds live.” Therefore inviting a yogin at a śrāddha is praised.
