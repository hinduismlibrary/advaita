VP 1.16.1

कथितो भवता वंशो मानवानां महामुने । कारणं चास्य जगतो विष्णुर् एव सनातनः ॥१॥

You have recounted the lineage of the great descendants of Manu, O great sage, and told me that Viṣṇu alone is the eternal cause of this universe.

Śrīdhara translation

He asks about Prahlāda’s story after the preceding narration, beginning “what was told”—such as the accounts of human beings, Uttānapāda, Dhruva and others.

VP 1.16.2

यच् चैतद् भगवान् आह प्रह्लादं दैत्यम् उत्तमम् । ददाह नाग्निनास्त्रैश् च क्षुण्णस् तत्याज जीवितम् ॥२॥

You told me that Prahlāda, the foremost of the Daityas, was not burned by fire or overcome by weapons and, though struck, did not give up his life.

Śrīdhara translation

That which fire did not burn, even when struck by weapons; he remained unharmed even when beaten.

VP 1.16.3

जगाम वसुधा क्षोभं प्रह्लादे सलिले स्थिते । बन्धबद्धे विचलति विक्षिप्ताङ्गैः समाहता ॥३॥

When Prahlāda was bound and situated in the ocean waters, the earth shook, struck by his limbs tossed about as he moved.

Śrīdhara translation

When Prahlāda, bound by bonds, moved about, the earth shook from being struck by his tossed limbs. In the reading “viksiptāṅgaḥ samāhitaḥ,” the latter phrase connects with what follows. In the reading “bound and moving,” this is a separate sentence.
