VP 5.9.3

क्ष्वेलमानौ प्रगायन्तौ विचिन्वन्तौ च पादपान् ।
चारयन्तौ च गा दूरे व्याहरन्तौ च नामभिः ॥३॥

They roared, sang, searched among the trees, herded the cows at a distance, and called them by name.

“Roaring,” thus: the two roar like lions. The word “kṣvelā” means a lion’s roar, according to the lexicon. “Searching,” they seek places suitable for climbing trees as a game. Being at a distance, they herd the cows and call them by name.

VP 5.9.4

शुशुभाते महात्मानौ बाल-शृङ्गाव् इव र्षभौ ॥४॥

Those two great souls, with tether-ropes on their shoulders and adorned with forest garlands, shone like young horned bulls.

Śrīdhara translation

“Niryoga,” thus: the ropes by which the cows being milked are restrained are called niryoga; they are ropes binding the feet, or ropes for drawing unseen cows. Those two have these niryoga-ropes on their shoulders, like young-horned bulls; this indicates a particular age.

“Just as water consumed by the submarine fire, taking the form of snow, is cast upon the Himālaya, that same water again becomes water through contact with the Sun’s rays.”

Śrīdhara translation

The cause of the universe’s dissolution and arising is shown by an example in two verses beginning “consumed.” “Consumed”: ocean water is swallowed by the fire called the submarine fire. Carried by that fire and wind into the channels of the Sun’s rays, it is cast on the Himālaya, assumes the form of snow, and remains there; in summer, through contact with the Sun’s rays, it again attains the state of water. In the same way, as stated earlier, water that has come in the form of rivers and the like, and has been swallowed in the ocean by the submarine fire together with wind, becomes dense and reaches the state of snow. Cast upon the Himalaya through the Moon by the Sun’s rays borne in the channels of wind, it again reaches the state of liquid water when melted by summer heat. The cited verses state: “Rivers, oceans, and earth-born waters, together with waters arising from living beings, are four kinds; the Sun takes them up, O sage. Vivasvat takes up waters whose essence is liquid during eight months; then the Moon releases them in the sky through channels of wind, having reached the seasonal condition that causes snow, heat, water, and rain.”

Śrīdhara translation

“Thus,” in the same way, at dissolution the universe is consumed by you in the form of Rudra and the others, who are like the submarine fire. Dependent on you, it is cast into you, the Supreme Person who is like the Himālaya, in the form of the causal Self, and remains until dissolution. Then, when you set out for creation as Hiraṇyagarbha, you are like the Sun; through the wish to create, the gross universe again assumes the form of Virāṭ, as nature. Here there is a figure of comparison between the meanings of whole sentences. As Daṇḍin says: “When one sentence-meaning is compared with another sentence-meaning, because the words are singular or plural, that is considered a comparison of sentence-meanings.”
