# Prasannapadā, Chapter 1: editorial review

**Last updated: 20 October 2026**

## Scope and presentation

This review covers Candrakīrti’s *Prasannapadā* Chapter 1 and Nāgārjuna’s fourteen root verses (MMK 1.1–1.14). The chapter index links to a dedicated introduction page and one page per root verse. Legacy scroll fragments on the former continuous-reader URL forward to the matching verse or introduction page. Each verse page contains the Sanskrit root verse, a readable English translation, a complete word-by-word gloss, Candrakīrti’s full translated commentary for that verse, and an expandable Sanskrit text panel. The sidebar identifies the current page and highlights exactly one verse.

The word-by-word gloss covers every word of the fourteen root verses. Sandhi is unpacked where necessary; compounds are retained as units and glossed. The gloss accompanies, rather than replaces, the smoother English translation.

## Textual basis

- Nāgārjuna’s root verses: GRETIL’s standalone Sanskrit transcription, input by Douglas Bachman and revised by Richard Mahoney.
- Candrakīrti’s *Prasannapadā*: GRETIL transcription based on P. L. Vaidya’s 1960 edition; GRETIL credits proofreader Milan Shakya.
- Anne MacDonald, *In Clear Words: The Prasannapadā, Chapter One* (2015), Vol. I (critical Sanskrit edition) and Vol. II (annotated translation). The publisher states that the publication underwent anonymous international peer review.
- Stephen Batchelor, *Verses from the Center* (1994), as the attributed base for the English root verses; selected wording revisions are disclosed on the source page.

GRETIL numbers the fourteen root verses as 3–16 within its edition; this site uses the conventional chapter numbering 1.1–1.14.

## Editorial corrections and notes

- Rendered *kṣaṇabhaṅga* as “momentary destruction,” correcting “the passing of an instant.”
- Distinguished *svabhāva* (“intrinsic nature”) from *parabhāva* (“other-nature”) in MMK 1.3.
- Clarified the negative scope of MMK 1.4 with Candrakīrti’s explanation.
- Clarified the object-condition and immediate-condition terminology in MMK 1.8–1.9.
- Clarified the standard dependent-arising formula in MMK 1.10.
- Rendered *asvayaṃmaya* as “not made of themselves” in MMK 1.13. The GRETIL transcription reads *asvayaṃmayāḥ* in the first half of the verse and *asvamayebhyaḥ* in the second; both are retained rather than silently regularized.
- Explained the compound *pratyayāpratyayāḥ* in MMK 1.14 as “conditions and non-conditions.”
- Revised several English root translations for grammatical clarity while retaining the disclosed Batchelor-based editorial basis.

## Integrity checks

The repeatable validator checks that all fourteen pages exist in sequence; each has one root verse, one word-by-word gloss, one commentary block, one Sanskrit panel, a unique current-page marker, and valid local navigation. It also checks that the contents hub, introduction, and sitemap point to the verse pages rather than scroll anchors.

This is an editorial and source-alignment review, not an external peer review or a new critical edition. Ambiguous textual variants should be checked against MacDonald’s apparatus and the manuscript evidence before stronger claims are made about disputed readings.
