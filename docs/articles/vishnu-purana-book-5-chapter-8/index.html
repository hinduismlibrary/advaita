“Look at the fruits of the palm trees, whose fragrance perfumes the directions. We desire these; let them be made to fall, if it pleases you.”

“Fruits,” thus. Look at those fruits by whose fragrance the directions are made fragrant. In the reading “gandhāmodita-diśāni,” the word “diśā” is feminine; the meaning is “those whose fragrance makes the directions fragrant.”

VP 5.8.10

ततः फलान्य् अनेकानि तालाग्रान् निपतन् खरः ।
पृथिव्यां पातयामास महा-वातो घनान्इव ॥१०॥

Then, as the donkey fell from the tops of the palms, he caused many fruits to fall upon the earth, like a great wind [bringing down] clouds.

Śrīdhara translation

“Then,” thus. In the alternate reading “mahāvātor itāni vā,” the particle “vā” has the force of comparison.
