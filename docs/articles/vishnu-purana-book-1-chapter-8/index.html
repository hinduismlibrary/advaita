VP 1.8.1

कथितस् तामसः सर्गो ब्रह्मणस् ते महामुने । रुद्रसर्गं प्रवक्ष्यामि तन् मे निगदतः शृणु ॥१॥

I have described to you, O great sage, the tamasic creation of Brahmā. I shall now explain the creation of Rudra; listen as I tell it.

Śrīdhara translation

After the lineage of Adharma, situated in intelligence and opposed to Dharma, had been stated as a cause of continual dissolution, its tāmasa lineage was described. The account now turns to the creation of Nīlalohita Rudra, husband of Dakṣa’s two daughters, to continue their story. Although Khyāti would fittingly be mentioned first in the sequence Khyāti, Satyā, and Sambhūti, her story is extensive and will be told later. Alternatively, this is a sāttvika Rudra creation included in the Kumāra creation, distinct from the tāmasa Rudra creation described earlier. “Hear this” gives the connection.

VP 1.8.2

कल्पादाव् आत्मनस् तुल्यं सुतं प्रध्यायतस् ततः । प्रादुरासीत् प्रभोर् अङ्के कुमारो नीललोहितः ॥२॥

At the beginning of the Kalpa, as Brahmā meditated on a son equal to himself, the youth Nīlalohita appeared on his lap.

Śrīdhara translation

Rudra appeared crying and running; this is the etymology of the name Rudra. The Vāyu says: “They cried and ran all around; from crying and running they became known as Rudras.”

VP 1.8.5

एवम् उक्तः पुनः सोऽथ सप्तकृत्वो रुरोद वै । ततोऽन्यानि ददौ तस्मै सप्त नामानि वै प्रभुः । स्थानानि चैषाम् अष्टानां पत्नीः पुत्रांस् च वै प्रभुः ॥५॥

Then the Lord gave him seven other names, and assigned stations, wives, and sons to the eight forms.

Śrīdhara translation

Even though he is the Supreme Lord, crying and the like is a display of infancy, meant to gather the world and indicate dependence on a father. Because it is wondrous, crying serves as the basis for the etymology of his name; since the names are distinguished by the designation of crying and the like, he is said to have eight names.

The eight stations for Rudra, Bhava, and the other names are the Sun and the rest. Since they are places of manifestation, they are called bodies or forms. In an initiated Brāhmaṇa who performs the proper duties and knows the field, the Supreme Self is manifested; that is one of those forms. The Self is Śiva’s eighth form, the Supreme Self. The universe of the other forms is of Śiva’s nature. The Vāyu says: “Those who meditate on and bow to Rudra in these stations attain the highest state.” The order is Sun, water, earth, wind, and fire. Accordingly, the order of wives such as Suvarcalā is connected. Śivā is the wife of Wind, and Svāhā is the wife of Fire. The later order of sons confirms Skanda as the son of Fire; therefore the recited order “Fire, Wind” is not intended.
