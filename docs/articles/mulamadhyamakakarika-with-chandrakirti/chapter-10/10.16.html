MMK 10.16

I do not think those who teach the identity or difference of self and things are wise in the meaning of the teaching.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

ātmanaśca satattvaṃ ye bhāvānāṃ ca pṛthakpṛthak | nirdiśanti na tānmanye śāsanasyārthakovidān || 16 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Those who say that the self and things are identical, or that they exist separately from one another, do not understand the meaning of the teaching. The self is not one with the aggregates, nor an independent thing apart from them. Dependent arising avoids both permanence and annihilation: things are designated in dependence on one another, like an echo, without being intrinsically one or intrinsically different.

The commentary cites verses on the one who understands phenomena through the fire-and-fuel analysis. Even if such a person is amid the fires of passion, hatred and delusion, they are not burned by them. The Buddha is said to teach: “The sky has never been burned, though it be burned for hundreds of aeons; one who knows phenomena as like the sky is never burned in the midst of fire.” Another verse compares the arising of fire from two fire-sticks and the effort of the hands: fire arises from conditions, performs its work, and quickly ceases.

The verses continue: if a wise person searches where fire came from or where it goes, no arrival or departure is found in any direction. The aggregates, sense fields and elements, inner and outer, are empty, free of self and without a fixed support, with the nature of space. The Buddha awakened to these marks and teaches them to gods and humans. The world burns because passion and hatred are imagined through mistaken views; the commentary closes with an appeal to the Buddha, “Leader, send down the cool rain of nectar from the cloud of compassion.”
