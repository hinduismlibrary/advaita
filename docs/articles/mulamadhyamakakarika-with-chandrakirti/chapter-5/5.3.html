MMK 5.3

Characteristics do not extend to that which has no characteristics; nor to what possesses characteristics. They also cannot extend to something other than what either possesses or does not have characteristics.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

nālakṣaṇe lakṣaṇasya pravṛttirna salakṣaṇe | salakṣaṇālakṣaṇābhyāṃ nāpyanyatra pravartate || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Could a characteristic operate on something uncharacterized, on something already characterized, or on something other than either? It cannot operate on the uncharacterized, like the horn of a hare. Nor is there a purpose in marking what is already established as characterized; that would lead to an endless regress, since each further mark would itself require another mark. A third option cannot be made from “characterized” and “uncharacterized” together, since those are incompatible. Thus a characteristic does not operate in any of these ways.

If one proposes that the characteristic operates somewhere beyond both the characterized and the uncharacterized, Candrakīrti asks what that “somewhere” could be. A thing cannot be both characterized and uncharacterized in the same respect, and no independent third entity has been established. The relation between a characteristic and its object is therefore not established.
