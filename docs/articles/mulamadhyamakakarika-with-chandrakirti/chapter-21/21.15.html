MMK 21.15

If you assert the existence of things, eternalism and annihilationism will not be, because the continuity of the rising and passing of cause -effect is becoming.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

bhāvamabhyupapannasya naivocchedo na śāśvatam | udayavyayasaṃtānaḥ phalahetvorbhavaḥ sa hi || 15 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The opponent argues that a sequence of cause and fruit avoids both permanence and annihilation: causes pass away while their fruits arise, so there is continuity without an eternal entity.

Candrakīrti accepts dependent continuity as a conventional description. A cause that ceased without conditions for a result would suggest annihilation; a cause that remained unchanged would suggest permanence. Neither conclusion follows when cause and fruit are dependently designated rather than self-existing.
