MMK 23.11

Something is called "unpleasant" in dependence on the pleasant. Since that would not exist without relation to the unpleasant, therefore, the unpleasant is not tenable.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

anapekṣyāśubhaṃ nāsti śubhaṃ prajñapayemahi | yat pratītyāśubhaṃ tasmād aśubhaṃ naiva vidyate || 11 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

The same argument applies in reverse. An unpleasant thing can be identified only in relation to what is taken as pleasant; without that contrast, “unpleasant” cannot be fixed on its own.

So neither pleasant nor unpleasant is independently established. Their conventional distinction remains useful, but it cannot support an intrinsically real affliction.
