MMK 27.8

In that way, it is not other than that-which-is-clung-to nor is it that-which-is-clung-to. The self is not not that-which-is-clung-to, nor can it be ascertained as nothing.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

evaṃ nānya upādānān na copādānam eva saḥ | ātmā nāsty anupādāno nāpi nāsty eṣa niścayaḥ || 8 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Thus the self is not other than the aggregates, is not the aggregates themselves, and is not found apart from them. This does not justify a fixed conclusion that the self does not exist at all. What is designated in dependence on the aggregates is not like the son of a barren woman, who is wholly absent and cannot be designated through them. The conventional self is dependently designated; its account is explained at length in the Madhyamakāvatāra, and has also been set out at relevant places earlier in this work.
