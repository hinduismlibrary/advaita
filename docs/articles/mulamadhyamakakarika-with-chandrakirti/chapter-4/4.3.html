MMK 4.3

If a cause of form existed apart from form, it would exist as a cause without fruit; causes without fruit do not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

rūpeṇa tu vinirmuktaṃ yadi syād rūpakāraṇam | akāryakaṃ kāraṇaṃ syān nāstyakāryaṃ ca kāraṇam || 3 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

If a cause of form existed entirely apart from the resulting form, it would be a cause without an effect. But a cause is called a cause because an effect proceeds from it; without that relation to an effect, no productive activity follows. A cause without an effect would be causeless and therefore impossible, like the horn of a donkey or horse. Thus no cause independent of its effect is established.
