MMK 14.6

If the other was other than the other, then, without the other, it would be other. Without the other it would not be other. Therefore, it does not exist.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

yady anyad anyad anyasmād anyasmād apy ṛte bhavet | tad anyad anyad anyasmād ṛte nāsti ca nāsty ataḥ || 6 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Suppose a pot were other than a cloth by its own nature. Then its otherness would have to exist even without the cloth, just as something that exists independently of a comparison does not rely on that comparison to be what it is. But no such independent otherness is seen. The claim that one thing is intrinsically other than another therefore fails.

The opponent objects that, if otherness is not real, even the statement “a pot is different from a cloth” would be impossible. Candrakīrti distinguishes ordinary convention from ultimate analysis: in everyday speech a pot is called other than a cloth, but that difference cannot be found as an independent property. A seed and a tree are not treated like a pot and cloth, since they stand in a causal relation. Conventionally a person says, “I planted this tree,” while ultimate examination finds no intrinsic otherness in cause and effect.
