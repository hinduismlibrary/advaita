MMK 22.1

Not the aggregates, not other than the aggregates; the aggregates are not in him; he is not in them: the Tathagata does not possess the aggregates. What is the Tathagata?

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

skandhā na nānyaḥ skandhebhyo nāsmin skandhā na teṣu saḥ | tathāgataḥ skandhavānna katamo 'tra tathāgataḥ || 1 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

An opponent infers an unbroken stream of rebirth from the Buddha’s existence: Buddhahood, they say, could not be reached in a single life. Candrakīrti replies that this argument rests on the very ignorance it claims to overcome. If a Tathāgata existed by intrinsic nature, a long chain of lives would be required to bring him about; yet no such independently existing Tathāgata is found.

The verse tests five possible relations between the Tathāgata and the aggregates: identity, difference, aggregates located in him, him located in the aggregates, and possession of the aggregates. None can be established under examination, so no intrinsically real Tathāgata is found.
