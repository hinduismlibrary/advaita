MMK 18.9

Not known through others, peaceful, not fixed by fixations, without conceptual thought, without differentiation: these are the characteristics of suchness.

Original Sanskrit (GRETIL)

aparapratyayaṃ śāntaṃ prapañcairaprapañcitam | nirvikalpamanānārtham etattattvasya lakṣaṇam || 9 ||

Candrakīrti’s Commentary

Candrakīrti explains that reality is not learned merely from another person’s words; it must be realized directly. It is peaceful because conceptual proliferation has ceased, and nonconceptual because thought cannot make it an object without imposing a mark.

The description “not differentiated” means that reality is not divided into inherently separate kinds. Its nature is one taste of emptiness, even though conventional language describes many things.
