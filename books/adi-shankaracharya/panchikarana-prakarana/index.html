The waking state

1

ॐ पञ्चीकृतपञ्चमहाभूतानि तत्कार्यं च सर्वं विराडित्युच्यते । एतत्स्थूलशरीरमात्मनः । इन्द्रियैरर्थोपलब्धिर्जागरितम् । तदुभयाभिमान्यात्मा विश्वः । एतत्त्रयमकारः ॥ १॥

The five great elements in their fivefold-combined form, together with all their effects, are called Virāṭ. This is the Self’s gross body. The apprehension of objects through the senses is the waking state. The Self identified with both is Viśva. These three correspond to the syllable A.

The subtle body

2

अपञ्चीकृतपञ्चमहाभूतानि पञ्चतन्मात्राणि तत्कार्यं च पञ्च प्राणाः दशेन्द्रियाणि मनोबुद्धिश्चेति सप्तदशकं लिङ्गं भौतिकं हिरण्यगर्भ इत्युच्यते । एतत्सूक्ष्मशरीरमात्मनः ॥ २॥

The five great elements before fivefold combination, the five subtle elements, and their effects—the five vital airs, ten senses, mind, and intellect—form the seventeenfold subtle body called Hiraṇyagarbha. This is the Self’s subtle body.

Dream and Taijasa

3

करणेषूपसंहृतेषु जागरितसंस्कारजः प्रत्ययः सविषयः स्वप्न इत्युच्यते । तदुभयाभिमान्यात्मा तैजसः । एतत्त्रयमुकारः ॥ ३॥

When the instruments are withdrawn, a cognition born from impressions of waking, still appearing with objects, is called dream. The Self identified with both is Taijasa. These three correspond to the syllable U.

The causal body

4

शरीरद्वयकारणमात्माज्ञानं साभासमव्याकृतमित्युच्यते । एतत्कारणशरीरमात्मनः । तच्च न सन्नासन्नापि सदसन्न भिन्नं नाभिन्नं नापि भिन्नाभिन्नं कुतश्चित् । न निरवयवं न सावयवं नोभयं किन्तु केवलब्रह्मात्मैकत्वज्ञानापनोद्यम् ॥ ४॥

The Self’s ignorance, which causes the two bodies and is associated with appearance, is called the unmanifest. This is the Self’s causal body. It is neither existent nor nonexistent, neither both nor, in any respect, different or nondifferent; it is neither partless nor made of parts nor both. It is removed only by knowledge of the oneness of Brahman and the Self.

Transliteration

Deep sleep and Prājña

5

सर्वप्रकारज्ञानोपसंहारे बुद्धेः कारणात्मनाऽवस्थानं सुषुप्तिः । तदुभयाभिमान्यात्मा प्राज्ञः । एतत्त्रयम्मकारः ॥ ५॥

When every kind of cognition has subsided, the intellect abides in its causal condition; that is deep sleep. The Self identified with both is Prājña. These three correspond to the syllable M.

Transliteration

The Self as Om

6

अकार उकारे उकारो मकारे मकार ओङ्कारे ओङ्कारोऽहम्येव । अहमात्मा साक्षी केवलश्चिन्मात्रस्वरूपः नाज्ञानं नापि तत्कार्यं किन्तु नित्यशुद्धबुद्धमुक्तसत्यस्वभावं परमानन्दाद्वयं प्रत्यग्भूतचैतन्यं ब्रह्मैवाहमस्मीत्यभेदेनावस्थानं समाधिः ॥ ६॥

A is resolved into U, U into M, and M into Om; Om is I alone. I am the Self, the witness, whose nature is consciousness alone—not ignorance or its effect, but inward consciousness, nondual supreme bliss, ever pure, awake, free, and of the nature of reality. Abiding without distinction as “I am Brahman” is samādhi.

Transliteration

Scriptural statements

7

तत्त्वमसि ब्रह्माहमस्मि प्रज्ञानमानन्दं ब्रह्म अयमात्मा ब्रह्म इत्यादिश्रुतिभ्यः ॥ ७॥

“That thou art,” “I am Brahman,” “Consciousness is Brahman,” and “This Self is Brahman”—such are the scriptural declarations [on which this recognition rests].
