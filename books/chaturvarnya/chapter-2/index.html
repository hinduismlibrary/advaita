The theory of two varṇas, and responses to it

The two-varṇa claim

According to what was said, “Having slain the Dasyus, he protected the Ārya varṇa” (Ṛg Veda Saṃhitā 3.34.9) and “He hid away the lowly Dāsa varṇa” (Ṛg Veda Saṃhitā 2.12.4), these two statements establish only two varṇas. Although words such as Brāhmaṇa and the rest occur in the Vedas, they do not refer to varṇas. The Ārya varṇa is fair and white; the Dāsa group is dark and black. The common saying is that the twice-born are fair and the Śūdra is dark, so that mixture would indeed result. Wherever, in the Purāṇas and elsewhere, a word such as Brāhmaṇa appears to denote a jāti, it should be understood as based on a designation. “Sub-varṇa,” “sub-jāti,” and “designation” are synonymous, as in “physician,” “astrologer,” and “judge.” The distinction is that jāti is enduring, remaining as long as its substratum, and characterized by common birth, whereas a sub-jāti is temporary and does not remain as long as its substratum.

Ārya and Dāsa

Only the words Ārya and Dāsa, like the word “human,” signify jāti; words such as Brāhmaṇa do not. In former times, those who were civilized and lived in towns and villages were called Āryas, while those who, though detached, lived in forests and mountain caves and were uncivilized were called non-Āryas. Thus two jātis were distinguished by differences in birthplace, customs, and the like. Among the non-Āryas too, a twofold division arose according to whether they followed the Āryas. Those who followed the Āryas were Dāsas; those who did not were Dasyus. The former were called Śūdras, and the latter Mlecchas. Thus there are five varṇas here. Since these five classes all pertain to human beings, the expression “five peoples” (pañcajana), a synonym for humanity, applies to them. Among these five peoples, Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, and Vaiśya are the three Āryas, distinguished by differences of qualities and actions; Dāsa and Dasyu are the two non-Āryas. This position arises from ignorance of the purport of the Vedas and other śāstras, since the fourfold varṇa has been established on the authority of the Vedas, consisting of mantras and Brāhmaṇa texts.

Ārya as excellence

In the phrase “Ārya varṇa,” however, the word Ārya denotes not a jāti but only excellence; you too have said this. Accordingly, the Chāndogya Upaniṣad says: “Those here whose conduct is delightful [are born] in a Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, or Vaiśya womb” (5.10). In agreement with this Śruti, only the three varṇas are said to belong to the Ārya varṇa. The singular is intended to refer to a jāti. The fourth has been stated to be the Dāsa varṇa, because service is its principal duty. Thus the Vyāsa Smṛti states that Ārya-ness rests on qualities alone: “One who is wise, content, peaceful, truthful, self-controlled, giving, generous, and compassionate is an Ārya by these eight qualities.”

Brāhmaṇa and the meaning of varṇa

What is said in reply—that although words such as Brāhmaṇa occur in the Vedas, they are not varṇas because the word varṇa is not used there—is worthless. If Brāhmaṇas and the rest are not varṇas, the word varṇa would have no referent. Nor can its referent be supplied by the word Ārya, since you accept that Ārya means only excellence. The word yoni in the phrase “birth in a Brāhmaṇa womb” also establishes that it refers to a jāti, since in expressions such as “born of a dog” the word yoni is certainly used for a jāti. For this very reason, the Amarakośa says that varṇa is used for the twice-born and for white and the like, and thus states that the word varṇa is used for the twice-born and the rest. You too have accepted Ārya as a synonym for jāti, and have accepted “Ārya varṇa,” “twice-born varṇa,” and “highest varṇa.” But to say “Ārya varṇa” means “white varṇa” and the like is simply a mistake, since it contradicts the acceptance that the word denotes a jāti.

Colour and the guṇas

White and the other varṇas do not refer only to colour, but also to qualities such as sattva. In the words “red, white, and black” (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.40.11), white is sattva because it illuminates and is clear; red is rajas, whose nature is passion and colouring; and black is tamas, which covers and obstructs. Thus, although Viṣṇu’s complexion is dark, he is white because of his sattva nature. Likewise, although Vyāsa, Śuka, Cāṇakya, and the others are dark-complexioned, they should be understood as white because of their sattva nature. Otherwise, only Englishmen would be Brāhmaṇas, and not dark-complexioned Indians, since their darkness would entail their being Śūdras.

Jāti and designation

By this, the claim that words such as Brāhmaṇa do not denote a jāti but only a designation is also refuted. On your reasoning, Ārya would likewise mean a designation, which would contradict what you accept. Just as you say that words such as Brāhmaṇa denote a designation, you also accept that Ārya denotes a designation because of the eight qualities. Then how does your own statement that Ārya denotes a jāti not contradict itself? The grammatical tradition, in statements such as “Brāhmaṇa denotes a jāti” (6.1.17), establishes that the word Brāhmaṇa denotes a jāti; this has also been explained elsewhere in detail.

The Ārya and non-Ārya distinction

Moreover, according to your reasoning, the difference between Ārya and non-Ārya is based only on such distinctions as dwelling in towns or in forests. Even by your reasoning, therefore, the use of a jāti designation is not justified there. Following the Āryas and the like is also only a designation. Thus even the Dāsa varṇa is not established. Therefore, in agreement with the Vedic Mantras and Brāhmaṇas, only the twice-born is the highest varṇa; the Śūdra is a lower varṇa; and one distinct from these is without a varṇa. By this same account, the expression “five peoples” also accords with Manu.
